## 1. ASX:ATG, Articore Group (reopened)

- Price A$0.355, market value about A$104m ([ASX company data](https://asx.api.markitdigital.com/asx-research/1.0/companies/ATG/header)).
- Where found: the watch list. It closed at its A$0.345 trigger on 10 November, before the December quarter update, and the evening desk marked it reopened (research/desk_log.md, research/picks/INDEX.md). The only announcements since the 6 November bid that set the trigger are two director notices for RSU plan issues and conversions (Naseema Sparks, [11 Nov](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261111/pdf/076hd8sg0m4gq8.pdf); Robin Mendelson, [13 Nov](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261113/pdf/076l6r18bx0mgj.pdf)), not buys and not news.
- Why the price could be wrong: the deep dive's middling value was A$0.39 against a price that has drifted to the trigger with no new information, so the market may be marking down a cost-led turnaround before the Christmas half that has carried all of its profit (research/picks/ATG.md).
- Hard floor: none. The A$40.5m of cash is all float (free cash about minus A$5.2m at June 2026, research/picks/ATG.md), and the value-pick level is about A$0.22.
- What Cam might read better: an online marketplace (Redbubble, TeePublic) whose profit now comes from cost cuts rather than sales; Cam's multi-site retail eye on whether cutting paid marketing is shrinking the customer base.
- The question: is gross profit after paid marketing holding at -4% to +3% in constant currency through the Christmas quarter, or is the sales decline speeding up? The deep dive is not re-run here; the reopen means it is re-run against today's price and the file's conditions.

## 2. ASX:GEM, G8 Education (a special situation)

- Price A$0.18, market value about A$144m ([ASX company data](https://asx.api.markitdigital.com/asx-research/1.0/companies/GEM/header)).
- Where found: the watch list's news and the week's announcements. On 12 November G8 received a non-binding A$0.26 cash scheme proposal from Majority Capital Partners, with a scrip option, bank "highly confident" and equity commitment letters, and up to six weeks' exclusivity; the directors say they intend to recommend it unanimously if a binding deal is signed ([proposal and process deed](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261112/pdf/076j2yjqrjg2r6.pdf)). On 13 November Allan Gray, manager of 19.59%, said it supports the proposal and intends to take scrip for half ([Allan Gray](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261113/pdf/076kwhtf32hxfv.pdf)). It is not binding, so it isn't in the deal sleeve.
- Why the price could be wrong: at A$0.18 the market prices roughly a 45% to 50% chance of a binding deal (taking the 8 Nov deep dive's middling A$0.09 as "nothing happens"), with the board and the largest holder already on side.
- Hard floor: none in the assets (the 8 Nov deep dive valued it at A$0.04 to A$0.20 on its own; research/picks/GEM.md). The deep dive is within three months, but the price has risen about 80% since (A$0.10 on 11 Nov), so it qualifies.
- What Cam might read better: a large childcare centre network, regulated, with a Fair Work Ombudsman case and covenant waivers: what a buyer's confirmatory due diligence will find in centre occupancy, wages and the regulator's view.
- The question: what are the odds that Majority Capital signs a binding scheme at or near A$0.26 after due diligence, given the covenant position and the court case, and what does G8 fall to if it walks away?

## 3. ASX:CTT, Cettire

- Price A$0.315, market value about A$128m ([ASX company data](https://asx.api.markitdigital.com/asx-research/1.0/companies/CTT/header)).
- Where found: the sweep's top name (director notices after a 66% fall; a holder that isn't a fund building a stake). Founder and managing director Dean Mintz bought 11,437,140 shares on market on 30 September for A$2,733,476, about A$0.239 each, taking him to 148.6m shares ([Appendix 3Y, 1 Oct](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261001/pdf/074t79swqmz54w.pdf)); his stake has gone from 32.98% to 38.98% (reports/holder_builds.md). UDN Eden Management has since built to 6.32% ([form 604, 3 Nov](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261103/pdf/0765k946dzw55v.pdf)).
- Why the price could be wrong: a founder who already holds about 39% is buying more with his own money after a 66% fall, which is either a strong signal on value or the start of a creep toward control at a low price.
- Hard floor: net cash of about A$32m (sweep, from the exchange's company data), plus a non-binding term sheet to factor €16.1m of VAT receivable for at least €13.7m (A$22.2m) ([market update, 29 Sep](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260929/pdf/074p9zw6s7pq29.pdf)); the founder's own price, about A$0.239.
- What Cam might read better: online luxury retail and how far discounting, returns and duties are eating margin.
- The question: is Mintz positioning to take it private cheaply (the creep rule lets him add 3% every six months), and does the 18 November AGM trading update show the business still burning cash?

## 4. NZX:WHS, The Warehouse Group

- Price NZ$0.695, market value about NZ$232m (reports/research_screen.csv, reports/research_rank.md, NZX closes to 12 Nov).
- Where found: the week's NZX announcements, and Cam's own ground (New Zealand consumer, multi-site retail). The Q1 FY27 update on 13 November: group sales NZ$678.8m (+0.7%), same-store sales +0.1%, gross margin 33.5% (+110 basis points), The Warehouse's margin +170 basis points to 37.1% and Warehouse Stationery's +240 to 47.7%, foot traffic -1.1% ([NZX, 13 Nov](https://www.nzx.com/announcements/483608)).
- Why the price could be wrong: the market values a NZ$3bn-a-year retailer at about 8% of sales on FY26's thin NZ$11.2m profit (research/picks/INDEX.md, quick look of 1 Nov), while each point of gross margin on that sales base is worth roughly NZ$30m a year before costs.
- Hard floor: none found yet; the balance sheet and any owned property are for the deep dive to check.
- What Cam might read better: whether store-level discipline and range changes at The Warehouse are real margin gains that last through the festive quarter, or promotional timing; how the move away from grocery and the soft big-ticket electronics at Noel Leeming play out.
- The question: does the margin recovery carry into FY27 profit well above FY26's, once costs (wages, rent, the higher interest rates) are counted? The 1 Nov quick look was not a deep dive and no longer counts as coverage.

Four directors then bought on market: Douglas Ritchie 120,000 shares for A$96,115 on 2 Nov ([notice](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261103/pdf/0765mhh2f458nf.pdf)), Andrew Lloyd 25,500 on 3 Nov, Paul Lucas 19,000 on 4 Nov, Jennifer Purdie 25,000 for A$20,250 on 3 Nov ([notice](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261106/pdf/0769lps1kzkzd8.pdf)). - Why the price could be wrong: a fund sold out after a weather and channel problem that looks temporary, and the directors bought straight after at about A$0.80.
- Hard floor: none in assets; the directors' price, about A$0.80. Balance sheet: cash A$16.8m and secured debt US$24.8m (A$35.6m) at 30 September ([quarterly](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261029/pdf/075y30tpq2d987.pdf)), small against the market value.
- What Cam might read better: little; a single-mine bauxite exporter.
- The question: what does Bauxite Hills earn at a normal 7m-tonne year and today's bauxite price, and was 2026's shortfall a one-off? The directors' buys are small (about A$190,000 in all), so the signal is modest.
