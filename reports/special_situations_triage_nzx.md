## The rules used (checked against the regulators' own guides)

- **New Zealand Takeovers Code** (KMD, TGG, WIN, BRW and DGL are all NZ companies, KMD and WIN also listed on the ASX). No one may go above 20% of the votes except by a full offer, a partial offer, an acquisition or allotment approved by the other holders, or a scheme approved by the holders (75% in each interest class plus a majority of all votes) and the High Court; a holder of 90% or more can (or must) buy out the rest ([Takeovers Panel, Basic Guide for Shareholders, July 2024](https://takeovers.govt.nz/assets/Guides/Basic-Guide-for-Shareholders-July-2024.pdf)). A holder of more than 50% and less than 90% may buy up to 5% of the votes in any 12 months, measured from its lowest holding in that 12 months, and associates can't pool holdings to use this rule ([Takeovers Panel, Creeping Acquisitions guidance note](https://www.takeovers.govt.nz/guidance/guidance-notes/creeping)). Buying control of a company that itself holds more than 20% of a Code company is also caught; the Panel normally exempts it only where the Code company is under 25% of the upstream group's value ([Guidance Note on Upstream Acquisitions, 7 April 2026](https://www.takeovers.govt.nz/assets/GuidanceNotes/UpstreamAcquisitions/Guidance-Note-on-Upstream-Acquisitions-7-April-2026.pdf)).
- **Australia, Chapter 6** (CDP and ATA). No acquisition taking a holder from 20% or below to above 20% except under section 611; the creep exception (item 9) lets a holder that has had at least 19% for six months rise by no more than 3 percentage points in any six months; there is no restriction above 90%, where compulsory acquisition applies; Chapter 6 covers listed trusts as well as companies ([ASIC Regulatory Guide 6, July 2026, RG 6.4, 6.9, 6.24, 6.41 to 6.46](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/ghyeyc1q/rg6-published-30-july-2026.pdf)). This is ASIC's guide; the search didn't turn up an Australian Takeovers Panel note that sets out the creep arithmetic. RG 6.46 says ASIC won't let a holder leave out shares acquired under other exceptions from the 3% count, which suggests buy-back increases count, but the guide doesn't say so directly.

## Data notes

- NZX liquidity is the 20-session median of on-market value traded to 6 November 2026, from NZX's own daily activity data (the address used by NZX's instrument pages: `api.nzx.com/public/instrument/<ISIN>/metrics/activity/past/2Y.json`). ASX liquidity is close times volume from Norgate's 2026 file, whose last day is 30 October 2026, over the 20 ASX sessions from 5 to 30 October with sessions that had no trade counted as zero.
