# Australian deal database, 2015 and 2016: notes (built 31 October to 1 November 2026)

## What is in the files

- `au_deals_2015_2016.csv` holds 35 rows covering 31 ASX-listed targets with binding announcements between 1 January 2015 and 31 December 2016 and equity value at the offer of A$100m or more (or, where the value could not be found in a filing, deals that are clearly above that size). There are 17 rows for 2015 and 18 for 2016. Asciano has three rows (Brookfield, the Qube consortium and the joint consortium), and Affinity Education and GPT Metro Office Fund two each.
- `au_candidates_2015_2016.csv` holds 33 entries for deals that were considered and left out: out-of-window, below threshold, controller buy-outs, non-binding or not a bid, plus in-scope deals that could not be built because no filing could be opened. Each entry gives the reason and the source opened.
- Header, column order, ID format (AU-YYYY-NN, numbered by announce_date within each year) and conventions follow SCHEMA.md and the 2025-2026 pilot. Every URL in `sources` was opened. Secondary sources (HSF and MinterEllison reports, the Mayne Report, FNArena) appear only as pointers or to record a value that `notes` marks as secondary; no field value is taken from them, with one exception flagged below (PanAust's announce_date).
- No deal is pending: every 2015-16 deal had reached an outcome by 31 October 2026.

## How the deals were found

1. Candidate spine. The Mayne Report tracker that the pilot relied on now covers only 2025 and 2026 (the live page is about 86,000 characters and its older year sections have been removed), and the Wayback Machine copy is blocked. The delisted.com.au yearly archives are behind a paywall. So the spine was built from the deal appendices of Herbert Smith Freehills' Australian Public M&A Reports: the 2016 report lists all 50 deals announced in FY16 (July 2015 to June 2016) and the 2017 report all 59 deals announced in FY17 (July 2016 to June 2017), each with bidder, structure and value, and both include non-binding proposals. HSF's FY15 report (July 2014 to June 2015) is available only on request, so January to June 2015 was built from HSF's FY15 summary pages (which name the large deals), MinterEllison's FY17 review, the Mayne Report's list of billion-dollar companies that left ASX, and my own list of known deals checked against filings.
2. Contested deals. The Takeovers Panel annual reports for 2014-15, 2015-16 and 2016-17 list every application. The 2015 and 2016 matters that touch control deals are Affinity Education, Australian Industrial REIT, Sedgman, Investa Office Fund, CIC Australia and Ainsworth; the rest are small or about rights issues. (The Panel's paginated media-release list returned the 2026 page for most page numbers, so the annual reports were used instead.)
3. Filings. Three routes gave primary documents:
   - ASX announcement lists (asx.com.au/asx/v2/statistics/announcements.do) for codes still listed, mostly acquirers (IGO, Beach, REA, Tabcorp, G8, APA, Growthpoint, Centuria Capital, Dexus, Superloop, oOh!media, Macmahon). These include the target's releases where the acquirer re-lodged them.
   - InvestSmart's announcement pages for delisted codes (investsmart.com.au/security/asx/CODE/NAME/announcements), which link each filing to the Aspect Huntley mirror. Only the two most recent pages (50 filings) load, which usually covers the booklet or target's statement, meeting, court and implementation, but often not the first announcement. Codes later reused by another company (Recall REC, Skilled SKE, Energy Developments EDE, Amcom AMM, Coffey COF) do not work.
   - The Aspect Huntley mirror itself (aspecthuntley.com.au/asxdata/YYYYMMDD/pdf/IDSID.pdf), which serves 2015 and 2016 ASX PDFs by their ASX document number. A few older PDFs are image-only and could not be read (PanAust's GRAM notices).
   For Recall (Iron Mountain) and Toll (Japan Post), the acquirers' own releases and SEC filings filled gaps, together with the Federal Court judgment approving the Recall scheme.
4. Limits. The shared web-search budget ran out part-way through, and the fetch tool's hourly limit and InvestSmart's rate limit slowed the later work. Deals that could only be reached by search were not built (see below).

## Rules applied where SCHEMA.md leaves a choice

The pilot's conventions were followed, with these additions:

- **bidder_type existing_holder** is used where the bidder held a direct stake of 10% or more before the deal (REA 22.67% in iProperty, G8 16.41% in Affinity, Centuria 16.1% in GMF, GRAM in PanAust, CIMIC in Sedgman). Small stakes (Beach 4.56% in Drillsearch, APA 6.08% in EPX) are recorded as trade. Growthpoint bought GPT's 12.98% of GMF on the day it launched its bid and is recorded as trade, as the pilot did for Fortescue's launch-day options over Red Hawk.
- **Parallel structures.** Brookfield's November 2015 takeover offer for Asciano ran alongside its scheme on the same terms and is kept in the scheme row. The Qube consortium's February 2016 bid implementation deed and the joint consortium's March 2016 scheme are separate rows because they are different bidders and documents.
- **Takeover outcome dates** are the offer close, as in the pilot. For bids that closed above 90% with compulsory acquisition (Broadspectrum, EPX, GMF, Sedgman, General Mining, UGL, Vision Eye, PanAust) the outcome is completed at the close.
- **Withdrawn** is used for Centuria's GMF bid (announced but never despatched, after its recommendation condition failed) and the Qube consortium's Asciano bid (dropped by agreement for the joint scheme). Brookfield's Asciano scheme and G8's Affinity bids are superseded_by_rival.
- **Mergers of equals.** Novion/Federation, M2/Vocus, Drillsearch/Beach, oOh!media/APN Outdoor and Tatts/Tabcorp are recorded as schemes for the target named in the filing as the one being acquired. Where the filing gives no premium (Novion, M2, oOh!media), premium_pct_announced is blank.
- **Trust schemes.** Investa Office Fund's unitholder resolutions plus Supreme Court judicial advice are recorded as shareholder_vote Y and court_approval Y.
- **Dividends inside the price** follow the pilot: Patties (up to 25c special), Pacific Brands (9.4c special), Cover-More (5c special), Sedgman (12.3c dividend deducted from the A$1.07 bid) and Asciano (up to 90c special) are included in the price, with notes. UXC's 2c and Toll's 13c interim dividends were paid on top and are not included.
- **ComCom** is used for New Zealand Commerce Commission clearance (M2/Vocus); **OIO** for New Zealand Overseas Investment Office consent.

## Count check against independent totals

There is no single published list of 2015 or 2016 control deals over A$100m, so the check is against HSF's appendices, which list every announced deal (binding or not) by financial year.

- **July 2015 to June 2016 (HSF FY16).** HSF lists 50 deals, 26 entries at A$100m or more. This database has 20 of those 26 entries (Affinity x2, Asciano x3, Broadspectrum, Drillsearch, EPX, General Mining, GMF x2, Investa Office Fund, iProperty, M2, Pacific Brands, Patties, Sedgman, UXC, Veda, Vision Eye/Jangho). Of the other six, Devine/CIMIC is excluded as a controller buy-out and five are in-scope or possibly in-scope deals that could not be built: Energy Developments/DUET (about A$1.36bn), Cardno/Crescent (about A$522m; nature of the bid unverified), Vision Eye/Pulse Health (about A$158m; rival row), Devine/Forum Partners (about A$143m; not confirmed binding) and Coffey/Tetra Tech (about A$109m). HSF's success rate for FY16 completed deals was 73% across all sizes; this database's completion rate for the same window is higher because it holds only larger, mostly binding deals.
- **July to December 2016 (HSF FY17).** HSF lists 59 FY17 deals, 24 entries at A$100m or more. The nine built here (ASG, BigAir, Cover-More, Intecq, oOh!media, Pulse Health, SAI Global, Tabcorp/Tatts, UGL) are all confirmed 2016 announcements. Macmahon (24 Jan 2017) and Generation Healthcare (target's statement May 2017) are confirmed or near-certain 2017 deals, Warrnambool is a controller buy-out, and Fantastic Holdings (implemented December 2016) is in scope but could not be built. The remaining nine (Afterpay, Bradken, Brookfield Prime Property Fund, DUET, Grays, Payce, Seymour Whyte, SMS x2, Spotless, Vitaco) were not date-checked because of fetch limits; most are believed to be 2017 announcements, which belong to the 2017-18 agent. MinterEllison counts 39 FY17 deals of A$50m or more.
- **January to June 2015 (HSF FY15).** HSF's full FY15 list is not public. Its summary says FY15 had 55 deals and seven deals over A$1bn, all announced in January to June 2015. This database has five or six of them (Novion, Toll, iiNet, Recall, Sirius, and PanAust if it was over A$1bn); the seventh was not identified. Skilled Group/Programmed and Australian Industrial REIT/360 Capital are likely January to June 2015 deals that could not be built.
- **Bottom line.** Built rows: 17 for 2015 and 18 for 2016. Adding the in-scope deals that could not be built (Energy Developments, Skilled, Australian Industrial REIT, Coffey, the Pulse bid for Vision Eye, Fantastic, and possibly Cardno, Devine/Forum and the SMS schemes) gives a likely true population of about 22 to 24 rows for 2015 and 19 to 22 for 2016. That is close to the pilot's 25 to 30 a year once its larger 2025-26 market is allowed for.

## Rows I am least sure of

- **AU-2015-04 PanAust.** The thinnest row. The announce_date (30 Mar 2015) and the A$1.71 initial price come only from press reports (the price is left blank); GRAM's notices on the mirror are image-only. Only the A$1.85 final price, the board's recommendation and GRAM's 86% holding come from a filing.
- **AU-2016-13 ASG Group.** No document could be opened (InvestSmart gives no PDF links for ASG). Price and dates come from dated filing titles; equity value, expert and conditions are blank.
- **AU-2015-16 Broadspectrum.** The original Ferrovial price was not found (the December 2015 filings would not load), so the initial price, premium and equity value are blank; price_raised Y rests on the filings calling A$1.50 a "Revised Offer".
- **AU-2016-08 General Mining.** announce_date is the 22 June 2016 bidder's statement date; the merger was probably announced a few weeks earlier. Implied value and equity value are blank.
- **AU-2015-08 and AU-2016-02 Asciano (Brookfield and Qube consortium).** Terms come from the history section of the later joint booklet. No implied per-share value or equity value was found for either; price_raised N for Brookfield is not fully confirmed (the booklet records no change, but Brookfield's own November 2015 filing was not opened).
- Also worth a second look: AU-2015-09 Vision Eye (announce_date is the bidder's statement date and the initial price is the price in later notices), AU-2015-06 Recall (offer price computed from the bidder's presentation at the US dollar share price and FX rate given there), AU-2016-18 oOh!media (equity value is a rough 45% share of the stated combined market capitalisation) and AU-2016-14 UGL (CIMIC's pre-bid stake and the board's original stance not captured).

## Fields most often blank (out of 35 rows)

- By design: failure_reason 29 (only failed deals), related_deal_ids 28, min_acceptance_pct 28 (mostly schemes), meeting_date and court_date (none for takeovers).
- Thin for real: ier_low and ier_high 17, premium_pct_announced 15, break_fee_m 15, undisturbed_price 13, equity_value_m 12, ier_name and ier_verdict 11 to 12, intentions_pct 28, pre_bid_stake_pct 24, first_approach_date 25 (many deals had no public approach). Equity value is blank mainly for takeovers whose first announcement could not be opened (Broadspectrum, PanAust, Sedgman, UGL, General Mining) and for mergers stated only as combined values (Novion, M2); HSF's values are given in notes as secondary.
- Regulators are blank for 8 rows where the conditions schedule was not read.

## Sources that worked, for later years

- The Aspect Huntley mirror (aspecthuntley.com.au/asxdata/YYYYMMDD/pdf/IDSID.pdf) serves 2015-16 ASX PDFs reliably when the date and ASX document number are known, and it is not rate limited.
- ASX's announcement list works for any code still listed, whatever the year, which makes still-listed acquirers the best way into a delisted target's history.
- InvestSmart's delisted-company pages give document numbers, but only the first two pages (50 most recent filings) load, page 2 needs the exact company-name slug, codes later reused by another company show the new company, and the site rate limits to roughly one page every three to four minutes.
- HSF's annual report appendices (FY16 and FY17 PDFs are public) are the best free candidate spine for this period.
