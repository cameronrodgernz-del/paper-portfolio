---

I'd like you to write the strongest honest case against buying stapled securities in GDI Property Group (ASX: GDI) today, as an independent analyst. Another analyst has written the case for buying. You haven't seen it, and that's deliberate: I want your own view, worked from the evidence. Afterwards the two cases will be put side by side and argued out, so be specific enough that each of your points can be checked.

The setting:
- Today is 10 November 2026. GDI last traded at A$0.64, with 539.2 million securities on issue, a market value of about A$345m. Net tangible assets were A$1.21 a security at 30 June 2026. FY26 funds from operations (FFO) were 8.24 cents; the distribution is 5.0 cents a year, guided again for FY27 ("may be paid wholly or partly out of capital"). Drawn debt was A$343.3m.
- GDI owns Perth CBD offices (Westralia Square WS1 and WS2, 197 St Georges Terrace, 5 and 1 Mill Street), two Perth car parks, part of a Townsville office trust, half of a mining-town accommodation business (the Co-living JV), and a small funds management business.
- Robert Rockefeller, a Hobart-based private investor, built from 5.0% to 10.1% between April and September 2026. A new chair, James Warburton, joined in August and has said the board will close the gap to NTA. A buy-back of up to 5% has bought about 0.1%.
- It's for a simulated portfolio. The position would be small (about A$2,250), bought to hold for up to three years, and judged on what it's worth by 31 August 2028, with distributions. The securities trade about A$200,000 a day.
- The portfolio's rules only allow a buy if all of these hold:
  - the bad case is no more than 30% below today's price;
  - the middling case is above today's price by at least 1.5 times the bad case's fall, and never by less than 15%;
  - the expected gain after costs beats about 12.9% over the period (the cash rate plus 5% a year).

  A "special situation" (for example a holder building a stake toward a bid) skips the first two rules but still needs the third, with its bad case set at the value if nothing happens.

What I need from you:
1. What today's price assumes. Work it out from the price, NTA, FFO, the cash GDI actually keeps after rent-free periods and fit-outs, and the distribution. Show the arithmetic.
2. The strongest reasons not to buy at A$0.64. Cover:
   - cash earnings against the distribution: operating cash flow, rent-free periods given (FY24 to FY26), fit-out spending, and how much of the 5.0 cents is paid from asset sales or debt;
   - the valuations: WS1 and WS2 at 6.50% cap rates against Perth prime yields and recent Perth sales, and what NTA looks like marked to that evidence;
   - lease expiries from FY28 at 197 St Georges Terrace, 5 Mill Street and WS1, at Perth incentives of about 47%;
   - interest rates (the cash rate is 4.85% after five rises in 2026) and the actual hedging: caps that step up and end, a swap the bank can cancel from 2028, and debt falling due in February 2028;
   - the Co-living JV's exposure to mining contracts (including the Moranbah take-or-pay ending in March 2027);
   - why the gap to NTA may persist: other office REITs trade 37% to 47% below NTA, and GDI has traded at about half of NTA since 2023;
   - whether Rockefeller, the new chair, the buy-back or the A$100m of non-core sales actually change anything;
   - anything else in the accounts that makes GDI look better than it is.

   Weigh the obvious arguments for buying, and say why they're weaker than they look, where they are:
   - a 47% discount to NTA and a 7.8% distribution yield;
   - Perth's lack of new office supply and falling vacancy;
   - the core towers now fully leased, with fit-out spending falling;
   - a 10% outside holder and a board focused on the gap.
3. Your bad, middling and good outcomes for the price by 31 August 2028, including the four distributions due before then. Give each a chance (they should add to 100%), and say what has to happen in each. Then say plainly whether GDI passes the rules above at A$0.64, both as an ordinary pick and as a special situation.
4. The price, or the event, that would change your mind.
5. Every fact with its source and link.

Some ground rules:
- Use primary sources wherever you can:
  - GDI's ASX announcements, annual and half-year reports and results presentations (asx.com.au and announcements.asx.com.au);
  - the substantial holder notices and directors' interest notices;
  - peers' filings, and property agents' published research on the Perth office market.

  Use broker notes and press only as pointers, and say when you have.
- Be fair. If the case against turns out to be weak at this price, say so. I'd rather have an honest "the bear case is thin" than a one-sided brief.
- Write in plain English, with figures in Australian dollars. Keep it to about 2,000 words, plus a sources list.
