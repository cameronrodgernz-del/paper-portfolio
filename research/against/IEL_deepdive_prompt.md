I'd like a deep dive on IDP Education Limited (ASX: IEL), done to the standard of a professional analyst who goes well past the news. That means the company's filings and the notes to its accounts, its takeover-related announcements, substantial holder and directors' interest notices, government policy documents and visa data, and its competitors' filings. I want your own view, worked from the evidence. Others will look at IDP separately. Your answer will be checked point by point, so be specific, show your working and source every fact.

## The setting

- Today is 10 November 2026. IDP last traded at A$2.20, with about 278.3 million shares on issue, a market value of about A$612m.
  - The shares were A$5.78 at the end of 2025 and reached a low of A$1.49 on 14 September 2026.
  - IDP left the S&P/ASX 200 in June 2026.
  - The shares trade about A$3.5m to A$4m a day.
- What IDP does:
  - places international students with universities in Australia, the UK, Canada, the US, Ireland, New Zealand and, more recently, Malaysia and the UAE, earning commissions from the universities;
  - runs and co-owns the IELTS English test with the British Council and Cambridge University Press & Assessment (IDP bought the British Council's IELTS business in India in 2021);
  - runs English language schools.
- FY26 (year to June 2026), from the company:
  - revenue A$795.4m, down 11%;
  - student placement volumes down 27%, with yield (revenue per student) up 11%;
  - IELTS volumes down 8%, with yield up 7%;
  - adjusted EBIT A$122.9m against reported EBIT of A$61.8m, the gap being A$61.1m of "significant items", including A$36.2m of restructuring;
  - adjusted net profit A$57.1m against reported A$13.3m;
  - net debt A$118.6m (A$253.8m of borrowings, the first maturing in April 2028);
  - dividends of 9 cents for the year.
- FY27 guidance: adjusted EBIT of A$95m to A$115m, planning for market volumes down 20% to 30%, with net leverage at or below 1.5 times. It was reaffirmed at the AGM on 27 October 2026.
- A A$50m on-market buy-back was announced in June 2026; about A$11m had been bought by the AGM.
- Two takeover proposals:
  - On 22 September 2026 IDP said it had rejected an unsolicited, non-binding proposal from Blackstone at A$2.50 cash a share by scheme of arrangement (received 9 September), after an earlier one at A$2.30. Both were to be reduced by any future dividends, other than the 6-cent final.
  - The board called the proposal opportunistic and said it substantially undervalues IDP.
- At the 2026 AGM, 37.97% of votes were cast against the remuneration report. The proposed one-off "transformation" award for the chief executive was withdrawn before the meeting after shareholder feedback.
- This is for a simulated portfolio. The position would be small (about A$2,250), held for up to about three years, and judged on what it's worth on 31 August 2028, counting dividends received by then. Allow about 2.5% for costs in total (brokerage both ways on a small order, the bid-ask spread and a currency fee).
- The portfolio only buys if all three of these hold:
  1. the bad case is no more than 30% below today's price;
  2. the middling case is above today's price by at least 1.5 times the bad case's fall, and never by less than 15%;
  3. the expected gain after costs, weighting each outcome by its chance, is at least about 12.9% over the period to 31 August 2028.

  A "special situation" (for example a bidder likely to return, or a holder building toward a bid) skips the first two rules but must still pass the third, with its bad case set at the value if nothing happens.

Please check every number above against the filings and correct any that are wrong.

## What I need answered

### 1. What the price assumes
Work out IDP's enterprise value today: market value plus net debt, saying how you treat leases. Then the multiples it implies on FY26 and guided FY27 adjusted EBIT, on reported EBIT, and on free cash flow after capital spending, including capitalised software and technology, and after lease payments.

Do the restructuring costs and the other "significant items" recur? Count them year by year since FY22, and say what IDP's earnings look like if they keep coming.

Show the arithmetic, per share. Compare with listed peers on the same basis at current prices: Navitas's last listed numbers, Pearson (owner of PTE), any listed student recruitment platforms, and other education companies, plus private-equity deals in international education and English testing since 2019 and the multiples paid.

### 2. The market, country by country
For each destination (Australia, the UK, Canada, the US, Ireland and New Zealand), set out:
- the policy changes since 2023 and what's still to come;
- the latest visa or study permit data;
- what each government has said about 2027 and 2028.

For example: Australia's planning levels for new international students, its visa fees and its 2025–2026 legislation on education agents and agent commissions; the UK's international student levy and graduate route changes; Canada's study permit caps; the US visa and vetting changes since 2025.

Where is the bottom for student volumes, and on what evidence? IDP plans for market volumes down another 20% to 30% in FY27. Is that cautious or optimistic?

### 3. Student placement
- IDP's market share by destination, and its volumes, yield and revenue by destination for FY23 to FY26.
- How are university commissions and agent rules changing? Are universities paying less, moving to direct recruitment, or concentrating on fewer agents?
- Competitors: other large agents and aggregator platforms, and how they're faring.
- The voluntary change in revenue recognition announced on 18 December 2025: what changed, and what it did to reported revenue, earnings, contract assets and cash.
- The FY26 credit-loss provisions for customers in countries with foreign exchange controls: which countries, how much is at risk, and whether more is likely.

### 4. IELTS and English testing
- How IELTS ownership, revenue and costs are shared between IDP, the British Council and Cambridge. Which markets IDP runs on its own, and on what terms the partnership can change.
- IELTS volumes by market, especially India (down 22% in FY26) and the new China centres.
- Competition: PTE, TOEFL, the Duolingo English Test and newer tests. Which tests each government and university system now accepts, and changes since 2023, including the UK Home Office's English test procurement and Canada's renewed agreement with IRCC (Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada).
- How much of IELTS demand is for study, how much for migration and work, and how each is trending.

### 5. AI
Assess plainly whether AI is a threat or an advantage for each business:
- AI study advice and application tools against IDP's counsellors;
- AI-scored, at-home English tests against IELTS's test centres;
- test security and cheating.

What has IDP invested in, what is it spending, and is there evidence it's working: the conversion and NPS (customer recommendation score) claims, and the IELTS China AI assistant?

### 6. Costs, cash and the balance sheet
- The cost cuts: A$32m net in FY26 and A$15m more planned for FY27. Headcount down about 1,250 roles. IELTS venues cut from about 1,500 to fewer than 600. How much is permanent, and how much comes back when volumes recover?
- Working capital: cash conversion of 139% and contract assets down 56%. Is this a one-time release or lasting?
- Debt: facilities, covenants, maturities, interest costs and hedging. Dividend policy, and the buy-back so far: shares bought, prices paid and pace.

### 7. The Blackstone proposals and the chance of a bid
- What is known about the A$2.30 and A$2.50 proposals: dates, conditions, exclusivity terms, and what the board and Blackstone have said since. Has Blackstone or any other party built a stake?
- What would a private equity buyer be paying for, and at what price would a bid make sense to them on your numbers?
- Precedents on the ASX since 2018 where a board rejected a private equity proposal: how often did the bidder come back higher, walk away, or the shares fall back below the rejected price?
- How likely is a renewed or competing proposal by August 2028, and at what price?

### 8. Shareholders, the board and pay
- Who owns IDP now. Separate genuine holders from stock lending and index notices (there have been dozens of substantial holder notices since June 2026). Track short interest from ASIC's short position reports.
- The history of the university-owned Education Australia stake.
- The remuneration votes. At least 25% voted against the pay report in 2024, 2025 and 2026, and a spill resolution failed in 2025. What are shareholders objecting to? What would happen if 25% or more vote against again in 2027?
- Board changes since 2023, the chief executive's record and holding, and directors' own share purchases and sales.

### 9. Anything else
Anything else in the accounts or the history that makes IDP look better or worse than it seems: goodwill and impairment testing (the discount rates and headroom), customer remediation, legal or regulatory matters, tax, currency exposure, and related parties.

## What I want back

1. One paragraph up front: buy, wait or reject at A$2.20, and the main reason.
2. The strongest case for buying and the strongest case against, each in its best honest form. For each argument on one side, say whether the other side answers it.
3. Three outcomes for 31 August 2028 (bad, middling, good): the share price then, plus dividends received by then, or the bid price if a bid succeeds.
   - For each, say what has to happen, the value and its chance. The chances should add to 100%.
   - If you think a worse case is needed, fold it into the bad case for the rule tests: the portfolio's cards have only three outcomes, so the bad case is the weighted value of everything below the middling case.
   - Make the chances match what you say elsewhere: if you put a chance on a renewed bid or on earnings falling, the outcomes in which they happen should carry that chance.
   - Value on enterprise value: apply a multiple of EBIT or free cash flow to the business, then subtract the net debt you expect at the time. Don't apply a multiple of market value and then add or subtract cash or debt again.
   - Show the expected gain after costs, and say plainly whether IDP passes each of the three rules at A$2.20, both as an ordinary pick and as a special situation.
4. The highest price at which you'd buy, and the events that would change your mind either way, with the dates they're due. For example: government visa and planning announcements, the half-year result in February 2027, any renewed proposal, substantial holder notices, and the 2027 AGM vote.
5. A list of every source, with links. Use primary sources wherever you can:
   - IDP's ASX announcements (asx.com.au and announcements.asx.com.au);
   - government visa and policy data (Australia's Department of Home Affairs and Department of Education, the UK Home Office, IRCC, the US State Department and SEVIS);
   - ASIC short position reports;
   - competitors' filings.

   Use broker notes and press only as pointers, and say when you have. Mark anything you couldn't verify.

Some ground rules:
- Be fair. If one side's case is thin, say so.

- Write in plain English, and explain any industry or finance term the first time you use it. Use Australian dollars throughout.
- Aim for about 3,500 words, plus tables and the sources list.
