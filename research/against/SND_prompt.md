---

I'd like you to write the strongest honest case against buying shares in Saunders International Limited (ASX: SND) today, as an independent analyst. Another analyst has written the case for buying. You haven't seen it, and that's deliberate: I want your own view, worked from the evidence. Afterwards the two cases will be put side by side and argued out, so be specific enough that each of your points can be checked.

The setting:
- Today is 9 November 2026. Saunders last traded at A$0.725 on 6 November (no trade since; bid A$0.72, offer A$0.825), with 140.8 million shares on issue, a market value of about A$102m. At 30 June 2026 it had cash of A$29.8m and bank debt of A$9.5m, and owes an earn-out of up to A$11.85m (A$6.85m cash and A$5m in shares) for Aqua Metro in the first half of FY27.
- It's for a simulated portfolio. The position would be small (about A$2,250), bought to hold for up to three years, and judged on what it's worth by 31 August 2028, after the FY28 result. The shares trade about A$11,000 a day.
- The portfolio's rules only allow a buy if all of these hold:
  - the bad case is no more than 30% below today's price;
  - the middling case is above today's price by at least 1.5 times the bad case's fall, and never by less than 15%;
  - the expected gain after costs beats about 12.9% over the period (the cash rate plus 5% a year).

  A "special situation" (for example a holder building a stake toward a bid) skips the first two rules but still needs the third, with its bad case set at the value if nothing happens.

What I need from you:
1. What today's price assumes about Saunders' future. Work it out from the price, the net cash after the earn-out, FY26 (revenue A$305.6m, adjusted EBITDA A$9.8m, NPATA A$3.9m, with a weak first half and a much stronger second half), and the company's outlook for FY27 (revenue growth, and the second-half NPATA margin held through the year). Show the arithmetic.
2. The strongest reasons not to buy at A$0.725. Cover:
   - the record: the FY25 guidance cut and managing director's resignation (June 2025), the October 2025 warning, and the FY26 guidance cut (May 2026); whether the second half of FY26 is a recovery or a good half;
   - how thin the margins are (adjusted EBITDA 3.2% in FY26) and what one bad project does;
   - the Aqua Metro purchase: what it added, the goodwill, and the earn-out's cash and share dilution;
   - liquidity and the cost of getting in and out;
   - the holders: Ahrens Group (16.2%, a private South Australian construction group, buying since December 2024), Desmond Bryant (18.4%), NAOS (11.0%, a seller through 2025 and 2026), the Aqua Metro vendor (6.7%, out of escrow), Anacacia (6.6%, a seller) and Timothy Burnett (5.5%, a seller). Who might sell, and is a takeover by Ahrens likely or unlikely? Why might a strategic holder sit at 16% for years?
   - whether the federal fuel security package (2026-27 Budget) helps before 2028 at all;
   - anything else in the accounts that makes the business look better than it is.

   Weigh the obvious arguments for buying, and say why they're weaker than they look, where they are:
   - record work in hand of A$571.9m and a pipeline of A$5.1bn;
   - 42% of revenue from panels and programmes, and new water panels;
   - its first direct Defence head contract (A$23m, July 2026);
   - FY23 and FY24 net margins of about 4.5%;
   - a strategic holder that could bid.
3. Your bad, middling and good outcomes for the share price by 31 August 2028, including any dividends paid before then. Give each a chance (they should add to 100%), and say what has to happen in each. Then say plainly whether Saunders passes the rules above at A$0.725, both as an ordinary pick and as a special situation.
4. The price, or the event, that would change your mind.
5. Every fact with its source and link.

Some ground rules:
- Use primary sources wherever you can:
  - Saunders' ASX announcements, annual and half-year reports and results presentations (all at asx.com.au and announcements.asx.com.au);
  - the substantial holder notices (several are scanned images) and directors' interest notices;
  - competitors' filings, the federal contract register (AusTender) and official sources on the fuel security package.

  Use broker notes and press only as pointers, and say when you have.
- Be fair. If the case against turns out to be weak at this price, say so. I'd rather have an honest "the bear case is thin" than a one-sided brief.
- Write in plain English, with figures in Australian dollars. Keep it to about 2,000 words, plus a sources list.
