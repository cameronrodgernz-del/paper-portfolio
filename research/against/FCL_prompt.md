---

I'd like you to write the strongest honest case against buying shares in FINEOS Corporation Holdings plc (ASX: FCL) today, as an independent analyst. Another analyst has written the case for buying. You haven't seen it, and that's deliberate: I want your own view, worked from the evidence. Afterwards the two cases will be put side by side and argued out, so be specific enough that each of your points can be checked.

The setting:
- Today is 9 November 2026. FINEOS last traded at A$2.18, with about 345.0 million CDIs on issue, a market value of about A$752m (about €463m at A$1 = €0.616). It reported cash of €51.7m and no debt at 30 September 2026, and €39.0m at 30 June 2026.
- It's for a simulated portfolio. The position would be small (about A$2,250), bought to hold for up to three years, and judged on what it's worth by 31 August 2028, after the 1H28 result.
- The portfolio's rules only allow a buy if all of these hold:
  - the bad case is no more than 30% below today's price;
  - the middling case is above today's price by at least 1.5 times the bad case's fall, and never by less than 15%;
  - the expected gain after costs beats about 12.9% over the period (the cash rate plus 5% a year).

What I need from you:
1. What today's price assumes about FINEOS's future. Work it out from the price, the cash, the company's own figures, its FY26 revenue guidance (€147m to €152m) and its targets for FY27 and FY29 (EBITDA margins of 25% and 40%; R&D at 30% and 25% of revenue; subscriptions at 65% and 75% of revenue). Show the arithmetic.
2. The strongest reasons not to buy at A$2.18. Look for reasons growth may stall, may be weaker than it looks, or is already fully in the price. Cover:
   - capitalised development: how much of EBITDA it accounts for, what cash earnings look like without it, and whether the margin targets are credible;
   - client concentration (two clients each over 10% of FY25 revenue) and what the loss or shrinking of one would do;
   - whether AI weakens the position of core insurance software, given that listed software has been marked down across the board in 2026;
   - currency: about 71% of revenue in US dollars against mostly euro costs, and the A$ price;
   - the founder's control (about 50.6%, chief executive and executive chairman) and what it means for minority holders and for any takeover;
   - services revenue that isn't growing, and whether overall growth will stay in single digits;
   - the removal from the S&P/ASX All Technology index in June 2026 and a fund ceasing to be a substantial holder in November 2026;
   - anything else in the accounts that makes the business look better than it is.

   Weigh the obvious arguments for buying, and say why they're weaker than they look, where they are:
   - ARR growth of about 15%;
   - the return to profit and positive free cash flow;
   - demand from new US state paid family and medical leave schemes;
   - the founder buying shares on market in 2026;
   - private equity take-privates of insurance software companies at higher multiples.
3. Your bad, middling and good outcomes for the share price by 31 August 2028, including any dividends paid before then. Give each a chance (they should add to 100%), and say what has to happen in each. Then say plainly whether FINEOS passes the three rules above at A$2.18.
4. The price, or the event, that would change your mind.
5. Every fact with its source and link.

Some ground rules:
- Use primary sources wherever you can:
  - FINEOS's ASX announcements, annual and half-year reports, quarterly activity reports and results presentations (all at asx.com.au and announcements.asx.com.au);
  - the substantial holder notices and directors' interest notices;
  - competitors' and acquirers' filings, and official sources on US paid leave schemes.

  Use broker notes and press only as pointers, and say when you have.
- Be fair. If the case against turns out to be weak at this price, say so. I'd rather have an honest "the bear case is thin" than a one-sided brief.
- Write in plain English, with figures in Australian dollars (and euros where the company reports in them). Keep it to about 2,000 words, plus a sources list.
