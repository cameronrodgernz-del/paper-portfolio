# Prompt for the case against Alliance Aviation Services (ASX:AQZ)

Written 2026-11-09 for the second model (rulebook section 5a, docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md). Cam pastes everything below the line into the other model, ideally with web browsing or deep research switched on. Its answer is saved as research/against/AQZ.md, with this prompt at the top.

---

I'd like you to write the strongest honest case against buying shares in Alliance Aviation Services Limited (ASX: AQZ) today, as an independent analyst. Another analyst has written the case for buying. You haven't seen it, and that's deliberate: I want your own view, worked from the evidence. Afterwards the two cases will be put side by side and argued out, so be specific enough that each of your points can be checked.

The setting:
- Today is 9 November 2026. Alliance's last sale was A$0.40, with about 218.35 million shares on issue, a market value of about A$87m. It raised A$40m at A$0.70 a share in August and September 2026, and its FY26 accounts carry a going-concern material uncertainty.
- It's for a simulated portfolio. The position would be small (about A$2,250), bought to hold for up to three years, and judged on what it's worth by 31 August 2028, after the FY28 result.
- The portfolio's rules only allow a buy if all of these hold:
  - the bad case is no more than 30% below today's price;
  - the middling case is above today's price by at least 1.5 times the bad case's fall, and never by less than 15%;
  - the expected gain after costs beats about 12.9% over the period (the cash rate plus 5% a year).

What I need from you:
1. What today's price assumes about Alliance's future. Work it out from the price, the debt and the company's own figures and guidance, and show the arithmetic. Be careful to separate EBITDA from the cash left after maintenance, interest and aircraft purchases.
2. The strongest reasons not to buy at A$0.40. Look for reasons the turnaround may not work, may take far longer, or may need more equity first. Cover:
   - the Qantas wet lease and how long it lasts;
   - the FIFO (fly-in fly-out) customers and competitors;
   - the cost of maintaining an ageing fleet;
   - the asset sales the company is relying on;
   - the debt, the banking covenants and the refinancing due in September 2027;
   - management's record on guidance;
   - the share register, and anything in the accounts or the company's history that should worry a buyer.

   Weigh the obvious arguments for buying, such as the low multiple of EBITDA, the asset values and the director buying, and say why they're weaker than they look, where they are.
3. Your bad, middling and good outcomes for the share price by 31 August 2028, including any dividends paid before then. Give each a chance (they should add to 100%), and say what has to happen in each, including whether more shares are issued. Then say plainly whether Alliance passes the three rules above at A$0.40.
4. The price, or the event, that would change your mind.
5. Every fact with its source and link.

Some ground rules:
- Use primary sources wherever you can, all at asx.com.au and announcements.asx.com.au:
  - Alliance's ASX announcements, annual and half-year reports and results presentations, including the going-concern note and the borrowings note in the FY26 annual report;
  - the substantial holder and director notices;
  - Qantas's own filings and fleet plans.

  Use broker notes, the aviation press and forums only as pointers, and say when you have.
- Be fair. If the case against turns out to be weak at this price, say so. I'd rather have an honest "the bear case is thin" than a one-sided brief.
- Write in plain English, with figures in Australian dollars. Keep it to about 2,000 words, plus a sources list.
