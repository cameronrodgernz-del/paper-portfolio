I'd like you to write the strongest honest case against buying shares in Eureka Group Holdings Limited (ASX: EGH) today, as an independent analyst. Another analyst has written the case for buying. You haven't seen it, and that's deliberate: I want your own view, worked from the evidence. Afterwards the two cases will be put side by side and argued out, so be specific enough that each of your points can be checked.

The setting:
- Today is 10 November 2026. Eureka last traded at A$0.62, with 562.3 million shares on issue, a market value of about A$349m. After buying six NSW communities from Ingenia for A$123.8m in September 2026, its pro forma net debt is about A$214m, gearing 37% and net tangible assets 56.3 cents a share. It guides to FY27 underlying EPS of at least 4.2 cents (reaffirmed at the AGM on 30 October). Its "underlying EPS" is underlying profit before tax divided by shares.
- The register: Filetron (Ben Cottle, founder and chair of FDC) about 34.6%; Development Management & Constructions (Blake Cottle, his brother) about 5.1%; Copia Investment Partners about 23.6%; Australian Retirement Trust about 8.4%.
- It's for a simulated portfolio. The position would be small (about A$2,250), bought to hold for up to three years, and judged on what it's worth by 31 August 2028. The shares trade about A$40,000 a day.
- The portfolio's rules only allow a buy if all of these hold:
  - the bad case is no more than 30% below today's price;
  - the middling case is above today's price by at least 1.5 times the bad case's fall, and never by less than 15%;
  - the expected gain after costs beats about 12.9% over the period (the cash rate plus 5% a year).

  A "special situation" (for example a holder building a stake toward a bid) skips the first two rules but still needs the third, with its bad case set at the value if nothing happens.

What I need from you:
1. What today's price assumes. Work it out from the price, the FY27 guidance, NTA, the dividend and the pre-tax nature of the earnings measure. Show the arithmetic.
2. The strongest reasons not to buy at A$0.62. Cover:
   - the quality of underlying EPS: before tax, gains on home and unit sales, the tax losses left, and what earnings look like after tax;
   - the NSW purchase: the price against Ingenia's book value, Ingenia's own description of the assets, and Eureka's 8.1% ingoing yield;
   - debt, hedging and the loan-to-value ratio against the 40% internal ceiling, with the cash rate at 4.85% after five rises in 2026, and the likelihood of more equity raisings;
   - the holders: how the two Cottle groups have built their stakes, whether they act together (they are declared associates in FDC but report separately in Eureka), what they could do without a bid, Broadlands (a land lease operator that appears to be theirs), and what all that means for minority holders;
   - the valuation against Ingenia, Aspen, Lifestyle Communities and GemLife, and against Warburg Pincus's A$4.75 proposal for Ingenia;
   - liquidity and the cost of getting in and out;
   - anything else in the accounts that makes the business look better than it is.

   Weigh the obvious arguments for buying, and say why they're weaker than they look, where they are:
   - FY27 underlying EPS growth of at least 22%, from a management team led by Ingenia's former chief executive;
   - rents tied to the pension and CPI, and 97% seniors' occupancy;
   - two wealthy holders who keep buying and might bid;
   - brokers' targets of A$0.75 to A$0.85.
3. Your bad, middling and good outcomes for the share price by 31 August 2028, including any dividends paid before then. Give each a chance (they should add to 100%), and say what has to happen in each. Then say plainly whether Eureka passes the rules above at A$0.62, both as an ordinary pick and as a special situation.
4. The price, or the event, that would change your mind.
5. Every fact with its source and link.

Some ground rules:
- Use primary sources wherever you can:
  - Eureka's ASX announcements, annual and half-year reports and presentations (asx.com.au and announcements.asx.com.au);
  - the substantial holder notices from Filetron, DMC, Copia and Australian Retirement Trust (some are scanned images), and FDC's own holder notices;
  - Ingenia's announcements on the sale and on the Warburg Pincus proposal;
  - peers' results.

  Use broker notes and press only as pointers, and say when you have.
- Be fair. If the case against turns out to be weak at this price, say so. I'd rather have an honest "the bear case is thin" than a one-sided brief.
- Write in plain English, with figures in Australian dollars. Keep it to about 2,000 words, plus a sources list.
