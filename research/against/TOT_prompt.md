I'd like you to write the strongest honest case against buying units in 360 Capital REIT (ASX: TOT) today, as an independent analyst. Another analyst has written the case for buying. You haven't seen it, and that's deliberate: I want your own view, worked from the evidence. Afterwards the two cases will be put side by side and argued out, so be specific enough that each of your points can be checked.

The setting:
- Today is 9 November 2026. TOT last traded at A$0.405 (bid A$0.405, offer A$0.41), with 223.65 million units on issue, a market value of about A$90.6m. Its net tangible assets (NTA) were A$0.58 a unit at 30 September 2026 (unaudited). It owns half of 510 Church Street, Cremorne (Melbourne), 38 Sydney Avenue, Forrest (Canberra) and 34 Southgate Avenue, Cannon Hill (Brisbane), plus a preference equity investment in a 360 Capital apartment fund. Its debt is about A$82m, all floating.
- Tony Pitt, through TT Investments, holds about 52.5%. He controls the fund's manager, 360 Capital, which he took private in 2025 and 2026.
- It's for a simulated portfolio. The position would be small (about A$2,250), bought to hold for up to three years, and judged on what it's worth by 31 August 2028. The units trade about A$14,000 a day.
- The portfolio's rules only allow a buy if all of these hold:
  - the bad case is no more than 30% below today's price;
  - the middling case is above today's price by at least 1.5 times the bad case's fall, and never by less than 15%;
  - the expected gain after costs beats about 12.9% over the period (the cash rate plus 5% a year).

  A "special situation" (for example a controller building a stake toward a bid) skips the first two rules but still needs the third, with its bad case set at the value if nothing happens.

What I need from you:
1. What today's price assumes. Work it out from the price, NTA, the FY27 guidance (3.8 cents of earnings and a 3.6 cent distribution, given 12 August 2026) and the discount at which other listed REITs with large office holdings trade. Show the arithmetic.
2. The strongest reasons not to buy at A$0.405. Cover:
   - the controller: how Pitt has built his stake (the substantial holder and director's interest notices), his share of each distribution reinvestment issue since March 2026, the takeover rules on creeping and reinvestment, and what his 2025 takeover of 360 Capital Group (TGP) at A$0.31 says about the price minority holders might get in any bid, and about delisting;
   - the investment management agreement approved in February 2025: its term, its fees and the cost of removing the manager;
   - the property values: the cap rates (6.25% to 7.00%), the fact that June 2026 values were the directors' own, recent sales in Cremorne and Canberra, and the independent valuations due by June 2027;
   - interest rates: the cash rate went from 3.60% to 4.85% in 2026, and the debt is unhedged;
   - the earnings: what in FY27 doesn't repeat (Dentsu's surrender payment, the 16% preference equity being repaid), and Dentsu leaving in FY30;
   - the 2024 non-renounceable raising at A$0.40, and the fund's record since it bought its buildings in 2022;
   - liquidity and the cost of getting in and out;
   - anything else in the accounts that makes the fund look better than it is.

   Weigh the obvious arguments for buying, and say why they're weaker than they look, where they are:
   - a 30% discount to NTA and a yield of about 8.9%;
   - 100% leased, no expiries until FY29, and 35% of rent from the Commonwealth;
   - a controller who keeps buying and might take it private;
   - a debt facility extended to 2031.
3. Your bad, middling and good outcomes for the unit price by 31 August 2028, including any distributions paid before then. Give each a chance (they should add to 100%), and say what has to happen in each. Then say plainly whether TOT passes the rules above at A$0.405, both as an ordinary pick and as a special situation.
4. The price, or the event, that would change your mind.
5. Every fact with its source and link.

Some ground rules:
- Use primary sources wherever you can:
  - TOT's ASX announcements, annual and half-year reports, quarterly updates and results presentations (asx.com.au and announcements.asx.com.au);
  - the substantial holder and directors' interest notices, and the 2025 notice of meeting on the investment management agreement;
  - 360 Capital Group's 2025 announcements on the TT Investments takeover;
  - peers' results, and property agents' and valuers' published sales and research.

  Use broker notes and press only as pointers, and say when you have.
- Be fair. If the case against turns out to be weak at this price, say so. I'd rather have an honest "the bear case is thin" than a one-sided brief.
- Write in plain English, with figures in Australian dollars. Keep it to about 2,000 words, plus a sources list.
