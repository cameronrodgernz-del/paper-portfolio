# Prompt for the case against Mader Group (ASX:MAD)

Written 2026-11-09 for the second model (rulebook section 5a, docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md step 4). To run now: the case for passes every test at A$5.46, narrowly (research/picks/MAD.md). Cam pastes everything below the line into the other model, ideally with web browsing or deep research switched on. Its answer is saved as research/against/MAD.md, with this prompt at the top.

---

I'd like you to write the strongest honest case against buying shares in Mader Group Limited (ASX: MAD) today, as an independent analyst. Another analyst has written the case for buying. You haven't seen it, and that's deliberate: I want your own view, worked from the evidence. Afterwards the two cases will be put side by side and argued out, so be specific enough that each of your points can be checked.

The setting:
- Today is 9 November 2026. Mader last traded at A$5.46, with about 210.8 million shares on issue (211.4 million after an issue of staff shares on 2 October, less about 0.6 million bought back since). That's a market value of about A$1,151m. It reported net cash of A$35.7m at 30 June 2026.
- It's for a simulated portfolio. The position would be small (about A$2,250), bought to hold for up to three years, and judged on what it's worth by 31 August 2028, after the FY28 result.
- The portfolio's rules only allow a buy if all of these hold:
  - the bad case is no more than 30% below today's price;
  - the middling case is above today's price by at least 1.5 times the bad case's fall, and never by less than 15%;
  - the expected gain after costs beats about 12.9% over the period (the cash rate plus 5% a year).

What I need from you:
1. What today's price assumes about Mader's future. Work it out from the price, the cash, the company's own figures and its FY27 guidance (revenue of at least A$1.13bn, profit after tax of at least A$72.5m), and show the arithmetic.
2. The strongest reasons not to buy at A$5.46. Look for reasons growth may stall, may be weaker than it looks, or is already fully in the price. Cover:
   - how exposed Mader is to a slowdown in mining, especially Pilbara iron ore, and how mine operators treat contract maintenance labour when prices fall;
   - the slip in margins in FY26 (EBITDA margin from 12.5% to 12.1%, and 11.6% in the first half) and FY27 guidance for profit to grow more slowly than revenue;
   - the end of the dividend and the plan to become a "globally diversified industrial conglomerate", with an "opportunity warchest" for acquisitions;
   - control and governance: the founder is executive chairman with about half the shares; the finance chief stepped down on 29 October 2026; a long-serving non-executive director with about 15.7% of the shares, who has sold shares every year since 2022, leaves the board at the AGM on 24 November 2026;
   - the 7.965m shares issued to staff in October 2026 under the 2021 bonus scheme, and the new equity plan and performance rights put to the AGM;
   - labour supply and wages for heavy-diesel mechanics, and Mader's reliance on recruiting overseas;
   - North America, where revenue grew but EBITDA barely did;
   - anything in the accounts, such as receivables, capitalised costs or share-based pay, that makes the business look better than it is.

   Weigh the obvious arguments for buying, and say why they're weaker than they look, where they are:
   - net cash and a 25% return on capital;
   - a five-year record of beating profit targets;
   - a multiple below listed mining-services peers;
   - the buyback.
3. Your bad, middling and good outcomes for the share price by 31 August 2028, including any dividends paid before then. Give each a chance (they should add to 100%), and say what has to happen in each. Then say plainly whether Mader passes the three rules above at A$5.46.
4. The price, or the event, that would change your mind.
5. Every fact with its source and link.

Some ground rules:
- Use primary sources wherever you can:
  - Mader's ASX announcements, annual and half-year reports and results presentations (all at asx.com.au and announcements.asx.com.au);
  - the substantial holder notices and directors' interest notices;
  - peers' filings (Monadelphous, SRG Global, NRW, Perenti, Macmahon) and official data on mining activity and labour.

  Use broker notes and press only as pointers, and say when you have.
- Be fair. If the case against turns out to be weak at this price, say so. I'd rather have an honest "the bear case is thin" than a one-sided brief.
- Write in plain English, with figures in Australian dollars. Keep it to about 2,000 words, plus a sources list.
