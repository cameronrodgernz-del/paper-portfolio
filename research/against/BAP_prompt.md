# Prompt for the case against Bapcor (ASX:BAP)

Written 2026-11-09 for the second model (rulebook section 5a, docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md). Cam pastes everything below the line into the other model, ideally with web browsing or deep research switched on. Its answer is saved as research/against/BAP.md, with this prompt at the top.

---

I'd like you to write the strongest honest case against buying shares in Bapcor Limited (ASX: BAP) today, as an independent analyst. Another analyst has written the case for buying. You haven't seen it, and that's deliberate: I want your own view, worked from the evidence. Afterwards the two cases will be put side by side and argued out, so be specific enough that each of your points can be checked.

The setting:
- Today is 9 November 2026. Bapcor closed at A$0.855 on 6 November 2026, with 672,753,078 shares on issue, a market value of about A$575m.
- It's for a simulated portfolio. The position would be small (about A$2,250), bought to hold for up to three years, and judged on what it's worth by 31 August 2028, after the FY28 result.
- The portfolio's rules only allow a buy if all of these hold:
  - the bad case is no more than 30% below today's price;
  - the middling case is above today's price by at least 1.5 times the bad case's fall, and never by less than 15%;
  - the expected gain after costs beats about 12.9% over the period (the cash rate plus 5% a year).

What I need from you:
1. What today's price assumes about Bapcor's future earnings. Work it out from the price and the company's own figures, and show the arithmetic.
2. The strongest reasons not to buy at A$0.855. Look for reasons the turnaround may not work, may take far longer, or is already fully in the price. Cover the business itself, its competitors, costs, the balance sheet and the banking covenants, the share register, and anything in the accounts or the company's history that should worry a buyer. Weigh the obvious arguments for buying, and say why they're weaker than they look, where they are.
3. Your bad, middling and good outcomes for the share price by 31 August 2028, including any dividends paid before then. Give each a chance (they should add to 100%), and say what has to happen in each. Then say plainly whether Bapcor passes the three rules above at A$0.855.
4. The price, or the event, that would change your mind.
5. Every fact with its source and link.

Some ground rules:
- Use primary sources wherever you can: Bapcor's ASX announcements, annual and half-year reports and results presentations, the substantial holder and director notices, and competitors' own filings. They're all at asx.com.au and announcements.asx.com.au. Use broker notes and press only as a pointer, and say when you have.
- Be fair. If the case against turns out to be weak at this price, say so. I'd rather have an honest "the bear case is thin" than a one-sided brief.
- Write in plain English, with figures in Australian dollars. Keep it to about 2,000 words, plus a sources list.
