# Mader Group (ASX:MAD): the debate

Started 2026-11-09 under docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md step 5. The case for is research/picks/MAD.md (Claude). The case against is research/against/MAD.md (ChatGPT, run by Cam on 9 November 2026). Price throughout: A$5.46. This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## Where the two still disagree
After round 1 neither side says buy at A$5.46. The case for had it passing narrowly, up to about A$5.50. With the corrections below (waiting on Cam's go), it qualifies at about A$5.38, 1.5% under the price. The case against would look again at about A$4.60.

Where they still disagree, and what would settle it:
1. The chance that profit falls by FY28.
   - The case against has a 25% bad case in which FY28 EBIT falls to A$80m, from A$95m in FY26.
   - The case for has a fall in profit (to about A$55m) at 12%, and a flat-profit bad case at 25%.
   - The AGM trading update on 24 November and the 1H FY27 result in late February 2027 settle most of it: utilisation, margins and North American profit.
2. The multiple in the middling case.
   - The case against uses 11 times FY28 EBIT, about 15.7 times trailing profit, a little below today's 17.6.
   - The case for uses today's 16 times the coming year's profit.
   - Nothing settles this soon. It's a judgement about whether today's discount lasts.
3. Shares in 2028: the case against uses 215m, the case for now 212m (from 209m). How the AGM-approved awards are settled, with new shares or bought on market, will show which is closer.

Where they now agree:
- the operating business is sound: cash generation, receivables and the delivery record;
- the FY26 margin slip came mostly from overheads, and North America is the sharper worry;
- net cash is small against the market value, and the buyback is modest;
- the board has no independent director;
- Burton hasn't sold every year;
- dilution from staff awards matters.

## Round 1: Claude answers the case against
Each point is taken in the order the case against makes it. I say whether I agree, disagree, or think it's right but not important, and what it changes.

1. What the price assumes.
   - Its point: at the FY27 floors, profit grows 10.9% and the net margin slips. On its own assumptions, holding today's price needs about A$98.5m of FY28 EBIT at 11 times, and clearing the 12.9% hurdle needs about A$113.5m [A].
   - My view: agree with the arithmetic.
     - The case for's middling FY28 profit of about A$82m is about A$117m of EBIT, so it clears the hurdle on their own multiple, just.
     - Their own point 3 adds that FY27 guidance carries A$3m to A$4m of growth spending. That was confirmed in the FY26 announcement [D1]. Without it, profit growth is about 16% to 17%.
2. Mining exposure.
   - Its point: Australia is almost 80% of revenue and about 93% is at the extraction stage. The government expects Australian iron ore output to rise modestly. The real risk is miners trimming contractors' hours and rates, not maintenance disappearing [A].
   - My view: agree, and it's a fair, measured statement of the risk. Nothing in it changes the case for, which already has a 10% worse case for this.
3. Margins.
   - Its point [A]:
     - gross margin rose from 18.8% to 19.5% while administration costs rose 24.5%, so the FY26 slip was overheads;
     - the first half's 11.6% recovered to 12.5% in the second half;
     - North America's EBITDA margin fell from about 19.2% to 17.6%.
   - My view: agree. This is a fair correction in Mader's favour, and North America is the right place to look. It supports the middling case rather than the bad one.
4. Labour.
   - Its points [A]:
     - heavy-diesel technicians are 52% of the workforce;
     - 48% of trades are in shortage;
     - mining wages rose 3.5%;
     - a 1% wage rise that can't be passed on costs about A$4.8m after tax.
   - My view: right, but not important at this size. Mader prices by the hour and has passed on wage rises so far, with gross margin up in FY26. It's a risk for the bad case, not a new one.
5. Capital allocation.
   - Its point: no dividend, the "industrial conglomerate" plan, and a 25% return on capital that doesn't prove acquisitions will earn the same. Net cash is only 3.1% of market value [A].
   - My view: agree on all of it. The case for never treated net cash as a floor.
   - One inconsistency in its own numbers: its scenarios take A$20m to A$30m of acquisition spending out of cash but add no profit from what's bought. That understates value by a little in every case.
6. Control and governance.
   - Its points [A]: the company reports no independent directors, and my claim that Burton "has sold every year since 2022" is wrong.
   - My view: agree on both.
     - Mader's own governance statement says the board "does not currently have an independent Director" [D2]. All four directors are the founder, two executives and Burton, a 15.7% holder. When Burton leaves on 24 November, the board will be three executives. The case for called this "one fewer non-executive", which undersold it.
     - Burton sold 1m shares in March 2022, then nothing until November 2024, then about 5m in the ten months to September 2025 [S21]. The case for needs correcting on both points.
     - What it changes: a board with no independent check makes a poorly priced acquisition more likely. I'd lift the worse case from 10% to 12%.
7. Dilution.
   - Its points [A, D3]:
     - October's 7.965m shares were already in FY26 diluted EPS;
     - the AGM asks for authority to issue up to 10,571,336 securities over three years, plus 1.5m chief executive rights whose profit targets aren't set yet;
     - a fully used A$30m buyback buys only about 5.5m shares.
   - My view: agree. The case for assumed the buyback would roughly cancel new awards and used 209m shares in 2028. On the new plan's authority, about 212m is more realistic.
   - That alone flips the narrow pass: with 211m or more shares the middling test fails at A$5.46.
8. The buyback. Right: it helps, but it doesn't set a floor [A]. No change.
9. The accounts.
   - Its points [A]:
     - receivables and debtor days are healthy;
     - contract assets rose 23% to A$19.6m;
     - debts more than 91 days overdue rose from A$3.7m to A$7.0m;
     - no capitalised software issue.
   - My view: agree. The overdue figure is worth watching at the half-year, but it's under 4% of receivables. It doesn't change the numbers.
10. Delivery record and peers.
    - Its points [A]:
      - the delivery record is real but says more about the existing business than about acquisitions;
      - on underlying earnings Perenti is about 9.9 times and NRW 15.3 times, against my statutory figures of 23.4 and 18.5;
      - Mader is cheaper than Monadelphous and SRG, but not the whole group.
    - My view: agree. The case for's "cheapest of its peers" used statutory figures from ASX data. Those flatter contractors with large amortisation and fleets. It should read: cheaper than Monadelphous and SRG, the closest maintenance-led peers, but not cheaper than fleet-heavy Perenti or Macmahon.
11. The outcomes.
    - Its card [A]:
      - bad 25% at A$3.47;
      - middling 55% at A$6.56;
      - good 20% at A$9.37;
      - about +15.1% after costs.
    - My view: partly agree.
      - The bad case: its A$3.47 is close to my worse case (A$3.14 after the corrections) but given a 25% chance. It needs FY28 EBIT to fall about 16% from FY26, when FY27 guidance points to about A$105m. The case against itself calls the operating bear case "limited" and maintenance demand "resilient", which fits a fall in profit being the less likely bad outcome, not the usual one.
      - The middling case: the gap is mostly the multiple. Its 11 times trailing EBIT is about 15.7 times trailing profit, a little below today's 17.6, so it builds in a small de-rating. On today's trailing multiple, its own middling EBIT gives about A$7.25 a share.
