# Nuix (ASX:NXL): the debate

Started 2026-11-09 under docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md step 5. The case for is research/picks/NXL.md (Claude). The case against is research/against/NXL.md (ChatGPT, run by Cam on 9 November 2026). Price throughout: A$1.925. This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## Where the two still disagree
Both sides now say it's not a buy at A$1.925. The case for had it on the line, qualifying at about A$1.89. After round 1 it qualifies at about A$1.68 (changes proposed below, waiting on Cam's go). The case against would look again at about A$1.40.

Where they still disagree, and what would settle it:
1. The chance of a large class action loss. The case against puts A$75m of net legal cost into a bad case with a 30% chance. The case for puts a larger loss, about A$150m once Macquarie's share is counted, at about 10%. The Supreme Court of Victoria's judgment settles it. It's reserved, and no date is set.
2. How hard a slowdown hits profit. The case against has FY28 EBITDA falling to A$45m. The case for keeps it at about FY27's guided A$60m. The 1H FY27 result in late February 2027 settles most of it: ACV growth, the share of revenue from multi-year deals, contract assets and margins.
3. Cash by mid-2028. The case against has about A$80m of net cash after lease debt. The case for now has about A$110m, down from A$150m. The 1H FY27 cash flow settles it.

Where they now agree:
- the arithmetic of what the price assumes;
- Nuix Neo's growth is partly old customers moving across;
- revenue is running ahead of ACV because of multi-year deals;
- lease debt should be counted;
- there's no takeover premium in Macquarie's 27.8%.

## Round 1: Claude answers the case against
Each point is taken in the order the case against makes it. I say whether I agree, disagree, or think it's right but not important, and what it changes.

1. What the price requires.
   - Its point: at 10 times FY28 EBITDA the price needs about A$60m to hold and about A$70m to clear the 12.9% hurdle. Including A$20.6m of lease debt, the business is valued at 10.6 times FY26 EBITDA [A].
   - My view: agree with the arithmetic. My "about 8 times" was on FY27 EBITDA before the one-off A$15m development spend, about A$75m. Theirs is on FY26's A$59.8m. Both are right and neither makes Nuix look cheap.
   - Leases: right, but not important. They're about 6 cents a share. I'll count them from now on.
2. Neo's growth overstates the whole business.
   - Its point: the fall in the older components offsets 56% of Neo's increase. Organic ACV growth was 8.6%, or 11.1% in constant currency [A].
   - My view: agree, and the case for says the same. It also concedes the move to Neo runs to FY30, so there's no cliff before the 2028 judging date. No disagreement here.
3. AI and competition.
   - Its points [A]:
     - Relativity built AI review into its platform in June 2026;
     - Cellebrite cut its outlook;
     - Nuix's net dollar retention fell from 107.4% in FY25 to 105.2%.
   - My view: partly agree.
     - Net dollar retention did fall over the year. But it rose from 101.0% at the half to 105.2% at year end [D3], which the case against leaves out, and that cuts the other way.
     - AI is a fair worry, but no one can measure it before 2028, so I don't change the numbers for it.
     - Cellebrite is already in the case for. It reports on 12 November.
4. The class action and ASIC's appeal.
   - Its points [A]:
     - nothing sets a reliable maximum loss;
     - insurance only pays above a A$10m retention;
     - the bank facility can't be used for litigation;
     - every A$50m of uninsured loss is about 14 cents a share.
   - My view: agree, and it's worse than the case against says.
     - The HSBC revolving facility excludes litigation costs in so many words [D2, note 4.7]. A large judgment would have to come from cash, or from a raising.
     - The bigger point is Macquarie. The case for said Macquarie, as a co-defendant, "shares any liability". That was wrong:
       - the co-defendant is Macquarie Capital (Australia) Limited, one of the two lead managers of the 2020 float [D2, note 9.7];
       - in the underwriting agreement, Nuix agreed to keep the lead managers indemnified against losses suffered in connection with the offer. The only exclusions are their own recklessness, gross negligence, fraud or wilful misconduct [D1, section 9.8.1.5];
       - Macquarie Capital has already claimed A$9.15m of legal fees under that indemnity, A$4.17m of it in FY26 [D2, related parties].
     - So in most outcomes Nuix would carry Macquarie's share of any damages as well as its own. This is the most important thing round 1 turned up.
   - Where I disagree is on the chance. The case against puts A$75m of legal cost into a bad case it gives a 30% chance, alongside flat revenue and falling margins. That treats a court judgment and a business slowdown as one event, and they aren't linked.
     - ASIC's case, over Nuix's statements in early 2021, failed in full. The court found Nuix had reasonable grounds for its forecasts and the shortfall wasn't material [S18].
     - The plaintiffs have dropped their ACV and consumer law claims [S3].
     - The prospectus claims are a different test, so ASIC's loss doesn't decide them.
   - What I'd change: keep a large loss at about 10%, but make it bigger, about A$150m net including Macquarie's share. That's more than Nuix's cash, so that case includes a raising. A smaller A$20m of legal cost moves into the bad case.
5. Macquarie's 27.8%.
   - Its point: Macquarie is neither a likely seller nor a backstop [A].
   - My view: agree. The case for attached no premium to it. The indemnity (point 4) matters far more than the shareholding.
6. Management's forecasting record.
   - Its point: FY25 guidance of 11% to 16% constant-currency ACV growth was withdrawn, and the outcome was 6.9% [A].
   - My view: agree, and the case for says so too. FY26 met guidance under the new chief executive. The case against fairly notes that the FY28 result could turn on the timing of a few large deals.
7. The accounts.
   - Its points [A, D2]:
     - multi-year deals rose from 27% to 35% of revenue, which accounts for about A$32.3m of the A$41.7m revenue growth;
     - contract assets rose from A$50.9m to A$75.0m;
     - a stricter FY26 free cash flow is about A$32m, a 4.8% cash yield on the share price.
   - My view: agree. This is the strongest new point in the case against.
     - The case for valued the middling case on EBITDA, and EBITDA includes revenue booked up front on multi-year licences that are billed over several years. ACV, up 8.6% organically, and cash are the cleaner guides.
     - The case against is careful to say none of this shows anything improper, and it's right not to deduct development spending twice.
   - What I'd change: lower net cash at June 2028 in the middling case from about A$150m to about A$110m, after lease debt and continuing legal costs.
8. The outcomes.
   - Its card [A]:
     - bad 30% at A$1.01;
     - middling 50% at A$2.31;
     - good 20% at A$3.76;
     - about +13.8% after costs.
   - My view: partly agree.
     - The middling case: we're close on what the business is worth (A$741m for them against A$779m for me). The gap is cash, and after point 7 it's narrower.
     - The bad case puts three things together: flat revenue, EBITDA falling by a quarter to A$45m, and A$75m of legal cost. Without the legal cost it's A$1.225, and I think that's a fair bad case. It's close to my revised A$1.29 and to July's low of A$1.16. So the real gap between us is the chance of the legal loss, not the business.
