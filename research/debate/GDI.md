# GDI Property Group (ASX:GDI): the debate

Started 2026-11-10 under docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md step 5. The case for is research/picks/GDI.md (Claude, 8 November, with a second pass on 10 November). The other side is research/against/GDI_deepdive.md: an independent deep dive by a second model, run by Cam on 10 November, which worked both sides to its own verdict without seeing ours [A]. The red team's review (research/red_team/GDI.md, 8 November) is the third reading. Price throughout: A$0.64. This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## Where the two still disagree
After round 1 the two sides agree on the verdict and the price. The independent deep dive says wait, and buy at A$0.615 or below. With the corrections below (waiting on Cam's go), the case for says the same: wait, and it qualifies at about A$0.615 before the 30 December ex-date and about A$0.59 after it. The red team's line was A$0.605. All three now land within two cents of each other.

What's left is judgement on two points. Neither will be settled by more argument, only by GDI's next results.
1. How fast rent-free falls.
   - The deep dive has rent-free given at A$15.0m in FY27 and A$13.5m in FY28, so cash earnings are about 3.7c in FY28.
   - The case for had A$13m and A$10m, so about 4.8c.
   - GDI doesn't say how much of its rent-free is given up front and how much is spread over the life of each lease. The rent-free line in the 1H FY27 result (about 16 February 2027) settles most of this: about A$6m or less for the half supports the case for, and about A$7.5m or more supports the deep dive.
2. The chance of a distribution cut by August 2028.
   - The deep dive puts it at about 30%, most likely with FY28 guidance in August 2027, with the cut landing at about 3.5c to 4.0c a year.
   - The case for's bad case paid all four 2.5c payments in full. I now accept a cut belongs in the bad case (the red team said the same).
   - The December valuations, the loan-to-value ratio in February, and progress on the A$100m of sales are the early signs.

Where they agree:
- the cash GDI keeps, half by half, from FY24 to FY26 (the two tables match line by line);
- the bank will probably cancel the A$175m swap on 1 January 2028 if rates stay where they are;
- WS1 and WS2 at 6.50% sit at the tight end of the evidence, and the December 2026 valuations are likely to soften them;
- the distribution has been paid from debt and asset sales, not cash earnings;
- the board's plan (buy-back, A$100m of sales, joint ventures) hasn't yet done anything that would close the gap to NTA;
- Rockefeller has stated no intention, and a bid is a low-chance good case, not the thesis.

## Round 1: Claude answers the independent deep dive
Each point is taken in the order the deep dive makes it. For each one I say whether I agree, disagree, or think it's right but not important, and what it changes.

1. The numbers in the brief.
   - Its point: everything checks out except the median turnover (it gets about A$233,000 a day for 2026 from Yahoo, not A$175,000), and the swap rate: the results slides say "BBSY@3.43%" while the audited note says 3.72% [A].
   - My view: right, but not important.
     - Our A$174,000 was a 20-session window to 30 October. On Norgate the 2026 median is about A$226,000 a day [D1], close to theirs, and both are far above what a A$2,250 position needs.
     - I checked the swap: the note says 3.72% and the slides say 3.43% [G1, G2]. Whichever is right, the bank cancels while rates are above it, so it doesn't change the reading.
2. What the price assumes.
   - Its point: once debt and the other assets are taken out, the price values the buildings at about A$604m, 66 cents in the dollar of book, an implied 9.7% on face income. That's harsher than the two Perth sales at 8.2% and 8.3%, "so there is real value underneath" [A].
   - My view: the arithmetic is right, but it leaves out the head office, and that changes the conclusion.
     - A buyer of a building doesn't pay GDI's corporate costs of A$10.1m a year. A holder of GDI does, less about A$1.4m of ongoing fees from outside funds.
     - Count that and the price puts the buildings at about 8.2% on face income (8.4% if the Co-living JV is valued at six times its earnings instead of at book). That's the same as the two Perth sales, not harsher.
     - Put another way: at the sales evidence, NTA is about A$0.91 (the deep dive's own table). The net head office cost, capitalised at the same rate, is about A$105m, or 19 cents a security. That leaves about A$0.72, 12% above the price.
     - So the margin of safety is about 12%, not the 47% the discount to NTA suggests. It turns into the full gap only for a buyer who removes the head office: a bid, a wind-up, or sales of buildings near book. Those are the good case.
     - This strengthens the deep dive's verdict and weakens its main argument for buying.
3. Cash earnings, FY24 to FY26.
   - Its point: GDI's share of the cash it keeps was -A$10.0m, A$2.1m and A$17.2m, or -1.9c, 0.4c and 3.2c a security [A].
   - My view: agree. The deep dive took the same lines from the same notes and got the same figures to the decimal, working independently. That's the best check this table could have.
4. The FY27 and FY28 path.
   - Its point: cash earnings of about 5.2c in FY27 (4.0c without the one-off Autoleague fees) and about 3.7c in FY28. Rent-free falls only slowly (A$15.0m, then A$13.5m), because its steady rise as the buildings filled suggests much of it is spread over the leases. Fit-outs pick up to A$7.0m in FY28 for the Mill Green re-leasing. Counting the JV at the cash it pays out takes off about another 1c a year [A].
   - My view: mostly agree, and this is the point that moves the case for most.
     - Rent-free: the two FY26 halves were flat (A$8.3m and A$7.9m) even though the big FY25 leases had already started. If most of it were up front, the second half should have fallen more. I'd move the FY28 middling from about 4.8c to about 4.0c to 4.2c.
     - Fit-outs: A$7.0m is at the high end. GDI's spec suites can be re-let without starting again, so I'd say A$5m to A$7m.
     - The JV: right for whether the distribution is covered, since GDI can't take the cash without Tulla's consent [G1, note 14]. But not for value: the cash it keeps went into rooms that earn (140 more at Norseman, and Moranbah).
5. The distribution.
   - Its point: cash earnings covered none of the 5.0c in FY24, 8% in FY25 and 64% in FY26. Debt paid the rest in FY24 and FY25, and the Autoleague sales in FY26; that money is now used up. It puts the chance of a cut before August 2028 at about 30%. When office REITs have cut, prices fell a few per cent on the day and trailed the sector by about 10% over six months; GDI's own 35% cut in December 2022 cost 11% [A].
   - My view: agree.
     - The cut table is useful evidence we didn't have, and it supports a bad case near A$0.47 plus 8.5c of distributions.
     - 30% is fair. The board restated 5.0c at the AGM five days ago, which mostly protects FY27, and the deep dive puts the risk in August 2027, where it belongs.
6. The buildings and their values.
   - Its point [A]:
     - Cushman & Wakefield puts Perth premium yields at 6.50% and A-grade at 7.85% (from a press report).
     - A small Perth sale in April 2024 was at 6.1%.
     - WS1 is A-grade carried at a premium-grade yield, and 6.75% to 7.25% is defensible for WS1 and WS2.
     - GDI's own sensitivity note shows the offset: every A$25 a square metre of market rent adds 6.6c a security.
     - NTA is about A$1.15 to A$1.20 at December 2026, and about A$1.12 by June 2028 in the central case.
   - My view: agree. It's a fairer reading than the case for's A$0.97 to A$1.10, which marked everything to the sales evidence and ignored rent growth. The two new data points cut opposite ways and are both weak (a press report, and a A$26.5m private sale), so they don't change the range. The 256 St Georges Terrace sale (the Fortescue building, under offer at about A$200m) would be the best new evidence when it settles.
7. Debt and hedging.
   - Its point: the bank cancels the swap when rates are above 3.72%, which is when GDI needs it, so from January 2028 GDI is unhedged. Net interest is A$19.9m in FY27, A$21.5m in FY28 and A$22.8m in FY29. Tranche B (due February 2028) counts as a current liability from February 2027 [A].
   - My view: agree.
     - It's the red team's reading too, and the case for had it wrong (it said the bank wouldn't cancel while rates stay up).
     - The deep dive's interest figures sit between the case for's (A$21.0m in FY28) and the red team's (A$23.0m). The gap is about 0.3c a security, which doesn't change the verdict.
     - The current-liability point is a good catch. GDI has extended this facility four times in three years, so I expect it extended before the June 2027 accounts. If it isn't, that's a warning.
8. The Co-living JV and funds management.
   - Its point [A]:
     - The JV: the village-by-village customers.
     - Moranbah: GDI hasn't said whether Stanmore extended its take-or-pay contract, though the notice date has passed. Moranbah is worth about 0.3c a security.
     - The JV's own non-current liabilities rose from A$33.9m to A$57.5m, and GDI's half isn't in GDI's gearing.
     - Funds management: the goodwill test assumes performance fees of A$6m in FY27 and A$18m in FY28, and a new A$250m "Office JV Fund" paying 0.10%.
   - My view: agree, and I checked the two new items in the FY26 report [G1, notes 12 and 14].
     - The JV's debt is right but small. GDI's half, about A$29m, adds a little to its true gearing.
     - The A$250m Office JV Fund is the most interesting find in the report. It's about the size of 197 St Georges Terrace (A$234m), and the board lists joint ventures over core buildings as a strategy. A sale of part of 197 near book would be the best evidence yet that the valuations are real, and it would cut debt.
     - But it's an assumption management made to avoid writing off goodwill, not a plan it has announced. It's a watch item, not a reason to change the chances.
     - The same goes for the A$18m fee, which depends on the Broadmeadow site being rezoned: good case only.
9. Why the gap might close, or not.
   - Its point: price to NTA was 0.47 to 0.54 at four straight year ends, and the 2020 buy-back bought 2.4% in four years. The Elanor (2025), Hotel Property Investments (2024) and Australian Unity Office Fund (2019 to 2024) cases closed through a holder with a plan or a forced wind-up. GDI has a 10% holder with no stated aim, and a Perth market too thin to sell into at book [A].
   - My view: agree. It matches the case for's comparable cases (research/picks/COF_SS.md).
10. Who owns it.
    - Its point [A]:
      - Rockefeller bought 34.2m securities at an average of A$0.615, about 28% of the year's volume. He has lodged nothing in eight weeks and has no record of activism.
      - Paradice is half-owned by Charter Hall.
      - The chair's record is one clean sale (APN Outdoor) and a debt clean-up.
      - The managing director's rights can pay out in full with the stock at half of NTA.
    - My view: agree, with one point the other way.
      - Since his last notice the price has held between A$0.615 and A$0.645 to 30 October. Median turnover barely changed: about A$196,000 a day, against A$215,000 from March to mid-September [D1]. So the floor he provided hasn't visibly gone yet.
      - The Charter Hall link to Paradice is right but not important: Paradice's 8.4% belongs to its clients.
11. Could anyone bid?
    - Its point: about a 10% chance by August 2028, at A$0.80 to A$0.90. At A$0.85, the buildings would yield less than a bidder's cost of debt, so a bid makes more sense after rates fall [A].
    - My view: agree. It's consistent with the case for's 8% to 10%.
12. Anything else in the accounts.
    - Its point [A]:
      - the valuers aren't named;
      - the empty 180 Hay Street hasn't had an independent valuation since June 2025;
      - A$2.4m of fees owed by GDI's own funds have no provision;
      - a new A$1.0m loan to related parties is unexplained;
      - the auditor is mid-tier and also paid for tax advice;
      - the Townsville trust's A$10m loan was extended only three months, to November 2026.
    - My view: right, but not important one by one.
      - I checked the related-party loan: it's A$993,000, new this year [G1].
      - Together these say GDI's accounts are a little softer than a large REIT's, which is already in the discount.
      - The Townsville loan falls due about now. It's secured only on that trust, so it's a watch item rather than a risk to GDI.
13. The outcomes and the rules.
    - Its point [A]:
      - worse, 7%: A$0.4125 (A$0.35 plus 6.25c);
      - bad, 28%: A$0.555 (A$0.47 plus 8.5c, a cut to about 3.5c from August 2027);
      - middling, 45%: A$0.75 (A$0.65 plus 10c);
      - good, 20%: A$0.955 (A$0.85 plus 10.5c).
      - That gives an expected gain of +8.8% after 2.6% of costs. It fails rule 2 narrowly and rule 3 clearly, and buys at A$0.615.
    - My view: agree with the values and the chances.
      - Our card has three legs, so the worse and bad cases merge into a 35% bad leg at A$0.527, 17.7% below the price.
      - On that card, rules 2 and 3 both bind at about A$0.615: at A$0.615 the bad leg is -14.4%, the middling +22.0% (+21.6% needed) and the expected gain after costs +13.3%.
      - The middling A$0.75 matches the second pass's A$0.751. The bad leg is where the case for was too kind: it had A$0.589 to A$0.60, with every distribution paid.

## Questions Cam is better placed to judge
1. Whether to act on price alone. At A$0.615 the card passes every rule even with a cut in the bad case, so the trigger works without the February result. The alternative is to wait for 16 February whatever the price, because the rent-free line settles the biggest unknown. My lean is to keep the price trigger: the bad case already assumes the worse answer on rent-free.
