# Saunders International (ASX:SND): the debate

Started 2026-11-09 under docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md step 5. The case for is research/picks/SND.md (Claude). The case against is research/against/SND.md (ChatGPT, run by Cam on 9 November 2026, with its report kept as research/against/SND_bear_case.pdf). Price throughout: A$0.725, the last sale on 6 November. This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## Where the two still disagree
After round 1 neither side says buy at A$0.725. The case for had it on the line, qualifying at about A$0.69 to A$0.70. With the corrections below (waiting on Cam's go), it qualifies at about A$0.60 as an ordinary pick and about A$0.65 as a special situation. The case against would look again at about A$0.45.

Where they still disagree, and what would settle it:
1. How bad the bad case is.
   - The case against: 35% at A$0.32, with FY28 profit of A$4.5m (1.5% of revenue) at 10 times and no net cash added.
   - The case for, revised: 37% at about A$0.42 (a worse case at A$0.34 and a bad case at A$0.45, with profit of 1.8% of revenue at 9 times plus about A$10m of net cash).
   - The gap is mostly method: whether net cash counts on top of an earnings multiple, and what multiple a contractor gets on depressed earnings. The 1H FY27 result (about late February 2027) settles most of the business question: whether the second-half margin and cash flow held.
2. The chance of a bid. The case against has 10% at A$1.20, the case for 20% at about A$1.08. Only Ahrens settles it: a notice past 18%, a bid, or a sale.
3. Whether FY26 shows weak cash conversion. The case against reads the full year (operating cash flow -A$1.7m). The case for reads the halves: -A$8.3m in the first, +A$6.6m in the second. The 1H FY27 cash flow settles it.

Where they now agree:
- it fails the rules at A$0.725 and fails badly at the A$0.825 offer, so any entry is a patient limit order;
- the second-half recovery is real, but it is one half after two bad ones;
- the FY26 headline was flattered by a A$2.85m property gain in the first half;
- net cash after the earn-out is about A$13.4m, and the earn-out shares dilute holders by about 4.7%;
- the record work in hand is mostly Aqua Metro's long programmes;
- the fuel security package adds little before 2028;
- Ahrens' stake makes a bid credible but not likely on the evidence so far;
- NAOS bought back in August and isn't simply a seller.

## Round 1: Claude answers the case against
Each point is taken in the order the case against makes it. I say whether I agree, disagree, or think it's right but not important, and what it changes.

1. What the price requires.
   - Its point: net cash is A$20.3m before the earn-out (not the presentation's A$22.3m) and A$13.4m after its cash part; the earn-out shares are issued at the payment-date 30-day VWAP with a A$0.65 floor and a 7.69m-share cap; at A$0.725 that is about 6.9m shares [A, D1].
   - My view: agree. The case for used the same A$13.4m and about 6.7m shares; 6.9m is the better figure at today's price. Right but not important to the value.
2. The second-half margin.
   - Its point: first-half amortisation of acquired intangibles was A$1.723m, so first-half NPATA was about -A$1.1m and second-half NPATA about A$5.0m on A$162.1m, a 3.08% margin [A, D2].
   - My view: agree. The case for estimated first-half amortisation at A$1.27m and got 3.36%. The interim report's note 5 shows A$1.723m [D2]. The correction lowers FY27 profit on the company's outlook to about A$10.2m; because the middling case uses today's multiple of that same outlook, it moves the middling value only a little.
3. The record.
   - Its point: three disappointments in a year, with the guidance numbers [A].
   - My view: agree, and the case for says the same. Its figures add one detail: June 2025's EBITDA guidance fell from A$14m to A$16m to A$9m to A$10m.
4. The property gain.
   - Its point: adjusted EBITDA keeps a A$2.848m gain on disposal; without it FY26 adjusted EBITDA is A$6.9m, 2.26% of revenue [A, D3].
   - My view: agree, and it matters more than the case for allowed. The gain was a A$4m property sale in August 2025 [D2], so it sits in the first half: the first half's A$0.4m of adjusted EBITDA was really about -A$2.5m. The second half isn't affected. FY26 NPATA without the gain was about A$1.9m, 0.6% of revenue; FY25's net margin was 1.0%. The last two years averaged under 1%, which is the strongest evidence for a lower bad case.
5. Cash conversion.
   - Its point: operating cash flow was -A$1.674m; plant spending A$5.08m and leases A$1.86m; the cash balance rose on A$5.3m of new shares and A$9.5m of net borrowing; receivables and contract assets absorbed A$12.5m [A, D3].
   - My view: right on the year, but the halves tell a different story. The first half's operating cash flow was -A$8.27m [D2]; the second half's was therefore about +A$6.6m, against second-half adjusted EBITDA of about A$9.4m. The second half converted. The first half didn't, and the case for's line that working capital "swung in Saunders' favour" was wrong for the year. What it changes: a slower build of net cash in the middling case (about A$18m by June 2028, not A$25m).
6. Thin margins.
   - Its point: an extra cost of 1% of revenue takes 31% of EBITDA and 55% of NPATA [A].
   - My view: agree. The case for said the same in words.
7. Aqua Metro.
   - Its point: total consideration A$32.3m against A$1.6m of net identified assets; goodwill A$22.9m; Water earned A$10.2m of EBITDA on A$107.5m; 42.5% growth is bought, not organic [A, D3].
   - My view: agree. One thing it didn't draw out cuts the other way: the business Saunders had before Aqua Metro (the Multi-Sector segment) went from an EBITDA loss of about A$1.6m in the first half, once the property gain is removed, to about A$8.8m in the second [D2, D3]. The second-half recovery wasn't only Water.
8. Work in hand.
   - Its point: the 8.1% growth compares with A$529m that includes A$360m of Aqua Metro's work; the pipeline is opportunities, and the water panels guarantee no volume [A, D3].
   - My view: agree, and it's important. Saunders' own work in hand was A$169m at June 2025 [D3], under a year of its revenue; Aqua Metro's A$360m is more than three years of its revenue, mostly long programmes. The case for leaned on "record work in hand" for the middling case. It should lean less.
9. The Defence contract.
   - Its point: the AusTender notice (A$26.1m, executed 15 July 2026) doesn't name the site, so it neither asserts a match nor counts it as a new win [A].
   - My view: disagree, but it's not important. A$26.1m including GST is A$23.7m, the "circa A$23m" announced on 16 July, the day after the contract was executed [S8, S13]. Neither side counts it twice.
10. Fuel security.
    - Its point: reserve procurement starts from July 2027, full operation targeted for 2030, and the government would own fuel, not storage; consultation closed in September 2026 [A].
    - My view: agree. It adds detail to what the case for said, and the case for gave the package no value in the middling case.
11. Ahrens.
    - Its point: strategic fit is credible, but there's no proposal, and at 16% Ahrens can keep influence and the option to buy later without funding a bid [A].
    - My view: agree with all of it as fact. On the chance, I'd still put it nearer 20% than 10%. Ahrens has spent about A$19m over 16 months on a stock it can't easily sell, and its August purchase of SBS Tanks shows the strategy is live. But a 10% view is reasonable, and nothing in the evidence settles it.
12. The other holders.
    - Its point: Bryant's 18.4% has no obligation to support Ahrens; NAOS bought 1.502m shares on 28 August [A].
    - My view: agree. The case for noted the August purchase but still called NAOS "a holder that may need to sell". That should go.
13. Its outcomes.
    - Its point: bad 35% at A$0.319, middling 50% at A$0.891, good 15% at A$1.584, all including dividends, valued on FY28 profit with no cash added; about 4.0% after costs [A].
    - My view: partly disagree.
      - Its bad case puts a trough multiple on trough earnings and adds no cash. Through the actual trough (FY25's guidance cut and a loss-making first half of FY26) the market never valued Saunders below about A$0.68, or A$85m [S14]. A bad case at A$0.32 is a deeper fall than the market took then. The shares did trade at A$0.25 to A$0.40 in 2019, when the business was much smaller, so a fall that far isn't unheard of.
      - Not adding cash is a method choice. The case for adds net cash on top of an earnings multiple, as every deep dive in this process has. Part of it does fund working capital, so the case for now uses a lower figure.
      - Its middling case (A$0.891) is almost exactly the case for's (A$0.89 after the corrections).
14. Execution.
    - Its point: A$2,250 is about 20% of a day's turnover; paying the A$0.825 offer turns the return negative [A].
    - My view: agree. Only a limit order at or below the qualifying price makes sense, and it may take days to fill.
15. Special situation.
    - Its point: with a 10% chance of a A$1.20 bid, about 10.3% after costs, a fail; it needs a little over 14% at A$0.725 [A].
    - My view: agree with the method. On my revised no-deal outcomes (a lower bad case and a smaller middling cash build), the bid chance needed at A$0.725 rises to about 40%, and about 21% at A$0.65. With my 20%, it passes as a special situation at about A$0.65.
