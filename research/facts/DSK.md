The labels change (restructuring in FY25; a logistics move, store closures and a legal provision in FY26), but costs of about A$0.8m to A$1.3m a year have been left out in both years, and store impairments appear in both [1]. The legal costs will continue while the ACCC case runs. Free cash flow after leases and capital spending was A$1.9m in FY26 (after a A$3.9m build in inventory) and A$8.1m in FY25 [1].

What the headline numbers hide:
- An unprovided penalty. On 21 Jul 2026 the ACCC sued dusk's operating subsidiary in the Federal Court over 25 product lines with button batteries supplied in 2023 and 2024 before safety testing was finished (they passed when tested), 4 lines without the required warnings (recalled in December 2024), and a breach of an undertaking given to the ACCC in 2023 [5]. The directors "consider it probable that an economic outflow will be required" but have made no provision because the amount can't be measured reliably [1]. The one precedent: City Beach was ordered to pay A$14m in December 2025 for about 57,000 supplies of products that actually failed the standards; the Full Federal Court upheld it on 19 Aug 2026, noting City Beach's revenue of A$314m to A$331m and that the penalty was about half its average annual profit; City Beach had proposed A$3.15m [15][16].
- Seasonality. The December half made A$14.3m of underlying EBIT and the full year A$8.9m, so the June half lost about A$5.4m [1][3]. The company says FY27 will be "most determined by how we trade in November and December" [1].
- Leases. The A$18.3m of net cash sits beside A$40.9m of lease liabilities and A$17.4m a year of lease payments [1].
- Earnings history. Underlying EBIT was A$16.5m in FY23, A$6.2m in FY24, A$7.7m in FY25 and A$8.9m in FY26 [1].
- One country, imported product: gross margin was helped by "FX tailwinds" in the June 2026 half [2].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: how big will the ACCC penalty be, and is FY26's A$6.6m underlying profit the real level once Christmas 2026 is in? A deep dive would bound the penalty from the ACCC's pleadings and the City Beach judgments, and test the turnaround against the December half.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: specialty retailers are priced on earnings, at a price to earnings multiple. The named peer is Shaver Shop (ASX:SSG), a mall-based specialty retailer: 131.01m shares at A$1.185 (6 Nov 2026) over FY26 profit of A$14.805m is 10.5 times [13]. Beacon Lighting (ASX:BLX) is 16.0 times (229.34m shares at A$1.885 over A$26.991m) [14]; the lower, closer-sized peer is used. The price would get there through the half-year result in February 2027 and a known penalty.
- If it goes our way: underlying profit holds at FY26's A$6.6m and the penalty is about A$3m, near what City Beach itself proposed for worse conduct by a business twice dusk's size. 10.5 x A$6.598m = A$69.3m, less A$3.0m: A$66.3m on 62.27m shares, A$1.06 a share.
- If it goes against us: profit falls back to FY24's underlying A$5.0m (close to FY26's statutory A$5.4m with the left-out costs counted) and the penalty matches City Beach's A$14m. 10.5 x A$5.0m = A$52.5m, less A$14.0m: A$38.5m, A$0.62 a share.
- Cross-check at the two peers' average of 13.25 times: A$1.36 and A$0.84.
- Price: A$0.72 (last trade on 6 Nov 2026, ASX company data; A$0.685 at the 30 Oct close), market value A$44.8m: 6.8 times underlying EPS and about 3.0 times underlying EBIT after the June net cash. Gap if it goes our way: 1.06 / 0.72 - 1 = +48%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.62 / 0.72 - 1 = -14%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is 48%. The earnings half of the question can be answered from filings and the 1H FY27 result (about 13 Feb 2027), inside six months, will show how Christmas went. The penalty half probably won't be settled inside six months, so the deep dive has to bound it from the ACCC's concise statement and the City Beach decisions rather than wait for it. Not a value pick: the value if it goes against us is 14% below the price, more than the 10% allowed. The A$3,690 liquidity cap per position (section 1) is well under the 2.5% sizing limit.
