# SkyCity Entertainment Group (NZX:SKC): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 1 point, #21 in the NZX table of reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-08).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Casino and entertainment precincts in Auckland (casino, Sky Tower, hotels and the New Zealand International Convention Centre, opened 11 February 2026), Hamilton, Queenstown and Adelaide, plus SkyCity Online, run from Malta by a licensed third party. FY26 underlying revenue NZ$822.7m [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. PwC's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified. The directors note negative working capital of $78.8m, mainly because the $175m bond due 21 May 2027 is now a current liability, and conclude there is no material uncertainty about going concern, with $275m of undrawn bank facilities at balance date [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. NZX's own daily trading data: median on-market value over the 20 sessions 9 October to 6 November 2026 was NZ$239,959 a day (lowest NZ$74,867; all 20 sessions above NZ$12,500). The 60-session median is NZ$292,797 and the 250-session median NZ$403,894 [30]. The shares also trade on the ASX.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries, and Cam lifted the gaming, wagering and racing hold on 8 November 2026.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? Yes, on three counts:
  - Approaches: on 25 August 2026 SkyCity disclosed that in May 2026 it received two confidential, unsolicited, conditional, non-binding indicative proposals for the whole company: NZ$0.70 cash a share from a special situations fund managed by Oaktree Capital Management, and an implied NZ$0.75 cash a share from another party. Both needed at least eight weeks of due diligence and debt financing, and one or both asked SkyCity to stop asset sales, grant exclusivity and keep its debt facilities. The board rejected both unanimously; neither party came back with a better proposal [3].
  - A whole-company process: on 30 September 2026 the board said it would evaluate opportunities "under a structured process, including engagement with interested parties", with UBS and Chapman Tripp appointed [4]. At the 21 October annual meeting the chair said the board "remains aware of continued interest in the whole business", will give interested parties some due diligence information, has set up a Structured Review sub-committee, and that the process "will take some time to progress", with no certainty of a transaction [5].
  - A sale process for Adelaide: a strategic review of SkyCity Adelaide began on 20 August 2026; after inquiries from "credible interested parties" a formal sale process led by UBS is to start [2][4].

That is a live sale process and a strategic review, so the sheet ends here and the company goes to the special situations watch list. config/live_deals.csv has no SkyCity row and research/deals/ has no SkyCity file, since there is no binding agreement or formal offer; the desk log of 1 November 2026 already lists SkyCity's structured process as a non-binding approach to watch. Sections 2 to 6 are not written as a research pick. The facts the brief asked for are set out below for the watch list.

## For the special situations watch list

### Price against the approaches
The close on 6 November 2026 was NZ$0.69 (bid $0.685, offer $0.70), a market value of $761.1m on 1,103,055,047 shares [31]. That is just under Oaktree's rejected $0.70, 8% under the other party's implied $0.75, and the same as the $0.70 price of the August 2025 equity raise [3][16]. Net tangible assets were about $0.90 a share at 30 June 2026 (equity $1,545.0m less intangibles of $555.3m, mostly casino licences; NZX shows $0.86) [1][31].

### Regulatory matters, with dates
New Zealand:
- Licence suspension: in September 2023 the Secretary for Internal Affairs applied to the Gambling Commission to suspend SkyCity Casino Management's casino operator's licence for "in the range of 10 days", over failures to detect a customer's continuous play from 2017 to 2021. Settled on 18 July 2024 by closing the Auckland gambling area for five days (about $5m of EBITDA); the application was withdrawn and the Commission confirmed the matter at an end on 22 July 2024; the closure ran 9 to 13 September 2024 [6][7][8].
- Department of Internal Affairs anti-money laundering case: notice on 12 February 2024 of High Court civil proceedings (maximum liability assessed at $8m); settled 21 May 2024; the High Court imposed a $4.16m penalty on 26 September 2024, ending the proceedings [9][10][11].
- Carded play: in place across the New Zealand casinos from July 2025, costing $20m to $30m of EBITDA in FY26 as guided [2][16].
- Licences: Auckland's casino venue licence runs to 2048; Queenstown's was renewed on 11 November 2025 for 15 years from 7 December 2025; Hamilton's runs to September 2027 and a 15-year renewal application has been filed [1][12].
- Online casinos: the government set out its approach on 30 July 2024; the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 took effect on 1 May 2026 [13][14]. The Department of Internal Affairs ran an auction for up to 15 rights to apply for licences from 29 September to 12 October 2026, at $15m a licence; 11 entities won the 15 rights, including TAB New Zealand and Stake. Licences last up to three years, renewable for five; providers without an application must stop serving New Zealanders from 1 December 2026; the regime is to be fully in place in 2027 [32]. On 13 October 2026 SkyCity said it had won the right to apply for two licences at $15m each, excluding GST, $30m in all [15]. SkyCity expects licences from early 2027 and the market to open in the first half of 2027; the Department estimated the market at $1.36 billion of gross gaming revenue a year (March 2026) [1][14].
- SkyCity Online proceedings: on 6 March 2026 SkyCity was served with proceedings testing the lawfulness of the Malta-run SkyCity Online, with an application to bring a funded class action for money lost from February 2020 to February 2026. SkyCity denies liability [17].

Australia (Adelaide):
- AUSTRAC: civil penalty proceedings filed 7 December 2022; agreement on a A$67m penalty on 17 May 2024, approved by the Federal Court on 7 June 2024 and paid in FY25 (NZ$75.7m of regulatory penalties paid) [18][19][1].
- Derivative action: notice received on 2 May 2025 of a proposed derivative action, funded by LCM Finance, against former SkyCity Adelaide directors and senior managers over the AUSTRAC penalty; a hearing on whether leave would be granted was set for November 2025 [16]. FY26 covenant EBITDA adds back $2.4m of costs for this and the online action [2]. The outcome of the leave hearing was not found in the filings read.
- Independent review (Brian Martin) for the South Australian regulator: started July 2022, paused February 2023, resumed 11 June 2024; report released 12 August 2025 found SkyCity Adelaide suitable to hold the licence and SkyCity suitable as a close associate [20][21]. On 19 June 2026 a non-binding heads of agreement settled all matters for a A$21m fine in three A$7m instalments (within 28 days of a binding deed, then one and two years later), an Adelaide board with an independent majority and chair by 1 January 2028, cash transactions over A$4,999 phased out, and powers for the Commissioner to direct SkyCity on services Adelaide depends on [22]. On 30 September 2026 the binding deed was "well advanced"; it has not been announced [4].
- Remediation: the Building a Better Business programme (approved 11 September 2024, about $60m over FY25 to FY27) now expects final approval in early FY28; Adelaide was written down by A$86.2m at June 2024 and A$43m at June 2026 [1][21][23].
- Casino duty: the South Australian Court of Appeal ruled for the Treasurer on 22 February 2024 on loyalty points in casino duty; the High Court of Australia confirmed it on 16 October 2024; on 31 January 2025 SkyCity Adelaide settled for A$38.1m (A$13.1m of duty, A$24.8m of interest, A$0.2m of costs) [24][25][26].

Tax: New Zealand removed tax depreciation on long-life commercial buildings (enacted 28 March 2024), giving SkyCity a one-off, non-cash NZ$129.6m deferred tax charge in FY24 [23]. No change to New Zealand land-based casino duty appears in the filings read. The duty or levy on licensed online casinos was not verified (see the report back).

### Debt and covenants
- At 30 June 2026: US private placement notes of US$75m (due 28 February 2030) and US$150m (due 15 September 2031), hedged to NZ$375.9m; NZ$175m of 3.02% retail bonds (SKC050) due 21 May 2027; bank facilities undrawn. Drawn debt NZ$550.9m less cash of $84.4m and a $30.0m short-term deposit: net debt of $436.5m excluding leases; lease liabilities add $154.2m, giving SkyCity's own figure of $590.7m [1][2].
- In FY26 SkyCity repaid A$65.4m of private placement notes due March 2028. On 29 July 2026 it merged the July and September 2027 bank tranches into one $140m facility to 15 September 2029, leaving $277.5m undrawn ($137.5m to September 2028, $140m to September 2029) [1][27].
- Covenants: net debt to covenant EBITDA was 3.1 times at 30 June 2026 (covenant EBITDA $189.1m), against a covenant of 3.75 times stated on 6 June 2024; all covenants met. Target below 2.0 times by the end of FY27 after the Grand Hotel sale and before any online licence payment. S&P rates SkyCity BBB- with a negative outlook, to be reviewed when the Grand Hotel sale settles [1][2][28].
- What uses the sale proceeds in FY27: the $175m bond in May 2027, $30m of online licence fees, the A$21m Adelaide fine, $17m to $22m of one-off cost-out costs, and capex of about $90m (the middle of $80m to $100m) [5][22].

### Asset sales
- Gaming Innovation Group stake sold in June 2024 ($56.8m received in FY25) [1].
- 99 Albert Street and Victoria Street commercial properties: heads of agreement 1 May 2026, unconditional 17 July, settled 1 September 2026 for $74.5m (Mainland Capital with Russell Property Group) [29].
- The Grand Hotel: heads of agreement 22 July 2026; sale and purchase agreement signed 16 October 2026 for $221.0m with Eden Global Capital and Auckland City Collective, conditional on Overseas Investment Office consent, settlement targeted for mid-December 2026; SkyCity will run the hotel for the buyer until 30 June 2027 [29][33].
- Together $295.5m, against the $275m to $300m programme [4][33].
- Adelaide: formal sale process to be run by UBS [4].
- Claim against Fletchers over the convention centre delay and fire: liquidated damages of over $330m plus interest claimed, trial in May 2029; held as a contingent asset, not on the balance sheet [1][34].

### Dividends
The last dividend was the FY24 interim of 5.25c, paid in March 2024 [31]. On 6 June 2024 the board suspended dividends for 2H24 and FY25 to keep headroom under the 3.75 times covenant, then expecting to resume in FY26 [28]. None was paid for FY26 [2]. At the 21 October 2026 meeting the chair said the board aims to resume dividends once its capital management and operating goals are met, "in the FY28 financial year" [5].
