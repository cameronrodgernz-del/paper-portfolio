## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Cedar Woods develops and sells residential land, townhouses, apartments and some strata offices across WA, Queensland, Victoria and South Australia: 36 projects, a pipeline of about 9,600 lots and homes, revenue A$502.4m in FY26 [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Audited with no modified opinion or emphasis of matter [3].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$924,703 a day (lowest day A$462,968), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is the Chair, William Hames, and related entities (12.90% at last notice; 11.26m shares, about 13.2%, at his June 2026 notice) [4][14].

There's no gap between statutory and underlying profit to recur; the company reports statutory only. The items that flatter FY26 are in finance costs: interest and charges before capitalisation were about A$17.0m in FY26 and A$17.5m in FY25, but the expensed figure fell from A$15.3m to A$9.0m because of a A$3.0m swing on interest rate hedges and A$2.7m more interest capitalised into inventory, which comes back through cost of sales when those stages settle [4].

What else the headline numbers hide:
- The cycle has turned. Gross sales in the September 2026 quarter were 167, down 57% on a year earlier and 24% on the June quarter; enquiry fell in Q4 FY26; the company cites three interest rate rises in 2026, cost-of-living pressure and tax changes [1][2][5]. Presales fell from A$830m at June to A$763m at September, of which about A$380m settles in FY27, A$316m in FY28 and A$67m in FY29 [1][5].
- FY26 is a peak on several measures: gross margin 30% (28% in FY25, 26% in H1 FY25), EBIT margin 20.5% against 12.7% to 18.0% in FY22 to FY25, and profit of A$65.6m against a five-year average of A$44.7m [4][6].
- The debt is bigger than the bank figure: A$157.7m of net bank debt plus A$78.3m owed to land vendors is A$236m, and the company keeps buying land on deferred terms (Kealba A$30.85m settling mid-FY28; Fairfield A$27.25m mid-FY27, subject to approvals; Bushmead A$15.55m paid in Q1 FY27) [1][4][8][5].
- Operating cash flow was negative in FY26; dividends (A$27.3m in the equity statement) and payments to project partners (A$14.2m) were funded by A$31.0m of new borrowing and A$11.3m of underwritten dividend reinvestment equity [4].
- Buyer mix leans to first home buyers and government incentives, and to WA and Queensland [2].

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance (FY27): NPAT growth of about 15%, "approximately A$75 million", subject to market conditions; given 25 Aug 2026, reaffirmed 27 Oct 2026 (Q1 update, "on track") and 5 Nov 2026 (AGM) [1][5][15]. FY27 settlement target 1,100 to 1,200 lots; 311 in Q1, "ahead of budget" [5]. The company says more than 90% of FY27 revenue was settled or presold at 30 September and expects gross margin "broadly consistent" with FY26 [1][5].

Changes in FY26:
- 26 Aug 2025: NPAT growth of 10% [9].
- 29 Oct 2025: raised to about 15% [10].
- 17 Dec 2025: raised to at least 20% [11].
- 24 Feb 2026: raised to 30% to 35% [6].
- 30 Apr 2026: reaffirmed [12]; 5 Jun 2026: "top end" [8].

Record:
- FY26: guidance raised four times and beaten.
- FY25: 15% growth targeted; delivered 18.9% (A$48.1m) [9].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is FY27's A$75m (largely presold) the peak of a cycle that has already turned, so profit falls back towards its five-year average from FY28, or does the land bank and a rate peak carry profit at about this level? A deep dive would rebuild FY28 and FY29 from presales, the stage release schedule and margins by project, and test the land vendor commitments and the A$236m of total debt against a slower sales rate.
- If it goes our way: FY27 guidance, A$65.6m x 1.15 = A$75.5m, holds as the run rate, valued at the company's own five-year average price-to-earnings of 10.4 times (year-end price over EPS for FY22 to FY26: 8.1, 13.1, 9.6, 12.1 and 8.9 times, from its five-year table [4]): A$781.8m, or A$9.15 a share on 85.48m shares. Finbar (ASX:FRI), a WA developer, at 9.7 times in the ASX company data gives A$8.60.
- If it goes against us: profit returns to its five-year average (A$37.4m, A$31.6m, A$40.5m, A$48.1m, A$65.6m: A$44.7m) at the same 10.4 times: A$462.5m, or A$5.41 a share. That is below net tangible assets of A$6.35, as the shares were for FY22 to FY24 (0.72 to 0.97 times NTA at year end) [4].
- Price: A$6.14 (close 30 Oct 2026, after the 25c final dividend went ex; also the last trade in the ASX data on 6 Nov 2026), market value A$525m. Gap if it goes our way: 9.15 / 6.14 - 1 = +49%. Gap if it goes against us: 5.41 / 6.14 - 1 = -12%. At the price the market pays 7.0 times FY27 guidance.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is +49%; FY28 and FY29 earnings can be rebuilt from the filings (presales by year, stage schedules, margins, the land book), and the H1 FY27 result in late February 2027 and the Q3 update in April 2027 will show whether sales recover and how much of FY28 is presold, inside six months. It just misses being a value pick: the "against" value is 12% below the price, against the 10% limit. The earlier quick look rejected it; nothing in the filings is a kill fact, and the deep dive should face that judgement directly (a falling sales rate, peak margins, and debt that is A$236m once land vendors are counted).
