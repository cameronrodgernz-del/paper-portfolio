# The Colonial Motor Company (NZX:CMO): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 4 points, #4 in the NZX table of reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. A group of franchised motor vehicle dealerships (Ford, Mazda, BYD and others), Southpac Trucks (including DAF) and tractor and implement dealerships, which owns most of the land and buildings it trades from [1].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Grant Thornton's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified [1].
- Too thin to trade? Not measured, and probably yes. I could not get 20 sessions of daily volume (see the last section). Investing.com shows a three-month average of 2,640 shares a day (about NZ$19,000 at $7.18) but only 33 shares on 30 October [9]; 8 of the last 20 sessions in our price feed traded at a single price, and 28 to 30 October all closed at $7.18 with no range [10]. An average of NZ$19,000 built from occasional larger trades points to a median below NZ$12,500. This needs to be measured; if it fails, the sheet is a C on that fact alone.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. Gibbons family interests appear in several substantial holder groups, the largest at 5.71%, and P L Bennett holds 7.42% [1]. Family members move shares between themselves off market at around the market price (section 4); no single holder or disclosed association is over 50%.

The gap between statutory and trading profit is small and does recur (property revaluation movements through profit every year: minus $0.05m in FY25, plus $0.4m in FY26). The bigger point is the run of trading profit: $33.3m in FY22, $30.3m in FY23, then $17.9m, $17.8m and $18.7m. The five-year average is $23.6m, or 72.2c a share, and return on equity has been 5.9% for three years [1].

Land and buildings are carried at $251.4m on an independent Quotable Value valuation at 30 June 2026 (cost basis $143.3m); the revaluation reserve is $122.5m [1]. The bank facilities carry a negative pledge, so the property is unencumbered.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is the property worth its valuation, and is a 5.9% return on equity the new normal or a low point? The price values the whole group below its property less its debt.
- If it goes our way: property at its stated value and everything else at book less debt, the reported NTA of $9.77 a share.
- If it goes against us: property 15% below valuation ($213.7m) and the dealerships valued at 6 times what they earn after paying a notional rent. Notional rent is 6.5% of $251.4m, $16.3m before tax and $11.8m after tax at 28%; FY26 trading profit of $18.7m less that is $6.9m, and 6 times is $41.4m. $213.7m plus $41.4m, less net debt of $39.0m and the $5.8m minority interest, is $210.3m, or $6.43 a share. The 6.5% rent and the 6 times are my stated assumptions.
- Only now the price: the close on 30 October 2026 was $7.18 [10]. Gap if it goes our way, 9.77 / 7.18 less 1 = +36.1%. Gap if it goes against us, 6.43 / 7.18 less 1 = -10.4%. At the price, the shares are 12.6 times trading profit and 0.73 times NTA.

## 7. Grade
B on the gap, which is 36%: above 25%, below 40%. But it is likely to become a C on liquidity, which I could not measure. Measure the 20-session median first; if it is below NZ$12,500, it is a C with that kill fact.
