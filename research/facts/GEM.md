# G8 Education (ASX:GEM): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 6 points, #13 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. It runs 350 long day care centres across Australia, almost all on leases [1].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The half-year accounts are prepared as a going concern; the group met all its bank covenants and forecasts it will keep doing so for at least 12 months [2]. But the lenders loosened the fixed charge cover covenant up to the 31 December 2027 test as part of the July 2026 extension [2], and the Fair Work Ombudsman began Federal Court proceedings on 29 June 2026 alleging 28 underpayment contraventions and seeking penalties [2]. Neither is a kill on the template's terms; both are in the "against us" figure.
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$228,169 a day (lowest day A$87,974), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries; childcare is outside the hold.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. No holder is near 50%. Allan Gray built its stake from 15.35% to 19.87% between February 2024 and 23 February 2026, just under the 20% takeover threshold, and Washington H. Soul Pattinson went from 6.53% to 9.66% between October 2025 and 12 February 2026 [18][19][20][21][22]. That is a build-up worth noting, but it finished more than eight months ago and there is no approach or review. Not routed to special situations.

The gap between statutory and operating profit recurs. CY25 left out a A$349.1m goodwill write-down and centre exit costs; H1 CY26 left out another A$47.1m of centre impairments and A$11.1m of new onerous lease provisions [2]. Centre write-downs and exit costs have appeared in each of the last three reporting periods (A$5.7m in H1 CY25 as well), so they are a running cost of a shrinking network, not one-offs.

What the headline hides:
- Occupancy. Group occupancy was 70.7% in CY24, 65.8% in CY25 and 57.0% in H1 CY26, down 7.5 points on a year earlier [3][1]. Spot occupancy on 21 August 2026 was 61.9%, down 5.1 points; year to date at end of July was 58.0%, down 7.0 points [5]. Each point of occupancy has been worth about A$3.4m of operating EBIT a half (A$40.5m at 64.5% in H1 CY25 against A$14.7m at 57.0% in H1 CY26, with cost savings in between).
- The value of its centres. G8 owns almost no property: the centres are leased (right-of-use assets A$435.3m, lease liabilities A$613.3m) [2]. The balance sheet value is goodwill. The goodwill test at 30 June 2026 passed with almost no room: a 0.7 point rise in the pre-tax discount rate (11%), a 0.5 point fall in terminal growth (2%), or a 4% fall in forecast 2030 EBITDA would remove the headroom [2][4]. The test assumes occupancy returns to 2025 levels (65.8%) by 2030 [2]. Occupancy is now about 9 points below that. Exiting centres has cost money, not raised it: G8 paid A$9.6m in H1 CY25 and A$0.5m in H1 CY26 for divestments and lease surrenders [2].
- Rent on closed centres. The 40 centres suspended in April 2026 still carry leases (A$0.4m of lease interest on suspended centres in H1, onerous contract provisions of A$11.1m) [2][7].
- Cash after rent and capex turned negative in H1 CY26 (minus A$9.7m), and borrowings rose A$9.6m [2].
- Provisions still to be paid in cash: A$21.5m regulatory, legal and other (including the wage remediation and the FWO case) and A$11.1m onerous contracts [2].
- Lease commitments: A$795.3m of undiscounted lease payments, A$110.3m of them due within 12 months [2].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: has occupancy stopped falling, and is the equity worth anything if it settles near 57% instead of returning to the 65.8% the company's goodwill test assumes? A deep dive would work out from the filings and the Department of Education's data how much profit the network makes at each occupancy level, after rent on the suspended centres, and how close that runs to the bank covenants.
- If it goes our way: occupancy returns to the 2025 level, as the goodwill test assumes, so operating EBIT returns to the CY25 level of A$93.3m (the five-year average is A$93.9m [4]). At 9.09 times (the inverse of the company's own 11% pre-tax discount rate, no growth), A$848m, discounted three years at 11% for the recovery to arrive: A$620m. Less net bank debt of A$128.2m and A$32.6m of cash provisions (regulatory and legal, onerous leases): A$459.4m, or A$0.61 a share on 756.6m shares. This is close to book value of A$0.64 a share, which is what the company's own test implies.
- If it goes against us: occupancy keeps falling at the pace of the past year's spot decline, another 5 points. Operating EBIT starts from a CY26 run rate of A$41.7m (H1 CY26 A$14.7m, plus H2 at last year's A$52.8m less the same A$25.8m fall as H1) and loses about A$6.9m a year per point: A$41.7m less A$34.4m is A$7.3m. At 9.09 times, A$66m, which is less than the A$160.8m of bank debt and provisions. The equity is worth nothing: A$0.00 a share, before any covenant breach or penalty. For reference, if occupancy simply stays at 57%, the same arithmetic gives A$218m, or A$0.29 a share.
- Price: A$0.10 (close 30 Oct 2026), market value about A$76m. Gap if it goes our way: 0.61 / 0.10 - 1 = +507%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.00 / 0.10 - 1 = -100%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is over 40%; the size of the equity at each occupancy level, the lease load and the covenant room can be worked from the filings and the government's own childcare data; and the CY26 result on about 23 February 2027 will show full-year and January occupancy, the year-end goodwill test and the covenant test, inside six months. The equity is a thin slice over A$613m of leases and A$128m of bank debt, so the "against" figure is nil, and a deep dive must treat that as a live outcome.
