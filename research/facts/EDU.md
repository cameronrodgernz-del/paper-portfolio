# EDU Holdings (ASX:EDU): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #21 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. EDU runs Ikon Institute (higher education degrees, mostly in education, counselling and community services) and Australian Learning Group (vocational training). 7,489 enrolments in T3 2026; revenue A$82.4m in the year to December 2025 [1][2][3].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. A proposal to delist with an equal access buy-back (14 May 2025) was withdrawn on 29 May 2025 after shareholders objected [4]. No modified audit opinion [2].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$295,129 a day (lowest day A$57,486), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The CEO's family trust is the largest holder at 12.2% [5]. The pending event is an EGM on 26 Nov 2026 to let an on-market buy-back of up to about 12.5m shares start before February 2027 [6][7].

The gap between statutory and underlying profit is tiny, but it recurs: small due diligence and deal costs appear every period (A$72k, A$32k, A$92k), and H1 FY26 included A$55k of "transaction costs in relation to proposed acquisitions" in investing cash flow [2][3]. The company is looking at acquisitions.

What else the headline numbers hide:
- Customer concentration: international students paid A$49.0m of A$54.3m of H1 FY26 revenue (90%). Policy has already moved against the sector: from 1 April 2026 providers may no longer pay agents commission on students transferring from another provider, and the National Planning Level for new international students is 295,000 for 2027 [3]. The directors' own impairment test adds a 7.0% "specific company risk" to the discount rate for this [2].
- Growth now comes from students already enrolled, not new ones. Total enrolments in T3 2026 rose 26% to 7,489, but new student enrolments fell 13% to 1,194; higher education new enrolments were flat at 1,074 [10]. The company expects "larger graduating cohorts" from 2027 [11].
- The vocational arm is shrinking: ALG enrolments down 26% and new enrolments down 46% in H1 FY26 [3].
- The cash is mostly fees paid in advance: A$26.4m of contract liabilities against A$24.0m of cash at June 2026, and net tangible assets went negative [3][8].
- Capital returns are large relative to the company: about 24% of shares were bought back in the 18 months to February 2026, including 18.0m shares from Mulpha and Investec at A$0.55 in February 2026 [1][12].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: do Ikon's earnings keep growing once new-student intake has flattened and bigger cohorts start graduating in 2027, with 90% of revenue from international students under tightening rules? Or was FY26 the peak? A deep dive would rebuild enrolments by cohort (new intake, retention, graduations) to forecast 2027 revenue, and test how exposed the onshore-transfer channel is to the April 2026 agent rule.
- If it goes our way: the directors' own value-in-use figures at 31 Dec 2025 hold: higher education A$159.9m and vocational A$9.4m (pre-tax discount rates of 18.9% and 16.4%, including the 7% policy risk premium, with tuition revenue growth averaging 20% for higher education) [2]. Less unallocated corporate costs of A$2.87m a year capitalised at 18.9% with 3% growth (A$18.6m), plus December 2025 cash of A$18.5m, less A$20.1m returned to shareholders since (A$9.9m selective buy-back, A$2.66m on-market, two 3.0c dividends of A$3.8m each): A$149.1m, or A$1.20 a share on 124.57m shares.
- If it goes against us: growth stops and profit stays at FY25's level. FY25 profit before tax and one-off items of A$20.55m, with no growth, at the 18.9% pre-tax rate: A$108.7m (7.3 times FY25 profit after tax). Plus A$18.5m cash less A$20.1m returned: A$107.1m, or A$0.86 a share. A policy shock that cut international intake the way vocational intake has fallen would take value below this; this case doesn't price that.
- Price: A$0.815 (close 30 Oct 2026, the ranking's price), market value A$102m. The ASX's last trade was A$0.88 on 6 Nov 2026. Gap if it goes our way: 1.20 / 0.815 - 1 = +47% (+36% at A$0.88). Gap if it goes against us: 0.86 / 0.815 - 1 = +5% (-2% at A$0.88).

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now, and a value pick at the ranking price: the gap if it goes our way is +47%, and the "against" value is above the price. Enrolments can be rebuilt from the company's trimester reports and filings, and the EGM (26 Nov 2026), the FY26 guidance, T1 2027 enrolments and the FY26 result (late February 2027) all fall inside six months. Two cautions. The price has moved: at the 6 Nov price of A$0.88 the "our way" gap is +36%, under 40%. And the "against" case assumes FY25 profit holds; it does not price a regulatory cut to international intake, which is the main risk a deep dive must size. Directors have been buying on market (CEO, Mobbs and Pager) and the company is buying back shares.
