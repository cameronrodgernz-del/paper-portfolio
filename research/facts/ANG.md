# Austin Engineering (ASX:ANG): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 6 points, #8 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Austin designs and builds equipment for mine haul trucks and diggers: truck bodies (62% of FY26 revenue), buckets (10%),
water tanks and parts, from plants in Australia, Batam (Indonesia), the United States and Chile. Revenue by region in
FY26: Asia Pacific 44.7%, North America 38.6%, South America 16.7%. Miners are 83% of customers; copper and iron ore are
25% of revenue each, oil sands 18%, metallurgical coal 15%, gold 8%.

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. BDO's FY26 opinion is unmodified (annual
  report, 25 Aug 2026). Two related records, neither a qualification: the FY24 accounts were restated because Chile booked
  revenue on several truck bodies too early (announced 13 Aug 2025: FY24 revenue down 4.9m, EBIT down 3.3m, moved into
  FY25); and ASX sent an "aware letter" on the HY26 earnings and guidance cut, answered 4 Mar 2026.
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$81,203 (volume times close,
  data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz). Trading is lumpy: the lowest session was A$1,759. Two sessions at 10% of the
  median is about A$16,000, well above a 2.5% position.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. Thorney International holds
  21.55%, the largest stake.

Nothing ends the sheet.

Does the gap between statutory and underlying recur? Yes, in part. Inventory write-downs (3.3m, then 2.2m), restructuring
(0.6m, then 1.3m) and currency losses (1.4m, then 1.1m) appear in both years and are recurring costs of the business.
The FY25 items for expanding the US and Chile plants were real spending the company chose to call one-off. Underlying EBIT
of 45.9m in FY25 therefore overstates normal earnings; EBIT excluding currency (33.2m, then 10.8m) is the fairer measure.

What the headline numbers hide:
- Profit more than halved for an identified reason: a loss-making truck body contract with an equipment maker in Chile,
  signed in FY24 (Chile EBITDA loss 9.3m in FY26), low productivity and heavy outsourcing in the US (workshop
  productivity 62% in July 2025, about 80% by the June quarter), and fewer truck body orders in Asia Pacific and North
  America. The Chile contract was repriced in March 2026, with deliveries on the new terms from late June 2026.
- The strong FY26 cash flow came mostly from running down inventory (32.2m), which won't repeat.
- Customer concentration: the company says 51% of Asia Pacific work now comes from outside "single-customer
  relationships" (FY26 release). It doesn't name the customers or say how much any one of them takes; the deep dive
  should find out.
- Order book at 30 September 2026: 139m, up 18% on a year earlier; Asia Pacific up 52%, South America up 33%, North
  America down 43% (the company expects a softer first half there and has furloughed staff).
- Index selling: Austin left the S&P/ASX 300 on 23 March 2026.

## 3. Guidance and record
- FY27 (25 Aug 2026): underlying EBIT from continuing operations, excluding currency moves, of 17m to 21m. Reconfirmed at
  the AGM on 30 October 2026. No changes.
- FY26, cut three times: 26 Aug 2025, revenue 390m to 410m and underlying EBIT 40m to 46m; 5 Nov 2025, 370m to 380m and
  30m to 34m; 26 Feb 2026, revenue 350m-plus and EBIT 14m to 16m excluding currency (a narrower measure than before);
  16 Jun 2026, about 325m and 10m to 11m. Delivered 329.0m and 10.8m.
- FY25, cut once then helped by the restatement: guidance for underlying EBIT had been about 50m and was cut to about
  41m on 11 Jun 2025 (revenue raised to about 370m); after 5.2m of FY24 EBIT moved into FY25 (13 Aug 2025),
  reported underlying EBIT was 45.9m on revenue of 376.7m.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is FY26 a low point from problems that are now fixed (the Chile contract repriced, US productivity up,
  orders up 18%), so that earnings go back towards their five-year average as the FY27 guidance implies? Or is the record
  of four guidance cuts in two years, plus a restatement, the better guide?
- If it goes our way: EBITDA excluding currency returns to its five-year average (FY22 to FY26: 20.3m, 32.5m, 43.5m,
  43.0m, 20.4m, from the FY26 presentation p13; average 31.9m). Less FY26 depreciation of 9.7m, EBIT 22.2m. Less interest
  of 1.5m and tax at 25%: profit 15.6m. At the price-to-earnings of Emeco (ASX:EHL, mining equipment hire, 9.1 times in
  the ASX company data): A$142m / 623.1m shares = A$0.227.
- If it goes against us: FY26 repeats. Profit excluding currency 7.6m (FY26 presentation p12) at the same 9.1 times:
  A$69m / 623.1m = A$0.111.
- Only now the price: A$0.17 (close 30 October 2026). Gap if it goes our way: 0.227 / 0.17 - 1 = +34%. Gap if it goes
  against us: 0.111 / 0.17 - 1 = -35%.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is 34%: above 25% but short of the 40% an A needs. The
answer needs the event itself: the HY27 result due about 26 February 2027 will show whether Chile and the US have turned.
A higher peer multiple would widen the gap; at Civmec's 15.9 times (ASX company data) the our-way figure would be about
A$0.40.
