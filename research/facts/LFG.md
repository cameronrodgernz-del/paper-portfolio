# Liberty Financial Group (ASX:LFG): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 4 points, #63 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-08).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Liberty is a non-bank lender in Australia and New Zealand: home loans (50% of the A$15.2bn loan book), car, equipment
and commercial property loans (43%) and personal and small business loans (7%, including Moula from December 2025),
plus loan and insurance broking. It is a stapled company and trust; 75.2% is held by Vesta Funding BV, controlled by
director Sherman Ma [1][3]. All figures A$; the year ends 30 June.

A note on the ranking's numbers: the "net cash" of minus A$14.2 a security counts the A$15.1bn of loan funding as company
debt, and the 0.0% yield misses the distributions. LFG pays a quarterly distribution from the trust and special
dividends from the company [1][4].

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An operating lender; the trust half of the
  staple holds loans and pays out its income [1].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. KPMG's opinion on the FY26 accounts is
  unqualified [3]; the company says there is no sign it may have trouble meeting its corporate debt covenants in the
  next 12 months [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$308,634 a day (lowest day
  A$60,364), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz [17].
- On Cam's excluded list? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries (confirmed by Cam on 8 Nov 2026).
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? There is a holder over 50%, and
  the desk judges it is not making the kind of moves this test means, but the facts are set out so the desk can
  disagree. Vesta Funding BV holds 228,227,245 securities (75.2%), the same number at 30 June 2025 and at 31 July 2026; it
  has filed no substantial holder notice in the last 12 months and has made no offer [3][5]. In FY26 it repaid a
  A$76.7m loan from Liberty, and Liberty paid A$20.9m on 30 Dec 2025 to Beat Services, a company Sherman Ma controls, for
  a controlling 50% of Moula Money, which Liberty also funds through a warehouse (A$105.6m drawn of A$200m) [1]. No
  review or sale is announced. Noted for the special situations watch list in the report back.

Return on equity (statutory, on average equity): 12.1% in FY26, 12.8% in 1H FY26 annualised and 11.4% in 2H FY26
annualised (desk arithmetic from [1][6]); the company's "underlying cash" return, on NPATA, was 13.1% (FY25 12.1%) [2].

Does the gap between statutory and underlying profit recur? Yes, but it is one item: the A$11.8m amortisation of
intangibles bought before listing, the same in both years. Underlying NPATA is statutory profit plus that non-cash charge.

What the headline numbers hide:
- Tax. Profit is after only 8.3% tax (A$13.0m on A$156.8m), because the trust's income is passed to holders untaxed:
  "non-assessable income (distribution income)" cut the tax bill by A$33.7m [1]. Holders pay tax on the unfranked
  distributions themselves. Listed peers pay 30% company tax before profit, so LFG's 7.1 times earnings is not like for
  like. On a 30% tax charge, FY26 profit would be A$109.8m and return on equity 9.2% (desk arithmetic).
- The second half was weaker. Impairment rose from 5 basis points of the book in 1H FY26 to 14 in 2H FY26; 30-day arrears
  rose to 4.08% at 30 Jun 2026 (3.85% at 31 Jul 2026) and the company says "increasing interest rates may impact
  delinquency" [2].
- Payouts exceed profit. FY26 cash distributions and dividends were A$197.0m against A$143.8m of profit; the specials
  return surplus capital and are not steady income [1][4].
- The controlling holder and related dealings set out in section 1; free float is 24.8%.
- Group-level debt that falls due: the first of the A$1.35bn unsecured notes matures in April 2027 (amount not given in
  the accounts read) [1].
- Goodwill and other intangibles are A$227.4m, 19% of equity [1].

## 3. Guidance and record
Liberty gives no profit or distribution guidance. The FY26 outlook is in words only: favourable funding markets "will
continue to support NIM", while "increasing interest rates may impact delinquency" [2]. The first FY27 quarterly
distribution was set at 8.0c, up from 7.5c [4]. Nothing changed during 2026, and with no numeric guidance in FY25 or FY26
there is no record to test. The 2025 AGM said Liberty "enters FY26 with strong momentum"; FY26 underlying NPATA rose 7%
[8].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is the second half's weaker return (11.4% annualised, as arrears and impairments rose) the run rate, or
  does Liberty hold the first half's 12.8%? Book value barely moves because surplus profit goes out as specials, so the
  return earned on book sets the value.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: lenders trade on price to book against the return they earn. The named
  peers, on their latest filings and last trades on 6 Nov 2026 (ASX company data):
  - Pepper Money (ASX:PPM): equity A$886.9m over 449.38m shares is A$1.974; price A$1.775, 0.899 times book; return on
    equity 11.48% (A$50.4m first-half 2026 profit annualised, over average equity of A$878.4m) [15].
  - Resimac (ASX:RMC): equity A$367.9m over 395.53m shares is A$0.930; price A$0.83, 0.892 times book; return on equity
    13.42% (A$49.2m over average equity of A$366.5m) [16].
  - Average: the market pays 0.0724 times book for each point of return on equity. The price would move through half-year
    results that show the return.
- If it goes our way: the return holds at 1H FY26's 12.8%. Value 3.884 x 0.0724 x 12.80 = A$3.60 (0.93 times book). On
  the company's 13.1% cash return, A$3.69.
- If it goes against us: 2H FY26's 11.4% is the run rate. Value 3.884 x 0.0724 x 11.38 = A$3.20 (0.82 times book).
- Tax check: peers' returns are after 30% company tax. If Liberty's profit were taxed the same way (9.2% return), the
  value would be A$2.60. The market has not applied that discount.
- Only now the price: A$3.35, the last trade on 6 Nov 2026 (ASX company data; the session before also A$3.35). 0.86 times
  book, 1.06 times NTA, 7.1 times FY26 earnings, ordinary distribution yield 11.2%. Gap if it goes our way:
  3.60 / 3.35 - 1 = +7% (+10% on the cash return). Gap if it goes against us: 3.20 / 3.35 - 1 = -4%.

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive: the gap if it goes our way is +7% (+10% at most), under 25%. Priced on book value against Pepper Money
and Resimac for the return it earns, Liberty is about where they are, and its low tax rate means its earnings multiple
flatters it against fully taxed peers. Not a value pick, as the good case is under 40%. The controlling holder is noted
for the special situations watch list; the 1H FY27 result (about 23 Feb 2027) shows the return if the desk wants to look
again.
