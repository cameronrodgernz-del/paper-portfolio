# Fenix Resources (ASX:FEX): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 6 points, #10 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Fenix mines iron ore in Western Australia's Mid-West (Beebyn-W11 and W10 at the Weld Range, the tail of Iron Ridge, Shine), trucks it to Geraldton in its own fleet and ships it from its own port sheds: 4.4Mt in FY26 [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Grant Thornton's opinion on the June 2026 accounts is unmodified [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$187,905 a day (lowest day A$41,146), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. No holder is above 10%. The former director Craig Mitchell is selling down (section 4). Fenix's own bid for CZR Resources lapsed in April 2025.

There is no gap between statutory and underlying profit to judge: Fenix reports no adjusted profit.

What the headline numbers hide:
- The iron ore price the value rests on. FY26 realised price A$146.8/dmt CFR (FY25 A$144.0) [2]. In the September 2026 quarter the Platts 61% index averaged US$96.9/dmt (June quarter US$105.3) and Fenix realised US$89.4, or A$126.6/dmt CFR, against A$138.7 in June and A$147.6 a year earlier; freight cost A$32.6/dmt and C1 cash cost was A$81.8/wmt, above the A$70 to A$80 guidance [4]. On 23 Oct 2026 Fenix closed out its iron ore swaps (480kt at A$151.31/t) for about A$10.2m, so its iron ore sales are now unhedged; diesel swaps and A$99m of currency options remain [8].
- The margin at that price is thin or negative. FY26 revenue was A$134/wmt and EBITDA A$18.3/wmt with C1 at A$73.7 [2]. In the September quarter revenue was about A$121/wmt (A$126.6 less 4.4% moisture) and freight about A$31/wmt; with C1 at A$81.8 and royalties and overheads of about A$15/wmt (taken from FY26, assuming FY26 freight averaged about A$28/dmt), the margin is about minus A$7/wmt, and about nil with C1 back at A$75. Second-half FY26 profit was only A$2.6m at about A$139/dmt [3][4]. The quarter's reported operating cash flow (A$3.9m, plus A$17.0m of late receipts and A$3.9m of hedge receipts) is better than this estimate; the difference, probably working capital, needs a deep dive.
- Reserves and mine life. Beebyn Hub Ore Reserve 26.9Mt at 60.0% Fe (W11 13.1Mt at 61.2%, W10 Stage 1 13.8Mt at 58.9%), estimated at US$80 to US$100/dmt and A$1 = US$0.65 [9]; Iron Ridge 0.35Mt at 66.7% Fe [1]. Shine has resources (9.6Mt) but no reserve [1]. About 27.3Mt in all, which the company says is "more than five years of mine life at current production rates" (about 5.5 years at 5Mt a year) [4][9].
- The long-life option has strings. The 290Mt Weld Range resource (288.6Mt at 56.7% Fe at 30 Jun 2026) is held under a 30-year right to mine from Sinosteel: A$60m in three A$20m payments, a royalty of A$4.00 to A$5.00/dmt, 10% of the project's after-tax profit (15% when iron ore is above US$100), a Sinosteel right to match any buyer, and best endeavours to produce more than 6Mt a year after ramp-up [1][10]. The December 2025 scoping study for 10Mt a year needs A$521m of development capital [11].
- One partner in several roles: ResInvest markets Fenix's ore through Fenix Commodities Pty Ltd (incorporated June 2026, 50% Fenix, jointly owned "marketing and sales vehicle", paid a marketing fee), lends the US$44m facilities secured on stockpiles, receivables and Fenix's stake in that vehicle, and holds 5.8% of the shares [1][5].

Capital over FY27 and FY28: sustaining about A$20m, trucks and trailers A$40m to A$50m, growth about A$30m in mining and about A$20m in logistics and port [2].

Changes this year:

- 23 Dec 2025: Weld Range scoping study; a definitive feasibility study (DFS) "targeted for completion during the June 2026 quarter" [11].
- 7 Jul 2026: FY27 guidance as above [12].
- 22 Oct 2026: DFS "now targeted for completion in the first half of calendar year 2027 (previously the second half of calendar year 2026)"; final investment decision during 2028 [4].

Record: FY26 sales of 4.4Mt were inside both the original and the raised range, and C1 of A$73.7/wmt inside A$70 to A$80 [2][12]. FY25: the FY24 result gave no tonnage guidance; it targeted first Beebyn-W11 production in the first quarter of 2025 [14]. Mining there began on 30 Jun 2025 and the first shipment was in August 2025, about six months late [27]. The DFS date has moved twice. Two filings give different FY25 C1 costs: A$76.2/wmt (7 Jul 2026) and A$72.8/wmt (26 Aug 2026) [2][12].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is Fenix a road-haul miner with about five years of ore that makes little at today's iron ore price, or the owner of a 10Mt a year Weld Range project worth several times its market value? The DFS and its funding plan should settle it.
- If it goes our way: the DFS confirms the scoping study (pre-tax NPV at 10% of A$1,184m at US$85/dmt and A$1 = US$0.65, which already deducts the A$521m of capital) and the project is funded without selling shares below value. A$1,184m x 0.70 (company tax) / 1.10 squared (two years to a 2028 decision) = A$685.0m, less net debt of about A$112m (September 2026, including the A$20m still owed to Sinosteel) = A$572.9m on 770.7m shares, A$0.74 a share. The existing operation is counted at nil, because the scoping study excludes it. The study's base price in A$ (US$85 / 0.65 = A$131/dmt) is a little below the September-quarter index price (US$96.9 / 0.706 = A$137/dmt), but the study also shows that a 10% lower price cuts the NPV to A$427m [11].
- If it goes against us: the DFS does not lead to a funded project and iron ore stays near the September-quarter level. The existing operation earns roughly nil to A$20m of EBITDA a year once C1 returns to guidance; even at A$20m and four times, A$80m, it is worth less than the A$112m of net debt. Equity value about nil, A$0.00 a share.
- Price: A$0.195 (last trade in the ASX company data fetched 7 Nov 2026; Norgate close 30 Oct 2026 also A$0.195), market value A$150.3m. Gap if it goes our way: 0.74 / 0.195 - 1 = +281%. Gap if it goes against us: -100%.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is +281%, but the answer needs the DFS and its funding package, due "in the first half of calendar year 2027" after two delays, so probably after 8 May 2027. Before then, the December quarter (about 22 Jan 2027) and the half-year result (about 25 Feb 2027) will show what the existing business earns unhedged at today's price, which decides whether Fenix can carry about A$112m of net debt until a decision in 2028. Not a value pick: the value if it goes against us is far below the price.
