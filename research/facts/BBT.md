# Betr Entertainment (ASX:BBT): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 2 points, #122 of 195 ASX names in reports/research_rank.csv (2026-11-08), outside the top 40; written now that gaming, wagering and racing names are open.
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An online bookmaker (the betr brand, Australia only), plus a 27.72% stake in PointsBet Holdings (ASX:PBH) carried at A$90.1m at 30 Jun 2026 [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The FY26 accounts are prepared on a going-concern basis and the auditor's report has no material uncertainty paragraph (opinion, basis for opinion, key audit matters) [1]. The directors' going-concern note records a A$40.2m loss, A$25.7m of operating cash outflow and net current liabilities of A$10.2m, and relies on forecast cash flow and the NAB loan extension to 30 Sep 2027 [1]. The September quarter cash report shows 2.9 quarters of funding at the Q1 burn rate [3].
- Too thin to trade? No, but only just. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$10,009 a day, against the A$10,000 limit; two of the 20 days traded under A$1,500 (A$95 on 9 October) (data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz) [24]. That caps a position near A$2,000.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries; Cam confirmed on 8 Nov 2026 that there is nothing to avoid at the outset.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? Not for Betr itself: no bid or scheme, and no holder near 50% (the Tripp family interests are about 19%) [1]. Two related facts, for the report back: Betr's biggest asset is its blocking 27.72% stake in PointsBet, where MIXI Australia holds 66.43% [4][5]; and Betr said on 14 Jan 2026 that it is "in active discussions with a number of existing and new industry participants regarding consolidation and partnership opportunities" [6]. Neither is a move by a holder of Betr, so the sheet continues.

The gap between statutory and normalised results recurs. Deal costs, rebrand costs and share-settled pay were left out in both years (A$14.5m of add-backs in FY25, A$17.2m in FY26), and Betr has done a deal every year: the merger with betr's founders' business (July 2024), TopSport (April 2025) and the PointsBet bid (2025). Share-settled pay (A$2.8m, then A$4.7m) is a running cost of the business, not a one-off; FY27 guidance excludes it [8].

What the headline numbers hide:
- The PointsBet stake is most of the value. 96,354,403 PointsBet shares [5], worth A$78.0m at PointsBet's last trade of A$0.81 (28 Oct 2026) [24], against Betr's market value of about A$131m. Betr paid about A$1.07 to A$1.10 a PointsBet share (A$72.5m for the first 19.9%; 30,338,524 shares for Betr scrip at 4.375 to 1, valued at A$32.5m) [1][2]. MIXI's winning 2025 offer was A$1.25 cash [9]. PointsBet's free float is only about 5.9%, so its quoted price is thin.
- The NAB loan (A$33.7m, fully drawn) is secured on that stake, with a loan-to-value limit of 60% at all times and an interest cover test of 1.5 times each quarter from 31 Dec 2026 [1]. The loan-to-value limit would be breached if PointsBet fell below about A$0.59 (the desk's arithmetic: A$34.1m / 0.6 / 96.35m shares).
- The wagering business uses cash: A$8.7m of capitalised development in FY26 against FY27 normalised EBITDA guidance of A$13m to A$19m [1][8]. Q1 FY27 net win margin was 8.9%, below the "above 10%" the guidance assumes, and operating cash flow was minus A$6.8m [3].
- Share count: 296.3m shares on 1 Jul 2024 became 1,046.3m by 30 Jun 2026 (detail in section 4) [1].

Licences, regulators and tax changes:
- Licence: a Tasmanian Gaming Licence from the Tasmanian Liquor and Gaming Commission, effective 7 Jul 2026 for an initial five years; the Northern Territory licence it replaced is being surrendered [1][10]. Betr also depends on race fields and sports product fee approvals in each state [1].
- Open regulator matters: none disclosed (no fine, inquiry, licence condition or AUSTRAC action in the FY26 annual report or the 2025 and 2026 announcements). The report notes AUSTRAC's specialist unit for gambling and the stronger AML/CTF powers in force since 7 Jan 2025 as general risks [1].
- Federal law: the Interactive Gambling Amendment (Gambling Reform) Act 2026 (assent 26 Aug 2026) starts on 1 Jan 2027. It limits wagering advertising (none during live sport, at most three TV ads an hour from 5am to 8.30pm, no school-run radio ads, no ads at venues or on uniforms, no celebrity endorsement, online ads only to logged-in adults who can opt out), bans direct marketing of inducements in a customer's first 14 days and to customers at risk, and adds cost-recovery levies for the opt-out register and BetStop (amounts not yet set) [11][12]. Betr says the reforms are "not expected to materially impact" its trading [8].
- Taxes and fees: FY27 guidance assumes "no material regulatory, taxation or cost of sales changes" [8]. No dated change to point of consumption tax or race fields fees was found in primary sources; not verified.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: what will Betr's 27.72% PointsBet stake (about 59% of Betr's market value at PointsBet's last price, and the security for its only loan) realise for Betr holders, and can the wagering business deliver FY27 guidance after a first quarter below its own margin assumption? The first half depends on what MIXI, with 66.43% of PointsBet, chooses to do, which filings can't settle.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: online bookmakers are priced on enterprise value to EBITDA, and a listed stake at its market price. The named peer is Tabcorp (ASX:TAH): 2,292,644,230 shares at A$0.89 (30 Oct 2026 close), A$2,040.5m, plus FY26 net debt of A$533m including leases, over FY26 EBITDA before significant items of A$431.7m = 5.96 times [22]. Betr's guidance excludes share-settled pay, so A$4.7m is taken off before applying the multiple. The price would move on the half-year result and on any deal for the PointsBet stake.
- Items used throughout: the PointsBet stake A$78.0m (96,354,403 x A$0.81), own cash A$7.4m, NAB loan A$34.1m and leases A$4.9m, all at 30 Sep or 30 Jun 2026, on 1,049.59m shares.
- If it goes our way: FY27 normalised EBITDA at the top of guidance, A$19m, less A$4.7m is A$14.3m; at 5.96 times, A$85.4m. Plus A$78.0m and A$7.4m, less A$34.1m and A$4.9m: A$131.8m, or A$0.126 a share.
- If it goes against us: the bottom of guidance, A$13m, less A$4.7m is A$8.3m; at 5.96 times, A$49.6m; A$96.1m in all, or A$0.092 a share.
- Not used for the grade (no sale is under way): if MIXI bought the stake at its 2025 offer price of A$1.25, the stake would be worth A$120.4m and the our-way value A$0.166.
- Only now the price: A$0.125, the last trade on 6 Nov 2026 (ASX company data; A$0.13 at the 30 Oct close used in the ranking), a market value of A$131.2m. Gap if it goes our way: 0.126 / 0.125 - 1 = +0.5%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.092 / 0.125 - 1 = -27%.

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive: the gap if it goes our way is under 25% (+0.5%). On Tabcorp's multiple and PointsBet's own quoted price, Betr is priced about where its parts add up, even at the top of its FY27 guidance. Any gap rests on the PointsBet stake fetching more than PointsBet's thin quoted price, which is a question for MIXI, not the filings; that belongs on the special situations watch list, not in a research pick (noted in the report back). Liquidity is also at the limit (median A$10,009 a day, a position of about A$2,000).
