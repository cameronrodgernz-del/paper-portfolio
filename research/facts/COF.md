# Centuria Office REIT (ASX:COF): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 7 points, #6 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

COF owns 18 office buildings worth A$1.84bn (30 June 2026) across Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Canberra, Perth and
Adelaide, 97% A-grade by value. It is run by Centuria Property Funds, part of Centuria Capital Group, which also owns
18.9% of the units and charged A$10.9m in fees in FY26.

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? A reading, not a kill. COF is a registered managed
  investment scheme (a unit trust) with an external manager. It owns and leases its buildings directly and earns rent
  from tenants; it does not hold other listed securities, and the ranking did not treat it as an investment company. I
  have treated it as a property business to value. Cam may want to rule on whether the rule's word "trust" is meant to
  catch REITs.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The FY26 audit opinion is unmodified
  (FY26 financial report, 4 Aug 2026).
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$376,558 (volume times close,
  data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz; lowest session A$244,977).
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The two large holders,
  Centuria Capital Group (18.9%) and the Lederer family (16.9%), hold 35.8% together. The Lederers bought on market in
  February and March 2026 (section 4), a holder build-up but not over 50% and with no stated intention.

Nothing ends the sheet.

Does the gap between statutory and underlying recur? The revaluation and swap lines swing with markets and are fairly
left out. The rest recurs. Rent-free periods and incentives are added back every year (20.1m, then 25.6m) and are a real
cost of keeping buildings let; the refinancing write-off appears in both years. FFO therefore overstates the cash
available to unitholders. FY27 distributions are cut to 9.0c while FFO guidance rises to 11.3c.

What the headline numbers hide:
- Valuations against recent sales: the one sale this year, 9 Help Street, Chatswood (B-grade, 9,397 sqm), sold for A$90m,
  12.5% above book and a 5.5% passing yield, about A$9,578 a square metre against the portfolio average of A$6,936
  (contract December 2025, settled June 2026). 154 Melbourne
  Street, South Brisbane is held for sale at A$88.25m; no sale has been announced. Values rose 2.2% in the
  December half and fell in the June half (NTA A$1.72 to A$1.66; cap rate 6.92% to 7.04%). Of the 18 buildings, 12 worth
  A$1,157m (63%) had an external valuation at 30 June 2026; 6 worth A$686m (37%) were last externally valued at
  31 December 2025 (FY26 property compendium). A 0.25-point rise in cap rates takes A$61.2m off values.
- Gearing against covenants: values can fall 25.3% before loan to value reaches 60%. Interest cover has fallen from
  2.4 to 2.0 times; it reaches 1.75 if earnings before interest fall 12.5% or interest costs rise 14.3% (about A$7.0m a
  year, 0.84 points across all drawn debt). Hedging falls from an average 79% in FY26 to 69% in FY27 and 62% in FY28, at
  about 3.7%, while the RBA has raised its cash rate to a 15-year high (AFG AGM address, 16 Oct 2026).
- Lease expiry by income: vacant 9.0%, FY27 4.7% (cut to 2.7% by October 2026), FY28 16.9%, FY29 12.4%, FY30 17.7%,
  FY31 12.9%, FY32 and later 26.4%. Average lease length 4.0 years; occupancy 91%. The biggest vacancy is 818 Bourke
  Street, Docklands (10,894 sqm, 4.5% of income). Tenants are 74% government, multinationals and listed companies.
- The manager's fees (A$10.9m a year) and the 18.9% held by the manager's parent are a structural reason a listed trust
  can trade below its NTA.
- Index selling: COF left the S&P/ASX 300 on 23 March 2026; 29.8m units (about 5.0% of the trust) traded on
  20 March, the last day before (price file).

## 4. Who owns it and who is moving
Substantial holders at 24 July 2026 (2026 annual report): Centuria Capital Group 112.7m units (18.9%); LOR Assets (Paul
and Eva Lederer) 101.0m (16.9%). Top 20 registered holders 61.5%; the largest is HSBC Custody Nominees with 173.6m.

Substantial holder changes in the last 12 months:
- Vanguard: 7.34% to 6.29% (16 Feb 2026), to 5.19% (3 Mar 2026), ceased at 3.39% (20 Mar 2026).
- Lederer family (PEJR Investments and PELSF Nominees): 15.08% to 16.45% (20 Mar 2026), after buying about 8.1m units on
  market between 13 February and 20 March 2026 for about A$8.0m (average about A$0.99). On 30 June 2026 the 101.0m units
  moved to LOR Assets, a Lederer company (initial notice; consideration shown as about A$0.915 a unit), with a matching
  ceasing notice from the old entities. That internal transfer is the "substantial holder left" the ranking picked up.
- Centuria Capital Group: no notice in the period.

Directors' on-market buys or sells in the last 12 months: no Appendix 3Y notices were lodged for COF in 2025 or 2026.

Capital raisings in the last two years: none. Units have been 597,336,931 throughout.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: are COF's book values (7.04% cap rate, NTA A$1.66) what the buildings would sell for? The deep dive would
  test the values building by building against sales evidence, the cost of leasing up the vacancies and FY28 expiries,
  and how much room the interest cover leaves as hedges roll off. The one sale this year was above book; the price
  implies values far below it.
- If it goes our way: property at its stated value less debt, which is NTA: A$990.7m / 597.3m units = A$1.66.
- If it goes against us: cap rates rise another 1.52 points, the same rise as June 2022 to June 2026 (5.52% to 7.04%),
  to 8.56%, with no rent growth. Values fall 1 - 7.04 / 8.56 = 17.8%, or A$327m on the A$1,842m book value of the 18
  buildings, leaving NTA at A$1.11 and loan to value at 55.0%, inside the covenant. Then count the manager's base fee as
  a permanent cost that NTA ignores: A$10.9m a year at 10 times is A$109m, 18c a unit. Value A$0.93.
- Only now the price: A$0.875 (close 30 October 2026). Gap if it goes our way: 1.66 / 0.875 - 1 = +90%. Gap if it goes
  against us: 0.93 / 0.875 - 1 = +6%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is 90%, above 40%. The question can be answered from filings (the property
compendium, valuation dates, lease expiries, debt and hedging terms), and the HY27 result with December 2026 valuations,
due about 4 February 2027, is inside six months. Even the bad case above is worth more than the price, so the deep dive
should look for what that bad case misses.
