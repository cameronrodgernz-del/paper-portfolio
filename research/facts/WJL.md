# Webjet Group (ASX:WJL): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 7 points, #7 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Webjet Group is the consumer travel business left after the September 2024 demerger from Web Travel Group: the Webjet
online travel agency (flights, hotels, packages), Cars and Motorhomes (GoSee), and Webjet Business Travel (Locomote,
bought October 2025). Its year ends 31 March.

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The FY26 audit opinion is unmodified
  (annual report, 20 May 2026).
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$155,767 (volume times close,
  data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz; lowest session A$33,141).
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No live process and no holder
  over 50%. Helloworld (A$0.90 cash scheme proposal) and BGH Capital (A$0.91 cash takeover proposal) both did due
  diligence from November 2025; on 13 February 2026 the board ended talks because neither had made a proposal consistent
  with its indicative price or capable of being put to holders. (An earlier BGH approach at A$0.80, 13 May 2025, was
  rejected on 16 May 2025.) The largest holders now are Helloworld 19.93%, BGH 13.26%
  and Ariadne/Gary Weiss 6.25% (Weiss is interim Chair). This register belongs on the special situations watch list as
  well (noted in the report back), but none of it is a kill fact.

Nothing ends the sheet.

Does the gap between statutory and underlying recur? Mostly yes. Restructuring, defence and advisory costs appear in both
years (3.4m, then 5.3m), share-based payments in both (4.0m, 2.0m), and the Locomote earn-out (up to 6.0m over three
years, 1.0m accrued in FY26) will recur until it is paid. Only the ACCC settlement looks like a true one-off.

What the headline numbers hide:
- The "net cash" is partly customers' money. The ranking's "net cash about 83% of market value" uses the 93.9m cash at
  bank. Webjet collects the money for a trip before it pays the airline or hotel, so part of that cash is owed: trade
  payables were 55.9m. If every current liability were paid, 47.7m would be left. Cash then fell after 31 March: the 2c
  final dividend (about 7.9m, paid 10 June 2026) and about 3.8m of buybacks.
- FY27 earnings face three named cuts the company calls material: Virgin Australia cut commissions from 1 July 2026
  (about 3.0m of FY26 revenue); the RBA's ban on card surcharges from 1 October 2026; and lower variable revenue (FY26 had
  "higher-than-usual" variable items). Only the Virgin figure is quantified.
- Trading to 18 August 2026 against a year earlier: Webjet OTA bookings down 11% and TTV down 8%; Cars and Motorhomes
  bookings down 5% and TTV flat; Business Travel bookings up about 25%.
- Concentration: Australian and New Zealand leisure travel, with airlines as the main suppliers.
- Index selling: Webjet left the S&P/ASX 300 on 21 September 2026; 8.8m shares (about 2.3% of the company) traded on
  18 September, the last day before (price file).

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: how much of Webjet's cash is truly spare once the money owed to airlines and hotels is set aside, and
  what does the business earn after Virgin's commission cut and the surcharge ban? The H1 FY27 result on about
  19 November 2026 should answer both.
- If it goes our way: the company's cash at bank is spare. 93.9m less the final dividend (7.9m) and buybacks since
  31 March (3.8m) = A$82.3m. Add the business at 8 times FY26 underlying EBIT less the Virgin revenue (15.6m - 3.0m =
  12.6m; 8 x 12.6 = A$100.8m). Total A$183.1m / 382.35m shares = A$0.479.
- If it goes against us: only net current assets are spare (47.7m less the 11.6m paid out since = A$36.1m), and underlying
  EBIT halves to 7.8m, valued at 6 times (A$46.8m). Total A$82.9m / 382.35m = A$0.217.
- For reference only: in November 2025 Helloworld and BGH proposed A$0.90 and A$0.91 cash; neither became binding.
- Only now the price: A$0.275 (close 30 October 2026). Gap if it goes our way: 0.479 / 0.275 - 1 = +74%. Gap if it goes
  against us: 0.217 / 0.275 - 1 = -21%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is 74%, above 40%. The question can be answered from filings (the half-year
balance sheet splits cash, restricted cash, supplier payables and gift cards; the segment results show the commission and
surcharge effects), and the H1 FY27 result due about 19 November 2026 is inside six months.
