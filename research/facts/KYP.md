## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Workforce screening and compliance software for Australian and New Zealand employers: police and background checks paid per check, and subscription software (Cited and, from October 2025, Kinatico Compliance). - Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. RSM's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified, with no going-concern note in the audit report [2]. Cash A$11.8m at 30 June 2026 and no borrowings [2].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$27,036 a day (lowest A$10,933; all 20 sessions above A$10,000), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz [16]. Over 60 sessions the median is A$35,740.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest named holder is director Jon Birman's family company with 5.23%; the two larger lines are custodians (HSBC 9.92%, J P Morgan 9.91%) [2]. No approach or review announced. The company is buying back shares on market (section 4).

Recurring and one-off revenue. Subscription revenue is the recurring part: A$20.5m in FY26, up 37.5%, and an annualised A$22.6m on the September 2026 quarter [3][5]. Per-check revenue is paid per search with no contract and has fallen as larger customers moved to subscriptions: A$17.2m to A$14.6m in FY26, and A$4.26m to A$3.75m (down 12%) in the September quarter [3][5]. The gap between statutory profit and EBITDA is the same every year (amortisation and interest), not a one-off; there are no other adjustments.

How much of the profit comes from capitalised development. FY26 capitalised development was A$3.74m against A$2.35m of amortisation of past development, so capitalising added a net A$1.39m to profit before tax of A$1.98m, about 70% of it. In FY25 the net figure was A$1.91m (A$3.66m capitalised, A$1.75m amortised) against profit before tax of A$1.22m: without it FY25 would have been a pre-tax loss of about A$0.7m [2]. EBITDA (A$5.6m) is lifted by the full A$3.74m.

Cash earnings after capitalised development (operating cash flow excluding interest and tax, less capitalised development, equipment, lease payments and insurance funding repaid): A$1.37m in FY26 and A$0.29m in FY25 [2]. The company's own "free cash flow" of A$2.5m leaves out the lease and insurance funding payments [3]. Tax: the group pays almost none; it has carried-forward losses (accumulated losses A$19.2m), mostly not booked as a tax asset [2].

What the headline numbers hide:
- Subscription growth has slowed every period while management said it would speed up: 49.5% in H1 FY26, about 29% in H2 FY26 (A$10.8m against A$8.4m), 17.8% in the September 2026 quarter [3][4][5].
- The September 2026 quarter: revenue A$9.41m (+3.8%), EBITDA A$1.51m (+4.3%), profit after tax A$0.47m (down 10.5%); free cash flow 74% lower than a year earlier after bonus payments [5].
- Customers are spread across many Australian and NZ employers; no concentration is disclosed. All revenue is ANZ.

## 3. Guidance and record
Kinatico does not give numerical guidance ("We do not provide formal guidance", FY26 annual report) [2].
- H1 FY26 report, 17 Feb 2026: management expected "a re-acceleration of SaaS revenue growth", continued improvement in operating margins and positive operating cash flow [4].
- FY26 annual report, 6 Aug 2026: "As the pipeline we have built converts, we expect SaaS growth to re-accelerate", and operating leverage to continue [2].
- AGM and September quarter update, 23 Oct 2026: chair said results through the first quarter of FY27 continued "its proven growth trajectory"; the figures show subscription growth of 17.8% and EBITDA growth of 4.3% [5][8].

Record: the EBITDA margin rose as said (13.5% in FY25 to 15.9% in FY26) [3]. The repeated expectation of faster subscription growth has not been met: it slowed from 57.3% (FY25) to 37.5% (FY26) and 17.8% (Q1 FY27) [3][5].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: does the shift to subscriptions turn into cash, or has growth stalled? FY26 EBITDA grew 28%, but after capitalised development the business made only A$1.37m of cash, and the September quarter showed EBITDA up 4% and subscription growth down to 18%. A deep dive would rebuild the subscription pipeline, the per-check run-off and the development spend, to judge whether FY27 EBITDA can grow anywhere near FY26's 28%.
- Peer multiples (the desk's arithmetic from filings, prices at the 6 Nov 2026 close): enterprise value of operations over pre-tax cash earnings after capitalised development, equipment and leases, with interest excluded. hipages (ASX:HPG): A$65.3m (A$104.3m market value, less A$31.3m net cash and a A$7.7m net tax asset) over A$8.56m in FY26 = 7.6 times [18]. Objective Corporation (ASX:OCL): A$567.8m (A$634.0m market value, less A$73.7m net cash, plus A$7.5m of deferred tax owed) over A$26.67m in FY26 = 21.3 times [19]. Objective is a quality software company that the market prices for durable growth; hipages is a small ANZ subscription business priced after a guidance cut. The multiple used follows the answer: growth gets Objective's, no growth gets hipages'.
- If it goes our way: FY27 EBITDA grows 28% again (the FY26 rate), adding A$1.58m, with development, leases and other cash costs flat: cash earnings A$1.37m + A$1.58m = A$2.95m. At Objective's 21.3 times: A$62.7m, plus A$11.16m net cash = A$73.9m, on 434.34m shares = A$0.170 a share. (At hipages' 7.6 times it would be A$0.077.)
- If it goes against us: growth stalls and cash earnings stay at FY26's A$1.37m. At hipages' 7.6 times: A$10.4m plus A$11.16m = A$21.6m, or A$0.050 a share. Unbooked tax losses and the loan-funded share repayments (about A$1.6m if the 12c shares are paid up) would add a little to either figure.
- Price: A$0.12 (close 6 Nov 2026, ASX company data), market value about A$52m. Gap if it goes our way: 0.170 / 0.12 - 1 = +42%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.050 / 0.12 - 1 = -59%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now, but only just. The gap if it goes our way is 42%, it rests on Objective's multiple and a repeat of FY26's EBITDA growth, and the September quarter (EBITDA +4%) points the other way. The question can be worked from filings (subscription and per-check revenue, development spend and cash flow are reported each half), and the December quarter flash (mid-January 2027) and the H1 FY27 result (about 17 February 2027) fall inside six months. Not a value pick: the value if it goes against us is 59% below the price.
