# Fleetwood (ASX:FWD): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 6 points, #12 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An operating business: modular buildings (Building Solutions) and worker accommodation it owns and runs in the Pilbara (Community Solutions).
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. EY signed the FY26 accounts without qualification; the Smithfield closure was a key audit matter only [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$409,622 a day (lowest day A$96,147), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz. That is about 41 times the A$10,000 limit.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries, and the business is not gaming, wagering or racing.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The June 2026 announcement says the RV exit followed "a strategic review", but that review is finished and covered one division, which has now been sold [5][9]. No holder is over 50%. Sandon Capital, an activist fund, is the largest holder at 13.79% [1]. Not a special situation.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs. Restructuring and write-downs were left out of underlying profit in FY25 (A$11.0m) and again in FY26 (A$29.6m), and H1 FY27 will carry more: the AGM said costs for carry-over staff, stock and lease surrenders from the RV sale continue through the first half [6], and current provisions rose from A$12.8m to A$21.7m, which is cash still to go out [1]. The FY26 cash figure is also flattered by early customer payments. On the other side, the RV and Smithfield costs were tied to exits that are now complete (Camec sold 1 September 2026 [10]; Smithfield handed back end of September [6]), so the FY27 base should be cleaner.

What the headline hides:
- Customer concentration. Community Solutions earned A$50.0m EBIT in FY26, more than the whole group's underlying EBIT of A$35.6m, because Building Solutions lost A$8.7m and corporate cost A$7.5m [1]. Searipple Village (1,250 rooms, Karratha) drives almost all of it. Rio Tinto holds 800 contracted rooms, about 64% of the village, under a take-or-pay agreement that ends 30 April 2027 [7][14]. Fleetwood is in a tender with Rio Tinto for "their next 5 year contract", with an award expected "within the next few months" (AGM, 27 Oct 2026) [6].
- Rio Tinto's contract terms. A five-year deal from 1 July 2022 gave Rio Tinto 250 exclusive rooms a night, nominated Searipple its preferred third-party provider in Karratha, Dampier and Wickham, and was expected to bring A$52m to A$70m over the term [15]. In June 2023 Rio Tinto took additional rooms from 1 April 2024 to 30 April 2027 on a take-or-pay basis, with a rebate if rooms are not occupied, worth a further A$100m to A$120m [14]. The October 2024 notice describes the whole as a take-or-pay agreement to 30 April 2027 [16]. Earlier Rio Tinto contracts ran in one-year and six-month extensions (2012 to 2022) [17][18].
- How fast earnings can fall. Community Solutions EBIT was A$8.3m in FY22, A$10.2m in FY23 and A$11.5m in FY24 (Searipple occupancy 34% in FY24), before A$39.2m in FY25 (84%) and A$50.0m in FY26 (96%) [2][12]. The five-year average is A$23.8m. FY27 contracted occupancy is 72%, with the company expecting 82% to 92% in total [2].
- Land tenure. The company calls Searipple "built, owned and operated by Fleetwood" [2] and the revenue note says it rents its owned accommodation units at the Searipple site [1]. The balance sheet carries leasehold property and improvements at A$6.7m net (A$54.6m cost) and freehold land at only A$1.4m cost [1]. None of the FY12, FY16, FY18, FY19, FY21 to FY26 annual reports, the FY26 results presentation or the Red Dog documents states who owns the Searipple land or when any land lease ends. Not verified; a deep dive should get this from the WA land register or the company.
- Red Dog Village (2,169 rooms, Karratha, built 2022) costs A$20m plus GST, debt funded, on a Crown Lease. Conditions include removing the use restriction to Bechtel's Pluto 2 workforce and extending both the development approval and the Crown Lease by five years to October 2032 [8]. So Red Dog's tenure is at most six years. The conditions are "taking longer than we expected" and the agreement now runs to end January 2027 [6]. Company estimate: A$10m to A$20m extra EBIT a year [8].
- Book value. Goodwill is A$34.4m, of which A$32.2m sits in Building Solutions, the division that lost money in FY26 [1]. Its impairment test relies on "external valuations of land" of A$31.0m (FY25 A$24.5m); the notes don't say which land, as freehold land is only A$1.4m at cost [1].

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance (FY27):
- 27 Aug 2026 results: Searipple FY27 contracted occupancy 72%, expected 82% to 92% with 10% to 20% more bookings in H2; Building Solutions revenue growth over 5%; Smithfield closure cuts fixed costs A$8m to A$9m a year from Q2 FY27 [2][1].
- 27 Oct 2026 AGM: Searipple "fully booked to the end of this calendar year"; Building Solutions H1 FY27 loss "consistent with" H2 FY26 (H2 FY26 underlying loss was about A$7.1m, worked from A$8.7m full year less A$1.6m H1), with a return to profit in H2 FY27; about A$6m of Smithfield savings in FY27; Red Dog completion now expected January 2027 [6].

Changes to guidance this year (FY26):
- 25 Feb 2026 (H1): Building Solutions FY26 revenue to fall 5% to 10%, replacing the August 2025 forecast of high single-digit growth [3][12].
- 22 Jun 2026: FY26 underlying EBIT A$35m to A$39m; H2 restructuring costs A$20m to A$24m; "a final dividend will not be declared"; Building Solutions would not return to profit in H2 [5].
- 3 Jul 2026: closing cash A$61.5m against A$44m to A$46m guided on 22 June [4].
- 27 Aug 2026: final dividend of 9.5c declared after all, reversing the June statement [1].

Record:
- FY26 (guidance from Aug 2025 [12]): Searipple contracted 82%, delivered 96% (beat). Building revenue high single-digit growth, delivered down 9.2% (miss). RV back to profit, delivered A$1.7m underlying EBIT (met, then sold). June range of A$35m to A$39m underlying EBIT, delivered A$35.6m (bottom of the range). H2 restructuring A$20m to A$24m, delivered about A$24.8m (A$29.6m less A$4.8m in H1; top of or just above the range).
- FY25 (guidance from Aug 2024 [19]): Searipple contracted 65%, delivered 84% (beat). Building revenue high single-digit growth, delivered 15% (beat). RV "expected to remain profitable", delivered a A$2.8m underlying loss (miss).
- Pattern: Searipple has beaten its contracted occupancy figure two years running; Building Solutions and RV forecasts have been missed more often than met.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: does Rio Tinto sign a new five-year contract for its 800 Searipple rooms from May 2027, and what does Community Solutions earn if it doesn't? The FY26 profit of A$50m from one village depends on that contract, and a deep dive would size the earnings with and without it from the contracted occupancy, Red Dog's terms and the Karratha room data the company publishes.
- If it goes our way: Rio Tinto renews; Searipple runs at the FY27 midpoint of 87% occupancy, so Community Solutions earns A$50.0m x 87/96 = A$45.3m; Red Dog adds A$10m (bottom of the company's range); Building Solutions earns nothing (no credit for a recovery); corporate cost A$7.5m. EBIT A$47.8m, after 30% tax A$33.5m. At 8.33 times after-tax profit (the inverse of the company's own 12.0% post-tax discount rate in its impairment test [1]), A$279m. Add net cash of A$62.4m (A$61.5m at June, less the A$8.6m final dividend paid 9 Oct, plus A$9.5m Camec proceeds [10]) and take off the A$20m Red Dog debt: A$321.3m, or A$3.56 a share on 90.28m shares.
- If it goes against us: Rio Tinto leaves or takes far fewer rooms; Community Solutions falls to its five-year average EBIT of A$23.8m; Red Dog doesn't complete; Building Solutions loses A$0.7m (FY26 loss of A$8.7m less the A$8m Smithfield saving); corporate A$7.5m. EBIT A$15.6m, after tax A$10.9m, at 8.33 times A$91m. Net cash on the company's own pre-close guidance of A$45m (the early customer payments unwind) less the dividend plus Camec: A$45.9m. Total A$136.8m, or A$1.51 a share. A harsher floor, Community Solutions back to its FY24 level of A$11.5m, gives about A$0.90 a share.
- Price: A$2.04 (close 30 Oct 2026), market value A$184m. Gap if it goes our way: 3.56 / 2.04 - 1 = +75%. Gap if it goes against us: 1.51 / 2.04 - 1 = -26%.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is 75%, well over 40%, but the deciding fact is Rio Tinto's tender decision, which no filing can settle in advance: the answer needs the event itself. The event is close (an award expected within months of 27 October, and the contract ends 30 April 2027), so the deep dive should run as soon as the award is announced or the H1 result on about 25 February 2027 shows the FY27 and FY28 bookings, whichever comes first. A deep dive should also settle the Searipple land tenure, which the filings don't state.
