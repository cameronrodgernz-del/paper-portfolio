# Mayne Pharma Group (ASX:MYX): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 9 points, #2 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. A specialty drug company: US women's health (BIJUVA, IMVEXXY, ANNOVERA, NEXTSTELLIS), US dermatology and the DistributeRx pharmacy platform, and an international segment built on the Salisbury, South Australia plant [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The FY26 audit opinion is unmodified [1]. Removed from the S&P/ASX 300 on 23 Mar 2026 [3]; still listed.
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$300,863 a day (lowest day A$129,054), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No live one. Cosette Pharmaceuticals agreed on 20 Feb 2025 to buy Mayne at A$7.40 cash a share [4]; the Treasurer objected on 21 Nov 2025 after Cosette told FIRB it intended to close or sell the Salisbury plant, and Mayne terminated the deal on 11 Dec 2025 for Cosette's "material breaches" [5][6][7]. What is left is litigation: Mayne won at first instance (15 Oct 2025) and on appeal (14 Oct 2026), and on 18 Feb 2026 sued Cosette, its parent, Avista and Avista's CEO for "substantial damages" on behalf of shareholders and itself [8][9]. No holder is over 50%; the largest is Funicular Funds at 9.90%, whose manager joined the board on 14 Aug 2026 [10][11]. Not routed by the rule, but the damages claim is a dated legal event of the kind the special situations watch list exists for; see section 7.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs. Earn-out revaluations swing profit every year (a A$125.6m gain in FY26, a A$16.6m loss in FY25), the unwinding of the earn-out discount costs about A$34m a year (A$34.2m in FY26, A$34.8m in FY25), and scheme and litigation costs have run for two years (A$12.6m net in FY26 after A$14.4m recovered from Cosette; A$8.5m in FY25; A$6.0m of legal costs in the September 2026 quarter alone) [1][2][13].

What the headline numbers hide:
- "6.6 times earnings" is the non-cash A$125.6m earn-out gain. Without it, continuing operations lost about A$89.8m before tax in FY26 [1].
- "Net cash about 18% of market value" leaves out A$264.8m of earn-out and deferred consideration liabilities: royalties of 8% of net sales of the TherapeuticsMD products and 10% of ANNOVERA sales to the Population Council, plus US$40m milestones at US$400m and again at US$1bn of cumulative ANNOVERA sales [1]. Royalty payments are paid out of the liability, so they never touch underlying EBITDA (A$3.5m in the September 2026 quarter) [13].
- The A$41.1m convertible notes mature on 31 Dec 2026. They convert at A$5.356 a share; the company valued the option on its A$2.79 share price at 30 Jun 2026, so repayment in cash is the likely path [1]. Cash was A$67.3m at 30 Sep 2026 [13].
- "0.52 times book": book includes A$463.1m of intangibles; tangible equity is negative [1].
- Underlying EBITDA is falling: A$28.6m in H1 FY26, A$5.6m in H2, A$5.3m in the September 2026 quarter (down 52% on a year earlier), as a generic RHOFADE launched in July 2026 and women's health selling costs rose [12][13]. Twelve months to September 2026: A$28.5m.
- Concentration: the business is mostly US; three of the four US women's health brands (ANNOVERA, IMVEXXY, BIJUVA) are licensed from TherapeuticsMD [1].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is the damages claim against Cosette worth anything? On the filings the business alone, once the A$264.8m of earn-out royalties and the A$41.1m convertible are counted, is worth about the share price or less, so any value above that has to come from the claim over a A$7.40 deal that two courts have now said Cosette had no right to walk away from.
- If it goes our way: the claim succeeds for the full lost premium, so holders end up with about the A$7.40 deal price; take off 20% for legal costs and the years a damages case takes: A$5.92 a share. For comparison, the business on its own at 10 times FY25's A$47.0m underlying EBITDA, less the A$230.1m of net financial liabilities including earn-outs, is A$239.9m, or A$2.93 a share.
- If it goes against us: the claim is worth nothing, and the business is valued on the last twelve months' underlying EBITDA of A$28.5m (FY26 A$34.2m, less the A$11.0m of the September 2025 quarter, plus the A$5.3m of the September 2026 quarter) at 10 times, A$285m, less net financial liabilities at 30 Sep 2026 of A$242.8m (earn-outs A$264.8m, convertible A$41.1m, leases A$4.1m, less cash A$67.3m). That is A$42.2m, or A$0.52 a share.
- Price: A$2.54 (close 30 Oct 2026), market value A$208.0m. Gap if it goes our way: 5.92 / 2.54 - 1 = +133%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.52 / 2.54 - 1 = -80%. The business alone at FY25 EBITDA gives 2.93 / 2.54 - 1 = +15%: the gap comes from the claim, not the business. With earn-outs counted, the price is about 15.8 times the last twelve months' underlying EBITDA.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is +133%, but all of it rests on the damages claim, and the deciding event (a damages judgment or a settlement) has no date and is unlikely inside six months. On the business alone the gap is +15%, which would be a C. The steps inside the window are Cosette's 11 Nov 2026 special leave deadline and the 31 Dec 2026 convertible repayment (A$41.1m out of A$67.3m of cash). This looks more like a special situation than a research pick: a failed-scheme damages claim, with a 9.9% holder's manager on the board. Suggest it goes on the special situations watch list, keyed to the High Court deadline and the first hearing date of the damages claim.
