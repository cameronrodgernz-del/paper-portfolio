# Reckon (ASX:RKN): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #37 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Two software businesses, year to 31 December: the Business Group (accounting and payroll software for small businesses in Australia and NZ: legacy desktop Reckon Accounts, cloud Reckon One, and Cashflow Manager, bought January 2025) and the Legal Group (Zebraworks, 78% owned, document and billing software for US and UK law firms). FY25 revenue A$62.4m [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. BDO's opinion on the FY25 accounts is unmodified [2]. Net debt A$1.5m at 30 June 2026 [3].
- Too thin to trade? No, but close. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$18,583 a day (lowest A$2; 13 of the 20 sessions above A$10,000), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz [17]. Over 40 sessions the median is A$12,509 and over 60 sessions A$13,029. Two sessions at 10% of A$18,583 allows a position of about A$3,700.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is Microequities at 15.32% [10]. No further sale or review has been announced in 2025 or 2026 [16]. The FY25 annual report says only that the company "will continue to assess corporate transactions" [2]. For the record, the earlier sale was not of the legal business and not in 2023: Reckon sold its Accountants Practice Management Group to The Access Group for A$100m (4.6 times FY21 revenue, 8.4 times FY21 EBITDA), completed 1 August 2022, and paid a A$0.57 special dividend, 60% franked, on 21 November 2022 [13][14]. The Legal Group was kept and given more capital in 2023 [15].

Recurring and one-off revenue. About 95% of revenue is subscription: 95% of the Business Group's FY25 revenue (93% in H1 FY26, A$14.4m of it cloud) and 95% of the Legal Group's (subscription A$13.2m) [4][5]. The rest is licences, services and hardware.

How much of the profit comes from capitalised development. Very little at the profit line. FY25 capitalised development was A$15.40m against A$14.98m of amortisation of past development: a net A$0.42m, 4% of profit before tax of A$9.44m. FY24: A$14.80m against A$14.53m (net A$0.27m, 6% of A$4.45m). H1 FY26: A$7.86m against A$7.34m (net A$0.52m, 9% of A$6.07m) [2][3][8]. EBITDA, by contrast, is lifted by the whole A$15.4m: 59% of it.

Cash earnings after capitalised development (operating cash flow excluding interest and tax, less capitalised development, equipment and lease payments): A$4.53m (FY23), A$3.31m (FY24), A$10.58m (FY25), and A$11.06m for the 12 months to June 2026 [2][3][6][9]. After the tax actually paid, the 12-month figure is A$7.56m. The gap between statutory profit and EBITDA (amortisation) recurs every year; there are no other regular adjustments.

What the headline numbers hide:
- The Business Group is a legacy base being moved to the cloud. Its revenue fell 2.9% in H1 FY26 (A$25.4m against A$26.1m), including a A$0.6m deferred-revenue effect from moving users to Reckon One; Reckon One is about 22% of its subscription revenue [4]. Its cash is strong: H1 FY26 EBITDA A$14.0m less A$5.3m of development [3][4].
- The Legal Group uses cash. FY25 EBITDA A$3.3m against A$5.1m of capitalised development; H1 FY26 EBITDA A$1.5m against A$2.4m [4][5]. Minority holders own 22%, and in 2023 a US management incentive plan was set up to "pursue growth and exit opportunities" [15].
- FY25 was helped by Cashflow Manager (A$6.1m revenue, A$3.3m EBITDA) [5]; organic revenue grew 4%.
- Cash is seasonal: in FY25, A$7.31m of the A$10.58m pre-tax cash earnings came in the first half [6].
- Book value is all intangibles: net tangible assets are negative [3].
- Executives have a cash incentive plan paid on dividends, distributions and takeover consideration, which rewards selling assets or the company [2].

## 3. Guidance and record
Reckon gives no numerical guidance.
- FY24 commentary, 11 Feb 2025: "2025 is poised to be a pivotal year"; the Legal Group funded to grow Billing Workflows, the Business Group to move legacy users to Reckon One [12]. Delivered: Reckon One revenue up 27% and Legal subscription revenue up 15% in FY25 [5].
- FY25 commentary, 10 Feb 2026: "The Business Group is expected to be much the same as 2025", with Billing Workflows driving Legal growth [5]. H1 FY26 (4 Aug 2026): Business Group revenue down 2.9%, EBITDA stable at A$14.0m; Legal revenue up 5% in constant currency [4].
- Dividend: the board intends one dividend a year after the half [4]. Paid at 2.5c in each of 2024, 2025 and 2026 [2][7].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is the cash the Business Group throws off, after paying for all its development, durable while its legacy users move to Reckon One, and will the Legal Group stop absorbing cash? A deep dive would split cash earnings by division, track legacy and Reckon One user numbers and pricing, and test whether the A$11m of pre-tax cash earnings over the last 12 months can hold.
- Peer multiple (the desk's arithmetic from filings, price at the 6 Nov 2026 close): hipages (ASX:HPG), the closest listed peer in size and type (an ANZ small-business subscription platform), is valued at 7.6 times its pre-tax cash earnings after capitalised development, equipment and leases: enterprise value of operations A$65.3m (A$104.3m market value, less A$31.3m net cash and a A$7.7m net tax asset) over A$8.56m in FY26 [18]. Used for both cases, because the answer here is about holding cash earnings, not growing them.
- If it goes our way: cash earnings hold at the 12-month figure of A$11.06m. At 7.6 times: A$84.4m, less net debt of A$4.28m (A$1.45m at June plus the A$2.83m dividend paid since) = A$80.1m, on 113.29m shares = A$0.707 a share.
- If it goes against us: cash earnings fall back to the FY23 to FY25 average of A$6.14m. At 7.6 times: A$46.8m less A$4.28m = A$42.6m, or A$0.376 a share.
- Cross-checks. On an after-tax basis (Reckon pays tax, hipages uses tax losses), using A$7.56m and the FY23 to FY25 after-tax average of A$4.45m at hipages' 8.75 times actual cash earnings: A$0.546 and A$0.306. At hipages' 30 October price, before its guidance cut, its multiple was 9.3 times, which would lift both figures by about a quarter (to A$0.870 and A$0.466).
- Price: A$0.40 (close 6 Nov 2026, ASX company data), market value A$45.3m. Gap if it goes our way: 0.707 / 0.40 - 1 = +77%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.376 / 0.40 - 1 = -6%. (After-tax cross-check: +37% and -23%.)

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now, as a value pick (rulebook section 5a): the gap if it goes our way is 77% and the value if it goes against us is 6% below the price, inside the 10% limit, so no forcing event is needed. The FY26 result (about 10 February 2027) will still show much of the answer. The deep dive should settle which tax basis is fair, because on the after-tax cross-check the value if it goes against us is 23% below the price and it would not qualify as a value pick. Liquidity is thin (median A$18,583 a day), which caps a position near A$3,700.
