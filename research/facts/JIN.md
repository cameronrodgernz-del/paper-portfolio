# Jumbo Interactive (ASX:JIN): fact sheet

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Four operating businesses: Lottery Retailing (Oz Lotteries, an authorised online reseller of The Lottery Corporation's games), SaaS (the Jumbo Lottery Platform licensed to government and charity lotteries), Managed Services (Gatherwell and StarVale in the UK, Stride in Canada) and Dream Giveaways (prize draws in the UK and US, bought in October 2025) [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. FY26 accounts audited by Ernst & Young with an unqualified opinion [3].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$847,504 a day (lowest day A$591,701), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz [27].
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries; Cam confirmed on 8 Nov 2026 that there is nothing to avoid at the outset, so gaming names are open.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is the founder and CEO, Mike Veverka, with about 9.0m shares (about 14%) [1][14]. No bid, review or sale process has been announced. The company is a buyer itself (Raffle Management Group in Canada, completion expected about January 2027) [5].

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs in kind. There were "one-off" items in each of the last three years (A$1.4m in FY24, a A$0.4m gain in FY25, A$8.7m in FY26) [4][7], and the biggest FY26 item, deal costs, belongs to a company that buys a business most years (Stride, StarVale, the two Dream businesses, now Raffle Management Group). NPATA adds back A$9.1m after tax of acquired amortisation, which will now recur every year.

The lottery retail business and its contract with The Lottery Corporation (TLC), kept apart from the rest:
- Four reseller agreements with TLC subsidiaries (Victoria and Tasmania; New South Wales and the ACT; South Australia; the Northern Territory and eligible overseas customers), each signed in August 2020 for ten years to 25 August 2030, "with renewal negotiations 9 months prior to expiry", which is about 25 November 2029 [1].
- Jumbo paid A$15m for the 2020 extension (amortised at A$1.5m a year). The service fee to TLC stepped up from 1.5% of the subscription price in FY21 to 2.5% in FY22, 3.5% in FY23 and 4.65% from FY24 on [1].
- The agreements are linked: a material breach or unremedied default under one lets TLC terminate all of them. TLC audits Jumbo's compliance every year [1].
- They produced 47% of group revenue in FY26 [1]. The SaaS segment's revenue is 72% a charge to Lottery Retailing, and the company says Lottery Retailing contributes about 75% of SaaS revenue [1][4]. Counting that share, about A$52.4m of FY26's A$85.2m EBITDA depends on TLC (the desk's arithmetic: A$33.2m plus 72% of A$26.7m).
- TLC's own words: its June 2026 investor day called the period when "third-party operators captured the first digital lottery sales, outside TLC's commercial control" the "reseller era", made its digital business a standalone unit from FY27, and said "each 1% lift in digital share = c.$6m in EBITDA" for TLC [21]. TLC's Victorian licence was extended by 40 years in May 2026 [22].
- Jumbo's impairment test assumes "TLC reseller agreements continue beyond current agreement periods" [1].

The software and managed services businesses:
- SaaS: RSL Queensland went live in FY27, adding about A$200m of ticket sales a year to the platform [5]. Lotterywest gave the contract for its new gaming and digital system to Brightstar Lottery in December 2025 (go-live targeted for the third quarter of 2027); Jumbo could not agree terms for its part of the player account system, talks on the website and app part continue, and its existing Oz.Lotterywest SaaS agreement is unchanged (term not disclosed) [9][2]. FY27 assumes an outside revenue margin of 3.25% to 3.5% [4].
- Managed Services: Canada's FY27 outlook flags "renewal risk on some key contracts where retention pricing outcomes are uncertain" [4]. The UK businesses hold Gambling Commission external lottery manager licences whose fees rise 25% [1].
- Dream Giveaways: FY27 guidance assumes the existing UK VAT treatment of prize-draw entries continues, which the annual report calls uncertain; the UK's voluntary code for prize draw operators applies from 20 May 2026 and the new subscription rules under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act (expected spring 2027) will apply to Dream UK. The UK founders leave by December 2026 [1][4][10].

What else the headline numbers hide: book value is acquired intangibles (net tangible assets are negative); the FY26 Australian result came in what the company calls a "1-in-45 year" jackpot year (no Powerball of A$100m or more), while the first four months of FY27 had nine large jackpots worth A$430m against seven worth A$300m a year earlier [5]; 2.75m of the CEO's shares are held by LGT Crestone, and the 1m moved there on 4 June 2026 were "provided as security for a secured financing arrangement" [14].

Licences, regulators and tax changes:
- Australia: Jumbo holds no lottery licence of its own; it sells under TLC's licences through the reseller agreements above. The Interactive Gambling Amendment (Gambling Reform) Act 2026 received assent on 26 Aug 2026 and its schedules start on 1 Jan 2027, including a schedule on online lottery products (it bans foreign-matched lotteries and online keno) and a tighter definition of trade promotion lotteries; Jumbo expects limited impact and calls the reforms a net positive [1][5][23].
- UK: Gambling Commission ELM licence fees up 25% for Gatherwell and StarVale; prize draws sit outside the Gambling Act under a voluntary code; VAT on prize-draw entries is an open question [1].
- US and Canada: no licence matter disclosed; privacy rules (COPPA, state laws, Canada's Bill C-36) noted [1].
- No fines, inquiries or licence conditions against Jumbo are disclosed.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: at about five times FY27 EBITDA, is the market right to price Jumbo as if the TLC reseller agreements (47% of revenue, about A$52m of FY26's A$85m EBITDA counting the platform charge) might not be renewed when they end on 25 August 2030, and do the software, managed services and Dream businesses carry the value on their own? A deep dive would value the TLC-dependent earnings to 2030 separately, read everything TLC has said about resellers and its own digital push, and test the Dream UK VAT and prize-draw rules.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: gambling distributors are priced on enterprise value to EBITDA. The named peer is Tabcorp (ASX:TAH), the listed wagering and media business that, like Jumbo, sells gambling products under licences and agreements it doesn't fully control: 2,292,644,230 shares at A$0.89 (30 Oct 2026 close) is A$2,040.5m, plus FY26 net debt of A$533m including leases, over FY26 EBITDA before significant items of A$431.7m = 5.96 times [25]. TLC itself (A$5.42 close on 30 Oct 2026, 35.2 times FY26 EPS before significant items of 15.4c) owns licences running for decades and is not a fair comparison for a reseller [26]. The price would get there through results that show the jackpot recovery and debt falling (1H FY27 about 25 Feb 2027), the buy-back as a steady buyer, and in the end a TLC renewal.
- Deductions used throughout: net debt A$42.6m, lease liabilities A$16.6m (Tabcorp's figure includes leases) and the earn-out at A$22.4m, A$81.6m in all, on 62.71m shares.
- If it goes our way: TLC renews on similar terms as it has since 2005, Australian EBITDA returns to FY25's A$61.1m (a year with 31 large jackpots against 23 in FY26) and International reaches the top of guidance, A$40.1m (Dream UK £11.8m at 0.52 is A$22.7m, Dream US US$6.2m at 0.72 is A$8.6m, Managed Services up 5%). A$101.3m at 5.96 times is A$603.8m, less A$81.6m, A$522.2m, or A$8.33 a share.
- If it goes against us: TLC does not renew. The TLC-dependent A$52.4m of EBITDA, less an 11.6% share of capital spending and lease payments, runs to 25 August 2030 and then stops; valued as cash to that date at the company's own 17.5% pre-tax discount rate for Lottery Retailing, it is worth A$129.0m. The rest (A$2.5m of non-TLC Australian EBITDA plus International at the bottom of guidance, A$37.9m) at 5.96 times is A$241.2m. A$370.2m less A$81.6m is A$288.5m, or A$4.60 a share.
- Check at the company's own discount rates from its impairment test (pre-tax 17.5% for Lottery Retailing and SaaS, 19.3% Managed Services UK, 16.1% Canada, 22.8% Dream UK, 27.6% Dream US; growth 2%, 2.5% for Dream US), after capital spending and leases: our way A$7.32, against A$3.90 [1].
- Middle case for reference: FY27 at guidance (Australia at FY26's A$55.0m, International A$38.0m) at 5.96 times is A$7.54.
- Only now the price: A$6.31, the last trade on 6 Nov 2026 (ASX company data; A$6.01 at the 30 Oct close used in the ranking). Market value A$395.7m; enterprise value about 5.1 times FY27 guidance EBITDA. Gap if it goes our way: 8.33 / 6.31 - 1 = +32%. Gap if it goes against us: 4.60 / 6.31 - 1 = -27%. At the company's own discount rates: +16% and -38%.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is +32% on Tabcorp's multiple, which clears 25% but not 40%, and the deciding event, the TLC renewal (talks from about November 2029, expiry 25 August 2030), is three years away. Most of the our-way gap is jackpots returning to FY25's level, which no filing can settle; at the company's own discount rates the gap is +16%, which would be a C. Not a value pick: the against value is 27% below the price. The 1H FY27 result (about 25 Feb 2027) can test the earnings half of the question; the renewal half waits for TLC.
