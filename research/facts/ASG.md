# Autosports Group (ASX:ASG): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 8 points, #3 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01). Red flag in the ranking: heavy debt (enterprise value 7.7 times market value).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. A prestige and luxury car dealer with more than 90 businesses in Sydney, Canberra, Melbourne, Brisbane, the Gold Coast, Adelaide and Auckland [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The FY26 audit opinion is unmodified [3]. The accounts carry a note on a A$192.5m excess of current over current liabilities (floorplan is all current), with the directors concluding the going-concern basis is appropriate on forecasts to August 2027, A$70m of undrawn corporate debt and A$204.2m of undrawn floorplan [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$388,724 a day (lowest day A$97,477), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No, but at the line. Ian Pagent and Nicholas Pagent (the CEO) and related entities held 51.95% until shares issued for acquisitions diluted them to 49.42% (notice of 10 Apr 2026) [4]. They have since bought about 180,000 shares on market, to 105,626,489 shares or 49.49% [5][6]. That is slow buying, not a bid, and no review or sale process is announced. Worth a line on the special situations watch list as a holder build-up.

The gap between statutory and normalised profit recurs. Acquisition and restructure costs (A$4.3m in FY26, A$3.6m in FY25) come with a group that buys dealerships every year, and acquisition amortisation (A$4.3m, A$4.7m) is the same every year [3][8]. Only the FY25 impairment reversal was a one-off.

What the headline numbers hide:
- The second half. H1 FY26 normalised NPBT was A$35.3m; H2 was A$18.2m. In FY25 the second half had been about 33% better than the first [2][7][11].
- Interest. Floorplan interest A$38.6m (FY25 A$33.3m), corporate interest A$21.8m (A$18.6m), lease interest A$18.4m (A$14.1m): A$78.2m in total against statutory PBT of A$39.1m [1]. Normalised NPBT is after floorplan and corporate interest.
- Floorplan bigger than stock. Bailment of A$832.2m exceeds inventory of A$715.8m by A$116.4m [1].
- Operating cash flow is floorplan-funded in FY26 (minus A$54.2m without the extra bailment) [1].
- Rates. Floorplan plus corporate debt is about A$1.15bn; each 1 percentage point on that is about A$11.5m a year before tax, about a fifth of FY26 normalised NPBT. The June 2026 update blamed "three interest rate rises in H2 FY26" for higher inventory and corporate interest [12]; the RBA raised the cash rate a fourth time, to 4.60%, on 30 Sep 2026 [27].
- The market. National new vehicle sales in September 2026 were 102,924, up 0.9% on a year earlier; battery electric vehicles were 24.2% of sales against 11.3% a year before, and China supplied 35.5% of the market [28]. That is the shift the company says left it short of BEV stock.
- Property is at cost: A$230.2m across seven owner-occupied sites [1], below corporate net debt of A$290.1m. Property does not cover the debt the way it does at Peter Warren.
- Covenants (net leverage and fixed charge cover) are tested every half-year; the June 2026 amendment "improved certain financial covenant terms" [9][10].
- Book value includes A$681.3m of intangibles; tangible equity is negative [3].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: was the halving of normalised profit in H2 FY26 (A$18.2m against A$35.3m in H1) a delivery delay that reverses as the BEV order bank converts, as the company says, or the new level once higher rates bite on about A$1.15bn of floorplan and corporate debt?
- If it goes our way: profit returns to the H1 FY26 rate, 2 x A$35.3m = A$70.6m normalised NPBT a year (already after floorplan and corporate interest and on a cash-rent basis), less the recurring A$4.3m of acquisition and restructure costs, A$66.3m. After 30% tax A$46.4m, at 8 times A$371.2m, or A$1.74 a share on 213.42m shares. Eight times is well below Eagers Automotive at about 22 times trailing earnings (ASX company data).
- If it goes against us: H2 FY26 is the new level, 2 x A$18.2m = A$36.4m, less A$4.3m, A$32.1m; after tax A$22.5m, at the same 8 times A$179.7m, or A$0.84 a share.
- Price: A$1.00 (close 30 Oct 2026, the day after the 3.0c dividend went ex), market value A$213.4m. Gap if it goes our way: 1.74 / 1.00 - 1 = +74%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.84 / 1.00 - 1 = -16%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is +74%. The question can be worked from the filings (the half-year splits, the order bank, floorplan size and cost, the rate exposure and the covenants), and the AGM on 20 Nov 2026 and the H1 FY27 result due about 19 Feb 2027 should show whether profit is heading back toward the H1 FY26 rate. The full test, the order bank unwind in January to June 2027, ends just after the window, so the deep dive should look for the H1 FY27 margin and interest lines as the early read. The Pagents bought more shares in August at A$1.45 to A$1.63, above today's price.
