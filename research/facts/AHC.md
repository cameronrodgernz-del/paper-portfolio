# Austco Healthcare (ASX:AHC): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #17 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Austco makes and installs nurse call and clinical communication systems (Tacera, Pulse Mobile) for hospitals and aged care, with installation and maintenance businesses bought from its own resellers. Revenue A$94.2m in FY26 [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. A$16.3m cash and A$0.1m of borrowings at 30 June 2026 [1].
- Too thin to trade? No, but close to the floor. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 2 to 30 October 2026 was A$27,640 a day (lowest day A$825), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz. At 10% of daily volume over two sessions that allows a position of only about A$5,500.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The two substantial holders are Asia Pac Holding/Technology (14.62%) and Australian Ethical (14.06%) [3]. Austco is the buyer in two pending deals (RTM and CareTech), not a target.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs. Austco grows by buying its resellers on earn-outs, and the earn-out estimate is revalued through profit every year: a A$2.2m charge in FY25 (Amentco) and a A$0.9m gain in FY26 (G&S) [1]. Take those out and profit after tax was A$8.1m in both years, so the reported 52% rise in profit is the earn-out swing, not the business. FY26 tax also included a A$0.88m benefit from accelerated R&D deductions [1].

What else the headline numbers hide:
- The second half slowed. H2 FY26 revenue rose 3.4% and H2 EBITDA was A$6.6m against A$8.3m in H1 [1][5]. Asia revenue fell from A$10.8m to A$6.7m and Europe from A$5.0m to A$4.4m; growth came from Australia and New Zealand (largely acquired) and North America [1].
- The order book (unfilled contracted revenue) has not grown: A$55.8m in July 2025, A$54.6m in October 2025, A$47.2m in February 2026, A$45.2m at June 2026 and A$51.2m on 17 August 2026. About 80% is in foreign currencies [2][6][11].
- Customer concentration: the "largest US customer" has a Pulse Mobile rollout across about 180 hospitals expected during calendar 2026 [4]. Its share of revenue isn't disclosed. North America was A$30.4m of FY26 external revenue [1].
- Book value is 45% goodwill and intangibles (A$28.2m of A$62.1m), and the pending deals add more [1][7][8].
- The first debt. RTM and CareTech cost about A$12.4m upfront (A$3.0m of it held in escrow until a key RTM customer contract is renewed), funded partly by a new A$12.0m bank facility. RTM is priced at 6.0 times average FY27 and FY28 EBITDA, uncapped, against the 3.5 times paid for every earlier deal [7][8]. RTM's figures (normalised revenue A$15.6m, normalised EBITDA A$2.2m) are unaudited.
- Development spending capitalised (A$2.0m) slightly exceeds its amortisation (A$1.6m), a small lift to profit [2].

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance (FY27): none in numbers. The FY26 result (27 Aug 2026) said the company is "confident in our ability to sustain earnings growth into FY27 and beyond", with the timing of hospital capital programmes "the principal variable" [2]. The AGM (19 Oct 2026) gave no figures for FY27 [12][13]. The two October 2026 deals are expected to add about A$10.2m of revenue and A$1.8m of EBITDA in FY27 (seven months), and MCS about A$0.8m of EBITDA a full year [7][8][9].

Changes this year (FY26):
- 29 Oct 2025: Q1 revenue A$23.2m, EBITDA A$4.2m; "continues to target 10–14% organic revenue growth" [6].
- 18 Feb 2026: no figures; "confident ... to sustain earnings growth into the second half" [14].
- 1 Jun 2026: revenue A$90m to A$95m, EBITDA A$14.0m to A$14.6m, profit after tax A$9.0m to A$9.4m (including about A$1.0m of earn-out gain), citing "tougher trading conditions in the second half" [4].
- Delivered (27 Aug 2026): revenue A$94.2m, EBITDA A$14.85m (above the range), profit A$9.0m (bottom of the range) [1][2].

Record:
- FY26: the June range was met. The 10–14% organic growth target can't be checked: the company doesn't split organic and acquired revenue, and says consolidation removes about A$3m of sales to acquired resellers [2].
- FY25: guidance of revenue A$80m to A$82m and EBITDA A$12.5m to A$13.5m (23 Jul 2025, after year end) [11]; delivered A$81.4m and A$13.0m [1].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: was H2 FY26 (revenue up 3.4%, EBITDA down a fifth on H1, order book flat for two years) a timing gap in hospital projects, or the organic business levelling off while acquisitions and earn-out swings carry the headline? And does the first debt-funded, 6 times EBITDA software deal (RTM) earn what it costs? A deep dive would separate organic from acquired revenue by region, test RTM's unaudited earnings and its key contract, and check what share of revenue the largest US customer is.
- If it goes our way: FY26 EBITDA A$14.85m plus a full year of the three new businesses (MCS A$0.82m, RTM A$2.2m, CareTech A$0.9m) is A$18.77m. Less FY26 capital spending (A$2.79m) and lease payments (A$1.41m): pre-tax free cash flow A$14.56m. Grow it 8% a year for five years (the sales growth in the company's own goodwill test), then 2.5%, discounted at the company's own pre-tax rate of 14.83% [1]: A$149.9m. Less pro forma net debt including all earn-outs (A$7.7m cash less A$7.0m drawn, less A$2.50m G&S, A$0.64m MCS, A$5.82m RTM and A$1.04m CareTech: A$9.3m): A$140.6m, or A$0.375 a share on 374.75m shares.
- If it goes against us: H2 FY26 is the run rate. H2 EBITDA A$6.59m a year is A$13.19m, plus the same A$3.92m acquired: A$17.11m; less A$4.20m capital spending and leases is A$12.90m of pre-tax free cash flow, with no growth, at 14.83%: A$87.0m. Less A$9.3m: A$77.7m, or A$0.207 a share.
- Price: A$0.28 (close 30 Oct 2026, the ranking's price), market value A$105m. The ASX's last trade was A$0.265 on 6 Nov 2026. Gap if it goes our way: 0.375 / 0.28 - 1 = +34% (+42% at A$0.265). Gap if it goes against us: 0.207 / 0.28 - 1 = -26% (-22% at A$0.265).

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is +34% at the ranking price, above 25% but short of the 40% an A needs (it crosses 40% only at the 6 Nov price of A$0.265, so it sits on the line). The H1 FY27 result on about 18 Feb 2027 is the deciding event: it is the first half with the debt, RTM and CareTech, and will show whether organic growth came back. Not a value pick: the "against" value is 26% below the price. Two practical limits for any deep dive: the liquidity ceiling of about A$5,500 a position, and the fact that every reported profit figure carries an earn-out revaluation.
