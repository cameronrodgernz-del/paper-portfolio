# Maritana Minerals (ASX:MRT): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 9 points, #1 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Maritana is the former Horizon Minerals (ASX:HRZ); the code changed to MRT in April 2026 and shares were consolidated 15 to 1 on 11 Dec 2025 [1]. The ASX announcement list for the old HRZ code was refused by the ASX site ("Request Rejected") each time it was tried, so announcements before 13 Apr 2026 were read only through later filings that summarise them (annual report, quarterly reports).

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No, but close. FY26 revenue was A$80.6m from selling ore and toll-treated gold from Boorara and Phillips Find [1], so it passes the funnel's rule. Both operations are now in care and maintenance and the September 2026 quarter had no gold sales [2][3]. The business today is A$214.5m of cash and the refurbishment of the Black Swan plant near Kalgoorlie, with first gold targeted for the second half of calendar 2027 [2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. KPMG's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified with no going-concern paragraph [1]. The directors' going-concern note does say "additional funding is required either through equity, debt funding, the sale of assets, or a combination" to progress the plant and planned exploration [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$307,461 a day (lowest day A$88,216), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is Add New Energy Investment Holdings Group at 19.97%, counting shares it has agreed to buy from Golden Crane Holdings and Ocean Wing Investments under a conditional sale agreement of 30 Mar 2026 [4][5]. That stops just under the 20% takeover threshold. A holder build-up, worth a line on the special situations watch list, but not a holder over 50% and no bid.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit does not recur in kind (one asset sale), but each of the last two years carried a large item: the A$54.0m gain in FY26 and the A$7.0m impairment in FY25.

What the headline numbers hide:
- "2.1 times earnings" is the Lake Johnston gain. Without it FY26 profit was about A$8.9m, from ore sales that have since stopped [1].
- "Net cash about 117% of market value" is spoken for. The February 2026 scoping studies put pre-production capital at about A$160.5m and the maximum cash draw at A$131m [7][8]. In June 2026 the plant's design capacity rose from 2.2 to 2.5 million tonnes a year and the company said plant capital "is likely to increase"; an updated estimate is still to come [9][1]. Early works of about A$31.5m are committed [2].
- Reserves cover less than half the plan. The plan is about 10.9Mt at 1.65 g/t for 536,000 recovered ounces over an initial five years, about 102,000 ounces a year [7]. Ore Reserves are 4.31Mt at 1.72 g/t for 235,600 ounces [6]: 44% of the plan's ounces and about 1.7 years of feed for a 2.5Mt a year plant. The company says the production target rests on about 74% Measured and Indicated and 17% Inferred resources [6][7]. Of the 2.15Moz resource, 1.10Moz (51%) is Inferred [6].
- Gold price assumption. The study uses A$5,500/oz [7]; reserves use A$4,500/oz (A$5,000/oz for the first year at some pits) [10]. The company gave spot as about A$5,800/oz on 31 Jul 2026 [8]; its August 2026 reserve tables quote "~AUD7,100/oz" spot [10]. The two filings don't agree.
- Costs. Study all-in sustaining cost A$3,353/oz, cash cost A$2,852/oz; the A$631m pre-tax NPV (8%) "excludes corporate costs from the start of commissioning" [8].
- Rehabilitation provisions of A$53.0m (FY25 A$82.1m, before Lake Johnston was sold) [1].
- Other assets: the Nimbus silver-zinc project (20.2Moz silver), a 3% net smelter royalty on the Windarra tailings project, where Encore made its investment decision and paid A$400,000 [2][11].

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance: first gold in the second half of calendar 2027 (Sep 2026 quarter, 26 Oct 2026) [2]. No production or capital figure is current: the A$160.5m estimate is under review [1][9].

Changes this year:
- 17 Feb 2026 (scoping studies, via the March quarter report): 2.2Mt a year plant, ~102koz a year for about five years, capex ~A$160.5m, first gold mid-2027 [7].
- 8 Jun 2026: capacity raised to 2.5Mt a year; plant capex "likely to increase"; first production "H2 2027" [9].
- 24 Sep 2026 (annual report): updated capital cost estimate "to follow a cost optimisation review currently underway" [1].
- 26 Oct 2026: all statutory approvals for the plant in place; first gold still H2 CY2027 [2].

Record: no production or cost guidance for FY25 or FY26 was found in the MRT-coded filings; FY26 output came from ore sale and toll milling agreements [1]. First gold has moved from mid-2027 to the second half of 2027 within eight months.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: can Black Swan deliver something close to the scoping plan (536,000 recovered ounces over five years) when only 235,600 ounces, 44% of it, are in ore reserves, and what will the revised capital cost be? The cash alone is about the market value, so the price gives the project almost nothing.
- If it goes our way: the plan delivers at the study's A$5,500/oz with plant capital 30% above the A$160.5m estimate (A$48.2m more). Pre-tax NPV A$631.3m less A$48.2m is A$583.1m; at 30% tax about A$408.2m. Add cash A$214.5m (30 Sep 2026) and listed investments A$2.5m, less rehabilitation A$53.0m and five years of corporate costs at about A$9m a year (A$45m, roughly FY26's staff, corporate and consulting costs). Total A$527.2m, or A$1.41 a share on 373.48m shares.
- If it goes against us: only the reserves are mined, at the reserve gold price of A$4,500/oz. 235,600 ounces at the plan's 92.7% recovery is 218,400 ounces; at a margin of A$1,147/oz over the study's A$3,353/oz all-in cost that is A$250.5m, less capital of A$208.7m (the estimate plus 30%), A$41.8m pre-tax, about A$29.3m after tax. Add cash and investments (A$217.0m), less rehabilitation (A$53.0m) and two years of corporate costs (A$18m): A$175.3m, or A$0.47 a share.
- Price: A$0.52 (close 30 Oct 2026), market value A$194.2m, about A$23m less than cash and listed investments. Gap if it goes our way: 1.41 / 0.52 - 1 = +171%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.47 / 0.52 - 1 = -10%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is +171%. Most of the question can be worked from filings: the scoping study, the reserve and resource tables, the cost and price assumptions and the cash. The company has promised a revised capital cost and mine plan, and the EPC award has to come before plant construction for first gold in the second half of 2027, so the answer should start to show before 2 May 2027. Both of those dates are inferred, not announced; if the capital update slips past May the grade would fall to B. The deep dive needs the 17 Feb 2026 scoping study itself, filed under HRZ, which this desk could not reach.
