# NZME (NZX:NZM): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 3 points, #8 in the NZX table of reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-08 run, prices to 6 Nov).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. NZME is a New Zealand media company in three divisions: Audio (radio and digital audio), Publishing (the NZ Herald in print and online) and the OneRoof property site, with 2025 revenue of NZ$345.6m [1][3].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The auditor's opinion on the 2025 accounts is unmodified, with no going-concern note [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. NZX's own daily trading data: median on-market value over the 20 sessions 9 October to 6 November 2026 was NZ$55,705 a day, lowest NZ$5,260; 18 of the 20 sessions above NZ$12,500 [16]. At 10% of the median for two sessions the largest position would be about NZ$11,100.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is director Jim Grenon at about 19.9% (section 4), just under the 20% at which the Takeovers Code stops further buying without an offer or holder approval. Noted for the watch list in the report, not a kill.

The gap between statutory and operating profit recurs: restructuring costs were left out in 2024 (NZ$4.5m) and again in 2025 (NZ$6.5m), each time tied to a new savings programme [2]. None was booked in 1H26 [3], but the print plant move and AI programme announced in August 2026 point to more.

What the headline numbers hide:
- Print is shrinking and the company is rebuilding around it. Reader revenue fell 3% in 2025; print and distribution costs fell 9% [2]. In August 2026 NZME agreed to buy Stuff's Petone press equipment, to spend up to NZ$15m over two years moving it to a new site, and expects about NZ$7m a year of savings once installed; its Ellerslie print lease runs to the end of 2028 [5].
- Book value is all intangibles: tangible assets are negative [1].
- 2025's free cash flow (NZ$25.4m) was helped by low tax paid (NZ$1.9m against NZ$5.2m in 2024 and NZ$2.5m in 1H26 alone) [1][3].
- Radio licences: the Crown has offered to renew NZME's commercial radio spectrum from April 2031 for NZ$11.2m, paid in four five-year instalments from 2030, below the 2011 renewal price [6].
- Profit history, operating NPAT (NZ$m): 2021 21.1, 2022 23.3, 2023 14.1, 2024 12.1, 2025 17.7; five-year average 17.7. Statutory: 2022 22.7, 2023 12.2, 2024 -16.0, 2025 13.1 [2][7][8].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is the 2026 lift in operating profit (operating NPAT NZ$6.6m in 1H26 against NZ$3.4m) the start of a recovery that the announced savings (about NZ$7m a year from the new press, NZ$7m from AI and automation, a cheaper Auckland lease) can carry further, or does print's decline absorb it and keep the restructuring costs coming?
- How the market prices this kind of business, and the mechanism: listed media in New Zealand is priced on trailing earnings with a high payout. The only profitable listed peer, Sky Network Television (NZX:SKT), trades at 8.5 times trailing earnings with a 12.1% gross yield (NZX instrument data, 6 Nov 2026) [19]. The listed Australian radio and TV peers (ARN Media, Southern Cross Media, Nine) made statutory losses in their latest years, so their multiples can't be used [20]. The price would move with the full-year result in February 2027 and the dividend.
- If it goes our way: 2026 operating NPAT comes in at the first half's pace, 2025's NZ$17.7m plus 1H26's NZ$3.2m improvement, NZ$20.9m, with no further restructuring. At 8.5 times, NZ$177.6m, or 94c a share on 188.18m shares.
- If it goes against us: profit falls back to the 2023 to 2025 average of statutory profit before impairments, the level the business has earned once restructuring is counted: NZ$12.8m (2023, owners' share), NZ$8.7m (2024, before NZ$24.7m of impairments) and NZ$13.1m (2025), an average of NZ$11.5m. At 8.5 times, NZ$97.9m, or 52c a share.
- Only now the price: NZ$0.98, the close on 6 November 2026 (bid NZ$0.965, offer NZ$1.04), a market value of NZ$184.4m [16]. Gap if it goes our way: 0.944 / 0.98 - 1 = -4%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.52 / 0.98 - 1 = -47%.
- For scale: at NZ$0.98 the market pays 10.4 times 2025 operating NPAT and 8.8 times the first-half pace, close to Sky TV's multiple, and the 2025 free cash flow yield is 13.8%. If the full NZ$14m of announced savings were added to the first-half pace (NZ$10.1m after tax), the value would be NZ$1.40 (+43%), but the press savings can't start before it is installed (the old site runs to the end of 2028), it costs up to NZ$15m first, and no timetable has been given for the AI savings.

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive: the gap if it goes our way is -4%, under the 25% line. At Sky TV's multiple the price already reflects the first-half recovery. The case for more value rests on savings that land in 2028 or later and would need the events themselves. Worth noting for the watch list: a director holds about 19.9% and bought NZ$3.1m of shares on market this year, a fraction under the Takeovers Code's 20% line.
