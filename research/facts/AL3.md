## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. AML3D designs and sells large wire-arc metal 3D printing systems (ARCEMY) and prints metal parts, mostly for the US Navy's shipbuilding and submarine supply chain. FY26 revenue, including equipment lease income, was A$12.47m, of which A$9.44m came from US customers [1].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. KPMG's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified. It draws attention to restated FY25 comparatives (A$10m of deposits moved from cash to other financial assets, A$0.15m of accrued interest and A$0.42m of grant money recognised), but says the opinion is not modified on that matter [1]. Cash A$24.42m at 30 September 2026 and no bank debt [3].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$66,224 a day, and all 20 sessions were above A$10,000 (data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz) [14]. Two sessions at 10% of the median allows a position of about A$13,200.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries, and this is an industrial company.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The only substantial holder is Netwealth Investments (a wrap platform holding for its clients) at 6.29% [1]. No approach, review or sale has been announced in 2024, 2025 or 2026 [13].

Does the gap between statutory and underlying profit recur? The gap between the loss and EBITDA is depreciation and interest, the same every year. Two items inside both figures also recur: share-based payments (A$1.03m in FY26, A$1.42m in FY25) and government support (R&D tax offset A$1.10m and A$0.23m; grants A$0.43m and A$0.19m) [1]. Neither is a one-off.

What the headline numbers hide:
- Customer concentration. Four customers were 77% of FY26 revenue (35%, 19%, 12% and 11%). The two largest customers of FY25 (32% and 31% of that year) fell to 8% and 5% in FY26 [1]. Revenue moves with a handful of system orders.
- The profitable half leans on the R&D offset. Without the roughly A$0.98m of R&D tax offset booked in H2, H2 EBITDA would have been a loss of about A$0.4m. H2 share-based payments were also low, about A$0.11m (A$1.03m for the year less A$0.93m in H1) [1][4].
- The rise in interest income on the cash from the 2024 raising, from A$0.41m to A$1.01m, accounts for about a fifth of the A$2.8m smaller loss [1].
- The cash has a job. The planned A$17m of capacity spending is 70% of the A$24.42m held at 30 September 2026, before operating cash use of about A$2m a quarter [3].
- Dilution. Shares on issue rose 50% in two years, from 377.1m at June 2024 to 567.4m at June 2026, mainly through the A$30m placement at A$0.19 in November 2024 [1][6].
- Orders. A$16.8m of contracted work rolled into FY27, against A$9m into FY26. The company's sales pipeline of "more than A$80 million" is its own estimate, not contracted [3].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is FY26's step up (sales up 76% and a first positive half) the start of steady demand from the US Navy's supply chain, or a few lumpy system orders? A deep dive would test whether FY27 revenue at least matches the A$16.8m of work in hand, whether EBITDA stays positive without the year-end R&D offset, and how much of the A$80m pipeline turns into repeat orders.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: a loss-making maker of capital equipment is priced on enterprise value (market value less net cash) to revenue. The price would get there through results that show the order book turning into revenue, starting with the half-year report in February 2027.
- The multiple, from AML3D's own figures (five-year table in the FY26 annual report [1]): enterprise value to revenue at the last three year-ends was 3.86 times (2024: A$35.82m market value less A$7.79m cash plus A$0.22m debt, over A$7.32m), 10.43 times (2025: A$107.55m less A$30.48m cash and deposits, over A$7.39m) and 3.77 times (2026: A$73.77m less A$26.75m, over A$12.47m). Average 6.02 times, median 3.86 times.
- If it goes our way: FY27 revenue at least the A$16.8m in hand, at the three-year average of 6.02 times: A$101.1m, plus net cash of A$24.42m (30 Sep 2026; leases left out) = A$125.5m, on 572.42m shares = A$0.219 a share. At the median 3.86 times it is A$89.3m, A$0.156.
- If it goes against us: orders fall back and revenue returns to FY25's A$7.39m, at the lowest year-end multiple of 3.77 times: A$27.9m plus A$24.42m = A$52.3m, or A$0.091 a share. A further year of FY26-style cash use (A$6.2m of operating and capital spending) would take that to about A$0.081.
- Cross-check against a listed peer: Titomic (ASX:TTT), another metal additive manufacturer selling into US defence, is valued at about 20 times its sales revenue: 1,734.4m shares at A$0.09 (30 Oct 2026 close) is A$156.1m, less A$18.1m of cash at 30 Sep 2026 plus A$14.0m of borrowings at 30 Jun 2026, an enterprise value of A$151.9m, over A$7.52m of sales revenue in the 12 months to 30 June 2026 [12]. At that multiple AML3D's FY26 revenue alone would be worth A$0.48 a share. Titomic used about A$10m of operating cash in the September quarter and is moving its domicile to the US, so this is a ceiling, not the base.
- Only now the price: A$0.105 (last trade on 6 Nov 2026, ASX company data; A$0.115 at the 30 Oct close) [15], a market value of A$60.1m and about 2.9 times FY26 revenue net of cash. Gap if it goes our way: 0.219 / 0.105 - 1 = +109% (+49% at the median multiple). Gap if it goes against us: 0.091 / 0.105 - 1 = -13%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is 109% (49% even at the median of the company's own three year-end multiples), the question can be answered from filings (half-year revenue, EBITDA before the R&D offset, customer receipts and new orders), and the December quarterly report (about 27 January 2027) and the half-year report (about 27 February 2027) should show the answer. It is not a value pick: the value if it goes against us is 13% below the price, beyond the 10% limit. The deep dive should widen the peer set beyond Titomic and test how much of the cash the US and UK capacity spending will use.
