# Mayfield Group Holdings (ASX:MYG): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 4 points, #69 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-08).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Mayfield designs and builds electrical infrastructure in Adelaide, Perth and Kalgoorlie: switchboards, transportable
switchrooms, kiosk substations and power skids for data centres, mines, utilities, renewables and transport projects
(Mayfield Industries), commercial switchboards (BE Switchcraft, bought August 2025), underground mining electrical gear
(SMEC Power & Technology, bought March 2026), the N-Series switchboard range (bought from Nilsen, completing by
31 Oct 2026), plus communications and control systems and power products [1][2]. Revenue is project based. All A$.

  revenue [1].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The FY26 accounts carry no going-concern
  note or qualification (annual report, 27 Aug 2026) [1].

  A$102,214), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz [25].
- On Cam's excluded list? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries (confirmed by Cam on 8 Nov 2026).
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is
  Nightingale Partners (director Lindsay Phillips is its executive chairman), 26.3% after selling 10m shares in May 2026;
  it said it has no intention of selling more in the next 12 months [13]. No bid or review.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs in part. The Nicon legal costs appear in both years (A$0.53m, then
A$0.79m) and A$1.27m sits in a solicitor's trust account pending the case [1]. Deal costs and earn-out changes come with a
company that has bought a business in each of the last two years and a third since June.

What the headline numbers hide:
- Cash conversion. FY26 operating cash flow of A$5.0m against A$18.5m of underlying EBITDA; the second half was negative
  as work in progress and debtors built up [1][4].
- Work in hand is falling: A$114m (31 Dec 2025), A$135m (14 Apr 2026, with SMEC), A$151m (6 May 2026), A$135m (30 Jun
  2026), A$114.7m (30 Sep 2026). The A$1.4bn tender pipeline is unweighted and "not a forecast of revenue" [2][8][9][10].
- Acquired growth: about three quarters of the A$60m of FY27 revenue growth at the guidance midpoint comes from
  acquisitions (desk arithmetic: SMEC's forecast A$41m a year less the A$11.9m it added in FY26, BE Switchcraft's A$18.6m
  for 10 months grossed up to 12, and N-Series A$10m to A$15m), leaving about 9% organic growth [2][11][12]. SMEC was
  bought at about 5 times its FY26 EBITDA [12].
- A customer has written about equipment it says may not meet technical specifications and has reserved its right to seek
  remediation; Mayfield denies it and has made no provision [1].
- Goodwill and acquired intangibles are A$28.3m, 35% of equity [1].
- Dilution: shares on issue rose 23% in FY26 (the A$30.5m placement and A$3.0m SPP at A$1.90 in November 2025, plus
  acquisition scrip), so statutory EPS rose only 2% while profit rose 19% [1][6].

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance (FY27): revenue A$226m to A$232m (up 34% to 37%) with an EBITDA margin of 10% to 11%, given at the AGM
on 20 Oct 2026 [2].

Changes this year:
- 27 Jan 2026: 1H FY26 NPAT "approximately $4.9 million" [14]. Delivered A$4.51m a month later (27 Feb 2026), 8% below
  [4]. The release also called FY25's A$7.47m "audited NPAT"; that is total comprehensive income, NPAT was A$6.76m [1].
- 27 Aug 2026: FY27 revenue A$224m to A$229m [3].
- 20 Oct 2026: raised to A$226m to A$232m, with the 10% to 11% margin added; Q1 revenue and gross margins "ahead of
  expectations" against internal budget [2].

Record: FY26 had no full-year number at the FY25 result; the FY25 outlook spoke of "a strong order book extending
through 2026" [15]. FY26 delivered record revenue and underlying EBITDA [3]. The one numeric guidance in the last two years
(1H FY26 NPAT) was missed by 8%.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: can Mayfield win enough new orders to deliver FY27 guidance, and turn its profit into cash, when work in
  hand has fallen from A$151m (May) to A$114.7m (September) and FY26 operating cash flow was a quarter of EBITDA? The price
  has more than halved from its A$3.525 high while guidance went up.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: listed electrical equipment makers trade on enterprise value to EBITDA,
  with net cash on top. Named peer: IPD Group (ASX:IPG), an electrical equipment distributor and switchboard maker with
  data centre exposure: 104.11m shares at A$5.15 (last trade 6 Nov 2026) is A$536.2m, plus net debt A$16.4m, over FY26
  underlying EBITDA of A$55.4m = 9.97 times; underlying P/E 17.3 on 29.7c [5][24]. Other peers on P/E at their 30 Oct
  2026 closes: Korvest (ASX:KOV), steel cable supports for electrical work, A$15.70 over 104.9c, 15.0 times [26]; Duratec
  (ASX:DUR), building and defence remediation contractor, A$1.885 (6 Nov) over 9.25c, 20.4 times [27]. IPG is the middle
  of the three. The price would move with the half-year result and order updates.
- Net cash used: A$21.83m at 30 June less the earn-out cash A$6.3m, Nilsen A$4.0m and the final dividend A$2.82m,
  A$8.71m, on 117.34m shares.
- If it goes our way: FY27 at the middle of guidance, A$229m at a 10.5% margin, EBITDA A$24.0m. At 9.97 times plus net
  cash: (24.04 x 9.97 + 8.71) / 117.34 = A$2.12. Check on P/E: EBITDA less depreciation at FY26's 2.0% of revenue, taxed
  at 30%, is A$13.6m or 11.6c a share; at IPG's 17.3 times, A$2.02 (at Korvest's 15.0 times A$1.74, at Duratec's 20.4
  times A$2.37).
- If it goes against us: new orders don't replace the work burned and EBITDA stays at FY26's A$18.46m, the acquisitions
  only covering a shrinking core: (18.46 x 9.97 + 8.71) / 117.34 = A$1.64. On FY26 statutory EPS at IPG's P/E, A$1.28.
  At the 5 times EBITDA Mayfield paid for SMEC, A$0.86.
- Only now the price: A$1.55, the last trade on 6 Nov 2026 (ASX company data; A$1.535 the session before). Market value
  A$181.9m; enterprise value about 7.2 times FY27 guidance EBITDA and 15.4 times FY26 underlying earnings. Gap if it goes
  our way: 2.12 / 1.55 - 1 = +37% (P/E check +30%; +12% at Korvest's multiple). Gap if it goes against us: 1.64 / 1.55 - 1 = +6% (statutory P/E
  -17%).

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is +37%, above 25% but under 40%, and the answer needs
the event itself: the 1H FY27 result (about 27 Feb 2027) and the order updates before it will show whether work in hand is
being refilled and whether cash follows profit. Not a value pick (the good case is under 40%). Directors have been net
sellers by value over the year (A$40m sold by Nightingale and Steele at A$2.32 to A$2.84, against small buys by Rowe and
Henderson at A$1.56 to A$2.69).
