# BSP Financial Group (ASX:BFL): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #24 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

BSP (Bank South Pacific) is the largest bank in Papua New Guinea, with banks in Fiji, Solomon Islands, Samoa, Tonga,
Vanuatu and Cook Islands, plus life insurance and funds management [1]. It is incorporated in PNG, regulated by the Bank of
Papua New Guinea (BPNG), not by APRA, and listed on PNGX (code BSP) and the ASX (BFL) [2]. It reports in kina (K); the
financial year ends 31 December; the latest half is to 30 June 2026 and there is a third-quarter trading update to
30 September 2026 [3].

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An operating bank.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The FY25 audit opinion is unmodified [1];
  the 1H26 accounts were reviewed with an unmodified conclusion [4].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$57,256 a day (lowest
  session A$1,638), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz. Two sessions at 10% of that is about A$11,450. The ASX
  line is a small part of the register: the largest ASX custodian on the top 20, HSBC Custody Nominees (Australia), held
  0.52% at 31 Dec 2025 [1].
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv lists no companies.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is the PNG
  state's Kumul Consolidated Holdings at 18.15% [1]. No bid, review or approach.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs in kind: each of FY24 and FY25 had tax items worth K40m to K95m,
in both directions, from PNG's changing bank tax.

What the headline numbers hide:
- "Net cash about 74% of market value" is a bank artefact: the cash and bills are funded by deposits.
- Kina against the Australian dollar. The kina is managed against the US dollar ("pegged", the company says), so a
  stronger A$ against the US$ shrinks BSP's earnings in A$ [8]. Average rates the company used: A$0.3875 per kina in
  1H25, A$0.3290 in 1H26, A$0.3198 in 3Q26 [8][3]. The 1H26 interim dividend was converted on the record date (27 Aug
  2026) at A$1 = K3.3244, which is A$0.3008 per kina [7]. In 1H26 kina profit rose 8.4% while A$ profit fell 7.9% [8].
- PNG foreign exchange and getting dividends out. BSP pays ASX holders who are not PNG residents in A$, converted at the
  record-date rate, and has done so for each dividend in this period [2][7]. No BSP filing reports any delay. The
  filings say nothing about PNG's foreign exchange rationing; secondary sources do: a Westpac note (7 Aug 2026) says BPNG
  keeps a "crawl-like" regime, the kina fell about 3.6% against the US$ in 2026 to date, shortages have "eased" but FX
  auctions and central bank intervention are still needed [S1]; a Devpolicy blog (12 Jan 2026) says international firms
  still face a backlog repatriating profits and that PNG's IMF loan program ends at the end of 2026, a year before the
  2027 national election [S2]. Foreign exchange income is 23% of BSP's income and grew 34% in 1H26 [8].
- The PNG bank tax. Commercial banks were taxed at 45% from 2023 against 30% for other companies, and in 2022 the state
  imposed a one-off K190m Additional Company Tax on banks with over 40% market share; BSP settled its challenge in
  February 2024, recovering K95m and paying K95m [5][9]. The rate is now stepping down: taxable income below K300m 40%
  in 2025 and 35% in 2026; above K300m, 44% in 2025 then down 1% a year to 35% (FY25 annual report) [1]. The 1H26 report
  says the rate above K300m "drops to 41% in 2026", which does not match the annual report's schedule (43% for 2026)
  [2]. The effective tax rate was 43.1% in FY24, 36.0% in FY25 and 37.1% in 1H26.
- The state as customer and rival: in September 2025 the cabinet told departments and state companies to move their
  accounts to the new state-owned National Banking Corporation; BSP said the impact could be "material", then after the
  Prime Minister's assurances said it did not expect a material effect on 2025 [10][11]. Government lending is K1.7bn of
  K18.5bn [2].
- Capital: total capital adequacy 25.3% at 30 Jun 2026 and 26.0% at 30 Sep 2026 against a 12% minimum; Tier 1 20.2%
  (half-year profit counts as Tier 2 until audited); leverage ratio 8.3% at Sep against 6% [2][3].
- Credit: net impaired loans 1.6% of gross loans; provisions 3.3% of loans [2].
- A cyber incident: data was taken from a non-production test environment in May 2026; no access to production systems
  or customer accounts was found [12].

## 3. Guidance and record
BSP gives no profit guidance. Its stated targets are a cost-to-income ratio of 42% to 45% and a 75% dividend payout [8][13].

This year: cost-to-income 43.8% in 1H26 and 43.6% in 3Q26, inside the range; return on equity 23.9% (1H26) and 24.9%
annualised (3Q26) [2][3]. 3Q26 unaudited profit K334m, up 8% on the 1H26 quarterly average and 18% on 3Q25 [3]. Key
dates were set on 2 Mar 2026 and the full-year result was fixed for 19 Feb 2027 on 25 Sep 2026 [14][15].

Record: FY25 cost-to-income 42.9% (company figure) and FY24 about 42.5% (operating expenses K1,267m over income K2,979m,
this desk's calculation), both inside 42% to 45%; payout 75% in both years [13][5][2].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is a PNG bank earning about 25% on its equity worth more than the 8.6 times earnings its smaller listed
  PNG peer Kina Securities trades on, once the kina's fall against the Australian dollar and the end of PNG's IMF program
  are allowed for? Valued on that peer's multiple, the answer is about where the price is.
- The peer: Kina Securities (ASX:KSL), 8.60 times earnings and 1.40 times book on the ASX's own company data (price A$1.06,
  book A$222.9m, FY25 profit K114.6m; data/research_data/asx_company.json, 7 Nov 2026). Kina's return on equity is about
  16.5% (K114.6m on about K697m of book at A$0.3198), against BSP's 24.9%.
- If it goes our way: 2026 earnings run-rate of K1,288m (1H26 K620m plus 3Q26 K334m twice), K2.757 a share, at 8.6 times:
  K23.72, or A$7.59 at the 3Q26 average of A$0.3198. On book: NTA at 30 Sep 2026 estimated at K10.83 a share (K4,979m
  plus 3Q26 profit K334m less the 54 toea interim, K252m), at Kina's 1.40 times scaled up by the return on equity (24.9%
  against 16.5%, so 2.12 times): K22.99, or A$7.35.
- If it goes against us: trailing twelve months' earnings to September (K1,271m, K2.720 a share) at 8.6 times, converted at
  the dividend's record-date rate of A$0.3008: A$7.04. On book at Kina's unadjusted 1.40 times: K15.19, A$4.57 to A$4.86.
- Only now the price: A$7.62 (Norgate close 30 Oct 2026; the ASX's last trade on 6 Nov was A$7.80). Gap if it goes our
  way: 7.59 / 7.62 - 1 = -0.4% (book method -3.5%). Gap if it goes against us: 7.04 / 7.62 - 1 = -7.6% (unadjusted book
  -36% to -40%). The price is 8.6 times the 2026 run-rate in A$ and 2.1 times book; the last twelve months' dividends,
  A$0.599, are a 7.9% yield, unfranked.

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive. The gap if it goes our way is under 25% (about nil): on the named peer's multiple, the market already
prices BSP's earnings in A$. A higher value needs a multiple above Kina's or a stronger kina, neither of which a filing
can settle. Worth noting for later: four directors bought about A$330,000 of shares on market this year with no sells,
and Credit Corporation's 2026 sales on PNGX averaged about K27.54, which at A$0.3008 to A$0.3198 a kina is A$8.28 to
A$8.81, above the ASX price; the PNGX price itself was not checked.
