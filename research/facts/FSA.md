# FSA Group (ASX:FSA): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 4 points, #58 of 195 ASX names in reports/research_rank.csv (2026-11-08).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

FSA is a Sydney non-bank lender. Its loan book of A$1,074.6m at 30 June 2026 was home loans A$361.5m (variable rate), asset
finance to small businesses A$437.1m, car loans A$267.1m and unsecured personal loans A$8.8m (all fixed rate), funded by
non-recourse warehouse facilities and asset-backed securities. A small services business (personal insolvency and debt
agreements) lost money. The home loan and asset finance arm, Azora Finance Group, is 76% owned; its founders hold the other 24% [1].

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An operating lender with A$81.3m of FY26 operating income [1].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. BDO's report on the FY26 accounts contains no
  qualification [1].
- Too thin to trade? No, but only just. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$10,041 a day,
  A$41 above the A$10,000 line, counting the four sessions with no trades as zero (volume times close,
  data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz) [14]. Two sessions at 10% of that is about A$2,000, barely above the A$1,500
  smallest ASX order. A slightly different window could fail.
- On Cam's excluded list? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no companies; Cam confirmed on 8 Nov 2026 there is nothing to
  avoid at the outset.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The three executive directors hold
  about 46.8% of the 121.7m shares now on issue (Tim Odillo Maher 42.81m, which matches Capital Management Corporation's
  26.0m plus Mazamand Group's 16.81m on the register; Deborah Southon 12.96m through ADST; Cellina Chen 1.25m) and none bought or sold in FY26 [1]. The on-market buyback
  pushes their share up slowly; no single holder is near 50%.

Nothing ends the sheet.

Does the gap between statutory and underlying profit recur? There is no adjusted profit, so no gap. Two items are worth
noting instead: the minority holders' share of profit (2.60m in FY26, 1.61m in FY25 restated) recurs every year and is left
out of profit to members; and FY25's profit to members was restated down by 0.95m for a classification error between the
minority holders and members, which makes FY26's growth look larger (48% on the restated figure, 34% on the one first reported) [1][5].

What the headline numbers hide:
- Credit losses are rising faster than the book. Impairment expense 4.5m (FY24), 12.2m (FY25), 18.4m (FY26); losses written
  off 4.6m, 5.5m, 9.5m. Arrears over 30 days rose in car loans (2.42%, 3.21%, 3.74%) and asset finance (3.43%, 3.59%, 3.76%),
  the two products driving growth [1] note 3.
- Interest rates. 66% of the book is fixed-rate (51% in FY24) while much of the warehouse funding floats. FY24's profit before tax fell
  40% because "the rising cash rate ... materially impacted the net margin on our fixed rate loans" [12]. The ABS notes and part
  of the warehouse funding are now swapped to fixed (swap value up 7.96m in FY26, which points to higher rates). The annual
  report puts the effect of a 50 basis point move at about 2.3m of profit after tax, and the table shows profit rising with
  rates, which does not sit easily with FY24's experience [1] note 17.
- Funding renewals: the car and personal loan and asset finance warehouses (385m of limits after the 2026 increases) both fall
  due in April 2027, the 400m home loan warehouse in October 2027. A third ABS deal, A$200m backed by car and equipment loans,
  was priced on 29 Oct 2026 and is due to settle in November [1][6].
- The profit target has slipped. In February 2026 FSA targeted profit before tax of "around $36m to $40m" on a 1.3bn loan
  book; the August 2026 annual report says "around $35.0m" [1][2].
- Minority holders: Azora's founders own 24% of Azora Finance Group. Because Azora's FY26 profit before tax (17.0m) fell
  between 15m and 30m, a proportion of their 12m Class C shares converts into Azora shares, so the minority share rises a
  little [1] note 24.
- Governance: the board is three executive directors and no independent director [1].

## 3. Guidance and record
- FY26: "profit growth of up to 30%" on FY25 and a 7c to 8c dividend (14 Aug 2025) [5]; upgraded on 6 Nov 2025 to profit
  before tax of A$23.5m to A$25.9m [7], repeated at the AGM on 20 Nov 2025 [8]. Delivered: A$23.9m, in the lower half of the
  range, and a 7.0c dividend, the bottom of its range [1].
- FY25: no numeric guidance in the FY24 report (26 Aug 2024) or AGM presentation (22 Nov 2024) [12][13]. Delivered profit
  before tax A$16.2m, up 29%.
- FY27: "Earnings guidance will be provided during the 2027 financial year"; dividend expected to be 8c a share, with the rest
  kept to fund loan growth and the buyback (14 Aug 2026) [1]. No change since.
- Longer-run target (repeated each year): new lending over A$600m a year, a loan book of about A$1.3bn, profit before tax of
  about A$35m (A$36m to A$40m in February 2026) and a return on equity above 25% [1][2].

## 4. Who owns it and who is moving
Twenty largest holders at 6 Aug 2026 held 77.4%: Capital Management Corporation 21.2% and Mazamand Group 13.7% (both Tim
Odillo Maher, 42.81m together), ADST 10.6% (Deborah Southon), BJR Investment Holdings 9.1%, Anacacia 4.9%. The notified
substantial holders are Mazamand (16.56m), ADST (11.89m) and BJR (11.11m) [1].

Substantial holder changes in the last 12 months: none announced (the ASX company data headline list from February 2025 to
6 November 2026 has no substantial holder notices).

Directors' on-market trades in the last 12 months: none. The FY26 shareholdings table shows no purchases or sales by the three
directors, and no change of director's interest notice has been lodged since 30 June 2026 [1].

Capital raisings in the last two years: none for cash. 3,700,000 shares were issued on 23 Jun 2025 under the long-term incentive
plan, paid for with limited-recourse loans from the company; up to 2,750,000 more may be issued at A$1.60 the same way [1].
The company is buying back shares: 2,313,000 in FY26 for A$2.74m, and under the current buyback (24 Jun 2026 to 23 Jun 2027,
up to 12,322,457 shares) 1,350,720 shares for A$1.70m by 3 Nov 2026, at prices from A$1.17 (3 Nov 2026) to A$1.35
(25 Jun 2026) [1][9][10].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: can FSA hold its 54% net margin and keep credit losses in check while its fixed-rate car and asset finance
  loans grow on partly floating funding, with impairment up from A$12.2m to A$18.4m in a year? FY27 guidance (likely at or
  before the 20 Nov 2026 AGM) and the 1H27 result (about 19 Feb 2027) will show it.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: non-bank lenders are priced on earnings and book. The named peers are the
  listed non-bank lenders in the ASX company data (prices of 6 Nov 2026): Resimac 6.7 times trailing earnings, Liberty 7.1,
  Pepper Money 7.6, Solvar (car loans, the closest match) 8.9, Plenti 10.6, Humm 15.2; median 8.3. Their median price to book
  is 0.88 [14]. The mechanism would be profit growth toward the target, shown in the half-year results.
- If it goes our way: FSA reaches its own target, profit before tax about A$35m. After 30% tax, A$24.5m; less the minority
  holders' FY26 share of profit (15.5%), A$20.7m to members. 20.7 x 8.27 = A$171m / 121.72m shares = A$1.41.
- If it goes against us: profit to members falls back to its five-year average, (17.2 + 13.0 + 7.3 + 9.56 + 14.14) / 5 =
  A$12.24m (FY22 to FY24 from the FY24 report [12], FY25 restated), at the same 8.27 times = A$101m / 121.72m = A$0.83.
- For reference: FY26's own profit at the peer median is A$0.96, and book value to members is 76.7c a share.
- Only now the price: A$1.165, the last trade on 6 Nov 2026 (ASX company data). Market value A$142.4m: 9.9 times FY26
  earnings and 1.5 times book, both above the peers. Gap if it goes our way: 1.41 / 1.165 - 1 = +21%. Gap if it goes against
  us: 0.83 / 1.165 - 1 = -29%.

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive: the gap if it goes our way is +21%, under 25%, even with FSA reaching its own profit target. The market
already prices FSA above the other non-bank lenders on earnings and on book. Only at the highest peer multiple (Plenti, 10.6
times) would the target be worth A$1.80 (+55%), and that needs both the target and a premium multiple. Not a value pick (the
against value is 29% below the price). Liquidity is also at the edge of the limit.
