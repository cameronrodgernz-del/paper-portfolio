## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Zimplats mines platinum group metals (PGMs) on Zimbabwe's Great Dyke through four underground mines and its own concentrators and smelter. The holding company is registered in Guernsey; the operating company, Zimbabwe Platinum Mines (ZPM), is 90% owned [1].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Axcentium (Harare) gave an unmodified opinion on the June 2026 accounts and reported no key audit matters; Axcentium has been the auditor since 31 Oct 2025 [1][2].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$85,792 a day (lowest day A$25,832), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz. Only 13% of the shares trade freely.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? A holder is over 50% but isn't making moves. Impala Platinum Holdings (Implats), through Impala Platinum BV, owns 93,644,430 shares (87.00%) [1]. It filed no substantial holder notice in the last 12 months. The changes this year are routine: the CEO of 19 years, Alex Mhembere, retired on 1 Nov 2026 and was replaced by ZPM's managing director, Dr Stanley Segula [3]; two independent directors joined on 1 Nov 2026 [4]; the AGM of 27 Oct 2026 passed an administrative change to how dividends are paid [5]. Not sent to the special situations list, but Implats decides the dividend.

The last dividend was US$1.4514 a share, ex-dividend 1 Sep 2023 (ASX company data) [15]; nothing has been paid in the three years since, including after FY26's US$276.7m profit.

The costs outside normal trading recur: currency losses were US$13.7m in FY25 and US$9.3m in FY26, and there was a credit loss or impairment on receivables in both years (US$5.9m and US$11.5m) [1].

What the headline numbers hide:
- The metal price the value rests on. FY26 gross revenue was US$2,171 per 6E ounce, against US$1,349 in FY25 [1]. Year-end prices were lower than the year's averages: platinum closed FY26 at US$1,567/oz (average US$1,789), palladium at US$1,220 (average US$1,437), rhodium at US$7,750 (average US$8,659) [1]. The impairment test assumes a long-term real basket price of US$2,297/oz and puts break-even at US$2,113/oz, at a 19.11% pre-tax real discount rate [1].
- Costs are rising: cash cost US$1,102 per 6E ounce in FY26 (up 23%, and 17% above plan), US$1,119 in the September 2026 quarter [1][8].
- Reserves and mine life. Ore Reserve 245.7Mt at 3.27g/t 6E for 25.8Moz of 6E metals (12.0Moz platinum, 9.7Moz palladium) at 30 June 2026; Mineral Resource 948Mt for 109Moz [1]. At 8.0Mt milled a year that is about 31 years. By mine: Bimha 30 years, Mupani 49 years, Mupfuti 3.3 years (primary reserves end in FY2030), Ngwarati pillar reclamation 0.8 years [1].
- One customer, the parent. All metal is sold to Impala Platinum Ltd (US$1,298.8m in FY26), which owed US$409.8m at 30 June 2026, up from US$273.7m a year earlier and US$365.8m at December 2025; that is about 3.7 months of second-half sales [1][7].
- Country and currency risk, and how cash gets out of Zimbabwe:
  - Part of export proceeds is surrendered for local currency (ZWG). In FY26 US$76.9m was surrendered (about 6% of revenue) and US$44.1m was used up, mostly by setting it off against tax and VAT; the balance is carried as a US$87.1m receivable after a US$5.9m impairment [1]. The company rates local currency risk "Moderate-High" even after its controls [1]. ZWG was 26.87 to the US dollar at year end (average 26.20, against 23.29 a year before) [1].
  - On 22 May 2026 the government classified PGMs as critical minerals under a new policy framework, with details still to come [9].
  - Government take: mineral royalty US$66.1m (5.1% of revenue) and export commission US$10.3m in FY26 [6]. A ZIMRA tax audit on exchange losses and capital allowances is open; the Supreme Court ruled for Zimplats on royalties for June 2018 to December 2021, and ZIMRA has appealed [6].
  - Cash reaches ASX holders in two steps: ZPM in Zimbabwe pays dividends to the Guernsey holding company, which pays its shareholders. In FY26 the holding company booked US$9.0m of dividend income (nil in FY25) but was owed US$61.2m of dividends by ZPM (US$53.6m a year before) and held US$11.7m of cash [1]. Of anything it pays out, 87% goes to Implats.

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance: no production, cost or dividend guidance is published; the FY2027 objectives are to "achieve planned FY2027 production volumes" and cost performance [1]. Projects: Mupani to full 3.6Mt a year by FY2029 (US$368m spent of US$386m at September 2026); smelter expansion US$480m of US$544m; 45MW solar phase commissioning in the December 2026 quarter; base metals refinery US$36m of US$190m spent by June 2026 [6][8].

Changes this year: none to guidance, as none is given.

Record: against its own FY2026 plan, ore mined and milled were above plan, 6E production below plan and cash cost per ounce 17% above plan [1]. FY2025: the same style of internal plan; no numeric public guidance to test.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: with the US$1.8bn investment programme nearly finished, will Zimplats start paying out its cash, or will capital spending, the growing amount owed by its parent, Zimbabwe's currency rules and lower PGM prices keep the cash away from the 13% of shareholders on the ASX? There has been no dividend since September 2023, even after a US$276.7m profit.
- If it goes our way: dividends resume and the basket holds at about US$2,000 per 6E ounce (between the FY26 average of US$2,171 and the June 2026 closing prices). On FY26 volumes and costs (cost of sales less royalty and export commission, US$784.4m; royalty and commission 5.9% of revenue; about US$21.6m of other costs) and the FY26 tax rate of 28.6%, profit is US$228.7m, US$2.12 a share; at 6 times earnings (a stated assumption) US$12.75, or A$18.05. Cross-checks: free cash after US$150m of capital spending would be about US$1.99 a share; net tangible assets are A$27.70 a share.
- If it goes against us: no dividend, the basket falls to US$1,800 and costs rise 5%. Profit US$120.2m, US$1.12 a share; at 4 times US$4.47, or A$6.33.
- Price: A$14.19 (last trade in the ASX company data fetched 7 Nov 2026; Norgate close 30 Oct 2026 A$12.67), market value A$1,527m, about 3.9 times FY26 earnings and 0.51 times net tangible assets. Gap if it goes our way: 18.05 / 14.19 - 1 = +27%. Gap if it goes against us: 6.33 / 14.19 - 1 = -55%. At the 30 October close the gaps were +42% and -50%.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is +27% at the latest price: above 25% but below the 40% that an A needs. The answer needs the event itself: the dividend is the board's call (and so Implats'), and the half-year result about 26 Feb 2027 is the next time it is made. The deep dive should read Implats' own filings for its plans for Zimplats' cash and the intercompany receivable, and should get current PGM prices, which drive both cases. Not a value pick: the value if it goes against us is 55% below the price.
