## 1. Kill check

- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. EY's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified, with no emphasis of matter [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. NZX's own daily trading data: median on-market value over the 20 sessions 9 October to 6 November 2026 was NZ$30,324 a day, lowest NZ$4,067; 17 of the 20 sessions above NZ$12,500 [11]. At 10% of the median for two sessions the largest position would be about NZ$6,000, above the NZ$2,500 smallest order.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No live one. The review announced on 28 April 2026, with Cameron Partners market-testing interest in an ownership transaction in New Zealand and overseas, ended on 4 November 2026 with no transaction: the company stays listed on a standalone plan [4][5]. The largest holder is Long Term Food Group LP with Waterman Capital at 17.2% [1][8]. Noted for the watch list in the report, not a kill.

There is no gap between statutory and underlying profit to explain: My Food Bag reports statutory profit as its headline and adds no one-off adjustments.

What the headline numbers hide:
- Book value is almost all goodwill and brands from the 2021 listing structure: $82.0m of the $72.4m of equity, so tangible assets are negative [1].
- The five-year profit record is lifted by one year: $20.0m in FY22 (a year of COVID-19 lockdowns), then $7.9m, $6.0m, $6.35m and $6.69m [1][6]. The five-year average is $9.4m; without FY22 it is $6.7m.
- The board's impairment test assumes revenue growth of 6.1% a year from FY28 and an EBITDA margin of 12.0% from FY32, against 9.6% in FY26; the discount rate is 11.7% [1].
- Customer numbers are flat: 57.1k active customers at March 2026 against 57.0k a year earlier; growth came from order frequency and basket size [2].
- Costs: in May 2026 the company said fuel disruption had raised distribution costs and ingredient cost pressures were emerging [2].
- Staff equity: 7,469,953 performance rights on issue after the June 2026 vesting (2.9% of shares); the chief executive was granted 2,075,829 more on 8 July 2026 [9][10].

## 3. Guidance and record
- FY25: on 1 April 2025, earnings "broadly in line with the prior year" ($6.0m); delivered $6.35m, up 5% [6][7].
- FY26: on 17 March 2026, profit after tax of $6.4m to $6.8m; delivered $6.69m [7][2].
- FY27: no earnings guidance. On 21 May 2026, revenue up 5.1% in the first seven weeks, with fuel and ingredient cost pressure [2]. On 4 November 2026, a new dividend policy of 70% to 90% of profit after tax, fully imputed, and an evaluation of a return of surplus capital "next year", with no decision made [5].
- Record: earnings guidance met in each of the last two years.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is the $6m to $7m of profit earned in each of FY24 to FY26 the business's level, or can it climb back toward the five-year average of $9.4m, which the board's own impairment test implies by assuming margins rise from 9.6% to 12%? A market test this year found no buyer willing to do a deal, which is evidence on what outside owners will pay [5].
- If it goes our way: profit back to its five-year average, $9.39m, at 11.1 times, the median trailing price to earnings ratio of eight profitable NZX consumer and food companies on the NZX instrument pages (Briscoe 16.4, Warehouse 20.4, Hallenstein Glasson 14.9, Delegat 10.7, Sanford 8.9, Scales 11.5, Comvita 7.7, Seeka 5.6) [15]: $104.0m, less $1.9m of net debt, $102.1m, or 40.1c a share on 254.5m shares.
- If it goes against us: profit at FY24's $6.0m, the lowest of the last four years, at the same 11.1 times: $66.5m, less $1.9m, $64.6m, or 25.4c a share.
- Only now the price: the close on 6 November 2026 was $0.32 (bid $0.31, offer $0.32) [15]. Gap if it goes our way, 0.401 / 0.32 less 1 = +25.4%. Gap if it goes against us, 0.254 / 0.32 less 1 = -20.7%.
- For scale: at $0.32 the company is valued at $81.5m, 12.2 times FY26 profit (close to the peer median, so not cheap on current earnings), 5.7 times EBITDA including leases, with a 5.9% fully imputed cash yield. The four-year average profit without FY22 ($6.7m) at 11.1 times gives 28.6c, 11% below the price. The "our way" gap depends on the lockdown year.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is 25%, above the 25% line but below 40%, and it rests on counting FY22's lockdown profit in the average. The evidence that would settle it is the profit trend over the next results (the first on 19 November 2026) and what the board does with surplus capital in 2027. On current earnings the company trades at about the peer multiple, and the failed market test points the same way.
