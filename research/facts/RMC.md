# Resimac Group (ASX:RMC): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #29 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Resimac is a non-bank lender: prime and non-conforming home loans (A$14.7bn) and asset finance (A$1.8bn) in Australia
and New Zealand, A$16.5bn under management at 30 June 2026. All figures are A$. Financial year ends 30 June.

A note on the ranking's numbers: the "net cash" of minus A$50.72 a share and the "heavy" balance sheet come from the ASX
company data counting the A$16.7bn of warehouse and bond funding as company debt. That funding sits in special purpose
trusts secured on the loans, with no recourse to the rest of the group (FY26 financial report note 13.1). As the brief
asks, Resimac is valued here on book value and return on equity against listed peers, Pepper Money (ASX:PPM) and Liberty
Financial Group (ASX:LFG).

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An operating lender.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Deloitte's opinion on the FY26 accounts is
  unmodified (25 Aug 2026).
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$92,276 (volume times close,
  data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz; lowest session A$22,668). Two sessions at 10% of that median is about A$18,500.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv lists no
  companies yet, and Resimac is a lender.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? There is a holder over 50%, and I
  judge its moves are not the kind this test means, but the facts are set out so the desk can disagree. Somers Limited
  and UIL Limited (Bermuda companies associated with director Duncan Saville's ICM group) hold 254.7m shares, 64.40%.
  In the last 12 months: on 4 Nov 2025 Somers sold 62,183,452 shares off-market to UIL for A$67.5m (A$1.085, the
  3 Nov 2025 close), a move between associates that didn't change the group's 64.40% (notice dated 7 Nov 2025); in FY26
  Resimac lent A$25m to Somers and A$15m to UIL short term "on market terms", both repaid by 30 June 2026 (FY26
  remuneration report 9.1.7.1); and the group took 64% of A$65.3m of dividends paid in FY26, including a 9c special.
  No offer, review or sale process has been announced. Noted for the special situations watch list in the report back.

Nothing ends the sheet.

Return on equity: 13.4% statutory, 13.6% normalised in FY26 (company); by half, 15.5% in 1H26 and 10.6% in 2H26.
FY25 8.9%, FY24 8.3%, FY23 16.4%, FY22 29.9% (FY26 remuneration report table).

Does the gap between statutory and underlying profit recur? Yes. "Professional fees and restructuring" appear in both
years (A$4.8m, then A$2.4m) and the ASIC case behind part of them is still in court. Derivative revaluations come and go
every year. The gap is small, so statutory profit is the base used here.

What the headline numbers hide:
- The second half fell hard. Normalised profit was A$29.5m in 1H26 and A$20.4m in 2H26; costs rose (cost to income
  50.0% to 56.3%) and loan losses rose (A$9.7m to A$11.7m). The full-year return of 13.6% is the average of 15.5% and
  10.6%.
- The 23.6% "yield" in the ranking is two years of special dividends (12c in June 2025, 9c in March 2026) on top of
  ordinary dividends: FY26 paid A$75.2m against A$49.2m of profit. Specials returned surplus capital; they are not a
  steady income. A$103.3m of franking credits remain, which would let Resimac pay more fully franked specials, but
  franking credits are of no use to a New Zealand holder.
- The controlling holder borrowed A$40m from the company during FY26 (repaid), and Resimac buys its directors' and
  officers' insurance (A$1.25m a year) from General Provincial Insurance, a company related to a director.
- ASIC has civil penalty proceedings in the Federal Court (May 2025) over how Resimac handled hardship notices from
  1 Jan 2022 to 15 Feb 2024. No provision or contingent liability amount is booked.
- The CFO, James Spurway, resigned on 24 Apr 2026 and left on 23 Jul 2026 (annual report p45); no replacement had been
  announced by 8 Nov 2026.
- Concentration: almost all Australian home loans and car and equipment finance, broker-sourced, priced off wholesale
  funding markets.

## 3. Guidance and record
Resimac does not give profit guidance. The FY26 result (26 Aug 2026) says it enters FY27 "with a stronger earnings base"
and lists aims for home loan growth and "higher risk-adjusted returns" in asset finance, with no number. Nothing changed
during 2026. With no numeric guidance in FY25 or FY26, there is no record to test.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is 2H26's 10.6% return on equity (after rising costs and loan losses) the run rate, or was 1H26's 15.5%
  closer to it? Book value per share hardly moves because surplus profit goes out as specials, so the return earned on
  that book sets the value.
- How the value is worked: for a lender, price to book should rise with return on equity. The peers, on their own
  latest filings and the 30 Oct 2026 closes (price file):
  - Pepper Money: equity A$886.9m over 449.38m shares is A$1.974 a share; price A$1.745, 0.88 times book; statutory
    return on equity 11.5% (A$50.4m first-half 2026 profit, annualised, over average equity of A$878.4m).
  - Liberty Financial: equity A$1,179.1m over 303.6m securities is A$3.884; price A$3.37, 0.87 times book; statutory
    return on equity 12.1% (A$143.8m over average equity of A$1,187.8m).
  - Average: the market pays 0.0744 times book for each point of return on equity.
  Resimac's book value is A$0.930 a share.
- If it goes our way: return on equity holds at FY26's 13.6%. Value: 0.930 x 0.0744 x 13.6 = A$0.94 a share (1.01 times
  book).
- If it goes against us: 2H26's 10.6% is the run rate. Value: 0.930 x 0.0744 x 10.6 = A$0.73 a share (0.79 times book).
- Only now the price: A$0.83 (last trade before 8 Nov 2026, ASX company data; A$0.805 at the 30 Oct close). Gap if it
  goes our way: 0.94 / 0.83 - 1 = +13%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.73 / 0.83 - 1 = -12%. At A$0.83 Resimac trades at
  0.89 times book, in line with its two peers.

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive. The gap if it goes our way is 13%, under 25%. Priced against Pepper Money and Liberty for the return it
earns, Resimac is about where they are; the high yield is returned capital. The 1H27 result (about 25 Feb 2027) shows the
return on equity if the desk wants to look again, and the controlling holder is noted for the special situations watch
list.
