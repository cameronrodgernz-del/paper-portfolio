# Southern Cross Media Group, now Seven Media Group (ASX:SXL, becoming ASX:SMG on 10 Nov 2026): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 3 points, #113 of 195 ASX names in reports/research_rank.csv (2026-11-08), outside the top 40; written now that gaming, wagering and racing names are open.
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Since the merger with Seven West Media (scheme implemented 7 Jan 2026), it owns the Seven Network and 7plus (television), the Hit and Triple M networks and LiSTNR (audio), and The West Australian, The Nightly and PerthNow (publishing) [1][2][3]. Holders approved the new name, Seven Media Group, at the AGM on 5 Nov 2026; the ASX code changes from SXL to SMG on 10 Nov 2026 [4].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. FY26 accounts audited by PricewaterhouseCoopers; no going-concern uncertainty is reported [1][5].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$55,038 a day (lowest day A$1,827), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz [24].
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries; Cam confirmed on 8 Nov 2026 that there is nothing to avoid at the outset.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No holder is near 50%, but the largest is moving. SGH and its associates (Kerry Stokes's Australian Capital Equity entities, and Wroxby Pty Ltd, an associate outside the SGH group) went from 20.20% to 23.19% when Wroxby bought 14,315,496 shares on market at A$0.56 on 17 Aug 2026, which uses the 3% that a holder above 19% may add in any six months without a bid [6][7]. SGH's chief executive, Ryan Stokes, became chair on 7 Sep 2026 [8]. Not a holder over 50%, so the sheet continues; noted for the special situations watch list in the report back. The Seven West Media scheme itself was completed in January 2026 [3].

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs. Significant items were left out in both pro forma years (A$49.0m between reported and pre-significant NPAT in FY25, A$32.9m after tax in FY26), and restructuring has appeared in each half reported: A$4.1m after tax in 1H FY25, A$2.5m in 1H FY26, and about A$20m more in the June 2026 cost program [9][12]. The non-cash onerous contract release will flatter reported EBITDA again: a new A$65m to A$70m provision raised in the merger accounts is expected to add about A$30m to reported EBITDA in FY27 [12].

What the headline numbers hide:
- Leases: A$289.9m of lease liabilities sit outside the A$362.8m net debt, and EBITDA is before A$38.3m a year of lease payments [1][11].
- Television is two-thirds of revenue and fell hardest: TV EBITDA (before onerous releases) fell 32.3% to A$109.6m as the TV advertising market shrank 9.9%; audio EBITDA rose 15.5% to A$100.4m [2].
- FY26 costs included A$15m of relief from the Commercial Broadcasting Tax, which the government has suspended [2][11].
- Google's payments for news have stopped, which cut 1Q FY27 revenue in television and publishing [13].
- Book value is all intangibles; net tangible assets are negative.

Licences, regulators and tax changes:
- Licences: commercial TV and radio broadcasting licences under the Broadcasting Services Act, renewable every five years for a small fee; the company treats them as having indefinite lives and knows of no licence ever being revoked in Australia [1]. Regulator: the Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA). No open ACMA matter against the company is disclosed.
- Wagering advertising: the Interactive Gambling Amendment (Gambling Reform) Act 2026 starts on 1 Jan 2027. It bans wagering ads during live sport (from 15 minutes before to 5 minutes after, 5am to 8.30pm), allows no more than three wagering ads an hour on TV from 5am to 8.30pm, bans them in radio's school drop-off and pick-up times, and bans celebrity endorsements and odds promotion on broadcasts [14][15]. Seven carries the AFL and other sport. The company's impairment tests allowed for "wagering reform"; it has not put a number on it [1].
- News Bargaining Incentive: the legislation has passed, and the company will "seek to recover our full share from the pool"; the amount and timing are not disclosed [13].
- Commercial Broadcasting Tax: suspended; the impairment test assumes a possible permanent suspension [1][11].

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance (FY27): no profit figure. Total operating costs to grow below inflation, with one-off costs for the Commonwealth Games and the Rugby League World Cup; the expanded cost program targets A$145m to A$150m of annual run-rate savings (including A$30m of merger savings already delivered) "offsetting future cost inflation and funding targeted growth investments" [2][12]. At the AGM on 5 Nov 2026: 1Q FY27 revenue was lower than a year earlier, the advertising market is "short and volatile", costs are down on the previous year despite the sporting events, and the cost base has been set "on the assumption the broader economy deteriorates in the second half" [13].

Changes during FY26:
- 26 Aug 2025 to 16 Oct 2025 (SCA alone): FY26 EBITDA A$78m to A$83m for the audio business, reaffirmed [16].
- 24 Feb 2026 (first merged guidance): pro forma revenue A$1.91bn to A$1.92bn, costs about A$1.70bn, EBITDA A$200m to A$220m (FY25: A$233m), audio EBITDA A$78m to A$83m [10].
- 11 Jun 2026: revenue cut to A$1,860m to A$1,870m and underlying EBITDA to A$185m to A$190m; the cost program and a A$20m restructuring charge announced [12].

Record:
- FY26: revenue A$1,869.6m (within the June range, below February's); EBITDA A$191.9m before onerous releases, above the June range but below the A$200m to A$220m given in February [2]. Audio EBITDA was reported as A$100.4m on the merged group's new segment basis, which moves costs into Corporate, so it can't be compared with the A$78m to A$83m.
- FY25 (SCA alone, before the merger): not checked against guidance.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is FY26 the low point? Will the A$145m to A$150m cost program hold EBITDA (before the non-cash onerous releases) at FY26's A$191.9m in FY27, while TV advertising keeps shrinking, Google's news payments have stopped and wagering ads are limited from 1 January 2027? The half-year result in February 2027 will show most of the answer.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: listed media companies are priced on enterprise value to EBITDA after lease payments. The named peer is Nine Entertainment (ASX:NEC), the other merged TV, publishing and digital group: 1,584.1m shares (FY26 weighted average) at A$0.67 (30 Oct 2026 close) is A$1,061.4m, plus net debt of A$657.9m at 30 Jun 2026, is A$1,719.3m, over EBITDA after cash lease payments of about A$387.0m (the desk's arithmetic: net debt divided by Nine's stated net leverage of 1.7 times) = 4.44 times [21]. Nine's mix (Stan, outdoor advertising, digital mastheads) is better than a TV-heavy group's, and the 1.7 times is rounded (1.65 to 1.75 times gives 4.31 to 4.57 times). The price would get there through half-year results that show the cost program working and debt falling, and the return of dividends.
- If it goes our way: FY27 EBITDA holds at A$191.9m; less A$38.3m of lease payments, A$153.6m; at 4.44 times, A$682.4m; less A$362.8m net debt, A$319.6m, or A$0.667 a share. If the program got EBITDA back to FY25's A$227.6m (A$184.5m after leases), A$0.954 a share; not used, as nothing in the guidance points to it.
- If it goes against us: EBITDA after leases falls again at FY26's pace (down 16.7%, from A$184.5m to A$153.6m), to A$127.9m; at 4.44 times, A$568.3m; less A$362.8m, A$205.5m, or A$0.429 a share. If television alone repeats its 32.3% fall, EBITDA after leases is A$118.2m and the value A$0.339.
- Only now the price: A$0.48, the last trade on 6 Nov 2026 (ASX company data; A$0.50 at the 30 Oct close used in the ranking), a market value of A$229.8m and an enterprise value of 3.86 times FY26 EBITDA after leases. Gap if it goes our way: 0.667 / 0.48 - 1 = +39%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.429 / 0.48 - 1 = -11% (-29% if TV repeats its fall). With the onerous release counted, as in the company's reported EBITDA, the our-way gap would be +55%, but that release is not cash.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is +39%, just short of the 40% an A needs, and the answer needs the 1H FY27 result itself (about 11 Feb 2027). Start the deep dive in late January 2027. Not a value pick, narrowly on both tests: the against value is 11% below the price (the limit is 10%) and the our-way gap is under 40%. The grade depends heavily on Nine's multiple: at 4.31 times the our-way gap is +30%, at 4.57 times +48%. The SGH group's creep (23.19%, chair's seat, room for 3% more from about February 2027) is a separate reason to watch it.
