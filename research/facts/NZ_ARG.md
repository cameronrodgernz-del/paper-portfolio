# Argosy Property (NZX:ARG): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #1 in the NZX table of reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01). The only property company in this batch.
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An internally managed company that owns and leases 49 industrial, office and large format retail buildings, $2,243m at valuation [1]. It is a listed property company (a portfolio investment entity), not a fund with an outside manager.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Deloitte's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 28 September to 23 October 2026 was NZ$481,596 a day, lowest day NZ$193,670 (close times volume, from Investing.com's historical data page, a secondary source [14]).
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest substantial holder, ACC, has 9.26% [9]. The non-core property sales are asset sales, not a sale of the company.

The gap between statutory profit and net distributable income recurs. There is a revaluation every half year, a gain in each of the last three years ($72.7m, $58.5m), and swap values move every year. Neither is cash.

Property values at 31 March 2026 by sector, with the valuers' average market yields: industrial $1,234.2m at 6.25%, office $789.7m at 7.05%, large format retail $219.2m at 6.94%; occupancy 94.6% by rent, average lease term 5.0 years [1]. Office occupancy by area was 88.1%. Since then occupancy has risen to 97.3% (31 July 2026) on a 16-year, $3.8m a year industrial lease from 1 March 2027 [11]. Two properties sold in FY26 for $46m, above their previous book values (a $4.4m gain on marking them to the sale prices); six more, $139m at book, are marked non-core and are to be sold over the medium term [2].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: are Argosy's book values (average market yield 6.62%, office at 7.05%) what the buildings would sell for? The price implies values about 22% lower. The evidence that settles it is sale prices against book: two sales in FY26 came in above book, and six non-core properties ($139m) are to be sold.
- If it goes our way: property at its stated value less debt, the reported NTA of $1.60 a share. Net of the yearly administration cost of $11.4m, capitalised at the 6.62% market yield ($172m, 19.7c a share), it is $1.40.
- If it goes against us: office values 25% lower ($197m), industrial yields 0.5 points higher (6.25% to 6.75%, 7.4% lower, $91m) and large format retail 7% lower ($15m), $304m in all (13.6% of the portfolio). Equity of $1,397.6m less $304m, less the $172m of capitalised administration cost, is $921m, or $1.05 a share.
- Only now the price: the close on 30 October 2026 was $1.04 [13]. Gap if it goes our way, 1.60 / 1.04 less 1 = +53.8% (+34.8% net of administration cost). Gap if it goes against us, 1.054 / 1.04 less 1 = +1.3%.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is 54%, but the answer needs sale prices against book on the non-core properties, which the company expects over the medium term, not on a date inside six months. The 18 November interim revaluation is the valuers' own view and can't by itself show the price is wrong. Bring it forward when a non-core sale is announced.
