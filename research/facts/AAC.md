# Australian Agricultural Company (ASX:AAC): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 4 points, #31 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

AACo is Australia's largest integrated cattle company: 21 pastoral properties in Queensland and the Northern Territory
carried at A$1.74bn, a herd valued at A$774m, feedlots, and branded Wagyu beef sold around the world (beef sales A$314m
and live cattle sales A$108m in FY26). All figures are A$. Financial year ends 31 March; the latest half is to
30 September 2025, and the half to 30 September 2026 is due about 19 to 20 November 2026.

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An operating cattle and beef company.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. KPMG's opinion on the FY26 accounts is
  unmodified (annual report p133).
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$47,594 (volume times close,
  data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz; lowest session A$12,005). Two sessions at 10% of that median is about A$9,500.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv lists no
  companies yet, and AACo is a cattle company.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No moves, but a holder over 50%.
  Bryan Glinton as trustee of The AA Trust holds 319,999,220 shares, 53.1%; Tattarang (Andrew and Nicola Forrest) holds
  134,891,347, 22.4% (annual report p152). Neither has lodged a substantial holding notice in the last 12 months.
  Tattarang last bought 6,055,726 shares on market between 3 Sep 2024 and 19 Feb 2025 for A$8.38m (A$1.38). Noted in
  the report back as a register to watch.

Nothing ends the sheet.

Does the gap between statutory and underlying profit recur? Yes, every year, and in both directions: the herd is
revalued to market each year. The mark-to-market was +128.6m in FY26, -4.7m in FY25, and drove a 94.6m statutory loss in
FY24. Operating profit, which leaves it out, is the better guide to what the business earns; FY26 also carried 9.0m of
flood costs the company calls one-off.

What the headline numbers hide:
- The 7.5 times earnings in the ranking rests on the herd revaluation. Without it, operating profit of 71.6m less
  depreciation (31.4m) and interest (27.1m) leaves about 13m before tax: about 1% of the 1.76bn of net tangible assets.
  Core free cash flow was 0.8m in FY26 and -10.6m in FY25.
- Net tangible assets are mostly revaluations. Pastoral property is carried at 1,741.5m on independent LAWD valuations
  at 31 March 2026, mostly on a dollars-per-adult-equivalent basis (average A$3,624, up from A$3,207 a year earlier);
  property values rose 153.0m in FY26. The herd is carried at market value (774.1m). Both move with cattle and land
  prices.
- Seasonal risk: the 2025/26 north Queensland floods killed about 7,000 head (about A$13m) across three properties.
- Concentration: beef prices and the A$/US$ rate drive revenue; the company named Middle East fuel and freight costs as
  a likely FY27 headwind (20 May 2026).
- Free float: the two largest holders own 75.5%, and the shares trade about A$50,000 a day.

## 3. Guidance and record
AACo gives no profit guidance. FY27 outlook (20 May 2026): a "dynamic operating environment", with the Middle East
conflict raising energy, transport and production costs. At the AGM (22 Jul 2026) the board said it "regularly
consider[s] a range of balance sheet and capital management options, including dividends and share buy-backs". No
changes during 2026. With no numeric guidance in FY25 or FY26, there is no record to test.

## 4. Who owns it and who is moving
Substantial holders at 29 May 2026 (annual report p152): The AA Trust (Bryan Glinton as trustee) 53.1%; Tattarang
(Peepingee Trust, Andrew and Nicola Forrest) 22.4%. The register's top two are custodians (HSBC Custody Nominees 56.15%,
Citicorp Nominees 27.76%); 6,653 holders.

Substantial holder changes in the last 12 months: none lodged. The last was Tattarang's on 19 Feb 2025 (21.37% to
22.38%).

Directors' on-market buys or sells in the last 12 months: none found. The notices in that time (David Harris, 4 Dec
2025, 2 Sep 2026 and 16 Oct 2026; Zoe Kenneally's initial notice, 17 Dec 2025) are grants, vesting and lapses of
performance rights.

Capital raisings in the last two years: none.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: the market has never paid AACo's net tangible assets in the last decade, and the discount is the widest
  it has been. Is that because the land and herd values can't be turned into cash for minority holders (two holders
  own 75%, no dividends, about 1% earned on the assets), or will the company or its holders act on it (buy-backs, land
  sales at book value, an offer)? A deep dive would test the valuations against actual property sales and what the
  board has done with the gap before.
- What the market has paid. Price at 31 March (price file) over net tangible assets per share at 31 March (each year's
  results):

  | 31 March | Price | NTA | Price / NTA |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 2015 | 1.605 | 1.43 | 1.12 |
  | 2016 | 1.30 | 1.66 | 0.78 |
  | 2017 | 1.66 | 1.82 | 0.91 |
  | 2018 | 1.185 | 1.62 | 0.73 |
  | 2019 | 1.015 | 1.42 | 0.71 |
  | 2020 | 1.095 | 1.53 | 0.72 |
  | 2021 | 1.20 | 1.75 | 0.69 |
  | 2022 | 1.63 | 2.27 | 0.72 |
  | 2023 | 1.54 | 2.59 | 0.59 |
  | 2024 | 1.36 | 2.51 | 0.54 |
  | 2025 | 1.45 | 2.55 | 0.57 |
  | 2026 | 1.35 | 2.92 | 0.46 |

  Ten years to March 2026: average 0.66, range 0.46 to 0.91. The share price has stayed between about A$1.00 and A$1.66
  while net tangible assets doubled.
- If it goes our way: the market goes back to its ten-year average discount on today's net tangible assets: 2.92 x 0.665
  = A$1.94 a share.
- If it goes against us: cattle and land values give back FY26's gains, so net tangible assets fall to FY25's 2.55, and
  the market keeps paying its lowest ratio (0.46): 2.55 x 0.462 = A$1.18 a share. If nothing happens (net tangible
  assets hold, discount stays at 0.46), the value is 2.92 x 0.462 = A$1.35.
- Only now the price: A$1.325 (last trade before 8 Nov 2026, ASX company data; A$1.33 at the 30 Oct close), 0.45 times
  net tangible assets. Gap if it goes our way: 1.94 / 1.325 - 1 = +46%. Gap if it goes against us: 1.18 / 1.325 - 1 =
  -11% (nothing happens: +2%).

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is 46%, but the question is one of price, not of
results: nothing dated forces the discount to close. The 1H27 result (about 19 to 20 November 2026, under two weeks away) shows
whether net tangible assets held and whether the board has used the buy-back or dividend options it mentioned in July,
so the deep dive should be done around that result. As a value pick it narrowly fails: the "against" value is 11% below
the price, against the 10% limit (on the "nothing happens" case it would pass). The desk may want to decide which bad
case applies.
