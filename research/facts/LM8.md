# Lunnon Metals (ASX:LM8): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 6 points, #18 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Lunnon owns about 47km2 of mining leases at Kambalda, Western Australia, inside Gold Fields' St Ives gold camp: the
Foster-Baker area (gold and nickel rights) and nickel-only rights at Silver Lake and Fisher [1]. Its first gold mine, the
small Lady Herial open pit, ran from February to 8 August 2026 and sold its ore to Gold Fields' St Ives Gold Mining Co.
(SIGM) under an ore purchase agreement that gave Lunnon 70% of the free cash flow [2][3]. That pit is finished and the
company says "no further revenue is forecast" [2]. Financial year ends 30 June. All figures A$.

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No, but only just, and on the brief's own test: a
  defined resource with a study. The producing business has ended (Lady Herial mined out, "the Company has no remaining
  reported gold Ore Reserves") [2]. What is left to value is A$43.0m of cash, exploration ground, gold resources of
  73,200oz with a gold Scoping Study in preparation but not yet done [2][4][5], and the Baker and Foster nickel sulphide
  resources (over 3Mt at 3.0% Ni for more than 95,000t of nickel), which are the subject of a July 2025 Scoping Study [6][7].
  The sheet values the company on that nickel study, as the brief directs, at the study's nickel price of A$23,000/t
  (US$14,950/t at A$1 = US$0.65) [6]. The company itself says the study "is insufficient to provide assurance of an
  economic development case" because there is no agreement to process the ore [6].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Armada Audit & Assurance gave an
  unmodified opinion on the FY26 accounts, with exploration and evaluation assets as the key audit matter and no
  going-concern paragraph (annual report pages 129 to 135, read by text recognition) [1].
- Too thin to trade? No, but close. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$13,620 a day,
  counting two sessions with no trades as zero (A$16,836 on the 18 days that traded), from
  data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz. Two sessions at 10% of that median is about A$2,720, just above a 2.5%
  position (about A$2,250).
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv lists no companies.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is Gold
  Fields (through SIGM) at 29.61% [8]. Two things to watch, neither a trigger: Gold Fields has agreed to buy BHP's Kambalda
  Nickel Concentrator next door, the only nearby route for Lunnon's nickel (completion expected in calendar 2027) [7]; and
  SIGM holds a pre-emptive right over gold material from most of Lunnon's ground [7]. Gold Fields' stake has only been
  diluted (30.75% to 29.61%, 24 Aug 2026) [8].

The statutory result has no recurring gap to explain: FY25 carried a one-off impairment, FY26 a single pit's profit. That
is the point. The profit does not recur.

What the headline numbers hide:
- "7.5 times earnings" is one open pit that has now closed. Lady Herial produced 303,437t at 1.68 g/t for 14,910oz sold;
  Lunnon's 70% share of the pre-tax free cash flow was A$39.0m [2]. The September 2026 quarter carried the last ore
  (5,821oz) and the first half of FY27 will show one more profit from it before revenue stops [2].
- "Net cash about 36% of market value" is now larger (A$43.0m at 30 Sep) but the board has committed A$13.0m to
  exploration in FY27, the first year of a two-year program, and spends about A$1.3m a quarter on staff and
  administration [12][2].
- Tax losses: A$65.3m of unused losses not recognised at 30 June 2026, which is why FY26 showed no tax [1].
- Reserves and life: no gold or nickel Ore Reserves. Gold resources are Lady Herial's remaining 37,000oz (589,600t at
  2.0 g/t) and Hustler's 36,200oz (826,000t at 1.4 g/t) [2][4]. The company says updated estimates for both are "on
  schedule ... to feed into Scoping Study" for a Lady Herial cut-back and a first Hustler pit [5]. No date.
- Nickel: the July 2025 Scoping Study (plus or minus 30%) gave Baker a pre-tax NPV at 8% of about A$53m and pre-tax free
  cash flow of A$71m over 4.2 years, and Foster A$4m and A$30m, at A$23,000/t nickel, with a start date of 1 Jan 2027 [6].
  At 20% above that price the combined NPV is A$168m [6]. Everything rests on the ore being processed at the Kambalda
  concentrator, which has been on care and maintenance since 2024 and for which there is no agreement [6]. Gold Fields
  says it will evaluate "the concentrator's long-term use for both gold and nickel processing" [2].
- Nickel price today: about US$15,300/t in mid-October 2026 (Trading Economics, a secondary source) [S1], close to the
  study's US$14,950/t. The study's A$ figure assumes A$1 = US$0.65; this desk has no primary source for today's rate.

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance: none on production or earnings. FY27 exploration budget A$13.0m and over 43km of drilling (29 Jun
2026) [12]; 9.9km drilled in the September quarter [2].

Changes this year:
- 16 Jan 2026: Lady Herial Feasibility Study, Lunnon's 70% share of pre-tax free cash flow A$40.4m at A$6,250/oz [3].
- 29 Jun 2026: cash at 30 June estimated at about A$21.0m; FY27 exploration budget set [12]. Actual was A$22.1m [1].
- 7 Sep 2026 and 28 Oct 2026: final reconciliation, A$39.0m against A$40.4m (minus 3.5%); on the study's basis, before
  A$2.24m of grade control and test work costs, A$40.6m [2].

Record: the June 2025 Scoping Study and January 2026 Feasibility Study for Lady Herial were delivered closely (gold sold
100.7% of the reserve, revenue 0.8% above, operating costs 5.1% above, mining finished ahead of schedule) [2]. The nickel
study has never been tested. The promised gold Scoping Study is "taking slightly longer than expected" because drilling
had to wait for the pit to close [2].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: will Lunnon get a way to process its Baker nickel once Gold Fields owns the Kambalda concentrator, and
  will the gold Scoping Study find a second pit on Lady Herial-like terms? Without either, the company is cash plus an
  exploration budget.
- If it goes our way: Baker and Foster are developed on the Scoping Study at A$23,000/t nickel, one year later than the
  study's 1 Jan 2027 start (both NPVs divided by 1.08). Pre-tax values are used because A$65.3m of tax losses would
  shelter most of the tax. Cash A$43.0m, less two years of staff and administration costs at the September quarter's
  A$1.32m a quarter (A$10.6m), plus Baker A$49.1m and Foster A$3.7m: A$85.2m, or 37.5c a share on 227.4m shares (35.4c
  counting the 13.4m rights and options). No value is given to the gold resources, which have no study yet.
- If it goes against us: no processing route and no second pit. Cash A$43.0m less two years of staff and
  administration costs: A$32.4m, or 14.3c a share. This leaves the exploration budget's spending out, treating the
  drilling as worth what it costs.
- Only now the price: A$0.33 (Norgate close 30 Oct 2026, the ranking's price); the ASX's last trade on 6 Nov was A$0.30.
  Gap if it goes our way: 0.375 / 0.33 - 1 = +13.5% (+24.9% at A$0.30). Gap if it goes against us: 0.143 / 0.33 - 1 =
  -57% (-52% at A$0.30). At a nickel price 20% above the study's, the nickel value would be A$155.6m and the company
  A$188.0m, 82.7c a share (+150%), so the answer turns on the nickel price as much as on a processing deal.

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive. The gap if it goes our way is under 25% (+13.5% at the ranking's price, +24.9% at the last trade) when
the company is valued, as the brief directs, on its nickel Scoping Study at the study's nickel price, which is close to
today's. Two things would reopen it: a gold Scoping Study showing a second pit (Lady Herial returned A$39.0m on 14,910oz
sold, about A$2,600 an ounce to Lunnon), or a processing agreement for Baker together with a higher nickel price. Either
would be a dated filing worth re-grading on.
