# Promisia Healthcare (NZX:PHL): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #2 in the NZX table of reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01). The ranking's "2.7 times earnings" is on statutory profit, which is mostly a property revaluation (section 2).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An aged care and retirement village operator: five sites in Feilding, Christchurch and Cromwell (401 care beds, 76 care suites and apartments, 156 villas), plus Chatswood in Christchurch, bought on 1 October 2026 [1][7][8].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. William Buck's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified, on a going-concern basis [1].
- Too thin to trade? Not measured. I could not get 20 sessions of daily volume (see the last section). Investing.com shows a three-month average of 46,550 shares a day (about NZ$31,000 at 67c) but only 821 shares on 30 October [15]; 6 of the last 20 sessions in our price feed traded at a single price; and Gould's on-market buying in early October was 2,044,205 shares, the same number as one registered holder's whole holding at 31 March, so probably one block [10][1]. The average is lifted by blocks; the median may be below NZ$12,500. Needs measuring before any deep dive.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No by the letter, but close. The Brankin Family Interest Trust (director Tom Brankin) last disclosed 52.94% on an older share count; it was 41.57% when, on 5 October 2026, it agreed to sell 7 million shares to Gould Holdings at 70c, completing 1 April 2027, which takes it to 28.66% [9][10]. Gould now has a relevant interest in 16.69% of the shares and 21.48% of the warrants [10]. No bid or review is announced. Worth a line on the special situations watch list as a holder build-up.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs: there has been a large non-cash gain in each of the last two years (a revaluation of $11.7m in FY26; a bargain purchase of $6.6m in FY25), and revaluations happen every year. On underlying profit the shares are not cheap: about 28 times the $1.3m.

The business is small next to its balance sheet: $197m of property (investment property $170.1m, property and equipment $27.0m) against $88.5m of resident occupation right liabilities and $38.6m of debt, so a 10% change in property values moves NTA by about 35% [1].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is Promisia worth its book value or what its earnings support? Book NTA says one thing; the multiple Promisia itself paid for Chatswood (about 9 to 11 times its expected EBITDAF) says another. The half-year result in November shows the earnings run rate with Chatswood and the updated guidance.
- If it goes our way: the company's NTA with all warrants exercised, $0.885 a share ($57.6m of NTA plus $14.2m of warrant money, over 81.1m shares).
- If it goes against us: value it on earnings at 10 times, about what it paid for Chatswood. Run-rate underlying EBITDAF of $10.55m ($8.0m guidance plus the $2.55m midpoint for Chatswood) gives $105.5m. Less net debt after Chatswood and the warrant money (debt $38.6m, Chatswood $24.0m, convertible notes $3.4m, less cash $0.1m and warrant money $14.0m, so $51.9m) leaves $53.6m, over 82.2m shares once warrants are exercised: 65c a share.
- Only now the price: the close on 30 October 2026 was 67c [14]. Gap if it goes our way, 0.885 / 0.67 less 1 = +32.1%. Gap if it goes against us, 0.652 / 0.67 less 1 = -2.7%. Gould's 70c for the Brankin block is a negotiated price for a large stake, not a value.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is 32%, above 25% but below 40%. The half-year result in mid-November and the warrant expiry on 24 March 2027 are the events to watch; liquidity has to be measured first.
