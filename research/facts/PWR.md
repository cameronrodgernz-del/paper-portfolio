## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. A car dealership group with 80+ franchise operations on the east coast of Australia, plus a property segment that owns the Warwick Farm and Southport sites [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Audited accounts on a going-concern basis (KPMG) [1]. Removed from the S&P/ASX All Ordinaries on 21 Sep 2026 [17]; still listed and trading.
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$137,729 a day (lowest day A$18,657), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz, about 14 times the A$10,000 limit.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is the Warren family at 40.22% [3][4]. It did buy about 5.0m shares on market in June 2026 (section 4), taking it from 37.32% to 40.22%, which is close to the 3% that a holder above 19% may add every six months without a bid. That is a holder build-up worth a line on the special situations watch list, but it is not a holder over 50% and no bid or review has been announced.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs. Acquisition and restructure costs were A$3.0m in FY26, A$3.0m in FY25, A$3.4m in FY24 and A$0.8m in FY23 [2][5][10][11]. They are a running cost of a group that buys dealerships every year, not a one-off.

What the headline numbers hide:
- The second half. H1 FY26 underlying PBT was A$12.5m; the full year was A$14.5m, so H2 earned A$2.0m, against A$15.2m in H2 FY25 (A$22.3m less H1 FY25's A$7.1m). On a statutory basis owners lost about A$1.1m in H2 [1][5][6]. The 1 June 2026 trading update blamed new car margins, fuel prices after the war in the Middle East, three RBA rate rises and new brands competing for share [12]. The RBA has since raised the cash rate a fourth time, to 4.60% on 30 Sep 2026 [35].
- Earnings after floorplan interest. Floorplan (bailment) interest was A$24.6m in FY26 and A$27.9m in FY25; it is inside both the statutory and underlying PBT above [1]. Interest in total was A$43.9m, three times underlying PBT.
- Operating cash flow is propped up by floorplan. The A$42.0m statutory figure includes A$54.4m of extra floorplan borrowing as inventory rose A$68.2m [1].
- Book value. The "0.23 times book" in the ranking uses total equity of A$532.2m, of which A$324.3m is goodwill and other intangibles [1].
- Property. Land and buildings are carried at A$243.7m fair value (A$193.2m at cost) [1]. Warwick Farm (A$159.9m) was independently valued in April 2026, adding A$17.7m (A$12.4m after tax) through other comprehensive income, not through profit; Southport was last valued in April 2025 [1]. The property segment earned A$9.4m EBITDA and A$0.5m PBT after its own A$6.1m of loan interest [1].
- Concentration. All revenue is in Australia; new and demo vehicles were A$1,559.5m, 63% of revenue [1].
- Wakeling. About A$28m (A$21.7m goodwill plus net assets), 16 brands at 30 dealerships and about A$500m of turnover, roughly a fifth of the group [8]. The ACCC approved it on condition that eight dealerships are sold [9].
- Leases. A$218.2m of lease liabilities at 5.7% sit outside the net debt figure [1].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: was H2 FY26 (underlying PBT of A$2.0m, against A$12.5m in H1) the bottom for the dealerships, or their new level? The owned property, less all corporate debt, is worth more than the whole company on its own, so the answer decides whether the dealerships add value or destroy it.
- If it goes our way: property at the directors' fair value, A$243.7m, less pro forma net debt of A$76.4m (A$47.4m at June, plus A$28.0m for Wakeling and A$1.0m for the final dividend) is A$167.3m. Dealership profit returns to its FY24 to FY26 average: underlying retail PBT (after floorplan interest and after rent paid to the property segment, before the property loan interest) of A$57.0m, A$23.2m and A$14.0m averages A$31.4m; after 30% tax and A$1.2m to minorities that is A$20.8m, at 8 times A$166.2m. Total A$333.5m, or A$1.94 a share on 172.26m shares. Eight times is well below Eagers Automotive at about 22 times trailing earnings (ASX company data).
- If it goes against us: H2 FY26 is the new level, so the dealerships are worth nothing, and the property is taken at cost, A$193.2m, not at valuation. A$193.2m less A$76.4m is A$116.7m, or A$0.68 a share.
- Price: A$0.655 (close 30 Oct 2026), market value A$112.8m. Gap if it goes our way: 1.94 / 0.655 - 1 = +196%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.68 / 0.655 - 1 = +4%. At the price, the market values the dealerships at about minus A$55m once property is taken at valuation.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is +196%, well over 40%. The question can be worked from the filings (segment results, the property valuation note, floorplan terms and the Wakeling numbers), and the AGM on 25 Nov 2026 and the H1 FY27 result due about 20 Feb 2027 should show whether H2 FY26 was the bottom. Even on the bad answer the value is at the price, because the property backs it. The deep dive should test the April 2026 Warwick Farm valuation and the A$1.03 the Executive Chair paid for 4.2m shares in June.
