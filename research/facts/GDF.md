# Garda Property Group (ASX:GDF): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 4 points, #59 of 195 ASX names in reports/research_rank.csv (2026-11-08).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? Not yet, but it is becoming one. Garda is an internally
  managed stapled group (Garda Holdings Limited and Garda Diversified Property Fund) that owned Brisbane industrial property and
  lent to property developers in Southeast Queensland [3]. After the sale below it will hold cash and development loans
  only, which is close to a property credit fund. On its own this would be a reading, not a kill.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Pitcher Partners' FY26 report has no modified
  opinion, emphasis of matter or other matter paragraph [4]. Pitcher Partners is resigning after a tender and PwC is proposed
  from the 26 Nov 2026 AGM, with "no reportable matters" [9].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$46,184 a day
  (data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz) [12].
- On Cam's excluded list? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no companies; Cam confirmed on 8 Nov 2026 there is nothing to
  avoid at the outset.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? Yes: Garda is selling all of its
  property. On 21 Oct 2026 it signed contracts to sell its seven remaining investment properties to a Charter Hall managed fund
  for A$300.0m, on top of the Heathwood (A$17.5m) and Pinkenba (A$33.3m) sales of late September: A$350.8m in all, 1.4%
  below carrying value [1]. Settlements run from 20 Nov 2026 to about March 2027. All debt is to be repaid, special
  distributions of about 10c a security are intended, and the cash is to go mainly into lending, with security buybacks one
  of four stated uses [1][2][5]. This goes to the special situations watch list.

The portfolio sale ends the sheet and sends the company to the special situations watch list. Sections 2 to 6 were not
written, as the template requires once a kill check answer is yes.

For the watch list (facts only, not graded):
- The sales: Heathwood (The Landrich Trust, A$17.5m) and the Charter Hall properties at Acacia Ridge (two, A$68.5m),
  Berrinba (A$17.1m), Richlands (A$37.9m) and Wacol (two, A$51.8m and A$44.0m) are unconditional and due to settle on
  20 Nov 2026; Pinkenba (2MB Co, A$33.3m) is unconditional and due 22 Jan 2027; Morningside (Charter Hall, A$80.7m) is marked "subject
  to due diligence" in the settlement table, although the release calls all seven Charter Hall contracts unconditional; it is
  under construction and due on completion about March 2027 [1][5]. ASX said holder approval was not
  needed [1].
- After settlement (company figures): the A$185.3m drawn syndicated debt repaid, gearing 0%, about A$176.6m of cash plus
  A$144.4m of existing loans to invest, and NTA of A$1.56 a security [1]. NTA per security uses 200.7m securities; 218.07m are
  quoted, the other 17.4m being employee plan securities bought with company loans, so NTA spread over every quoted security is
  about A$1.44 [3][10].
- Earnings and payout: FY27 guidance raised on 21 Oct 2026 to funds from operations of 12.5c a security (from 10.5c, given on
  30 Jul 2026) and distributions of 12.5c (from 9.0c), paying out about all of it; plus intended special distributions of about
  7.3c in the December 2026 quarter and 2.7c in the March 2027 quarter, to help holders pay tax on about 20.8c a security of
  net capital gains [1][2][6]. FY26: revenue from operations A$38.3m, funds from operations A$22.4m, profit after tax A$23.8m,
  distributions 8.5c, NTA A$1.64 [3].
- The lending book: A$134.1m at 30 June 2026 (A$44.1m a year earlier), loans of 12 to 18 months to residential and
  industrial developers, mostly in Southeast Queensland; about 68% senior-type at loan-to-value up to 70%, 32% early-stage or
  higher-leverage positions (about A$63m), some with a share of project profit [4]. Garda says it takes "concentrated positions
  in individual projects, borrowers or sectors" [4].
- Holders: HGT Investments 18.61%, Madsen Nominees (Executive Chairman Matthew Madsen) 8.43%, HSBC Custody Nominees 5.82%,
  Netwealth 5.38% (FY26 annual report) [4]. Directors' on-market buys in the last 12 months: Paul Leitch 40,485 at A$1.2365
  (2 Dec 2025, A$50,060); Andrew Thornton 100,000 at A$1.16 (5 Feb 2026, A$116,000); no on-market sells. Madsen's December 2025
  change was an off-market transfer, bought and sold in equal number [7].
- Capital: no raisings in the last two years; an on-market buyback has been extended year to year (latest 2 Apr 2026). The
  AGM on 26 Nov 2026 asks for an additional 10% placement capacity and approval of employee security issues [8][10][11].
- Dated events: settlements 20 Nov 2026 (seven properties), 22 Jan 2027 (Pinkenba), about March 2027 (Morningside); AGM and
  auditor change 26 Nov 2026; special distributions in the December 2026 and March 2027 quarters; quarterly distributions of
  about 3.1c (12.5c a year); 1H27 result about 4 Feb 2027 (inferred from 4 Feb 2026); interest rate swaps of A$70m expire 4 Mar 2027 [1][2][4][8].
- Price for reference: A$1.12, the last trade on 6 Nov 2026 (ASX company data), a market value of A$244m on 218.07m quoted
  securities. That is 28% below the company's A$1.56 pro forma NTA (22% below the A$1.44 on every quoted security), 9.0 times
  upgraded FY27 funds from operations, and an 11.2% distribution yield before the specials [12]. The price was A$1.025 at
  the close on 20 Oct 2026, the day before the sale was announced [1], and A$1.145 at the 30 Oct close [12].

## 7. Grade
C, on a named kill fact: the sale of the whole property portfolio, with settlements, debt repayment and special
distributions still to come and the cash to be redeployed. Sent to the special situations watch list. The question there is
whether the discount to NTA closes once the balance sheet is cash and loans: through buybacks or a capital return, or only
through lending results over several years.
