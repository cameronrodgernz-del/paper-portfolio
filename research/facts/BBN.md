## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Baby Bunting is Australia's largest specialty retailer of maternity and baby goods, with 80 stores in Australia and New Zealand and online sales of 25.3% of the total. FY26 sales A$556.0m [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The auditor's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified [1]. Net debt A$16.2m; the NAB facility was extended to 30 September 2029 [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$192,729 a day, and all 20 sessions were above A$10,000 (data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz) [14].
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder, HMC Capital, rose from 14.87% to 16.81% (8 May 2026) and then fell to 13.15% (18 June 2026) [9]. No approach or review has been announced in 2025 or 2026 [13].

Does the gap between statutory and underlying profit recur? Yes. Employee equity incentive expense is left out of pro forma profit every year (A$2.7m in FY25, A$6.1m in FY26) and is a real cost of paying staff. FY27 guidance leaves it out again, along with about A$1.5m of spending on a new ERP and point-of-sale system [2][3]. The statutory figure is the one to value.

Free cash flow after leases and capital spending was minus A$11.6m in FY26 (A$64.9m less A$32.0m less A$44.5m) and plus A$8.5m in FY25 [1].

What the headline numbers hide:
- Leases. A$152.3m of lease liabilities against A$16.2m of net debt; before the lease accounting standard, FY26 pro forma EBIT would have been A$24.9m rather than A$32.2m [1][3].
- Capital spending is high while the refurbishment program runs: A$44.5m in FY26 and a planned A$33m to A$37m in FY27, which the company says operating cash flow will fund [2].
- Profit is weighted to the second half: guidance assumes about one-third in 1H and two-thirds in 2H, so the February result covers the smaller part of the year [2].
- Sales are running below the guidance assumption. Comparable store sales were up 2.2% to 4 October 2026 (Australia 2.0%, New Zealand 8.7%) on an "underlying" basis that excludes the seven stores closed for refurbishment and their neighbours, against the 3% to 5% assumed in FY27 guidance [5].
- Book value is half goodwill: A$45.3m of A$129.8m [1].
- Dilution: 9.2m rights at June 2026 are 6.7% of the shares, though the company says many from the FY23 to FY26 grant will lapse [1].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: can Baby Bunting turn FY27 guidance (pro forma NPAT of A$19m to A$21m, up about 24%) into statutory profit while comparable sales run below the guidance assumption and refurbishments absorb the cash? A deep dive would test the refurbished stores' sales uplift (20% on average over their first year), the gross margin path to 42% and how much of the guidance gap is the recurring share-based pay.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: specialty retailers are priced on earnings, at a price to earnings multiple. The price would get there through the half-year result in February 2027 showing the year on track.
- Peers (the desk's arithmetic from FY26 filings, prices on 6 Nov 2026):
  - Beacon Lighting (ASX:BLX): market value A$432.3m (229.34m shares at A$1.885) over FY26 statutory profit of A$26.99m = 16.0 times [16].
  - Shaver Shop (ASX:SSG): A$155.2m (131.01m shares at A$1.185) over A$14.81m = 10.5 times [17].
  - Average 13.25 times.
- If it goes our way: FY27 lands at the guidance midpoint of A$20.0m pro forma. Less the FY26 after-tax pro forma adjustments of A$4.9m, which recur, and the A$1.5m of ERP and point-of-sale spending (A$1.05m after tax), statutory profit is A$14.05m. At 13.25 times: A$186.2m, on 136.84m shares = A$1.36 a share.
- If it goes against us: profit stays at FY26's statutory A$11.2m. At 13.25 times: A$148.5m, or A$1.09 a share.
- Cross-check: at 13.25 times the company's own pro forma A$20m, the value would be A$1.94 a share, but that counts a cost that recurs every year as if it didn't.
- Only now the price: A$1.31 (last trade on 6 Nov 2026, ASX company data; A$1.06 at the 30 Oct close) [15], a market value of A$179.3m, 16.0 times FY26 statutory profit and 12.8 times the FY27 statutory figure above. Gap if it goes our way: 1.36 / 1.31 - 1 = +4%. Gap if it goes against us: 1.09 / 1.31 - 1 = -17%. At the 30 October close of A$1.06 the gaps would have been +28% and +2%; the price rose 24% in the week to 6 November with no announcement from the company [13].

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive: the gap if it goes our way is 4%, under 25%. The price already sits at the FY27 statutory figure times the peer multiple. It was a B at the 30 October price, so if the price falls back toward A$1.06 before the half-year result (about 17 February 2027), the sheet should be re-graded.
