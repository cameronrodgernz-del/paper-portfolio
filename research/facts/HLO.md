# Helloworld Travel (ASX:HLO): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #23 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Helloworld runs travel agency networks (about 2,600 members plus home-based brokers), wholesale, cruise, inbound tours, air ticket consolidation and, from 30 Oct 2026, Crown Currency Exchange. TTV A$3.96bn and revenue A$208.9m in FY26 [1][2]. It also holds 20.29% of Webjet Group (ASX:WJL), carried at market value [1].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. KPMG's opinion is unmodified [3].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$192,850 a day (lowest day A$59,577), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. Travel; config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. Helloworld was the bidder, not the target: its A$0.90 a share proposal for Webjet (19 Nov 2025) ended when Webjet stopped talks on 13 Feb 2026 [4][5]. The largest holder is the Burnes group (CEO Andrew Burnes and associates), 24.2% before the October 2026 share issue [3].

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs. Webjet fair value moves (A$5.0m gain in FY25, A$34.3m loss in FY26) are left out every year, and so are cash costs that keep coming back: new business start-up costs (A$1.9m, A$1.6m), acquisition costs (A$1.8m, A$5.5m), restructuring and legal costs [3]. Cash significant items were A$9.7m in FY26.

What else the headline numbers hide:
- Cash conversion is poor. Over FY25 and FY26 operating cash flow totalled A$6.6m against A$115.8m of underlying EBITDA [3]. FY26 pre-tax operating cash (A$46.5m) was 77% of underlying EBITDA; FY25's was 15%. Part is timing (client money, catch-up tax), but a deep dive has to show it.
- The "net cash about 28% of market value" in the ranking is client and IATA money. Ex the Webjet shares, current liabilities exceed current assets, and debt goes from A$35.0m to about A$136m with Crown [3][9].
- Webjet: Helloworld paid A$61.4m for Webjet shares in FY25 and FY26 (cash flow statements) and holds 78,250,205 shares, carried at A$32.1m (A$0.41) at June 2026 [1][3]. Its own offer of A$0.90 a share would have valued the stake at A$70.4m [4].
- Book value is all intangibles: NTA was minus 15.6c before Crown, which adds about A$135m more of goodwill and intangibles [7][9].
- Underlying EBITDA has fallen two years in three: A$66.3m in FY24, A$60.6m in FY25 as first reported, A$60.2m in FY26 [7][11].
- Concentration: the Qantas deal covers about A$1.5bn of TTV over three years, and FY26's fourth quarter lost override income from Middle Eastern carriers [12][13].

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance (FY27), 16 Oct 2026: TTV about A$4.8bn and underlying EBITDA A$78m to A$84m, including A$14m from Crown for eight months; Q1 FY27 TTV and underlying EBITDA "line ball" with a year earlier; forward bookings up "circa 10%" [14]. Crown made A$22m of underlying EBITDA in FY26 and was bought for A$135m (6.1 times) [9]. Without Crown the range is A$64m to A$70m against A$60.2m in FY26. The AGM on 23 Oct 2026 repeated it [15].

Changes in FY26:
- 23 Oct 2025: underlying EBITDA A$64m to A$72m [16].
- 25 Feb 2026: reaffirmed ("on track") [6].
- 5 Jun 2026: cut to A$57m to A$62m, citing the Middle East conflict [13].
- Delivered (26 Aug 2026): A$60.2m, inside the cut range, 6% below the bottom of the original [1].

Record (FY25):
- About October 2024: A$56m to A$62m; cut to A$52m to A$56m on 8 May 2025; raised to A$58m to A$62m on 28 Jul 2025 [17][18]. Delivered A$60.6m as first reported [11] (restated to A$55.6m in the FY26 accounts, for discontinued operations and the removal of the Webjet gain [3]).
- Two years, two mid-year cuts.

- Helloworld lodged a notice over its own escrowed vendor shares on 2 Nov 2026 [21]; a technical interest, not a purchase.

Director on-market trades, last 12 months: Martin Pakula bought 2,500 shares for A$3,750 (A$1.50) on 25 Jun 2026 [23]. No sells. Peter Costello joined the Board in May 2026 [24].

Capital raisings in the last two years: none for cash. ## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: does Helloworld's underlying EBITDA turn into cash (A$6.6m of operating cash over two years against A$115.8m of EBITDA), and does the A$135m, mostly debt-funded Crown purchase earn its cost? At the price the whole group, with Crown and its debt, is valued at about 4.4 times FY26 EBITDA plus a full year of Crown. A deep dive would rebuild cash conversion from the working capital and tax lines, separate client money from company cash, get the Crown debt terms, and value the Webjet stake.
- If it goes our way: FY27 guidance midpoint (A$81m) with a full year of Crown (A$81m - A$14m + A$22m = A$89m), less A$1.9m a year for the start-up and restructuring costs that recur outside underlying EBITDA: A$87.1m. Less leases (A$5.2m), capital spending (A$3.9m) and an allowance of A$2.2m for Crown's stores, and tax at 25% on EBITDA less A$19.9m of depreciation and amortisation: A$59.0m of free cash flow before interest. At the company's own 11.5% post-tax discount rate with its 2% terminal growth [3]: A$633.4m (7.1 times A$89m). Less about A$136.25m of debt (A$35.0m Citibank plus A$101.25m of Crown's A$135m not paid in shares): A$497.1m. Plus Webjet:
  - at market (78.25m shares at A$0.275, 30 Oct 2026: A$21.5m): A$518.6m, or A$2.77 a share on 187.11m shares;
  - at carrying value (A$32.1m, 30 Jun 2026): A$529.2m, or A$2.83 a share.
- If it goes against us: core EBITDA stays at FY25's restated A$55.6m with no growth, only 77% of it turns into pre-tax cash (FY26's rate, the better of the two years), Crown is worth only what was paid (A$135m), and the cash on the balance sheet is treated as client money. A$55.6m x 0.772 less A$5.2m leases, A$3.9m capital spending and A$9.5m tax is A$24.3m a year, at 11.5% with no growth A$211.4m. Plus Crown A$135m, less A$136.25m debt: A$210.1m. Plus Webjet:
  - at market (A$21.5m): A$231.7m, or A$1.24 a share;
  - at carrying value (A$32.1m): A$242.2m, or A$1.29 a share.
- Price: A$1.34 (close 30 Oct 2026; A$1.355 on 6 Nov 2026), market value A$251m on 187.1m shares. Gap if it goes our way: 2.77 / 1.34 - 1 = +107% with Webjet at market (+111% at carrying value). Gap if it goes against us: 1.24 / 1.34 - 1 = -8% with Webjet at market (-4% at carrying value).

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now, and a value pick: the gap if it goes our way is over 100% on either Webjet value, and the "against" value is 8% (market) or 4% (carrying) below the price, inside the 10% limit. Cash conversion, client money and the Crown terms can be worked from the filings, and the H1 FY27 result on about 25 Feb 2027 is the first look at Crown, the new debt and the cash, inside six months. Cautions for the deep dive: the "against" case leaves the balance-sheet cash out as client money, but it does assume FY26's 77% cash conversion holds rather than FY25's 15%; the Crown debt split and terms are unknown; the record has two mid-year guidance cuts in two years; and the Burnes group, Alysandratos and Crown's vendor together hold about 46%, so minorities have little say over further deals like the Webjet stake, now worth about a third of the A$61.4m paid for Webjet shares in FY25 and FY26 alone. The ranking's flag ("profit more than halved last year") is the Webjet mark, not the operations.
