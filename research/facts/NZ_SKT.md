# Sky Network Television (NZX:SKT): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 2 points, #15 in the NZX table of reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-08).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. New Zealand's pay television and sport broadcaster (Sky Box by satellite and internet, Sky Sport Now and Neon streaming, Sky Broadband, Sky Venue for pubs and hotels) and, since 1 August 2025, the free-to-air Three and ThreeNow (Sky Free, bought from Warner Bros. Discovery for $1). FY26 revenue NZ$824.8m [1][2][13].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. PwC's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified; the NZX page shows no suspension [1][30].
- Too thin to trade? No. NZX's own daily trading data: median on-market value over the 20 sessions 9 October to 6 November 2026 was NZ$250,895 a day (lowest NZ$150,685; all 20 sessions above NZ$12,500). The 60-session median is NZ$284,134 and the 250-session median NZ$231,847 [29].
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries, and the hold was lifted on 8 November 2026. Sky is a sports media business, not a gambling one.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. No approach, review or sale is live. The last approach was a non-binding indicative offer received in 2023 (disclosed 13 October 2023), which the board ended on 8 November 2023 as short of its view of value [21][22]. The 9 December 2024 "media speculation" release was about the rugby rights talks, not an approach [23]. The largest holder is ACC at 8.44% [27]. Noted for the report: an on-market buying cluster by the chair, deputy chair and chief executive in 2026 (section 4).

What the headline numbers hide:
- The ranking's 8.5 times earnings uses statutory earnings of 43.13c a share, lifted by the $31.4m non-cash bargain purchase gain and $8.2m from Optus. On underlying earnings of 30.08c the multiple is 12.2 times at the 6 November close [1][30].
- Concentration: Sky Box is 54% of revenue and its customer base has fallen 18% in three years (514,982 to about 422,000), 6% to 7% a year [1][4].
- Sport rights are long, large and mostly undisclosed. Programme rights commitments were $862.5m at 30 June 2026 ($307.6m within a year, $536.5m in one to five years), up from $474.7m, which is 1.8 times shareholders' equity. That figure excludes the NRL renewal (from January 2028, conditional on the 20 November vote) and the Premier League extension signed on 28 August 2026 [1][5][6].
- The rugby deal for 2026 to 2030 was approved by 99.99% of votes cast on 21 November 2025; Sky says it "improved economics" and kept programming costs within 47% to 49% of revenue, and expects the same at or below that level in FY28 with the NRL deal included. Both deals are paid monthly over their terms [3][5][7].
- The FY26 free cash flow had help: $6.2m from working capital and no tax paid (Sky deducted $15.2m of normal tax in its own measure). Lease principal due in FY27 is $30.5m against $26.1m paid in FY26 [1][2].
- Book value: goodwill is $244.3m of the $467.6m of equity (52%) [1].
- The current satellite lease ends on 31 March 2028; a further $61.2m of lease commitments sits off the balance sheet [1].
- The undrawn $100m bank facility ends on 30 September 2027 [1].
- HBO Max content left Neon in June 2026 after Sky chose not to renew on a co-exclusive basis [8].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: can Sky's free cash flow keep up with its promised dividend growth (at least 35c, about $48m, in FY27, rising 10% a year to about 42c, $58m, in FY29) while Sky Box revenue falls about 5% a year and the rugby (from January 2026), NRL (from January 2028) and Premier League deals come in? Their prices are confidential, but each needed holder approval because the rights and production obligations exceed half of Sky's total assets: for rugby more than about $313m to $336m over five years (total assets were $625.9m at December 2024 and $672.9m at June 2025), for the NRL more than $369m over seven years (half of $738.9m) [4][5][7]. On the filings, FY26's normalised free cash flow of $58.9m was about $52.7m without the working-capital help, and FY27 guidance converts to about $47m.
- How the market prices this kind of business: a cash-returning media company with a shrinking core is priced on the cash it hands back, through dividends and buybacks, as a yield. The mechanism that would move the price is the dividend stream itself (fully imputed, quarterly from December 2026) and a buyback after the February 2027 result; a bid is possible (a 2023 approach failed) but nothing is under way. The value below is free cash flow (cash from operations less lease principal and capex, before one-offs, at a normal tax charge and no working-capital help) at 10 times, a 10% cash yield, plus surplus cash. 10 times is close to the 9.2 times trailing earnings that NZX shows for NZME, the only other NZX-listed New Zealand media company [30]; there is no closer listed peer.
- The FY26 cash bridge, checked against Sky's own figure: underlying EBITDA $157.0m less underlying capex $59.1m and lease principal $26.1m is $71.8m; less Sky's normalised tax of $15.2m and net interest paid of $3.6m is $53.0m; add $6.2m of working capital and it matches Sky's $58.9m within $0.3m [1][2].
- Surplus cash: $79.1m at 30 June 2026 less the 17c final dividend ($23.4m) paid on 25 September and about $4.8m of Sky Free integration cash still to spend in FY27 [2] is $50.9m.
- If it goes our way: FY27 guidance midpoint met and the promised $10m of extra EBITDA from Sky Free by FY28 delivered. EBITDA $170m, less capex $62.5m (guidance midpoint), lease principal $30.5m (due within a year at 30 June 2026), tax at 28% of ($170m less depreciation of $97.0m less interest of $5.8m) $18.8m, and net interest $3.6m: free cash flow $54.6m. At 10 times, $545.6m, plus $50.9m, $596.6m, or $4.32 a share on 137.96m shares. That pays the FY28 dividend (38.5c, $53.1m) at 97% of free cash flow, above the board's 60% to 90% policy, so even the good case leaves little room for buybacks.
- If it goes against us: FY27 at the bottom of guidance (EBITDA $155m, capex $65m) and no growth after it, with the synergies used up offsetting Box decline and rights costs. Free cash flow $155m less $65m, $30.5m, tax of $14.6m and $3.6m: $41.3m. At 10 times, $412.6m, plus $50.9m, $463.6m, or $3.36 a share. On this answer the 35c dividend ($48.3m) is more than the cash the business makes.
- Sensitivity: at 8 times the two values are $3.53 and $2.76; at 12 times $5.12 and $3.96. If FY27 lease principal stays at FY26's $26.1m, each value rises by about 32c.
- Only now the price: the close on 6 November 2026 was $3.67 (bid $3.62, offer $3.67), a market value of $506.3m [30]. Gap if it goes our way, 4.32 / 3.67 less 1 = +17.8%. Gap if it goes against us, 3.36 / 3.67 less 1 = -8.4%.
- For scale: at $3.67 the guided 35c FY27 dividend is a 9.5% cash yield (13.2% gross with imputation), and the price is 12.2 times underlying earnings. The directors bought about $1.34m of shares between $3.10 and $3.74 in 2026.

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive: the gap if it goes our way is under 25% (+17.8% at 10 times free cash flow). The value if it goes against us is only 8.4% below the price, so the downside on the filings looks limited, but it does not meet the 40% needed for a value pick, and it would take a 12 times multiple (an 8.3% cash yield) on the good case to reach +39%. The NRL vote on 20 November 2026 and the H1 FY27 result in late February 2027, with its cash flow and buyback decision, are the events to watch. The insider buying cluster is noted for the special situations watch list.
