# Grange Resources (ASX:GRR): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 8 points, #4 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Grange mines magnetite at Savage River and makes iron ore pellets at Port Latta, Tasmania (2.5Mt+ a year); it also holds the undeveloped Southdown magnetite project in Western Australia [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. PwC's review of the June 2026 half-year is unmodified, with an emphasis of matter drawing attention to the impairment assessment [3]. Removed from the S&P/ASX All Ordinaries on 21 Sep 2026 [4]; still listed.
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$71,738 a day (lowest day A$31,685), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. Shagang International Holdings (Hong Kong), part of Jiangsu Shagang Group, holds 47.9% to 48.02% and buys 1Mt of pellets a year under an offtake running to 2032 [1]. It is just under 50% and has filed no substantial holder notice in the last 12 months. In May 2026 Grange said "the major shareholder has indicated that they will not provide financial support to the project at this stage" [5].

There is no recurring gap between statutory and underlying profit: 2024 and 2025 had no adjustments, and the 2026 impairment is a single write-down that followed the decision to give up the larger block cave mine [3].

What the headline numbers hide:
- No earnings multiple: H1 2026 is a loss because of the impairment. Underlying profit in the half was A$10.5m [7].
- "Net cash about 218% of market value" is committed. The Board approved the North Pit Underground sub-level cave (SLC) on 14 Oct 2026: A$287m of underground capital plus A$234m of sustaining capital over its life, "projected to be fully funded from existing cash reserves and cashflows" [9]. The company's own forecast "maintains funds above A$24M through the life of mine", so at the low point almost all of today's cash is spent [9]. Shagang will not help fund it [5].
- Closure costs. Decommissioning and restoration provisions are A$148.7m, up A$29.3m in six months [3].
- Price assumptions. The SLC case uses US$90/t for 62% iron fines and A$1 = US$0.68, giving an average product price of A$181/t against a C1 cash cost of A$132/t and an all-in cost of A$164/t [9]. In the September 2026 quarter Grange realised A$164.41/t (US$116.13/t at 0.7063) [2], 9% below the plan's average and level with its all-in cost. The June 2026 impairment test used higher pellet prices: US$119.64/t for 2026, US$119 to US$126 for 2027 to 2031 and US$129 to US$134 for 2032 to 2035, at an 11% nominal post-tax discount rate [3].
- Mine life. The open pit ends in 2028; the SLC takes mining to 2035, against 2040 under the block cave plan it replaces [3][9]. The production target is 44.1Mt of ore (27.8Mt SLC, 16.3Mt open pit) inside a 60.6Mt underground ore reserve [9].
- Customer and owner are the same: Shagang buys 1Mt a year of pellets to 2032 at index-linked prices and owns 48% [1].
- Southdown: the search for equity investors "remains ongoing" [2].

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance: no production, cost or price guidance is published in the quarterly reports [2][10][11]. The SLC plan (14 Oct 2026): underground development from Q1 2027, first SLC ore in 2028, about 4Mt a year by 2030, open pit finished during 2028 [9].

Changes this year:
- 13 May 2026: block cave financing not available from the major shareholder; work to cut the funding need [5].
- 30 Jul 2026: likely impairment of "between $300 million to $600 million pre-tax" [12].
- 31 Aug 2026: impairment of A$690.7m pre-tax, above the top of the range given a month earlier [7].
- 14 Oct 2026: SLC final investment decision; NPV A$357m (8.3% real, after tax), IRR 43%, at US$90/t fines [9].

Record: no numeric production or cost guidance to test for 2024 or 2025. The development plan has changed: the block cave definitive feasibility study of February 2024 is shelved, and the mine life has come back from 2040 to 2035 [9].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: will the A$287m sub-level cave return its money at today's pellet prices, or use up the cash and leave the A$148.7m closure bill? Cash less the closure provision is already more than the market value, so the price treats the mine itself as worth less than nothing.
- If it goes our way: the SLC plan delivers its stated value. Cash and liquid investments at 30 Sep 2026, A$300.4m, plus the plan's NPV of A$357m (which covers Savage River from 2027 including the last two years of open pit, and already deducts the A$287m of underground capital), less the A$148.7m decommissioning provision: A$508.7m, or A$0.44 a share on 1,157.34m shares. The directors' own after-impairment net tangible assets of 49c a share, on higher pellet prices, is a cross-check.
- If it goes against us: realised prices stay near the plan's all-in cost of A$164/t (where the September 2026 quarter was), so the mine only returns the capital put into it and is worth nothing: A$300.4m less A$148.7m is A$151.7m, or A$0.13 a share. A cost overrun, or a cave that doesn't perform, would take value below this, because the cash is spent before the first SLC ore in 2028.
- Price: A$0.105 (close 30 Oct 2026), market value A$121.5m. Gap if it goes our way: 0.44 / 0.105 - 1 = +319%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.13 / 0.105 - 1 = +25%.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is +319%, and even the bad answer sits above the price. But the deciding facts (what the SLC costs to build, how the cave performs from 2028, and pellet prices over the build) mostly arrive after the six-month window; the full-year result due about 27 Feb 2027, with a new impairment test and the first spending, is the first real read and the right time for the deep dive. The deep dive should also weigh that a 48% holder who is also the main customer decides how any cash is used; the forecast includes no distributions to shareholders [9].
