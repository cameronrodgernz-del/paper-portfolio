## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. EML runs prepaid card and payment programs (gift and incentive cards, government and payroll disbursements, and others) in Australia, the UK, Europe and North America, and earns interest on the A$2.2bn of customer balances it holds [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. KPMG's opinion on the June 2026 accounts is unqualified, with no material uncertainty paragraph; the key audit matters are account fees and breakage revenue, and goodwill (A$78.3m) [1]. The accounts show a A$447m net current asset deficiency, which the directors explain by stored value liabilities being current while A$500m of the matching funds sit in bonds of over a year [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$123,896 a day (lowest A$21,482), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz [24].
- On Cam's excluded list? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries (Cam confirmed the list on 8 Nov 2026).
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is Copia Investment Partners with 12.49% [11]. The register has turned over (section 4), and the executive chair bought shares in March 2026, but no approach or review has been announced.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs. Restructuring (A$6.5m then A$15.3m), Arlo running costs (A$2.8m then A$4.2m) and legacy tax (A$8.4m then A$1.2m) are left out in both years, and a new item, the stored value shortfall, appeared in FY26 [1]. The company forecasts about A$15.7m of further "non-recurring" Arlo spending in FY27, A$2.4m in FY28 and A$1.0m in FY29 [5]. Share-based pay of about A$15m a year has been issued as shares (6.6m in FY26) and leaves 63.6m rights outstanding, 16% of the shares on issue [1][4].

What the headline numbers hide:
- Interest on customer balances is 27% of revenue. Balances were A$2,205m at June 2026 (46% in pounds, 21% in euros, 24% in Australian dollars) earning 3.2% a year, down from 3.7% [2]. A 0.25% change in rates moves revenue by about A$5.5m.
- Customer concentration: the top 30 customers give about 47% of revenue excluding interest, the top 5 about 23% and the largest about 8% [2].
- Book value is mostly goodwill and software (NTA 6c a share against net assets of about 33c) [1].
- Regulators: APRA and the RBA require the Australian subsidiary to put a bank guarantee over certain stored value balances until it gets a banking licence, which EML expects within 12 months of May 2026; it says the guarantee's cost won't be material [6][7]. A review found a A$9.6m shortfall in stored value accounts in its North American and UK unregulated businesses [1].
- Guidance has been cut once already in FY26 (section 3), and FY28 free cash flow of A$30m to A$35m is a company forecast described as "proforma", without a stated definition [8].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: do the costs EML calls non-recurring actually stop after FY27, so that A$50m to A$54m of underlying EBITDA turns into the A$30m to A$35m of free cash flow the company forecasts for FY28? The costs it left out were A$28.2m in FY25 and A$33.9m in FY26.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: payment companies are priced on the cash they generate. The named peer is Tyro Payments (ASX:TYR): 533,172,818 shares at A$0.695 (30 Oct 2026 close; Tyro is not in the ASX company data file) is A$370.6m; less A$145.3m of its own funds (cash and investments, before regulatory requirements) is A$225.3m, 7.66 times its FY26 free cash flow of A$29.4m (before one-offs) [21][22]. The price would get there as the half-year results show the excluded costs falling to the Arlo budget.
- If it goes our way: FY28 free cash flow is A$30m, the bottom of the company's range. 7.66 x A$30m = A$229.9m, less net debt of A$46.9m (taking FY27 as cash-neutral): A$182.9m. On 454.89m shares, counting the 63.63m performance rights as vested in this case, A$0.40 a share (A$0.47 on shares alone).
- If it goes against us: the left-out costs keep recurring at their FY25 and FY26 average, leaving out the settled class action: restructuring A$10.9m, Arlo running costs A$3.5m, legacy tax A$4.8m and stored value shortfalls A$5.9m, A$25.0m before tax and A$17.5m after 30% tax. Free cash flow A$30m less A$17.5m is A$12.5m; 7.66 x A$12.5m = A$95.5m, less A$46.9m: A$48.7m on 391.26m shares, A$0.12 a share.
- Price: A$0.28 (last trade on 6 Nov 2026, ASX company data; A$0.215 at the 30 Oct close), market value A$109.6m. Gap if it goes our way: 0.40 / 0.28 - 1 = +44%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.12 / 0.28 - 1 = -56%. The price rose 30% in the week to 6 November with no company announcement; at A$0.215 the gaps would be +87% and -42%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is 44%. The question can be answered from filings: EML reconciles underlying to reported EBITDA, item by item, every half, and the 1H FY27 result (about 25 Feb 2027, inside six months, with the 18 Nov 2026 AGM before it) will show whether the excluded costs are falling towards the A$15.7m Arlo budget and whether operating cash flow has turned positive. The deep dive should pin down what "proforma" free cash flow includes, since the "our way" value rests on it, and test the interest income against rate cuts in the UK and Europe. Not a value pick: the value if it goes against us is 56% below the price.
