# EROAD (NZX:ERD): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 1 point, #15 in the NZX table of reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07); earlier quick look rejected. Also listed on the ASX.
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Vehicle telematics: in-cab units plus subscription software for truck and van fleets in New Zealand (where it collects road user charges for operators), Australia and North America. FY26 (year to 31 March 2026) revenue NZ$195.2m, of which NZ$185.9m subscription [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. KPMG's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified, with no material uncertainty paragraph [3]. Net debt NZ$16.0m against NZ$65m of facilities [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. NZX's own daily trading data: median on-market value over the 20 sessions 9 October to 6 November 2026 was NZ$39,195 a day (lowest NZ$787, highest NZ$240,432; 16 of the 20 sessions above NZ$12,500) [20]. The 250-session median is NZ$47,939. The shares also trade on the ASX.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. No approach, review or sale has been announced; the 24 November 2026 briefing is a results and "operational and strategic update" under the new chief executive [4][5]. No holder is near 50%, but two holders have been buying fast: Ampfield Management went from nothing to 17.81% between March and October 2026, and Anthony Kandziora from 5.14% to 8.41% between June and October (section 4). Noted in the report back as a holder build-up for the special situations watch list.

Recurring and one-off revenue: subscription revenue was NZ$185.9m of NZ$195.2m (95%) in FY26 and NZ$182.9m of NZ$194.4m in FY25; the rest is hardware, fees and other [2].

The gap between statutory and underlying profit. The impairment is a one-off write-down. Two of the "one-off" items recur in substance: the 4G upgrade costs have been taken out in three years running (NZ$9.5m of cash in FY24, NZ$7.6m in FY25, NZ$14.3m in FY26; the company says the program is now complete) [2][10], and the NZ$10.7m of development spending expensed under the new capitalisation minimum will be expensed every year from now on, so normalised EBIT flatters by about that much [2].

How much of the profit comes from capitalised development: none; it works the other way. FY26 capitalised development was NZ$7.2m against NZ$22.1m of amortisation of past development (FY25: NZ$14.5m against NZ$16.9m). Shorter asset lives adopted from 1 October 2025 add NZ$13.0m of amortisation in FY27 and NZ$11.2m in FY28, with no cash effect [3]. NZ$27.3m of research and development was expensed in FY26 (NZ$20.6m in FY25) [3].

Cash earnings after capitalised development (operating cash flow excluding interest and tax, less equipment and units, intangibles and lease payments): minus NZ$2.2m in FY26, or NZ$12.1m adding back the NZ$14.3m 4G program; NZ$16.0m in FY25, or NZ$18.3m adding back NZ$7.6m of 4G and taking out a NZ$5.3m one-off benefit from switching customers to annual billing [2][3]. The company's own "free cash flow to the firm" (before lease payments) was NZ$0.1m, or NZ$14.4m normalised [2].

What the headline numbers hide:
- North America is 38% of revenue and lost NZ$5.0m of free cash flow in FY26; a large customer did not renew in February 2026; most of its goodwill has been written off [2][1].
- Australia lost NZ$5.2m of free cash flow while building units for Cleanaway (over 3,000 trucks, about A$5m of ARR when complete in November 2026) [2][1].
- New Zealand produced NZ$24.7m of normalised free cash flow, but management told the June 2026 meeting the NZ business "has been under-invested relative to its strategic importance" [2][11].
- Debt falls due on 1 October 2027 [3].
- Leadership turnover: co-CEO left October 2025, CEO left June 2026, chair was executive chair from October 2025 to October 2026, new CEO David Leach from 12 October 2026; three directors have left or are leaving (Kinloch March 2026, Einsig June 2026, Gifford 1 December 2026) [5][12][13].

## 3. Guidance and record
FY26 (year to March 2026):
- 26 May 2025: revenue at least NZ$205m, ARR at least NZ$188m, free cash flow margin 8% to 10% excluding the 4G program; medium-term ARR growth of 11% to 13% a year [10].
- 17 Oct 2025: cut to revenue NZ$197m to NZ$203m, ARR NZ$175m to NZ$183m, free cash flow margin 5% to 8%; impairment of up to NZ$150m flagged [14].
- Delivered 25 May 2026: revenue NZ$195.2m and ARR NZ$174.3m, both below the reduced ranges; normalised free cash flow margin 7.4%, inside [1].
FY27: no numerical guidance. The FY26 result said "we expect the next six months to be challenging" and promised a trading update in September [1]; on 17 Sep 2026 that update was folded into the half-year result and briefing on 24 Nov 2026 [4][5].
FY25: guidance of NZ$190m to NZ$195m revenue and positive free cash flow; delivered NZ$194.4m and NZ$16.0m [15].
Record: met FY25; missed FY26 twice on revenue and ARR.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: with the 4G replacement finished, does EROAD's cash earnings recover to the NZ$12m to NZ$18m a year it showed on a normalised basis, and does North America stop losing money, or is FY26's slide (revenue and ARR below cut guidance, development now expensed, a rebuilding NZ business) the start of a longer decline? The 24 November half-year result is the first test under the new CEO.
- Peer multiples (the desk's arithmetic from filings, prices at the 6 Nov 2026 close): enterprise value of operations over pre-tax cash earnings after capitalised development, equipment and leases, interest excluded. hipages (ASX:HPG) 7.6 times (A$65.3m over A$8.56m); Objective Corporation (ASX:OCL) 21.3 times (A$567.8m over A$26.67m) [21][22]. The multiple used follows the answer: recovery with growth in Australia and NZ gets Objective's; no recovery gets hipages'.
- If it goes our way: normalised cash earnings of NZ$12.1m, plus North America reaching the company's target of break-even free cash flow (adding back its NZ$5.0m loss): NZ$17.1m. At Objective's 21.3 times: NZ$364.2m, less NZ$16.0m net debt, plus a NZ$9.6m net deferred tax asset (NZ$16.5m less NZ$6.9m) = NZ$357.8m, on 189.08m shares = NZ$1.89 a share. (At hipages' 7.6 times: NZ$0.66.)
- If it goes against us: cash earnings stay at the normalised NZ$12.1m with North America still losing money. At hipages' 7.6 times: NZ$92.3m less NZ$16.0m plus NZ$9.6m = NZ$85.9m, or NZ$0.45 a share. On FY26's reported cash earnings (minus NZ$2.2m) the business would be worth little beyond its assets.
- Price: NZ$0.79 (close 6 Nov 2026, NZX), market value NZ$149.4m. Gap if it goes our way: 1.89 / 0.79 - 1 = +139%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.45 / 0.79 - 1 = -43%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is 139%; the question can be worked from filings (regional free cash flow, the 4G spend and capitalised development are all reported); and the 24 November 2026 half-year result and strategic update fall inside six months. The gap rests on Objective's multiple: at hipages' multiple the value if it goes our way would be NZ$0.66, below the price, so the deep dive should test what a buyer of a regulated NZ road-charging franchise would pay. Not a value pick: the value if it goes against us is 43% below the price. The Ampfield and Kandziora buying is a separate special situation angle.
