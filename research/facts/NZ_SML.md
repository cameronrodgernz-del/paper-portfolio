## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Synlait processes milk from Canterbury farms into infant formula and other nutritional products, ingredients and foodservice and consumer dairy products, with FY26 total revenue of NZ$1.94bn [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? Yes, going-concern doubt. KPMG's report on the FY26 accounts (year to 31 July 2026) includes a paragraph headed "Material uncertainty related to going concern", drawing attention to the directors' note that "a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the Group's ability to continue as a going concern"; the opinion itself is not modified [1]. The note gives the reasons: the bank facilities step down from NZ$320m to NZ$260m on 1 March 2027 and NZ$200m on 1 June 2027 and mature on 30 June 2027; they "are not expected to be repaid solely from available cash resources" and replacement funding "has not currently been secured beyond June 2027"; covenant compliance depends on rising quarterly minimum EBITDA milestones; and the forecasts also need the Chinese regulator's (SAMR) infant formula registration renewed by 5 September 2027 [1][3]. The sheet ends here.
- Too thin to trade? No. NZX's own daily trading data: median on-market value over the 20 sessions 9 October to 6 November 2026 was NZ$43,983 a day, lowest NZ$8,327; 17 of the 20 above NZ$12,500 [9].
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? Yes, as well. Bright Dairy Holding Limited (a subsidiary of Shanghai-listed Bright Dairy & Food) holds 65.3%, after taking 65.25% through a NZ$185m placement at NZ$0.60 in October 2024; The a2 Milk Company holds 19.8% [1]. In the last six months Bright has: replaced its NZ$130m shareholder loan with a new two-year loan maturing in July 2028, which Synlait's new banks required (NZX waived the related-party approval rule, 5.2.1, for it) [3][4][5]; had Synlait move its balance date from 31 July to 31 December to match Bright's [3]; and put its Deputy Finance Director, Xu Yanqun, on the board in October 2026, to stand for election on 4 December 2026 [6][7]. On 17 August 2026 Synlait said it "is not involved in any discussions with The a2 Milk Company or Fonterra" about a transaction reported in the media [8]. This goes to the special situations watch list.

## 7. Grade
C, on a named kill fact: the auditor's material uncertainty over going concern in the FY26 accounts, with the bank facilities due on 30 June 2027 not yet refinanced. Sections 2 to 6 were not written, as the template requires once a kill check answer is yes. Separately, the 65.3% holder's moves send it to the special situations watch list.

For the watch list, from the FY26 accounts and announcements: total revenue NZ$1,936.7m; reported EBITDA NZ$8.1m (underlying NZ$46.3m); reported net loss NZ$75.4m (underlying loss NZ$21.6m), made up of a NZ$80.6m loss in the first half (manufacturing problems) and a NZ$5.2m profit in the second; operating cash outflow NZ$183.3m; net debt NZ$215.0m plus the NZ$130m Bright loan; net assets NZ$708.4m and net tangible assets NZ$0.97 a share; no dividend; 603,198,098 shares [1][2][9]. The North Island assets were sold to The a2 Milk Company (completed 2 April 2026) [10]. No quantitative guidance for the five months to 31 December 2026 [2]. At the 6 November 2026 close of NZ$0.495 the market value is NZ$298.6m, about half of net tangible assets [9].

Dated events, for the watch list: annual meeting 4 December 2026 (announced) [7]; results for the five months to 31 December 2026, date not yet announced (expected early 2027, inferred); facility step-downs 1 March and 1 June 2027 and maturity 30 June 2027 (announced) [3]; first test of the net senior leverage covenant 30 June 2027 [3]; SAMR registration renewal due by 5 September 2027 [1]; a strategy to "scale" from the Canterbury assets promised in 2027 [2]; Bright loan matures July 2028 [1].
