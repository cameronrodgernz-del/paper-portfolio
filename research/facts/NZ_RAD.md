## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An aged care operator: 25 care homes (13 owned, 12 leased) and four retirement villages, about 1,990 care beds at 12 August 2026, plus home support, an online shop and 51% of Cibus Catering [5][6].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Baker Tilly Staples Rodway gave an unmodified opinion on the FY26 accounts, which are prepared on a going-concern basis [1]. The NZX shows no suspension [13].
- Too thin to trade? No, but close to the line. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 21 September to 16 October 2026 was NZ$13,926 a day, with 9 of the 20 sessions below NZ$12,500 (close times volume, from Yahoo Finance's RAD.NZ history page, a secondary source [16]). Yahoo's table stopped at 16 October, so the nine sessions from 19 to 30 October are not in the median. The NZX instrument page shows NZ$35,029 traded on 30 October [13]. Worth re-measuring on the full latest 20 sessions before any deep dive.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder, Kade Kings Limited (Brien Cree, founder and executive chair), holds 33.45% [7]. Cree sold shares into the company's buyback in December 2025 to stay inside the Takeovers Code class exemption, which keeps his stake from rising, not a move towards control [7].

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs: there is an investment property revaluation every year ($3.1m in FY25, $1.7m in FY26), which the underlying measure swaps for realised resale gains, and the rent and lease accounting difference is there every year. The FY26 impairment ($1.4m) and the reversal of past revaluation losses ($1.5m) roughly cancel out.

Two things in the figures matter for the question below. The second half of FY26 made $12.5m of underlying EBITDA ($27.4m less $14.9m), below the first half; the company said second-half resale gains were lower because there was little vacant village stock to sell [2]. Profit to owners fell the same way, about $3.1m in the second half ($9.46m less $6.33m) against $6.33m in the first [1][3]. Core debt of about $85m after Karori is 3.1 times FY26 underlying EBITDA, or about 2.8 times with a full year of Karori, against the medium-term target of below 2.5 times [2][6]. And the change of Cree's holding vehicle on 22 May 2025 broke shareholder continuity and cost the company $7.2m of imputation credits; $3.6m remained at 31 March 2026 [1].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is FY26's $27.4m of underlying EBITDA, plus Karori, the base, or was the first half flattered by resale gains, so that the real rate is nearer the second half's $12.5m a half? The 1H27 result on 11 November shows the first half with Karori and 96.7% occupancy.
- If it goes our way: FY26 underlying EBITDA of $27.4m plus Karori at Radius's own FY26 rate of $31.1k of EBITDAR per occupied bed (90 beds at 95% occupancy is $2.66m; Karori is owned, so EBITDAR is EBITDA) gives $30.0m. At 8 times (after rent) that is $240.3m, less core debt of about $85m, $155.3m for the shares, or 54.8c a share.
- If it goes against us: the second-half rate ($12.48m times two) plus Karori is $27.6m. At 6 times that is $165.7m, less $85m, $80.7m, or 28.5c a share.
- The multiples are my stated assumptions, after rent, and the deep dive's first job is to test them against listed care operators and recent home sales. For reference, Radius paid $13.6m for Karori, about 5.1 times the $2.66m above. Each turn of the multiple is worth about 10.6c a share.
- Only now the price: the close on 30 October 2026 was 39c [13]. Gap if it goes our way, 54.8 / 39 less 1 = +40.5%. Gap if it goes against us, 28.5 / 39 less 1 = -27.0%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is 40.5%, the question can be answered from the filings (the half-year accounts and the underlying EBITDA reconciliation), and the 1H27 result on 11 November 2026 is inside six months. The A sits on the line: at 7.5 times rather than 8 the gap is 27%, a B. The liquidity check passed only narrowly on the data available and should be re-run on the latest 20 sessions first.
