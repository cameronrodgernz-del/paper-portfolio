# Australian Finance Group (ASX:AFG): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 7 points, #5 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

AFG has two parts: Distribution, a mortgage broker group (more than 4,300 brokers, about 1 in 9 Australian home loans,
68% of FY26 EBITDA), and Manufacturing, its own lender AFG Securities (loan book A$7.1bn at 30 June 2026, A$7.3bn at
30 September 2026), plus a 32% stake in commercial lender Thinktank and minority stakes in broker firms.

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. It is an operating broker group and lender.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Ernst & Young's opinion on the FY26
  accounts is unmodified (2026 annual report, 20 Aug 2026).
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$831,489 (volume times close,
  data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz; lowest session A$457,716). Two sessions at 10% of that median is about A$166,000.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv lists no
  companies yet, and AFG is not in gaming, wagering or racing.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is
  Australian Ethical at 7.56% (27 Aug 2026), and the company is buying back its own shares.

Nothing ends the sheet.

Does the gap between statutory and underlying profit recur? Yes, all of it. The trail book is revalued every half and
the change goes through profit each year; the amortisation is the same 2.4m charge each year from the Fintelligence
purchase; and "one-off non-recurring expenses" appear in both years (1.1m in FY25, 1.5m in FY26: project and staff
restructure costs both years, plus a lease make-good in FY25). By the template's test these are recurring costs, so statutory profit is the fairer base.

What the headline numbers hide:
- The lending book is not company debt. The ASX company data shows total liabilities of A$8,557.5m against equity of
  A$242.3m, which is why the ranking shows "net cash" of minus A$18.49 a share. A$7,246m of that is the warehouse and RMBS
  funding of AFG Securities, secured on the loans and with no recourse to the group. AFG's own corporate position is
  about flat: A$62.6m cash against A$66.1m drawn.
- What AFG does carry from the lender: A$43.2m of subordinated notes it holds in its own trusts, and the 5% risk
  retention required for its overseas bond investors (funded by separate risk retention facilities, A$154.9m in FY26).
- Margin above its own long-run aim. AFG Securities' net interest margin was 125bps in FY26 (exit 128bps) against a
  through-the-cycle aim of about 120bps. Each 5bps on a A$7.1bn book is about A$3.6m a year before tax. Six warehouse
  facilities expire 12 Oct 2026, 12 Nov 2026, 10 Dec 2026, 12 Apr 2027, 10 Jun 2027 and 13 Jun 2028; at the AGM one had
  been renewed below existing pricing and a A$1.2bn RMBS was priced inside warehouse costs.
- The trail book (future commissions on loans already written) is a A$1,225.4m asset against about A$1,141m owed to
  brokers, net A$83.9m, valued on run-off and discount-rate assumptions (discount rates 4.0% to 13.5%, average loan life
  3.5 to 4.1 years). The net value has fallen each year: A$96.2m (FY23), 90.6m, 86.8m, 83.9m.
- Concentration: all Australian. The company says about 10% of earnings comes from short-term residential upfront
  commissions and a 10% change in settlements moves NPAT by about 3% (AGM, 16 Oct 2026).
- Index selling: AFG left the S&P/ASX 300 on 21 Sep 2026; 13.85m shares (about 5.3% of the company) traded on
  18 September, the last day before (price file).

## 3. Guidance and record
AFG does not give profit guidance. What it has said:
- FY29 aspirations, set with the HY25 result (28 Feb 2025) and unchanged since: AFG Securities book A$9bn (A$7.1bn at
  FY26), net interest margin about 120bps (125bps), cost-to-income under 50% (55%), broker services 30% of Distribution
  gross profit (23%), 35 broker investments (6), residential settlements growing 8% a year (18% in FY26). Source: FY26
  presentation p5.
- FY27 (20 Aug 2026, repeated at the AGM on 16 Oct 2026): about A$13m of extra EBITDA carried into FY27 from FY26
  investments, assuming the AFG Securities book stays at A$7.1bn and the margin is 120bps. No profit number.
- Q1 FY27 trading (AGM): broker lodgements A$26.8bn, second-highest first quarter; AFG Securities lodgements about A$1.1bn,
  18% below a year earlier; book A$7.3bn; loans 90+ days in arrears 0.58% (0.74% a year earlier). The RBA has raised the
  cash rate to its highest level in 15 years (Chair's address).
- No changes to any of this during 2026.

Last two years against delivery: there was no numeric guidance to test. The FY25 release (27 Aug 2025) said it expected
AFG Securities' trading conditions to stay positive in the rate-cutting cycle; FY26 manufacturing EBITDA more than doubled
(up 107%), but the rate cycle has since turned.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is FY26's A$48.5m profit a base AFG can hold, or a peak? Its lending margin sits above its own long-run
  aim, half its warehouse funding renews within eight months, rates are rising and its own lending volumes fell 18% in
  the first quarter; against that, only about 10% of profit depends on new home-loan volumes. The price looks like it
  treats AFG as a bet on housing volumes.
- If it goes our way: FY26 statutory profit of A$48.5m holds and AFG is valued at its own average price-to-earnings at
  30 June over FY22 to FY26. From the FY26 annual report's five-year table (share price at 30 June and reported profit):
  FY22 10.3 times, FY23 13.0, FY24 12.9, FY25 17.7, FY26 8.9; average 12.5 (FY22 and FY23 earnings per share approximated
  as profit over about 270m shares). 48.5 x 12.5 = A$609m. Corporate net debt is only A$3.5m, so no adjustment.
  A$609m / 263.7m shares = A$2.31.
- If it goes against us: profit falls back to the five-year average (38.8, 37.3, 29.0, 35.0, 48.5: A$37.7m) and AFG is
  valued like the listed non-bank lenders, Resimac (6.5 times) and Pepper Money (7.5 times) in the ASX company data,
  average 7.0. 37.7 x 7.0 = A$264m / 263.7m = A$1.00.
- Only now the price: A$1.49 (close 30 October 2026). Gap if it goes our way: 2.31 / 1.49 - 1 = +55%. Gap if it goes
  against us: 1.00 / 1.49 - 1 = -33%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is 55%, above 40%. The question can be worked from filings (segment results,
the margin and funding terms in the annual and half-year reports, trail book assumptions, arrears, the quarterly index),
and the HY27 result due about 18 February 2027, inside six months, should show whether the margin and profit held
through the warehouse renewals.
