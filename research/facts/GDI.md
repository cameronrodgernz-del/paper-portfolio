# GDI Property Group (ASX:GDI): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 4 points, #60 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-08).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

GDI is a stapled company and trust that owns and leases Perth CBD office buildings (Westralia Square WS1 and WS2, the Mill
Green precinct of 197 St Georges Terrace, 5 Mill Street and 1 Mill Street, 180 Hay Street) and two Perth CBD car parks,
consolidates 43.7% of a Townsville office trust, manages unlisted property trusts for wholesale investors (A$260.4m of
assets) and owns 50% of a mining accommodation ("co-living") joint venture with Tulla Group [1][2].

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. A stapled property group with an internal
  manager that leases its own buildings, earns fund management fees and runs half of an operating accommodation business.
  It is valued as listed property (section 6), as the desk did for Centuria Office REIT [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. FY26 accounts audited by Hall Chadwick
  (NSW) with an unqualified opinion dated 24 Aug 2026; prepared on a going concern basis [3].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$173,832 a day (lowest day
  A$26,377), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz [27].
- On Cam's excluded list? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries (confirmed by Cam on 8 Nov 2026).
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. No bid, review or sale process
  has been announced. The largest holder, Robert Steven Rockefeller and associates, has built from 5.01% (1 Apr 2026) to
  10.11% (14 Sep 2026) by on-market purchases [18]; that is a holder build-up, not a holder over 50%, so it is noted in the
  report back rather than ending the sheet.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs, and so does the larger gap between FFO and cash. FFO adds back the
amortisation of lease incentives, and GDI has given large incentives in each of the last two years while it leased up
Westralia Square and 197 St Georges Terrace. The desk's cash earnings (FFO less rent-free given, leasing fees, maintenance
and fit-out spending, the measure the template calls AFFO) were A$16.4m or 3.04c a security in FY26 and A$0.9m or 0.17c in
FY25, against a 5.0c distribution [3]. The distribution was met from asset sales and debt; GDI says FY27's may again be
paid "wholly or partly out of capital" [1].

What else the headline numbers hide:
- One city. Every wholly owned building except Townsville is in Perth; Westralia Square (A$505m with WS2) is over half the
  A$962.6m portfolio, and the WA State Government leases 16,347 square metres of WS1 to FY34 [2].
- Vacancy outside the core: 180 Hay Street (A$18.8m) has no tenants shown, 1 Mill Street (A$36.5m) "is not being actively
  marketed for lease" and is excluded from the 90% occupancy figure, and Townsville is 86.2% let [2].
- The Co-living JV's FFO (A$10.1m) is GDI's share of earnings, but only A$5.2m came back in cash in FY26 [3]. Its Moranbah
  village has a take-or-pay contract with Stanmore Resources until 31 Mar 2027, which Stanmore can end or shrink on six
  months' notice with compensation [8].
- Funds management is shrinking as GDI sells its trusts' assets: assets under management fell from A$346.0m to A$260.4m,
  and the Autoleague trust has now sold its last properties, giving GDI about A$12.2m of capital back and about A$6.2m of
  disposal and performance fees in FY27, after which its management fee ends [3][10].
- Goodwill is small (A$18.1m, under 3% of equity), so book value is mostly property at independent valuation (weighted
  capitalisation rate 6.8%) [1][3].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: the price is about half of net tangible assets, and GDI's cash earnings after incentives and fit-outs
  (3.04c in FY26, 0.17c in FY25) have not covered its 5.0c distribution. Now that WS1, WS2 and the 197 St Georges Terrace
  offices are fully leased, does that spending fall away so cash earnings rise toward the distribution? A deep dive would
  read the lease expiry profile, the incentive terms on recent deals, and the 1H FY27 spending.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: listed office owners trade on cash earnings after leasing costs (AFFO: FFO
  less maintenance, fit-out and cash incentives, and rent-free given). Three named peers, each on its FY26 filings and its
  30 Oct 2026 close (price file):
  - Centuria Office REIT (ASX:COF): FFO A$66.9m less rent-free A$25.6m (taken from its amortisation), capex incentives
    A$10.5m and maintenance A$7.1m is A$23.7m, 3.97c on 597.3m units; at A$0.875, 4.54% [25][26][30].
  - Abacus Group (ASX:ABG): FFO A$81.2m less maintenance A$12.6m, tenant incentives A$10.3m and rent abatement A$18.1m is
    A$40.2m, 4.50c on 893.7m securities; at A$0.84, 5.36% [28][30].
  - Growthpoint Properties Australia (ASX:GOZ): FFO A$177.6m less maintenance A$15.2m, leasing capex A$19.2m, cash
    incentives and leasing costs A$11.3m and rent-free A$30.8m is A$101.1m, 13.40c on 754.3m securities; at A$1.965,
    6.82% [29][30].
  - The middle peer, Abacus at 5.36%, is used. COF is the most richly priced of the three; the research desk's COF deep
    dive (research/picks/COF.md) reached the same peer yields and also put Dexus at 7.2% to 7.8%.
  - Check basis, leaving rent-free in for everyone (FFO less maintenance and fit-out or capex incentives): COF 8.26c,
    9.44%; Abacus 6.52c, 7.77%; Growthpoint 17.49c, 8.90%; middle peer Growthpoint, 8.90%.
  - The price would get there through half-year results that show lower leasing spending and a covered distribution
    (1H FY27 about February 2027), helped by the buy-back as a steady buyer.
- If it goes our way: fit-out spending stops now the core towers are full, and rent-free, leasing fees and maintenance stay
  at FY26 levels. Cash earnings A$44.457m - 16.718m - 0.902m - 1.108m = A$25.73m, 4.77c a security; at 5.36% that is
  A$0.89. Check basis (FFO less maintenance and leasing fees, 7.87c, at 8.90%): A$0.88. At COF's yield it would be A$1.05.
- If it goes against us: FY26 spending recurs, including A$9.4m of fit-outs: 3.04c at 5.36% is A$0.57 (check basis 6.13c at
  8.90%, A$0.69). If spending ran at the FY25 and FY26 average instead (1.60c), the value would be A$0.30.
- The good case, not "our way": net tangible assets A$1.21 (A$1.25 after a full 5% buy-back at A$0.64). No sale, capital
  return of that size or bid is under way; the buy-back has bought 0.1% so far.
- Only now the price: A$0.64, the last trade on 6 Nov 2026 (ASX company data; the session before also closed at A$0.64).
  Market value A$345.1m; 0.53 times NTA; 7.8% distribution yield. Gap if it goes our way: 0.89 / 0.64 - 1 = +39% (check
  basis +38%). Gap if it goes against us: 0.57 / 0.64 - 1 = -11% (check basis +8%; at the two-year average spending -53%).
  At net tangible assets, +89%.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is +39% on the middle peer's cash yield (+38% on the
check basis), above 25% but just under the 40% an A needs, and the value if it goes against us is 11% below the price, just
outside the value pick limit of 10%. The answer needs the event itself: the 1H FY27 result (about February 2027) will show
whether leasing spending has fallen now the core towers are full. On FY26 cash earnings the market prices GDI between
Abacus and Growthpoint, so the case needs those earnings to rise; NTA (+89%) only counts if a sale, return or bid
appears. The holder build-up (Rockefeller at 10.11%) and the chair's stated aim to close the NTA gap are noted for the
special situations watch list.
