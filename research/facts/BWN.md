## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. A marine services company: more than 70 owned vessels plus leased ones, crews and divers, serving offshore energy and resources, ports and inshore work and defence around Australia; since 31 March 2026 it also owns Riverside Marine (Rivtow harbour towage, Riverside Industrial Sands, Magnetic Island Ferries, Riverside Oceanic charters) [1][3].

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs: deal costs were left out in both years (A$0.7m for the IPO in FY25, A$3.6m for Riverside in FY26), and there were impairments in both (A$0.2m, A$1.7m) [3][4]. More deals are part of the stated plan [3].

What the headline numbers hide:
- Almost all of the group's cash earnings now come from Riverside. The original Bhagwan business made A$40.0m of EBITDA in FY26, but paid A$13.6m on vessel and property leases and spent A$18.1m of sustaining capital on its fleet, leaving A$8.3m: sustaining capital spending is 68% of its EBITDA after leases [1][3]. Riverside has no vessel lease payments and A$7m to A$8m a year of maintenance spending against A$24.5m of FY26 EBITDA [6]. Statutory free cash flow for FY26 was negative: operating cash flow A$36.4m less A$27.2m of vessel spending and A$13.6m of lease payments is minus A$4.4m, before A$3.0m of dividends [1].
- Vessels are carried at a valuer's depreciated replacement cost: A$197.4m, against A$119.3m had they been kept at cost. A further A$8.5m (A$5.8m after tax) revaluation went through equity in FY26 [1]. NTA at cost, after tax on the uplift, would be about A$0.23 a share.
- The company's net debt figure counts as cash an unsecured A$12.0m loan to BM Fleet Pty Ltd, owned by the managing director Loui Kannikoski and the chair Anthony Wooles. A$6.1m of it bears 8% interest (accrued, not paid) and A$5.9m bears none; repayment comes only from BM Fleet's spare cash after its NAB loan, and none is expected within 12 months [1].
- Other related-party dealings: vessel leases from BM Fleet and from KFAMS Pty Ltd (the managing director's son), A$1.4m paid in FY26; the Dampier base leased from the Kannikoski Property Trust to 30 June 2027; family members employed [1].
- Riverside was bought for an upfront A$120m (A$100m cash, 48.8m shares at A$0.41, plus a A$2.6m working capital adjustment) on vendor guidance of A$25.2m to A$27.2m of FY26 EBITDA; it delivered A$24.5m, so the A$10m earn-out was not triggered [1][6][8].
- Customer concentration has fallen: one customer was 10.3% of FY26 revenue (A$24.2m), against two customers at 20.9% and 10.7% in FY25 [1].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is the original Bhagwan fleet a cash business? In FY26 it made A$40.0m of EBITDA but only A$8.3m after lease payments and A$18.1m of sustaining capital spending, so the group's cash earnings rest on Riverside, bought with A$70m of new debt. A deep dive would settle from the filings whether A$18m a year is the real cost of keeping the fleet running (depreciation of A$20.6m says it is about right) and whether the 1Q27 revenue recovery carries the core back to FY25-level EBITDA. The 1H27 result in February 2027 shows both.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: vessel operators are bought and sold on enterprise value against cash earnings. The best primary-source price is Bhagwan's own purchase of Riverside in February 2026: A$122.6m of enterprise value (cash-free and debt-free) for A$24.5m of EBITDA less A$7.5m of maintenance spending, or 7.21 times cash earnings (5.0 times EBITDA). The mechanism that would close the gap is results: half-year cash earnings and falling debt.
- If it goes our way: the core runs at its 1Q27 revenue rate (A$63.7m a quarter, A$254.8m a year) at its FY26 EBITDA margin of 18.1%: A$46.2m, less A$13.6m of leases and A$18.1m of sustaining spending, A$14.5m. Riverside grows 6% (its 1Q27 rate) to A$63.6m of revenue at its 40.8% margin: A$25.9m, less A$7.5m, A$18.4m. Cash earnings A$32.9m at 7.21 times is A$237.6m; less net debt of A$74.5m (the company's figure, counting the BM Fleet loan at face value), equity A$163.1m, or A$0.410 a share on 397.9m shares.
- If it goes against us: the core stays at its second-half FY26 rate (A$17.6m a half, A$35.2m a year), leaving A$3.5m after leases and sustaining spending; Riverside stays at A$24.5m, A$17.0m after maintenance. Cash earnings A$20.5m at 7.21 times is A$148.1m; less net debt of A$86.5m (counting the BM Fleet loan at nil), equity A$61.6m, or A$0.155 a share.
- The good case, not "our way": net tangible assets after removing goodwill and acquired contracts, A$147.1m or A$0.370 a share, with vessels at the valuer's A$197.4m. Valued at 5.0 times EBITDA after leases, the multiple Bhagwan paid on Riverside's EBITDA, the group would be worth about A$0.55, but that ignores how much more capital the core fleet needs than Riverside does.
- Only now the price: the last sale on 6 November 2026 was A$0.265 (bid A$0.27, offer A$0.29; the ranking used A$0.27), from data/research_data/asx_company.json. Gap if it goes our way, 0.410 / 0.265 less 1 = +55% (+52% at A$0.27). Gap if it goes against us, 0.155 / 0.265 less 1 = -42%. The good case, NTA of A$0.370, is +40%.
- For scale: the directors bought on market at A$0.28 to A$0.33 in May and June 2026, the Harvey trust paid about A$0.29 for 26.8m shares in May, and the February placement and Riverside scrip were at A$0.41 [6][13][16].

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is 55%. The question can be answered from filings: the half-year and annual reports split EBITDA between the original business and Riverside and give lease payments and sustaining capital spending. The 1H27 trading update (about late January 2027) and result (about 26 February 2027) fall inside six months. Not a value pick: the value if it goes against us is 42% below the price, because nearly all of the cash earnings sit in the newly bought business and the debt is new. The deep dive should also weigh the related-party loan and leases, and the escrow release of 6.1% of the shares on 31 March 2027.
