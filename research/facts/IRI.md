## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. IR sells Prognosis, software that monitors payments, unified communications and IT infrastructure for large banks, telcos and government agencies, mostly on one-to-five-year term licences; 138 staff [1].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Ernst & Young's opinion on the June 2026 accounts is unmodified; its key audit matter is revenue recognition on multi-element contracts [1].
- Too thin to trade? No, but close. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$12,362 a day (lowest day A$11), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz. Two sessions at 10% caps a position near A$2,470.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The founder, Stephen Killelea, holds 28.6%; no holder is over 50% and no approach or review has been announced [2].

The gap between statutory and the company's own measures recurs, but it is mainly timing, not one-off costs left out. Statutory revenue books a term licence in full when it is signed; pro forma revenue spreads it over the term. The gap has run both ways: statutory was A$8.5m above pro forma in FY24, then A$6.0m below in FY25 and A$8.2m below in FY26 [3]. The FY26 credit loss of A$5.0m is not a repeat of anything in FY25 (A$0.1m), and currency moved from a A$0.5m gain to a A$2.7m loss [1].

What the headline numbers hide:
- The 18.7% yield is a one-off. It counts the 2c special dividend, and the whole 5c was more than seven times FY26 earnings a share. The policy is a minimum of 25% of free cash flow [3].
- The "net cash about 103% of market value" uses cash before the October dividend. After it, net cash is about A$42.7m.
- Cash flow came partly from the receivable book running down. IR lets creditworthy customers pay for a licence over its one-to-five-year term, while statutory revenue books it in full at signing, so unpaid licence fees sit in receivables [1]. Receivables fell from A$73.7m in June 2025 to A$58.2m in June 2026; A$4.9m of the fall was the new credit loss allowance and most of the rest was cash collected [1][3]. When fewer multi-year licences are signed, that book turns into cash once; it is not a repeatable earnings stream.
- The business is shrinking. Pro forma revenue was A$75.6m in FY23, A$74.8m in FY24, A$74.3m in FY25 and A$65.8m in FY26. Term subscription revenue fell 11%, total contract value signed fell 19% to A$54.3m, renewals fell 21% to A$38.5m and new business fell 21% to A$10.9m [3]. Collaborate, the largest product, kept 83% of its revenue from existing customers in constant currency in both FY25 and FY26 [1]. Average contract terms are getting shorter (3.2 years on FY26 licences) [1].
- Costs are not falling. Operating costs before credit losses were A$53.1m against A$54.4m in FY25, with product and technology spending up 29% to A$17.4m, and management says new-product spending "will impact profit performance over the short to medium term" [1][3].
- Where the cash goes. The capital allocation framework puts 30% to 35% of available capital into product, 20% to 35% into a reserve for acquisitions and 30% to 35% into a contingency reserve [3]. The cash is not set aside for holders.
- Concentration. The Americas were A$41.3m of A$57.6m revenue (72%) [1]. The largest single customer balance was A$4.4m [1].
- FY24's A$27.1m profit and 15.57c of earnings a share followed an unusually large renewals year (statutory revenue A$83.3m); FY23 was a A$29.2m loss [1][3][6].

## 3. Guidance and record
IR gives no full-year guidance at the start of a year. It gives ranges in trading updates shortly before each result.

This year:
- 14 Nov 2025: first half FY26 ranges, revenue A$25m to A$30.5m, EBITDA loss A$3m to A$8m, contract value A$23m to A$28m [7]. Delivered: revenue A$28.3m, EBITDA loss A$3.1m, contract value A$26.8m [4].
- 26 Feb 2026: no numbers; "softer FY26 renewals book and investments in product led growth will impact profit performance over the short-medium term" [4].
- 24 Jul 2026: FY26 ranges, revenue A$56m to A$58m, pro forma revenue A$64m to A$66m, EBITDA loss A$2m to A$4m [8]. Delivered: A$57.6m, A$65.8m and a loss of A$2.1m [3].
- 27 Aug 2026: no FY27 numbers; "the FY27 renewals book is weighted to the second half" [3].

Last two years: every range given was met or beaten. For the first half of FY25 the ranges were raised on 16 January 2025 (revenue A$28m to A$29m, EBITDA A$3.9m to A$4.5m) [9]; for FY25 the 17 July 2025 update gave revenue A$67m to A$69m, pro forma A$73m to A$75m and EBITDA A$15m to A$17m [10], and the result was A$68.3m, A$74.3m and A$15.9m [11]. Because the ranges come within weeks of the period end, the record says little about forecasting.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: has the Prognosis contract book stopped shrinking? Pro forma revenue fell 12% in FY26 to A$65.8m against costs of about A$54m, so the business only just covers its costs, and the price seems to value it at no more than its cash. The first-half FY27 numbers will show whether new business and the new AI products hold pro forma revenue near A$65m or whether it keeps falling at 12% a year.
- If it goes our way: pro forma revenue holds near A$65.8m and costs stay at FY26's level, so pro forma EBITDA is about A$12.1m once the one-offs are taken out (reported A$8.7m, plus A$1.7m of credit losses left in it, plus A$2.7m of currency losses, less A$1.0m of other income). Less A$0.7m of depreciation and 30% tax, earnings are about A$8.0m. At 8 times earnings, a low multiple for a business that has stopped shrinking but isn't growing, that is A$63.8m. Add net cash after the October dividend of A$42.7m: A$106.5m on 182.39m shares, A$0.584 a share. Net tangible assets after the dividend, 48.8c a share, are a cross-check.
- If it goes against us: pro forma revenue keeps falling 12% a year for two more years (A$57.9m in FY27, A$51.0m in FY28) while costs stay at about A$53.7m, as management's product spending plans suggest. Pro forma EBITDA is A$4.2m in FY27 and a loss of A$2.7m in FY28, A$1.5m in total, and the business is then worth nothing. That leaves net cash of A$42.7m plus A$1.5m, A$44.1m, or A$0.242 a share. This leaves out interest on the cash (about A$1m to A$2m a year after tax). A third year of the same decline would take it to A$0.193; an acquisition from the reserve, or another large credit loss, would also take it lower.
- Price: A$0.2675 (last trade in the ASX company data fetched 7 Nov 2026; Norgate close 30 Oct 2026 A$0.26), market value A$48.8m. Gap if it goes our way: 0.584 / 0.2675 - 1 = +118%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.242 / 0.2675 - 1 = -10% (-9.6% unrounded).

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now, as a value pick (rulebook section 5a). The gap if it goes our way is +118%, and the value if it goes against us is 9.6% below the price, just inside the 10% allowed. No forcing event is needed for a value pick. The question can be answered from filings; the first-half FY27 trading update (about mid-November 2026) and result (about 26 February 2027) are the first reads, and the second-half renewals book, reported in July and August 2027, is the real test.

The margin on the bad case is thin: at A$0.26 the bad case is 7% below the price, but one more year of decline puts it 28% below. The deep dive should test it harder: what the pro forma revenue trend looks like by product in the first half; how safe the A$58.2m receivable book is after the A$3.8m loss on one client, with A$22.3m of it due after more than a year; whether the board would really spend the acquisition reserve; and whether costs can come down if revenue doesn't hold. With Killelea at 28.6% and van Paassen adding to 6.2%, the register is concentrated, so how the founder views a capital return is worth a look. Liquidity is the binding limit: at A$12,362 a day the position can be no larger than about A$2,470.
