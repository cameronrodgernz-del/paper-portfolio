# Trajan Group Holdings (ASX:TRJ): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #30 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Trajan makes consumables and components for scientific analysis (syringes, chromatography columns and parts, the core of
the group), capital equipment (lab automation, through Axel Semrau in Germany and others) and newer microsampling devices
for collecting blood at home. About 600 staff and seven factories in the US, Australia, Europe and Malaysia; about 85% of
revenue is earned in US dollars and euros (8 Oct 2026 presentation). All figures are A$. Financial year ends 30 June.

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An operating manufacturer.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. RSM Australia's opinion on the FY26
  accounts is unmodified, with no going-concern note (27 Aug 2026).
- Too thin to trade? No, but close to the line on some days. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 2 to 30 October
  2026 was A$16,133 (volume times close, data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz), above the A$10,000 floor; the lowest
  session was A$16. Two sessions at 10% of the median is about A$3,200.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv lists no
  companies yet, and Trajan is a scientific instruments maker.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? There is a holder over 50%, and I
  judge its moves too small to count, but the facts are here. Founder and CEO Stephen Tomisich's family company holds
  76,878,745 shares, 50.3% (51.17% in its last notice, 23 Jun 2022). In the last 12 months he bought 100,106 shares on
  market in November 2025 (0.07% of the company) and wrote to shareholders on 27 Mar 2026 that the valuation was
  "dramatically out of line with our industry peers". No proposal, review or sale has been announced. Noted for the
  special situations watch list in the report back.

Nothing ends the sheet.

Does the gap between statutory and underlying profit recur? Yes. The "non-recurring" costs came both years (A$1.6m then
A$1.2m), and both years included redundancies and refinancing legal fees. The amortisation of acquired intangibles is a
real, recurring charge. nEBITDA overstates repeatable earnings by about A$1.2m to A$1.6m a year.

What the headline numbers hide:
- The book is mostly goodwill. The ranking's "0.38 times book" uses equity of A$98.5m; A$80.9m of that is goodwill and
  acquired intangibles (Axel Semrau, LEAP PAL Parts and the consumables business). Tangible assets are 12c a share.
- Debt against earnings: net debt is 2.6 times FY26 nEBITDA (3.4 times including leases), and cash generation fell from
  A$10.8m to A$0.2m as working capital rose (buffer stock and slower collections). Principal repayments started on
  30 Jun 2026.
- Currency: about 85% of revenue is in US dollars and euros; the company says the stronger Australian dollar cut FY26
  revenue by about A$5m and nEBITDA by A$2.4m. Hedges run to June 2027, but their gains are left out of nEBITDA.
- Lost revenue: the Certus product line stopped (about A$2.1m of FY26 revenue).
- Concentration: the founder controls the company; a former related-party lease of the Ringwood site (with Bass Park
  Investments, a Tomisich family entity) ended when the property was sold in May 2024.

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance (AGM, 28 Oct 2026): FY27 revenue A$162m to A$166m and nEBITDA A$16.0m to A$17.5m at current exchange
rates, the nEBITDA midpoint 27% above FY26. Q1 FY27 nEBITDA was about A$3.5m from management accounts (8 Oct 2026), a
rolling 12-month A$16.2m.

Changes in 2026:
- 5 Feb 2026: FY26 guidance "maintained": revenue above A$170m and nEBITDA above A$16.0m.
- 27 Mar 2026: letter to shareholders, "we remain committed to our projections".
- 27 May 2026: stronger Australian dollar expected to cut H2 by about A$4m of revenue and A$2m of nEBITDA, an implied
  downgrade.
- 27 Aug 2026: FY27 guidance of "mid-single digit organic revenue growth" and "double-digit nEBITDA growth".
- 28 Oct 2026: the numeric FY27 range above.

Last two years against delivery:
- FY25: revenue guidance A$160m to A$165m, delivered A$166.5m (beat); nEBITDA guidance A$17m to A$19m, delivered
  A$15.5m (missed by 9% at the bottom of the range).
- FY26: revenue A$170m to A$180m and nEBITDA A$16m to A$19m (28 Aug 2025), delivered A$161.4m and A$13.2m (missed both:
  revenue 5% under the bottom, nEBITDA 18% under). The company says that, without the exchange-rate effect in H2,
  revenue and nEBITDA were within 2.5% of the guidance as updated in May.
Two misses on earnings in two years: the FY27 range deserves a discount.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: does Trajan reach its FY27 nEBITDA guidance of A$16.0m to A$17.5m (after missing in FY25 and FY26) and
  start paying down debt? With net debt and leases of about A$44m against a market value of about A$39m, a small change
  in earnings moves the shares a lot either way.
- How the value is worked: enterprise value (shares at the 30 June close, plus bank debt less cash, plus leases, since
  nEBITDA is struck before lease costs) over that year's nEBITDA, the multiple the market paid at each year-end:
  - 30 Jun 2024: A$1.025 x 152.2m + 32.9m + 12.1m = A$201.0m over A$12.3m = 16.4 times.
  - 30 Jun 2025: A$0.715 x 152.4m + 29.5m + 14.1m = A$152.6m over A$15.5m = 9.9 times.
  - 30 Jun 2026: A$0.20 x 152.9m + 34.6m + 10.9m = A$76.1m over A$13.2m = 5.8 times.
  - Three-year average 10.7 times.
- If it goes our way: FY27 nEBITDA at the guidance midpoint, A$16.75m, valued at the three-year average multiple: A$178.9m,
  less net debt of about A$33.0m (June A$34.6m less the A$1.6m Q1 cut) and leases of A$10.9m, is A$135.0m, A$0.88 a share.
  At the 30 June 2025 multiple alone (9.9 times) it is A$0.79.
- If it goes against us: nEBITDA stays at FY26's A$13.2m and the market keeps paying its lowest multiple (5.8 times):
  A$76.1m less A$33.0m and A$10.9m is A$32.2m, A$0.21 a share. Below that sits tangible book of 12c, where debt-funded
  goodwill would be worth nothing.
- Only now the price: A$0.255 (last trade before 8 Nov 2026, ASX company data; A$0.25 at the 30 Oct close). Gap if it
  goes our way: 0.88 / 0.255 - 1 = +246% (+211% at the June 2025 multiple). Gap if it goes against us: 0.21 / 0.255 - 1 =
  -18%. At A$0.255 the enterprise value is about A$83m, 6.3 times FY26 nEBITDA and 4.9 times the FY27 midpoint.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is far above 40%, the question can be worked from filings (half-year
segment results, the debt terms and covenants, working capital, two years of guidance misses and their causes), and the
1H27 result about 26 Feb 2027, inside six months, should show whether nEBITDA and debt are on track. The deep dive must
weigh the record of missed guidance, the thin trading on some days, and the bank's position if earnings slip again; the
"against" value is 18% below the price, so this is not a value pick.
