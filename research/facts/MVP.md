# Medical Developments International (ASX:MVP): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #25 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

MVP makes Penthrox, an inhaled non-opioid pain reliever used by ambulances, hospital emergency departments and the
defence force, sold directly in Australia and through partners in Europe and elsewhere (Pain Management, A$31.6m of FY26
revenue), and respiratory devices sold mainly in the US and Australia (Respiratory, A$10.9m). It is based in Scoresby,
Victoria. All figures are A$. Financial year ends 30 June.

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An operating drug and device maker.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Deloitte's opinion on the FY26 accounts
  is unmodified (20 Aug 2026).
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$65,899 (volume times close,
  data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz; lowest session A$8,046). Two sessions at 10% of that median is about A$13,200.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv lists no
  companies yet, and MVP is a healthcare company.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is David
  Williams at 11.62%. One holder is building a stake: Payne Media, from nothing to 7.55% between June and October 2026,
  all on market (section 4). That is not a bid, but it is a holder build-up, noted in the report back.

Nothing ends the sheet.

Latest quarter (Q1 FY27, to 30 Sep 2026, 4C of 15 Oct 2026): revenue 10.2m, down 0.7m on a year earlier; operating
cash flow -0.3m; cash 20.6m.

Does the gap between statutory and underlying profit recur? There is no underlying figure. The swings that matter are
foreign exchange (a 1.6m gain in FY25, a 0.9m loss in FY26) and FY26 items the company says won't repeat (below).

What the headline numbers hide:
- FY26 revenue was helped by partners filling their stores, partly because supply to France moved to a partner from
  1 July 2025; the company's FY27 outlook expects "non-recurrence of Penthrox inventory stocking benefits", and Q1 FY27
  revenue duly fell 6%.
- Respiratory's FY26 segment result was helped by a refund of US tariffs paid in FY25 and FY26; its revenue fell 15%.
- In-market demand is growing faster than the reported sales: Q1 FY27 European in-market volume up 37% (helped by the
  new paediatric label in the UK and EU) and Australian hospital volume up 24%.
- FY27 brings about 1m a year of amortisation of the capitalised European paediatric registration costs.
- Goodwill and intangibles are 21.2m, 38% of equity (Pain Management carries 3.8m of goodwill and 16.1m of other
  intangibles; the auditor reviewed the value-in-use test as a key audit matter and no impairment was booked).
- Concentration: one product, Penthrox, earns all of the group's segment profit beyond Respiratory's 1.0m; corporate
  costs of about 9m a year take most of it.
- Leadership: the CEO, Brent MacGregor, is leaving (announced 15 Oct 2026); CFO Anita James is interim CEO while the
  board searches.

## 3. Guidance and record
MVP gives no numeric profit guidance. FY27 outlook (20 Aug 2026, repeated 15 Oct 2026): higher in-market Penthrox
demand, stable Respiratory demand, no repeat of the stocking benefit, about 1m of new amortisation, and uncertain effects
from Middle East supply disruption and US tariffs. No changes during 2026. With no numeric guidance in FY25 or FY26,
there is no record to test.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: once the FY26 stocking benefit drops out, does faster in-market Penthrox demand (Europe up 37%, Australian
  hospitals up 24% in Q1 FY27) lift group earnings above break-even, or do corporate costs of about 9m a year keep taking
  nearly all of Pain Management's 11.4m?
- If it goes our way: Penthrox growth turns into profit and the market pays what it has paid for MVP's book over the
  last three year-ends. Price to book at 30 June: A$0.405 / A$0.530 = 0.76 times (2024); A$0.55 / A$0.489 = 1.12
  (2025); A$0.395 / A$0.495 = 0.80 (2026); average 0.90. Value: 0.90 x A$0.495 = A$0.44 a share. A check on that number,
  using a multiple chosen by the desk rather than one taken from a peer: net cash of A$20.6m (30 Sep 2026) plus FY26
  group EBITDA of A$2.9m at 10 times is A$49.6m, A$0.44 a share. At the best of the three years (1.12 times book) the
  value would be A$0.56.
- If it goes against us: earnings stay near break-even and the Penthrox registrations and goodwill are worth nothing to
  a buyer of the shares: net tangible assets of A$0.307 a share (cash, working capital and plant).
- Only now the price: A$0.40 (last trade before 8 Nov 2026, ASX company data; A$0.365 at the 30 Oct close). Gap if it
  goes our way: 0.44 / 0.40 - 1 = +11%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.307 / 0.40 - 1 = -23%. At A$0.40 the market values
  the business, net of cash, at about A$24m, 0.6 times revenue.

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive. The gap if it goes our way is 11%, under 25% (39% even at the best price to book of the last three
years, still under the 40% an A needs). The 1H27 result (about 19 Feb 2027) will show whether Penthrox growth reaches
profit, and the Payne Media stake build is worth watching.
