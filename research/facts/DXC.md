# Dexus Convenience Retail REIT (ASX:DXC): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #27 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

DXC is a stapled group of three trusts that owns 91 service station and convenience retail sites, valued at A$779m at 30
June 2026, mostly on Australia's east coast and leased to fuel and food operators [1]. It has no staff: Dexus Asset
Management (a Dexus subsidiary) is the responsible entity and another Dexus company is the investment manager, paid a fee
on gross assets [1]. Financial year ends 30 June. All figures A$.

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No, on the brief's rule: a trust that owns and runs
  real property counts as a business. It holds no listed securities or loans.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The FY26 audit opinion is unmodified [1].
  The accounts note a A$7.4m net current asset deficiency, mainly distributions payable (A$7.1m) and accrued capital
  spending (A$6.5m), against A$39.6m of undrawn facilities [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$380,244 a day (lowest
  session A$98,934), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv lists no companies.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. No approach, review or sale
  process has been announced since January 2025. The manager's group holds about 12.2% and MA Financial's funds 5.03%;
  neither has made a proposal [1][2]. Not for the special situations watch list.

The gap between statutory profit and FFO recurs every year (revaluation gains in each of FY25 and FY26, derivative swings
both ways). It is not cash.

What the headline numbers hide:
- "6.7 times earnings" is statutory profit inflated by A$27.4m of revaluation gains. On FFO the multiple is about 13.
- Tenant concentration: Chevron 25% of income, Viva Energy 22%, Ampol 14%, 7-Eleven 13%, BP, United and Mobil 4% each,
  convenience retail and other 14%; 62 tenant operators [6]. Chevron's Australian fuel business is being sold to ENEOS,
  expected to complete in 2027 [6]. Occupancy 99.2%, weighted lease expiry 7.6 years, 86% of income expiring in FY31 or
  later, 73% of income on fixed increases of about 3.1% a year and 27% on CPI [1][6].
- Valuations: 63 of 91 properties (71% by value) were valued by outside valuers during FY26, the rest internally; the
  average capitalisation rate is 6.18% (metropolitan 6.10%, highway 6.08%, regional 6.71%), down 14 basis points in the
  year [1][6]. The portfolio's FY26 property income before fees (A$44.75m) is 5.74% of book value, below the 6.18% cap
  rate, because book value includes a new development with a part year of rent and assets held for sale.
- Sales evidence: three small assets sold for A$8.0m at 1.4% above book (May 2026, settling after 30 June) [7].
- Fees to the Dexus group: base management fee 0.65% a year of gross assets up to A$0.5bn and 0.60% from A$0.5bn to
  A$1.0bn (A$4.80m in FY26), property management about 2.0% of gross income (A$0.97m payable), custody A$0.14m [1]. The
  base fee is about 11% of property income after property costs (A$44.75m) and 17% of FFO.
- Cost of debt 4.8% (FY25 4.5%), 66% hedged on average; FY27 distribution guidance of 20.9c is "modestly above 100% of
  FFO", so the payout will not be fully covered by FFO next year [1][8].
- Rent actually received: cash receipts of A$59.27m including GST, about A$53.9m without it, are 98.6% of property
  revenue [1].
- The buy-back has not narrowed the discount so far: A$2.76 on 9 Mar 2026, the day it began, and A$2.73 on 30 Oct
  2026; with distributions the return over that time was about +4.9% (Norgate closes and total-return series) [18].

## 3. Guidance and record

and current interest rate expectations (10 Aug 2026) [8]. The payout policy was changed to 95% to 110% of FFO [8].

Changes this year:
- 9 Feb 2026: FY26 guidance of 20.9c FFO and distributions confirmed [3].
- 19 Jun 2026: buy-back target raised from 2.5% to 5.0% of securities [9].
- 10 Aug 2026: FY26 delivered at 20.9c; FY27 distribution guidance 20.9c [8].

Record: FY25 guidance 20.6c, delivered 20.65c [10]. FY26 guidance 20.9c, delivered 20.9c [8].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: are the valuers right that these sites are worth A$779m (a 6.18% cap rate), and if so, does an external
  manager taking about A$4.8m a year mean holders never see that value? Sales at or above book and a buy-back below NTA
  support the valuations; the fee and the 47% of rent from two tenants are what the price seems to charge for.
- If it goes our way: the latest valuations less debt. Net assets A$524.03m at 30 June 2026, less about A$5.76m spent
  since then buying back 2,093,642 securities (135,674,399 less 133,580,757, at about A$2.75), on 133.58m securities:
  A$3.88 a security (A$3.86 as reported).
- If it goes against us: rent actually received, after the manager's fees and the trust's own costs, capitalised at the
  valuers' own 6.18%. FY26 property FFO A$44.75m less management fees A$4.80m and other costs A$0.92m is A$39.03m; at
  6.18% that is A$631.6m of property. Add the other assets and take off borrowings and other liabilities as in the
  accounts (net A$260.65m after the buy-back): A$370.9m, or A$2.78 a security. Two other bad answers give the same
  range or worse: property income before fees at a 7.0% cap rate, A$2.83; after fees and at 7.0%, A$2.22.
- Only now the price: A$2.73 (Norgate close 30 Oct 2026, ex the September distribution; the ASX's last trade on 6 Nov
  was A$2.76). Gap if it goes our way: 3.88 / 2.73 - 1 = +42.1% (+40.6% at A$2.76). Gap if it goes against us: 2.78 /
  2.73 - 1 = +1.7% (+0.6% at A$2.76); the harsher combined case is -18.6%. The FY27 guided distribution of 20.9c is a
  7.7% yield on A$2.73.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now, as a value pick (rulebook section 5a). The gap if it goes our way is +42% (just over the 40% line;
+40.6% at the last trade), and the value if it goes against us, on fees taken out and the valuers' cap rate kept, is
about the price rather than more than 10% below it. No forcing event is needed; the dated checks are the December
valuation update and the 8 Feb 2027 half-year result. The deep dive must test the harsher case (valuations 80 basis
points too generous and the fee drag together, A$2.22, -19%), which would fail the value pick test, and the tenant
risk from Chevron's sale to ENEOS.
