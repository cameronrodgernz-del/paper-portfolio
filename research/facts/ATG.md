## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Articore (formerly Redbubble) runs the Redbubble and TeePublic online marketplaces, where more than three million independent artists sell designs printed on demand by third-party printers, plus two small new businesses: Dashery (creator storefronts, launched January 2025) and Frankly Wearing (an Indian print-on-demand marketplace bought for US$0.9m, completed in May 2026). FY26 marketplace revenue was A$354.5m [1][2][8]. More than 80% of Redbubble and TeePublic marketplace revenue is in currencies other than the Australian dollar [2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The auditor's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified [1]. No borrowings [1].
- Too thin to trade? No, but close. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$16,421 a day, counting the one session with no trades as zero; 12 of the 20 were above A$10,000 (data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz) [16]. That caps a position near A$3,300.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No live one. The board ran a seven-month strategic review with Citizens Bank, which "included engagement with a range of potential counterparties", and on 24 March 2026 concluded that staying independent was the best path [8]. Before that, founder Martin Hosking and associates requisitioned a meeting to replace four directors; all eight resolutions failed on 22 August 2025, with 60% to 67% of votes against [9][10]. Hosking then sold 8.0m shares on 28 August 2026 and now holds 11.56% [11]. No holder is near 50%. A concluded review, a defeated board spill and a large founder sell-down: worth a line on the special situations watch list, but not a live situation.

Does the gap between statutory and underlying profit recur? Yes, partly. The "other" expenses that operating EBITDA leaves out appeared in both years (A$7.4m in FY25, A$1.2m in FY26), so they are not one-offs [2][4]. Depreciation and amortisation fell from A$11.7m to A$4.9m mainly because older capitalised development has now been written off or amortised, which flatters FY26 EBIT against FY25 [1][2].

What the headline numbers hide:
- All of FY26's profit came in the Christmas half. 1H operating EBITDA was A$15.3m and 2H A$1.1m; 2H EBIT was a loss [2][3]. FY27 guidance needs a bigger Christmas half than FY26's.
- Sales are shrinking. Marketplace revenue fell 6.5%. The profit came from a higher gross margin (45.6% to 49.6%) and lower operating costs (A$91.3m to A$85.0m), not from growth [2].
- Paid marketing is the largest cost after printing: A$74.5m in FY26, 21% of marketplace revenue, up 2.9% while revenue fell [2].
- Part of the cash belongs to others. Payables and unearned revenue (A$55.9m) exceed the A$40.5m of cash at 30 June [1]. The float is permanent while the business holds its size, but it shrinks if revenue keeps falling.
- Tax. Articore has A$186.5m of unused Australian tax losses (an unrecognised tax asset of A$70.2m) [1]. That shelters Australian profit, but the losses only have value if taxable income arises where they sit.
- Book value is intangible: net tangible assets are negative [1].
- Dilution. The 31.2m unquoted securities are 10.6% of the shares on issue; the 12.79m RSUs alone are 4.4% [1]. Diluted EPS was 3.62c against 3.73c basic [1].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is FY26's turnaround durable while marketplace revenue keeps shrinking, and will the Christmas half deliver the FY27 step up to A$17m to A$23m of operating EBITDA, when all of FY26's profit came from the Christmas half? A deep dive would split Redbubble from TeePublic, test the margin gains against paid marketing costs, and check the float behind the cash.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: online marketplaces are priced on enterprise value (market value less net cash and recognised tax assets) to cash earnings after capitalised development, equipment and lease payments, before interest and tax. The price would get there through the December quarter update and the half-year result, with the buy-back as a steady buyer.
- Peer multiples (the desk's arithmetic from filings, prices on 6 Nov 2026):
  - hipages (ASX:HPG), an ANZ trades marketplace: market value A$103.6m (136.34m shares at A$0.76), less A$32.85m net cash and a A$7.72m net tax asset, gives A$63.0m, over FY26 cash earnings of A$8.56m (operating cash flow A$26.35m, less interest received A$1.01m, plus deal costs A$0.22m and tax paid A$0.22m, less development A$14.68m, equipment A$0.40m and lease payments A$2.14m) = 7.36 times [18].
  - Kogan.com (ASX:KGN), online retail and marketplaces: market value A$435.6m (95.54m shares at A$4.56), less A$36.43m cash (no drawn debt) and a A$7.35m tax asset, gives A$391.9m, over FY26 cash earnings of A$33.15m (operating cash flow A$41.95m, less interest received A$0.80m, plus tax paid A$3.97m, less equipment A$0.13m, intangibles A$3.47m and lease principal A$8.37m; the A$1.59m of finance costs paid, mostly lease interest, stays in as a cost) = 11.82 times [19].
  - Average 9.59 times.
- Articore's cash earnings on the same basis, from EBITDA rather than operating cash flow, so the FY26 working capital inflow is left out: EBITDA A$15.3m (operating EBITDA A$16.4m less A$1.2m other expenses), less leases A$3.64m, development A$3.05m and equipment A$0.52m = A$8.09m for FY26 [1][2].
- If it goes our way: FY27 operating EBITDA at the guidance midpoint of A$20m with the same deductions gives A$11.59m. At 9.59 times: A$111.1m, plus A$40.46m of cash = A$151.6m, on 293.44m shares = A$0.517 a share (A$0.495 counting the 12.79m RSUs). At the top of guidance (A$23m): A$0.615.
- If it goes against us: cash earnings fall back to the FY24 to FY26 average of the company's own underlying cash flow, A$3.87m. At 9.59 times: A$37.1m plus A$40.46m = A$77.6m, or A$0.264 a share. If cash earnings simply hold at FY26's A$8.09m: A$0.402.
- Only now the price: A$0.37 (last trade on 6 Nov 2026, ASX company data; A$0.375 at the 30 Oct close) [17], a market value of A$108.6m and about 8.4 times FY26 cash earnings net of cash. Gap if it goes our way: 0.517 / 0.37 - 1 = +40% (+34% counting the RSUs). Gap if it goes against us: 0.264 / 0.37 - 1 = -29%. At hipages's multiple alone the our-way gap is +16%; at Kogan's alone, +64%.
