## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Beforepay is an Australian consumer lender: pay advances (average A$450 in FY26, now charged interest) and, since FY25 under its own Australian Credit Licence, personal loans of up to A$5,000 over 12 months. It also owns Carrington Labs, which sells its credit scoring models to other lenders. FY26 revenue A$50.6m, all but a little from Australian borrowers [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Ernst & Young's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified and the accounts are prepared as a going concern [1]. Its loan facility was refinanced on 28 July 2026 [6].
- Too thin to trade? No, but close. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$17,145 a day, counting the one session with no trades as zero; 12 of the 20 were above A$10,000 (data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz) [12]. That caps a position near A$3,400.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holders are Regal Funds Management (13.93%) and Microequities (9.15%) [1][8]. No approach or review has been announced in 2025 or 2026 [13]. The CEO, Jamie Twiss, hands over to deputy CEO Kasey Kaplan on 7 December 2026 after a succession process [7].

Does the gap between statutory and underlying profit recur? Yes. Cash NPAT adds back the growth in loan loss provisions, which is a real cost of lending more and recurs every year the book grows; it also adds back share-based payments, which recur. The statutory profit itself contains tax benefits from recognising past losses in both years (A$1.37m in FY25, A$1.50m in FY26) [1].

What the headline numbers hide:
- The low earnings multiple rests on tax credits. FY26 profit before tax was A$6.73m. At the 30% company tax rate that is A$4.71m after tax, a return on average equity of 10.7%, against 18.7% on the statutory A$8.23m [1]. With A$36.4m of accumulated losses, little cash tax will be paid for a few years, which is worth something but runs out [1].
- Profit is seasonal. FY26 quarterly profit before tax was A$3.4m, A$0.3m, A$0.4m and A$2.4m: the December and March quarters earned little [3][5]. The September quarter, the strongest, can't be multiplied by four.
- Bad debts are rising as advances grow. Net bad debts were 1.41% of advances in the September 2026 quarter, against 0.87% a year earlier (the company's revised method) [4]. The 45% revenue growth in that quarter (A$5.2m more than a year earlier) turned into A$1.3m more profit [4].
- One product carries the business: pay advances were A$946m of the A$963m advanced in FY26 [2]. Charging interest on them is recent (substantially complete by June 2026), and the annual report lists changes to lending law as a risk that could force changes to the business model [1][2].
- Dilution. The 8.48m options and rights are 17% of the shares on issue, and all but 300,000 (exercise price A$2.56) have exercise prices at or below A$1.09, under the 12-month low of A$1.10 [1][14].

## 3. Guidance and record
Beforepay gives no numerical guidance for profit. The FY26 result said a full year of interest charges on FY26's pay advance volumes "would have generated approximately $12.5m of annual interest income" [2], and that the new facility should cut funding costs by more than A$1m a year on a A$40m drawn balance [2][6]. FY27 outlook: more personal loans, new risk models and Carrington Labs, with no figures [2]. The September 2026 quarter delivered revenue of A$16.5m and statutory profit of A$4.7m [4]. There is no earlier numerical guidance to check against.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: does the interest now charged on pay advances (about A$12.5m a year at FY26 volumes, by the company's figure) reach profit, or do higher bad debts and costs absorb most of it? The first evidence says most is absorbed: the September quarter's revenue rose A$5.2m on a year earlier and its profit A$1.3m.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: listed lenders are priced on book value against their return on equity. The price would get there through half-year results that show a higher return on equity, starting with the December quarter report.
- Peers (the desk's arithmetic from FY26 filings, prices on 6 Nov 2026):
  - Cash Converters (ASX:CCV), small and medium personal loans: market value A$189.9m (716.59m shares at A$0.265) over equity of A$253.8m is 0.75 times book; profit after tax A$19.66m on average equity of A$240.9m is a return of 8.2% [15].
  - Solvar (ASX:SVR), car and personal loans to non-prime borrowers: A$256.3m (187.07m shares at A$1.37) over A$329.0m is 0.78 times book; A$29.47m on average equity of A$341.3m is 8.6% [16].
  - Both are priced at about their return on equity divided by 11.0% (8.2% / 0.75 = 10.9%; 8.6% / 0.78 = 11.1%). So the value of a lender here is about book value times its return on equity divided by 11.0%.
- Equity and shares used: A$54.1m at 30 September 2026 plus the A$4.07m the 8.48m options and rights would pay in (at A$0.48) = A$58.2m, over 58.27m shares counting them all.
- If it goes our way: every quarter of FY27 improves on a year earlier by as much as the September quarter did (A$1.3m), so profit before tax is A$6.73m + 4 x A$1.3m = A$11.93m, A$8.35m after 30% tax, a 15.4% return on A$54.1m. That is 1.40 times book: A$81.7m, or A$1.40 a share.
- If it goes against us: the return stays at FY26's 10.7% after tax. That is 0.97 times book: A$56.5m, or A$0.97 a share.
- A ceiling, not a case: if the whole A$12.5m of interest and the A$1m of funding savings were added to FY26's profit with no extra bad debts, the return would be 26% and the value about A$2.38 a share. FY26 already included part of that interest, and the September quarter shows bad debts rising with it, so this overstates.
- Only now the price: A$1.39 (last trade on 6 Nov 2026, ASX company data; A$1.25 at the 30 Oct close) [14], a market value of A$70.8m and 1.31 times September equity. Gap if it goes our way: 1.40 / 1.39 - 1 = +1%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.97 / 1.39 - 1 = -30%. (At the 30 October close: +12% and -22%.)

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive: the gap if it goes our way is about 1%, far under 25%. On the September quarter's evidence the market already prices the interest change, and the 8.3 times earnings in the ranking rests on tax credits and an untaxed profit. It would take most of the company's A$12.5m interest figure reaching profit for the case to work, which the December quarter report (about 28 January 2027) and the half-year result (about 26 February 2027) will show.
