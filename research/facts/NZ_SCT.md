Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Designs, builds and services automation and robotic systems: materials handling and logistics (palletising, conveyors, guided vehicles), protein (meat processing), mining (Rocklabs sample preparation equipment) and appliances (production lines for whiteware makers), built in New Zealand, Australia, China, Europe and the Americas. FY26 (year to 31 August 2026) revenue NZ$294.1m [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Deloitte's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified, with no material uncertainty paragraph [2]. WorkSafe told the company in May 2026 that it intends to bring proceedings over the fatal accident at the Dunedin site in April 2025 [12].
- Too thin to trade? No. NZX's own daily trading data: median on-market value over the 20 sessions 9 October to 6 November 2026 was NZ$45,645 a day (lowest NZ$569, highest NZ$234,241; 15 of the 20 sessions above NZ$12,500) [17]. The 60-session median is NZ$22,603 and the 250-session median NZ$15,443, so recent trading, around the FY26 result, is above its usual level; on the 250-session median the largest position would be about NZ$3,089.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No takeover, review or sale. JBS Australia holds 45,894,965 shares (54.06%) and its stake creeps up only by taking its dividends in shares (53.05% in May 2023 to 54.06% in May 2026; no purchases) [3][2]. In August 2026 the JBS seat on the board passed from John Berry (JBS Australia) to Jerson Nascimento Jr., JBS's global chief procurement and innovation officer, representing JBS USA [4]. No sign of a bid, but the controlling holder is noted for the watch list.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit. Non-recurring costs were excluded in FY24 (NZ$3.8m: operating EBITDA NZ$30.2m against reported NZ$26.4m) and FY26 (NZ$1.2m), but not FY25 [2][10]. Two of three years is close to a pattern, though the amounts are small beside EBITDA.

Pre-tax cash earnings (operating cash flow before tax and interest received, less equipment, capitalised development, lease payments and lease interest): NZ$11.0m in FY26 and NZ$14.2m in FY25, against operating EBITDA of NZ$36.1m and NZ$31.5m. Project working capital absorbs much of the profit each year [2].

What the headline numbers hide:
- JBS is both controller and customer: 11.4% of FY26 sales (13.1% in FY25), NZ$33.6m, with NZ$6.6m owed by JBS companies at year end; directors representing JBS take no fees [2].
- Cash conversion is weak, as above; operating cash flow was 68% of reported EBITDA in FY26 and 71% in FY25 [2].
- Earnings are weighted to the second half (H1 FY26 EBITDA NZ$13.0m, H2 NZ$23.1m), and forward work fell from NZ$177m at February 2026 to NZ$146m at August 2026 (NZ$169m at August 2025). Equipment forward work was NZ$118m against NZ$130m a year earlier, after about NZ$11m of recent cancellations in appliances and materials handling [1][6][5].
- Book value includes NZ$54.9m of goodwill from past acquisitions (Alvey, Normaclass and others) [2].
- Finance leadership: the chief financial officer resigned in May 2026; interim co-chief financial officers since 6 July 2026, with a permanent appointment to follow "later in the year" [13].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: can Scott turn its record order intake and the FY27 range (operating EBITDA NZ$36m to NZ$41m) into profit and cash at a steadier rate, so that the market prices it as a growing industrial company rather than a lumpy project shop controlled by its largest customer? The half-year in April and the FY27 result show whether forward work is rebuilt and cash conversion improves.
- How the market prices it: listed engineering and manufacturing companies are priced on enterprise value over operating earnings (EBIT), with leases counted as debt. Peer multiples (desk's arithmetic from filings, prices at the 6 Nov 2026 close): Skellerup (NZX:SKL), a New Zealand industrial manufacturer with ten years of EBIT growth, at 17.0 times (enterprise value NZ$1,518.6m, being NZ$1,490.1m market value, NZ$2.0m net debt and NZ$26.4m of leases, over FY26 normalised EBIT of NZ$89.3m) [18]; Delegat Group (NZX:DGL) at 7.9 times (NZ$825.8m over FY26 operating EBIT of NZ$104.1m) [19]. The mechanism is the results.
- If it goes our way: FY27 operating EBITDA at the NZ$38.5m midpoint, less FY26's NZ$11.7m of depreciation and amortisation, gives EBIT of NZ$26.8m. At Skellerup's 17.0 times: NZ$456.2m, less NZ$7.7m net debt and NZ$28.8m leases = NZ$419.7m, on 84.89m shares = NZ$4.94 a share.
- If it goes against us: operating EBIT stays at FY26's NZ$24.4m at Delegat's 7.9 times: NZ$193.8m, less NZ$36.5m = NZ$157.2m, or NZ$1.85 a share. (On the FY27 midpoint at Delegat's multiple it would be NZ$2.07.)
- Price: NZ$2.37 (close 6 Nov 2026, NZX), market value NZ$201.2m; enterprise value NZ$237.7m, 9.7 times FY26 operating EBIT, 12.8 times FY26 profit, and 21.6 times FY26 pre-tax cash earnings. Gap if it goes our way: 4.94 / 2.37 - 1 = +108%. Gap if it goes against us: 1.85 / 2.37 - 1 = -22%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is 108%; the question can be answered from filings (forward work, domain revenue, half-year EBITDA, cash flow and working capital are all reported); and the H1 FY27 result in mid-April 2027 falls inside six months, with the annual meeting on 26 November 2026 before it. The gap rests on Skellerup's multiple and on the FY27 range being met; at Delegat's multiple the FY27 midpoint would be worth less than the price, and on cash earnings rather than EBIT the shares already look fully priced, so the deep dive must test cash conversion first. Not a value pick: the value if it goes against us is 22% below the price. JBS's 54% and its new global board seat are noted for the watch list, without any sign of a bid.
