Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs. Leaving aside lease accounting and the Focus write-down, the items left out were A$8.9m before tax in FY25 and A$21.1m in FY26 [7][8], and FY24's underlying result also left out distribution centre transition and cloud software costs [12]. The software spending is a cash cost: development work on the new Adairs ERP system that accounting rules don't let the company capitalise. It is the same kind of cost three years running, so it is not a one-off. The company says about A$5m of the technology upgrade remains for FY27, and the new system adds about A$1.1m a year to running costs [1]. If the software and exit costs are counted, FY26 profit after tax was about A$19.8m (A$34.6m less A$13.0m and A$1.8m after tax), not A$34.6m; FY25 was about A$27.7m.

What the headline hides:
- Book value is mostly goodwill and brand names: A$118.3m of goodwill (Adairs A$69.9m, Mocka A$48.4m) and A$90.3m of brands, against net assets of A$171.4m [2]. Focus on Furniture's goodwill (A$41.0m) was written off in full and its brand cut from A$37.0m to A$14.4m [2].
- Focus on Furniture: FY26 underlying EBIT A$3.8m, down 68% [1]. Written sales were down 27.6% in the first 8 weeks of FY27 and 19.5% for the first quarter, improving to down 2.7% in weeks 9 to 13 [1][4]. The company's own impairment test assumes Focus EBITDA grows only 0.6% a year over five years, at an 18.0% pre-tax discount rate [2]. A difficult first half of FY27 is expected [1].
- First 8 weeks of FY27: group sales down 4.5% excluding New Zealand; Adairs up 0.4%, Mocka up 15.3% [1].
- Leadership: the CEO leaves around March 2027 and a successor search is under way [4]; Focus's previous executive team left in the third quarter of FY26 [1].
- Debt falls due in July 2028, outside six months. Leases are A$230.9m [2].

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance (FY27): no earnings guidance ("Adairs is not providing FY27 earnings guidance") [9]. Stated: moderate gross margin expansion, cost productivity, capex up to A$25m (FY26 A$15.6m), about A$5m of technology upgrade spend remaining, about 75% of FY27 US dollar purchases hedged at 67.4 US cents, a difficult H1 at Focus with benefits from H2 [1][9]. 29 Sep 2026: Adairs and Mocka trading in line with the August update; Focus Q1 written sales down 19.5%; ERP went live 16 Sep 2026 [4].

Changes this year (FY26):
- 27 Aug 2025: first-half sales and gross margin guidance given with the FY25 result [5].
- 22 Oct 2025: H1 group sales cut from A$324.5m to A$336.5m to A$319.5m to A$331.5m; Focus gross margin cut [13]. Delivered A$329.0m [6].
- 8 Jul 2026: FY26 underlying EBIT A$53.5m to A$55.5m; Focus impairment A$62m to A$68m; cloud software costs A$18m to A$19m and New Zealand exit A$3.5m to A$4.0m to be left out; statutory loss about A$43m [14].
- Delivered (24 Aug 2026): underlying EBIT A$55.0m, impairment A$63.5m, software A$18.6m, New Zealand exit A$2.5m, statutory loss A$39.4m [1][7].

Record:
- FY26: the July range of A$53.5m to A$55.5m was met at A$55.0m. The October half-year sales cut was needed; the cut range was met.
- FY25: 23 Jun 2025 guidance of A$53.5m to A$57.0m underlying EBIT (with cloud software costs guided at about A$3m to A$4m) [15]; delivered A$55.2m, with software costs of A$4.4m, slightly above the guide [5].
- Pattern: profit ranges given late in the year have been met; the costs left out have tended to come in at or above their guides.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: once the ERP project's last A$5m is spent in FY27, is "underlying" profit the real profit, and is Focus on Furniture worth anything? The software and transition costs left out have recurred for three years, so a deep dive would check from the filings whether they really stop, and what Focus earns at its current sales.
- If it goes our way: the costs left out end after FY27's A$5m; Adairs holds FY26 EBIT of A$41.1m plus the A$0.8m New Zealand loss that is gone, less the A$1.1m ERP running cost, A$40.8m; Mocka holds A$10.1m; Focus holds A$3.8m, in line with the company's own flat forecast. Each is capitalised at the company's own pre-tax discount rate less its 2.25% long-term growth (Adairs 13.6%, Mocka 15.8%, Focus 18.0% [2]): A$359.5m, A$74.5m and A$24.1m. Less net debt of A$47.6m and the A$5m of remaining software spend: A$405.5m, or A$2.25 a share on 180.28m shares.
- If it goes against us: the costs left out keep recurring at their FY25 and FY26 average of A$15.0m a year, cutting Adairs to A$25.8m (A$227.3m), and Focus is worth nothing; Mocka as before (A$74.5m). Less net debt: A$254.2m, or A$1.41 a share.
- Price: A$1.33 (close 30 Oct 2026), market value A$240m. Gap if it goes our way: 2.25 / 1.33 - 1 = +69%. Gap if it goes against us: 1.41 / 1.33 - 1 = +6%. The price is below even the "against" figure: the market values the group at about 5.2 times FY26 underlying EBIT (A$240m plus A$47.6m net debt, against A$55.0m), while the company's own discount rates imply about 8.8 times for Adairs.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is 69%; the history and size of the costs left out, the ERP budget and Focus's numbers are all in the filings; and the AGM on 11 November 2026 and the H1 FY27 result on about 23 February 2027 should show whether the software costs have stopped and how Focus is trading, inside six months. Because the price sits below the "against" figure, the deep dive should test the company's discount rates against what the market is paying for retailers, since that, more than the one-offs, is where the gap comes from.
