# Scales Corporation (NZX:SCL): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 1 point, #17 in the NZX table of reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07); earlier quick look rejected.
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. A diversified agribusiness: Global Proteins (pet food ingredients through Shelby in the US, Meateor in New Zealand and Australia and Esro in the Netherlands, plus edible protein trading through Fayman International and ANZ Exports), Horticulture (Mr Apple orchards and exports, Profruit juice) and Logistics (freight forwarding). FY25 (year to 31 December 2025) revenue NZ$899.9m [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Deloitte's opinion on the FY25 accounts is unmodified, with no material uncertainty paragraph [2]. One subsidiary, Fayman International, breached a capital ratio covenant at 31 December 2025 (NZ$2.1m of term debt reclassified as current); the lender issued a reservation of rights letter on 29 January 2026 and said it did not intend to enforce [2].
- Too thin to trade? No. NZX's own daily trading data: median on-market value over the 20 sessions 9 October to 6 November 2026 was NZ$594,156 a day (lowest NZ$216,198; all 20 sessions above NZ$12,500) [19].
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is the FirstCape group at 15.09% (section 4). Scales itself is the buyer in its deals: it took Meateor Australia and Fayman International to 100% and ANZ Exports to 85% in September 2025, and bought the other half of Esro Petfood for a nominal sum in September 2026 after its partner went bankrupt [3][4][5].

The gap between statutory and underlying profit does recur, though in both directions. Each year Scales excludes the effects of its own deal-making: FY24 reported was below underlying (NZ$30.3m against NZ$34.1m), FY25 far above it (NZ$101.0m against NZ$61.8m, on gains from revaluing stakes it took control of), and H1 FY26 well below (NZ$30.1m against NZ$52.0m, on the Esro loan impairment and settlement items) [1][6][7]. Because Scales buys into businesses every year, "acquisition accounting" is a standing feature of its results, not a one-off.

What the headline numbers hide:
- Minority holders take a large share: 20% of FY25 underlying profit and 17% in H1 FY26 went to the other owners of Shelby (32.5%) and ANZ Exports (15%) [1][6][13].
- Horticulture is volatile and concentrated: FY25 underlying EBITDA NZ$65.2m was up 73% on unusually good conditions; 91% of 2026 apple sales are expected to go to Asia and the Middle East, where the Middle East conflict has raised fuel and freight costs [1][6]. The board's own FY26 guidance assumed Horticulture "normalises" [14].
- Revenue doubled because Scales now consolidates low-margin edible protein trading; group EBITDA margin fell from 23.3% to 13.4% in H1 FY26 [6][7].
- Esro: the partner's meat business was investigated by the Dutch food safety authority in April 2026 and the partner went bankrupt in June; Esro Petfood lost NZ$5.8m in H1 FY26 and is consolidated from September 2026 [4][15][8].
- Debt has risen from net cash to about NZ$107m in 18 months, before NZ$38.9m of deferred purchase price and NZ$118.0m of leases [6][8].

FY24: NZ$30m to NZ$35m, held all year; delivered NZ$34.3m (NZ$34.1m restated) [17].
Record: met or beat guidance in both years, with FY25 raised six times, partly because the stakes it bought lifted the profit attributable to shareholders. Medium-term target: Global Proteins underlying EBITDA of NZ$85m in FY27 (raised from NZ$70m in September 2025; FY25 NZ$73.9m) [1][3].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: with Horticulture expected to come off FY25's record and Global Proteins carrying the Esro problem, can underlying profit to shareholders get back above FY25's NZ$61.8m, which the price already seems to assume? The December update and the FY26 result in February answer it.
- How the market prices it: listed New Zealand food producers are priced on earnings. Peer multiples (desk's arithmetic from filings, prices at the 6 Nov 2026 close): Sanford (NZX:SAN) at 9.8 times its twelve-month profit without stock revaluations (NZ$621.8m over NZ$63.2m; workings in research/facts/NZ_SAN.md) [22]; Delegat Group (NZX:DGL) at 7.3 times operating profit after tax (NZ$450.0m over NZ$61.5m for FY26 to June 2026) [23]. The mechanism is the results.
- If it goes our way: Horticulture holds its FY25 level and Global Proteins reaches its NZ$85m target, adding NZ$11.1m of EBITDA, or about NZ$6.6m after 28% tax and the 17% minority share: underlying profit to shareholders NZ$68.4m. At Sanford's 9.8 times: NZ$673.3m, on 146.58m shares = NZ$4.59 a share. Cross-check: the twelve months to June 2026 underlying EBITDA of NZ$153.1m at Delegat's 6.1 times enterprise value (including leases), less net debt, deferred purchase price, leases and minorities at book, gives NZ$4.52.
- If it goes against us: profit settles at the bottom of FY26 guidance, NZ$55.0m, at Delegat's 7.3 times: NZ$402.5m, or NZ$2.75 a share.
- Price: NZ$6.40 (close 6 Nov 2026, NZX), market value NZ$938.1m, 14.4 times underlying profit to shareholders for the twelve months to June 2026 and 16.3 times the FY26 guidance midpoint. Gap if it goes our way: 4.59 / 6.40 - 1 = -28%. Gap if it goes against us: 2.75 / 6.40 - 1 = -57%.

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive: the gap if it goes our way is negative (-28%), well under 25%. Scales already trades at a higher multiple than its listed New Zealand food peers, so the price gives credit for the growth the question asks about; nothing in the filings suggests the market is missing value. Not a value pick.
