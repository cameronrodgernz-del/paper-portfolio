# Boom Logistics (ASX:BOL): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 6 points, #10 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Boom hires out cranes with operators and runs lifting projects across Australia. FY26 revenue by sector: mining and
resources 55%, infrastructure and construction 20%, wind, energy and utilities 16%, industrial maintenance 9%; about 50%
came from contracted or recurring work. Its largest contract, at BHP's Olympic Dam, was renewed on 30 June 2026 for
five years at about A$40m a year (about 15% of FY26 revenue), with BHP able to end it on reasonable notice. Shares were
consolidated 10 for 1 on 29 November 2024.

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Grant Thornton's FY26 opinion is
  unmodified (annual report, 19 Aug 2026). Regulator record: Workplace Health and Safety Queensland is still investigating
  the death of a Boom employee at the Clarke Creek wind farm on 2 July 2025; timing and outcome unknown (contingent
  liability, note 19). The former CEO misused A$1.143m of company money; it was fully recovered by 16 December 2025.
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$157,233 (volume times close,
  data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz; lowest session A$18,922).
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is the
  Collins St Value Fund at 17.47%.

Nothing ends the sheet.

Does the gap between statutory and underlying recur? The FY26 gap is mostly one event (Clarke Creek) and one recovery,
which look like true one-offs. But losses on asset sales come up every year as cranes are traded, and the FY25 tax
benefit was a one-off gain that roughly doubled that year's statutory profit. A further 8.9m of tax losses is still
unrecognised and could produce another such gain.

What the headline numbers hide:
- The low earnings multiple is on untaxed profit. Boom pays no tax because of 26.7m of tax-effected losses (about 89m of
  income); at 30% tax, FY26 operating profit would be about 9.0m, or about 25c a share on the current count.
- The crane fleet is largely financed through leases. Lease repayments (29.3m) and lease interest (7.0m) took 36.3m in
  FY26 against operating cash flow of 52.8m. After leases and capital spending, cash generation was about 22.2m.
- Concentration: Olympic Dam alone is about 15% of revenue; mining is 55%.
- Profit "more than halved" in the ranking only because FY25 included the 14.0m tax benefit; operating profit rose from
  9.3m to 12.8m.

## 3. Guidance and record
- FY27 (19 Aug 2026): "budgeting for continued growth in EPS", about A$200m of contracted work, and returns to holders of
  40% to 60% of the prior year's operating profit, including buybacks of up to A$7.0m. No profit number. No change since.
- FY26, raised twice: 24 Oct 2025, EPS growth of 15% (on 22c, about 25c); 20 Feb 2026, statutory NPAT about 10.5m and
  operating 11.9m, statutory EPS about 26c and underlying 30c; 7 May 2026, statutory NPAT 10.5m to 11.5m and operating
  11.9m to 12.9m, statutory EPS 29c and underlying 32c. Delivered statutory 11.0m (28.5c) and operating 12.8m (32.9c):
  the operating figure in range, statutory EPS just short of the last 29c.
- FY25, raised once: operating NPAT about 8m, raised to 9m on 21 Feb 2025; delivered 9.3m.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is FY26's operating profit of A$12.8m (32.9c a share) Boom's new base, backed by the Olympic Dam renewal
  and A$200m of contracted work, or a good year to be followed by a return to its patchier past (a loss as recently as
  FY23)? And what is that profit worth once tax losses run out and the lease-financed fleet needs replacing?
- If it goes our way: FY26 operating profit holds. Taxed at 30% it is A$8.96m; at the price-to-earnings of Emeco
  (ASX:EHL, equipment hire to miners, 9.1 times in the ASX company data) that is A$81.5m. Add the tax losses, worth about
  A$19.9m in today's money if used at A$3.8m a year (8% discount rate). Total A$101.5m / 36.16m shares = A$2.81.
- If it goes against us: profit goes back to its five-year average with the FY25 tax benefit taken out (FY22 to FY26:
  3.8m, loss 5.2m, 6.6m, 9.3m, 11.0m; average A$5.1m), taxed at 30% (A$3.6m), at the same 9.1 times (A$32.6m), plus the
  tax losses used more slowly (about A$14.2m today): A$46.8m / 36.16m = A$1.29.
- Only now the price: A$2.21 (close 30 October 2026). Gap if it goes our way: 2.81 / 2.21 - 1 = +27%. Gap if it goes
  against us: 1.29 / 2.21 - 1 = -41%.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is 27%: above 25% but short of 40%. The answer needs the
event itself: the HY27 result due about 20 February 2027 will show whether operating profit is still growing. Book value
(A$3.28 a share without the tax asset) is higher than the our-way figure, but FY26 operating profit was only about 10%
of it before tax, so earnings are the better guide.
