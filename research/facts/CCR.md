# Credit Clear (ASX:CCR): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 4 points, #51 in the ASX table of reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. A debt collection business with its own digital platform: ARMA (agency collections, bought in 2022) and Force Legal in Australia, DTS (collections software and automated voice, bought from Experian NZ on 1 February 2026) and ARC Europe (a UK collection agency, bought 1 January 2026). FY26 revenue A$60.1m [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. PwC's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified, with the ACCC contingent liability disclosure as a key audit matter [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 2 to 30 October 2026 was A$35,594 a day (lowest A$700; six sessions under A$10,000), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz. The 60-session median is A$25,409.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is the Thorney group at about 17%, then the chair's family at about 10%; the company itself is buying back shares [1][13][14].

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs. Share-based pay (A$1.3m, then A$1.7m) and acquisition costs (A$0.8m, then A$1.7m) were left out of underlying earnings in both years, and buying businesses is the stated plan [1][4]. Statutory profit in both years also leaned on tax benefits, and in FY26 on a non-cash gain that reverses if the share price rises.

What the headline numbers hide:
- The ACCC case. On 24 June 2026 the ACCC sued ARMA and Force Legal in the Federal Court over debt collection notices sent between 6 February 2022 and 26 September 2025: at least 320,000 letters and emails from Force Legal and at least 16,000 communications from ARMA, allegedly misstating that debts were still due (including statute-barred debts), that Force Legal had instructions to sue and was independent of ARMA, that wages could be garnished without a hearing, and that consumers would pay all legal costs and interest. The ACCC seeks penalties, declarations, injunctions and compensation for consumers. Since 10 November 2022 the maximum penalty per contravention is the greatest of A$50m, three times the benefit, or 30% of turnover in the breach period. No amount is claimed, nothing is provided for, and no trial date is set; the company denies the allegations and has briefed Allan Myers AC KC [1][3][9].
- The clients named in the ACCC release include gyms, energy and telecommunications companies, education, health, funeral and transport providers and a streaming service; the ACCC says it doesn't allege the clients did anything wrong [9]. FY27 guidance assumes no material operational impact from the case [2].
- "0.83 times book" overstates the asset backing: A$61.8m of the A$84.6m of equity is goodwill and other intangibles [1].
- Underlying NPATA assumes no tax is paid until FY28, using past losses [1].
- 36.8m unlisted options and 9.6m performance rights are on issue; the AGM is asked to approve 4m more options at A$0.25 for the new managing director [1][10].
- Leadership changed: CEO Andrew Smith stepped down on 1 September 2026 and Joshua Reid, a director since 20 July 2026, became managing director [11].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: what will the ACCC case cost in penalties, compensation and lost clients, and can the business still grow into its FY27 guidance? A deep dive could size the exposure against past Federal Court penalties for misleading debt collection, but the answer comes from the court or a settlement, and no trial date is set.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: collection businesses are priced on earnings. The named peer is Credit Corp (ASX:CCP), the largest listed Australian collector and consumer lender: A$15.01 at the 30 October 2026 close on FY26 basic earnings of 155.0c, 9.68 times earnings [19]. The mechanism is results that meet guidance plus a settled ACCC case.
- Cash earnings used here: underlying EBITDA less lease payments (A$1.9m with a full year of the acquisitions, against A$1.49m in FY26), capitalised development (A$1.97m), equipment (A$0.28m) and share-based pay (A$1.70m), then tax at 30%. FY26 on this basis: A$5.05m before tax.
- If it goes our way: FY27 at the top of guidance, A$14.0m, less those costs, A$8.15m before tax and A$5.70m after, at 9.68 times is A$55.2m. Add net cash of A$16.9m and the A$7.4m tax asset, with no ACCC cost: A$79.5m, or A$0.158 a share on 503.3m shares.
- If it goes against us: FY27 at the bottom of guidance, A$12.0m, gives A$6.15m before tax and A$4.30m after, A$41.7m at 9.68 times, plus A$16.9m and A$7.4m, less A$15m for penalties, compensation and costs. The A$15m is the desk's assumption, not a figure from any filing; the ACCC's statutory maximum is far higher. A$51.0m, or A$0.101 a share.
- Only now the price: the ranking used A$0.14, the close on both 30 October and 5 November. On 6 November 2026 1.9m shares traded and the last sale was A$0.18, up 29%, with no announcement (bid A$0.165, offer A$0.18) [7][18]. At A$0.14: gap if it goes our way 0.158 / 0.14 less 1 = +13%; if against, 0.101 / 0.14 less 1 = -28%. At A$0.18: -12% and -44%.

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive: the gap if it goes our way is under 25% (+13% at A$0.14, negative at A$0.18). On Credit Corp's earnings multiple the business is priced about right even before the ACCC case is counted, and the case can only take value away. The answer to the case also needs the court or a settlement, which is unlikely inside six months. The unexplained 29% rise on 6 November 2026 is worth a look by the evening desk; nothing in the announcements explains it.
