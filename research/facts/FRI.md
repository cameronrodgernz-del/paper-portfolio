# Finbar Group (ASX:FRI): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 6 points, #14 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Finbar develops and sells apartments in Perth, with some rental property; 80 completed projects and 7,603 units to date; listed since 1995 [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. KPMG's opinion on the June 2026 accounts is unmodified; its key audit matter is the carrying value of inventories, 59% of total assets [3].
- Too thin to trade? Yes. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$8,860 a day, counting 30 October (no trades) as zero, from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz. Over the last 20 days that traded (2 to 29 October) it was A$9,416. Both are under the A$10,000 floor. Daily values ranged from nil to A$44,389; the screen's 60-session median, which skips days without trades, was A$23,900 [4].
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. Substantial holders at 30 June 2026: Chuan Hup Holdings (Singapore-listed) 25.65%, Forward International 12.66%, Thorney Holdings 10.59% [3]. No holder is over 50%.

The sheet ends here on the liquidity kill.

For the record, the facts the brief asked for (book value, what settlements have realised, where the profit comes from), with no valuation or price comparison:

Book value at 30 June 2026 [1]:
- Inventories A$128.5m: completed stock A$54.5m and work in progress, including development land, A$74.0m.
- Investment property A$35.5m (Fairlanes and other East Perth commercial property); Karratha (Pelago) units held for sale A$11.2m, after A$51.0m of Karratha assets were sold in FY26.
- Garden Towers, the 50% joint venture with Chuan Hup's subsidiary Ventrade: Finbar's equity is A$1.0m and its loans to the joint venture A$35.2m. At 100%, the venture had A$203.8m of assets and A$205.3m of liabilities, so none of its profit was in the books yet [1][5].
- Cash A$50.7m, borrowings A$13.9m (A$5.5m of it, the Bel-Air loan, repaid in July 2026), net assets A$257.1m, net tangible assets 94.5c a share on 272,123,142 shares [1][2].

What settlements have realised:
- FY26 settlements were A$263.7m across 287 lots, from completed stock at Civic Heart, Aurora and The Point (A$77m), Bel-Air and Pelago [2][6]. Gross margin on A$204.3m of revenue was 20.2%, against 12.1% in FY25 [2].
- Bel-Air (Belmont, wholly owned, completed June 2026): 193 of 194 apartments and both shops sold, A$113.4m by value; 53% settled in the second half of FY26 and the rest falls in FY27 [7][2].
- Garden Towers (East Perth, 342 lots, A$296.9m end value): completed with titles issued on 14 October 2026; 279 lots sold for A$210m (82%), with 56 apartments and 7 shops left. Settlements and the profit, through Finbar's share of the joint venture, fall in the first half of FY27 [5].
- Pre-sales were a record A$567.2m at 30 June 2026 (A$495m off the plan, A$71m completed stock) [2][6].

Where the profit comes from. The accounts report profit by segment, not by project [1]:
- FY26 profit before tax of A$28.4m: residential development A$23.5m on revenue of A$175.7m; commercial office and retail A$3.3m on A$21.8m; rental A$0.1m on A$6.8m; corporate A$1.6m. The joint ventures added A$0.03m [1].
- FY25 profit before tax of A$20.3m: residential A$13.2m, commercial A$0.5m, rental A$0.6m, corporate A$6.0m (which included A$3.6m of sales commission and a A$2.5m gain on selling a subsidiary) [1].
- Net revaluation losses on investment property and plant were A$2.6m in FY26 and A$2.8m in FY25; the company's underlying profit of A$22.1m adds these back [6].
- Projects under construction at 18 August 2026: Garden Towers (above), Riverbank Rivervale (A$119m, 97% sold, completion first half FY28), Palmyra West (A$100m, 85% sold, first half FY28) and Romeo Applecross (A$192m, 59% sold, construction started September 2026, first half FY29). The pipeline to FY32 is A$1.8 billion of end value [6][8].

Guidance and owners:
- FY26 profit guidance of A$18m to A$22m (given 26 February 2026, reaffirmed 15 June 2026) was met with A$20.3m [7][9]. No FY27 guidance has been given.
- Dividends: 5.5c fully franked for FY26 (2.5c interim, 3c final), 74% of earnings per share of 7.44c [1].
- Directors buying: John, Ronald and Melissa Chan each report the same indirect holding (an entity of which John Chan's spouse is a director and shareholder). In the notices read from 27 February to 16 September 2026 it bought 2,505,031 shares on market for A$1.90m, an average of about A$0.76, the largest lots on 20 and 21 August (516,624 shares, A$388,315) and 27 to 31 August (865,479 shares, A$675,867) [10]. That is about 0.9% of the company.
