# Objective Corporation (ASX:OCL): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 6 points, #15 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Not moves by the holder, so not routed to special situations; noted here.

There is no recurring gap between statutory and underlying profit: the company reports statutory NPAT as its profit figure, and the items Adjusted EBITDA leaves out are small. The gap that matters is in how R&D is counted. Of A$33.8m spent on R&D in FY26, A$17.4m was capitalised rather than expensed, against A$5.1m of amortisation [2]. That added about A$12.4m to pre-tax profit, and it has happened every year (A$15.7m capitalised in FY25, A$14.1m in FY24 [5]). With all R&D expensed and interest income taken out, FY26 profit after tax would be about A$26.7m, not A$37.2m.

What the headline hides, and the contract churn behind the guidance cut:
- Department of Defence. Defence Digital Group did not renew Objective's ECM Upgrade and Support Program (USP) agreement, in place for over 25 years; it told the company on 30 June 2026, after negotiations since March 2026 that "had included reaching a stated agreement on commercial terms, subject to final administrative sign off" [9]. The contract earned A$8.252m in FY26 and all revenue under it stopped on 30 June 2026 [3]. Defence still runs Objective ECM for about 140,000 users. The two sides have no agreement on licence entitlements: Objective's position is that licensed users above 85,000 were tied to the USP term that has ended [9]. That is either a future licence claim or a dispute; the filings don't say which.
- National Heavy Vehicle Regulator. A managed services contract from the 2019 iTree acquisition was discontinued after year end, removing about A$3.1m to A$3.2m of ARR in FY27 [10][11].
- Objective Connect: "higher than expected churn where customers required SharePoint connectors" (not quantified) [11].
- ARR bridge: A$120.2m at June 2025, plus A$8.6m growth, less A$8.3m Defence and A$3.3m currency, to A$117.3m at June 2026; less A$3.2m NHVR, A$114.1m adjusted [11]. Information Intelligence ARR fell 5% [2].
- Customer concentration otherwise is low: no customer above 10% of revenue in FY26 or FY25 [3]. Defence was 6.1% of FY26 revenue.
- The FY27 profit guide (Adjusted EBITDA "A$40m+", against A$51.5m in FY26 [11]) is about the A$11.5m of lost Defence and NHVR revenue, which carried little direct cost.

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance (FY27): Adjusted EBITDA of A$40m or more (FY26 investor presentation, 27 Aug 2026) [11]. No ARR growth figure given for FY27.

Changes this year (FY26):
- 21 Aug 2025: target ARR growth of 15% in FY26, "and profit growth above that level" [12].
- 26 Feb 2026: FY26 ARR growth of 10% to 14% at constant currency [6].
- 1 Jul 2026: FY26 closing ARR "approximately in line with FY25" after the Defence non-renewal; renewal would have put growth within the 10% to 14% range [9].
- 27 Aug 2026: FY26 ARR down 2%; FY27 Adjusted EBITDA A$40m+ [2][11].

Record:
- FY26: guided 15% ARR growth, then 10% to 14%; delivered minus 2% (minus 5% after the NHVR loss). Missed. Profit growth "above" 15% was guided; NPAT grew 5%. Missed.
- FY25: guided 15% ARR growth (Aug 2024 [13]); delivered 15.1% [12]. Met.
- FY24: targeted 15%; delivered 11% [13]. Missed.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: was FY26's ARR fall a one-time loss of two contracts, after which growth returns toward the 15% target, or has the business slowed for good? A deep dive would separate the Defence and NHVR losses from the rest of the base, size the Objective Connect churn, and check whether a licence settlement with Defence for users above 85,000 is likely.
- If it goes our way: earnings rebuild from the FY26 base of A$26.7m (profit after tax with all R&D expensed and interest income removed), grow 15% a year for five years, then 5% a year, valued at the company's own post-tax discount rate of 15.5% from its impairment test, which also uses 5% long-term growth [3]. That is A$393.6m (14.7 times the base) plus net cash of about A$77.7m: A$471.3m, or A$4.93 a share on 95.63m shares.
- If it goes against us: earnings settle at the FY27 guidance floor, A$26.7m less the A$11.5m EBITDA drop after 30% tax, A$18.7m, with no growth, at 15.5%: A$120.4m plus A$77.7m: A$198.1m, or A$2.07 a share.
- Price: A$6.22 (close 30 Oct 2026), market value A$595m. Gap if it goes our way: 4.93 / 6.22 - 1 = -21%. Gap if it goes against us: 2.07 / 6.22 - 1 = -67%. At the company's own 15.5% discount rate, the price implies earnings of A$26.7m growing about 10% a year for ever.

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive. The gap if it goes our way is under 25% (it is minus 21%): even a return to 15% growth for five years, valued at the company's own discount rate, comes out below the price. No kill fact applies; the grade rests on the gap alone. The Defence licence question (about 55,000 users above the 85,000 Objective says were tied to the old agreement) is the one fact that could change the arithmetic, and it is worth a line on the watch list if Objective or Defence announces terms.
