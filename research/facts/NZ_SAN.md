# Sanford (NZX:SAN): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 1 point, #16 in the NZX table of reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07); earlier quick look rejected.
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. A New Zealand seafood company: salmon farming, mussel farming and processing (Havelock in Marlborough, and the Coromandel), and wild catch (inshore and deepwater fishing, scampi, a Timaru factory and fishing partners) on its own fishing quota. FY25 (year to 30 September 2025) revenue NZ$584.1m [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. KPMG's opinion on the FY25 accounts is unmodified, with key audit matters but no material uncertainty paragraph [2]. Trading was halted for one day, 4 June 2026, at a holder's request for a block trade, and resumed on 5 June [3]. The shares carry NZX's Non-Standard designation because the constitution lets the board refuse transfers to, and suspend the votes of, overseas persons, so that Sanford never becomes an "overseas person" under the Overseas Investment Act (it owns fishing quota) [2].
- Too thin to trade? No. NZX's own daily trading data: median on-market value over the 20 sessions 9 October to 6 November 2026 was NZ$68,837 a day (lowest NZ$6,044, highest NZ$363,411; 18 of the 20 sessions above NZ$12,500) [18]. The 60-session median is NZ$75,876 and the 250-session median NZ$84,710.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No takeover, scheme or review has been announced and no holder is near 50%, so the sheet continues. But a strategic holder is moving in a way that points at a bid. On 26 October 2026 Umios Corporation (the Japanese seafood group formerly called Maruha Nichiro) agreed to buy Ngāi Tahu's remaining 9,638,100 shares (10.31%) at NZ$7.47 each, NZ$72.0m, settled 2 November, taking Umios from 5.22% to 15.53% [4][5]. Umios must top Ngāi Tahu up to any higher price if, within 12 months of settlement, it signs a scheme with Sanford or gives notice of a full or partial takeover offer that succeeds, or buys shares from others at a higher price once above 17.532% [4]. Any bid would also need Overseas Investment Office consent for the fishing quota and the board's use of its overseas-ownership powers [2]. Noted in the report back for the special situations watch list.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit. Impairments have been taken out in each of the last four years (NZ$1.3m, NZ$1.4m, NZ$16.9m, NZ$3.4m from FY22 to FY25) and restructuring costs in each of them too (NZ$0.3m, NZ$5.5m, NZ$1.5m, NZ$0.6m), so these are recurring costs rather than one-offs, though small beside FY25's profit [2]. The bigger point runs the other way: adjusted EBIT includes the yearly revaluation of fish and mussels in the water, which added NZ$12.3m in FY24, took off NZ$5.4m in FY25 and added NZ$17.5m in H1 FY26, against NZ$3.3m a year earlier [2][7].

Profit after tax without stock revaluations and the H1 FY26 fuel gain (desk's arithmetic, at 28% tax): FY25 NZ$67.6m; H1 FY25 NZ$31.6m; H1 FY26 NZ$27.2m. The twelve months to March 2026 on that basis: NZ$63.2m, so the "record" half-year profit was a weaker underlying half than a year earlier [2][7].

What the headline numbers hide:
- Management says FY25 was helped by "factors outside Sanford's control" and that it "should not be assumed" FY25 or the H1 FY26 result will be repeated [1][9].
- Markets: North America 27% and China 15% of FY25 sales; US tariffs hit mussel prices from mid-2025, and scampi prices are soft in China. Four species (salmon, mussels, hoki, scampi) are 81% of sales [2].
- Mussels' contribution fell 57% in H1 FY26 and frozen half-shell stock was building [6].
- Working capital swung against cash in H1 FY26 (operating cash flow NZ$13.7m against NZ$42.4m of profit) [6][7].
- Book value is mostly fishing quota held at cost: NZ$377m of NZ quota sits in the books, and the FY25 annual report says an independent valuation shows headroom over that figure larger than the NZ$224m gap between net assets and market value at 30 September 2025; an independent valuation of Sanford as a whole put net assets of NZ$741m within its range [2].

## 3. Guidance and record
Sanford gives no numerical earnings guidance. FY26 statements: the FY25 result (18 Nov 2025) said FY25 "should not be assumed" to repeat and that debt would be cut further [1]; the annual meeting (17 Dec 2025) expected the new scampi boat to help wild catch volumes in the second half [10]; the half-year (14 May 2026) said the half "should not be assumed" to repeat in H2, warned of higher interest rates, and kept the interim dividend at 5c given the war in Iran and higher capital spending in H2 [6][9]. No changes since. The last two years cannot be scored against guidance because none was given; adjusted EBIT rose from NZ$49.4m (FY23) to NZ$74.2m (FY24) and NZ$105.2m (FY25) [2].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: are FY25's profits (about NZ$63m after tax, before stock revaluations) Sanford's new level as a leaner company with lower debt, or a peak that falls back toward FY24's level as mussel and scampi prices soften? The FY26 result on 17 November shows the full year on a like-for-like basis. Behind it sits a second question for the watch list: what Umios intends with 15.5% and a 12-month top-up clause.
- How the market prices it: listed New Zealand food producers are priced on earnings, at multiples set by whether earnings are seen as lasting. Peer multiples (desk's arithmetic from filings, prices at the 6 Nov 2026 close): Scales Corporation (NZX:SCL) at 14.4 times underlying profit to shareholders (NZ$938.1m over NZ$65.0m for the twelve months to June 2026) [19]; Delegat Group (NZX:DGL) at 7.3 times operating profit after tax (NZ$450.0m over NZ$61.5m for FY26 to June 2026) [20]. The mechanism is the results, or a bid.
- If it goes our way: profit holds at the twelve-month NZ$63.2m (stock revaluations and the fuel gain taken out) and earns Scales' 14.4 times: NZ$911.5m, on 93.51m shares = NZ$9.75 a share. (At Delegat's 7.3 times it would be NZ$4.94; at the average of the two, NZ$7.35.)
- If it goes against us: earnings fall back to FY24's adjusted EBIT without its stock revaluation gain (NZ$61.9m), less FY25's NZ$12.1m of interest, taxed at 28% = NZ$35.8m, at Delegat's 7.3 times = NZ$262.3m, or NZ$2.81 a share.
- The good case, for the record: net assets NZ$8.34 a share at 31 March 2026, and above NZ$10.74 a share if the quota headroom stated in the FY25 report is added. These count only through a bid, which is not under way. Umios paid NZ$7.47 a share for its 10.3% block [4].
- Price: NZ$6.65 (close 6 Nov 2026, NZX), market value NZ$621.8m, about 9.8 times the twelve-month profit above. It was NZ$6.10 on 23 October, before the Umios purchase was disclosed. Gap if it goes our way: 9.75 / 6.65 - 1 = +47%. Gap if it goes against us: 2.81 / 6.65 - 1 = -58%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is 47%; the question can be answered from filings (stock revaluations, segment contributions, cash flow and debt are all reported each half); and the FY26 result on 17 November 2026 falls inside six months. The gap rests on Scales' multiple: at Delegat's it would be below the price, so the deep dive should test which multiple a lasting Sanford earns. Not a value pick: the value if it goes against us is 58% below the price. Separately, Umios's 15.5% and its 12-month top-up promise make Sanford a pre-bid holder situation for the special situations watch list; the overseas-ownership powers in the constitution and the need for Overseas Investment Office consent for fishing quota are the main hurdles to any bid.
