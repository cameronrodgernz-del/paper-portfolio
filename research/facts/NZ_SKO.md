# Serko (NZX:SKO): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 1 point, #25 in the NZX table of reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-01). Red flag in the ranking: no earnings in the latest year. Watched, with its 1H27 result and a guidance revision due 16 November 2026. Also listed on the ASX.
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Business travel booking software: it runs Booking.com for Business (Europe and other markets made $75.1m of FY26's $119.4m revenue), managed travel booking in Australasia and the US (including GetThere, bought in FY25), and is building an AI booking product, Serko.ai [1][2]. Supplier commissions, mainly from Booking.com for Business, were $73.4m, or 61%, of FY26 revenue [2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Deloitte's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified and the accounts are on a going-concern basis [2]. No debt, $54.1m of cash and deposits [1].
- Too thin to trade? Not measured. I could not get 20 sessions of NZX daily volume: the secondary sources I could reach either gave no daily table or were blocked (see the last section). The evidence I have points to a pass: FirstCape's notice of 28 October 2026 shows its funds bought $6.37m and sold 5,493,108 shares for $7.10m between 3 March and 27 October 2026, about $80,000 a session from one manager alone (venue not stated) [8]. The bid and offer on 30 October were $1.21 and $1.35, an 11% spread [13]. Needs a measured median before any deep dive.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder, FirstCape (including Harbour), has 15.36% and is a net seller since March [8]. The special meeting of 24 August 2026 approved share issue capacity for staff incentives, not a transaction [11].

## 3. Guidance and record
- FY25: total income $85m to $92m, set in May 2024, excluding GetThere and affirmed on 29 October 2024 [5]. Delivered $90.5m, including part of a year of GetThere [3].
- FY26: total income $115m to $123m and total spend $127m to $133m (20 May 2025) [3]. 18 November 2025: income reaffirmed, spend cut to $124m to $128m [4]. 10 March 2026: income narrowed to $119m to $121m, spend to $121m to $123m [6]. Delivered $120.9m of income and $122.9m of spend [1].
- FY27: total income $128m to $134m and total spend $132m to $140m (20 May 2026), with the range driven by the timing of a US corporate initiative [1].
- 29 July 2026 (first-quarter update): completed room nights up 39%, active customers up 35%, revenue per room night down 11% to €8.70; Middle East effect small (under 3% of room nights before the conflict) [7].
- 27 October 2026: first half stronger than expected; expects a modest upward revision to FY27 income guidance at the 16 November result, spend towards the middle of its range, and positive free cash flow for the half and the year. The US corporate initiative is not expected to add much to FY27 [9].

Record: the last two years' income guidance was met, and FY26 spend came in below the original range.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is the core business (Booking.com for Business and Australasia) now growing and paying for itself, so that it is worth well above what is left once the cash is taken out, given that most revenue depends on one partner whose agreement comes up in 2029? The 16 November result shows the half's free cash flow and the new guidance.
- If it goes our way: 1.5 times FY27 total income at the top of the current range ($134m) is $201m for the business, plus $54.1m of cash and deposits, $255.1m, or $2.02 a share on 126.14m shares.
- If it goes against us: Booking.com for Business weakens ahead of 2029. Value the business outside Europe and other markets ($119.4m less $75.1m, $44.2m of FY26 revenue) at 0.5 times, $22.1m, plus $54.1m of cash, $76.2m, or 60c a share.
- The multiples are my stated assumptions; the deep dive should test them against listed travel software and booking peers. Cash alone is 43c a share.
- Only now the price: the close on 30 October 2026 was $1.23 [13]. At that price the business outside cash is valued at about $101m, about 0.77 times FY27 income at the midpoint. Gap if it goes our way, 2.02 / 1.23 less 1 = +64.4%. Gap if it goes against us, 0.60 / 1.23 less 1 = -50.8%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now, once liquidity is measured. The gap if it goes our way is 64%, the question can mostly be answered from the filings (cash flow, the revenue split and what the annual report says about the Booking.com agreement), and the 16 November 2026 result is inside six months. The 2029 renewal itself can't be settled by any event in the window, which is why the "against" figure is so far below the price.
