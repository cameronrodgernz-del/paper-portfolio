## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. IVE is an Australian marketing services business: catalogue and commercial printing and letterbox distribution, packaging, direct mail and data, merchandise and uniforms, creative agencies and third-party logistics, with FY26 revenue of A$941.8m [1].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. KPMG's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified, with no going-concern note [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$359,022 a day (lowest A$120,124; all 20 above A$10,000), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz [25]. At 10% of the median for two sessions the cap would be about A$71,800, well above any position the sleeve would take.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries, and the hold ended on 8 Nov 2026.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The "Scheme Implementation Deed" headline of 21 September 2026 is IVE buying Motio Limited (ASX:MXO), a digital out-of-home screen business, for A$0.06 a share in cash (about A$20.9m at most), funded from IVE's debt facility; IVE is the buyer, not the target [5][6]. No holder is near 50%.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs. The pre-tax items left out of underlying profit were A$23.0m in FY24, A$9.1m in FY25 and A$20.4m in FY26 [1][9][17]. Two kinds come back every year: Lasoo's operating loss (A$5.8m, A$6.2m, A$6.6m) and restructuring (A$13.1m, A$3.0m, A$14.7m). FY27 guidance excludes both again: about A$3.5m of Lasoo loss and about A$3.5m of restructuring and other costs, both after tax [2]. They are ongoing costs of this business, not one-offs.

What the headline numbers hide:
- The core print and catalogue business is shrinking. Without acquisitions revenue fell about 5.2% in FY26, led by catalogue pagination and volumes; margin gains (material margin 51.4% against 49.3%) carried profit [1].
- Leases tripled. The Dandenong South and Kemps Creek moves lifted lease liabilities from A$112.5m to A$283.5m. Underlying profit before AASB 16 counts the rent; underlying profit after it does not, so the two measures will diverge for years [1].
- Cash after capital spending, leases and restructuring fell to A$12.5m in FY26 from A$49.8m, while dividends paid were A$27.7m and A$38.0m went on acquisitions; net debt rose A$58.8m [1]. Capital spending is guided down to about A$26m in FY27 [2].
- Book value is mostly goodwill: tangible assets are 15.5c a share [1].
- Customers are spread out: the largest is about 8.3% of revenue, the top 20 just under 40% [1].
- Since November 2025 IVE has bought Impressu (A$13.5m, about 3.0 times its expected EBITDA, with a 6+2 year Domino's supply agreement), Budget Mail Services (A$1m), Daily Press (A$25m upfront and up to A$10m more on earn-outs, against about A$5.5m of EBITDA) and has agreed to buy Motio (enterprise value about A$16.7m, 6.7 times FY26 cash EBITDA of A$2.5m) [5][6][14][15].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: do the costs IVE leaves out of underlying profit (Lasoo losses and site restructuring, A$9m to A$23m pre-tax in each of FY24 to FY26) really fall to about A$10m in FY27 as guided, and does profit hold while catalogue revenue keeps shrinking? The 1H27 result in February 2027 shows both, and shows whether cash after leases and capital spending recovers from A$12.5m toward FY25's A$49.8m.
- How the market prices this kind of business, and the mechanism: trade buyers price Australian marketing services businesses on enterprise value to EBITDA after rent (that is, before AASB 16). IVE itself agreed in September 2026 to pay 6.7 times FY26 cash EBITDA for Motio [6]; in the past year it also paid about 3.0 times for Impressu and about 4.5 times upfront (6.4 times with all earn-outs) for Daily Press [14][15]. The 6.7 times is used for both cases below. The price would get there through the 1H27 and FY27 results and the guided cash flow, with the buy-back as a steady buyer.
- If it goes our way: FY27 guidance holds. EBITDA before AASB 16 stays at FY26's A$112.6m, plus Motio's A$2.5m, less the guided Lasoo and restructuring costs taken as costs (A$3.5m plus A$3.5m after tax, about A$10.0m pre-tax): A$105.1m. At 6.7 times, enterprise value A$704.2m, less pro forma net debt of A$192.4m, is A$511.8m, or A$3.34 a share on 153.32m shares. That is about 11.2 times the A$45.5m of profit left after those costs.
- If it goes against us: the left-out costs and the catalogue decline keep cash EBITDA after rent at its FY24 to FY26 average, counting every cost. Statutory EBITDA less lease payments was A$69.1m (FY24: A$107.1m less A$38.0m), A$97.0m (FY25: A$129.6m less A$32.6m) and A$93.7m (FY26: A$133.8m before the A$6.7m of duplicated rent booked as restructuring, less A$40.1m), an average of A$86.6m; plus Motio's A$2.5m is A$89.1m. At 6.7 times, A$596.9m, less A$192.4m, is A$404.5m, or A$2.64 a share.
- Only now the price: A$2.29, the last trade on 6 November 2026 (ASX company data, header priceLast; bid A$2.27, offer A$2.35), a market value of A$351.1m [24]. Gap if it goes our way: 3.34 / 2.29 - 1 = +46%. Gap if it goes against us: 2.64 / 2.29 - 1 = +15%.
- For scale: at A$2.29 the market pays 5.2 times the our-way EBITDA (enterprise value A$543.5m including Motio's debt) and 7.7 times the A$45.5m of profit after the recurring costs. The result depends on the multiple: at the 4.5 times IVE paid upfront for Daily Press the values are A$1.83 (our way, -20%) and A$1.36 (against, -41%). At Motio's 6.7 times both cases sit above the price.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now, and it also meets the value pick test (rulebook section 5a): the gap if it goes our way is +46%, and the value if it goes against us is 15% above the price, not more than 10% below it. The question can be answered from filings (the segment and non-operating notes, the lease schedule, the Lasoo numbers and cash conversion), and the AGM on 25 November 2026 and the 1H27 result about 25 February 2027 should show it. The deep dive's first job is the multiple: the A grade rests on Motio's 6.7 times, and at the 4.5 times IVE paid upfront for Daily Press neither case clears the price. It should also test whether the AASB 16 split hides rent that has risen for good with the new sites.
