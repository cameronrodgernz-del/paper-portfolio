# Kina Securities (ASX:KSL): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #24 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Kina is the second-largest bank in Papua New Guinea (16.9% of system lending, 10.9% of deposits at June 2026, HY26
presentation p7), with a wealth arm (funds administration and management, PGK14bn under management). It reports in kina
(PGK), is incorporated in PNG, and is listed on the ASX and PNGX. All figures are PGK unless marked A$. Financial year
ends 31 December; the latest half is to 30 June 2026.

A note on the ranking's numbers: the "net cash about 107% of market value" comes from the ASX company data treating a
bank's balance sheet like a company's. For a bank, cash and government bills are funded by customer deposits, so there is
no net cash in the usual sense. As the brief asks, Kina is valued here on book value and return on equity against a
listed peer, BSP Financial Group (ASX:BFL, PNGX:BSP), the largest PNG bank.

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An operating bank.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Deloitte's opinion on the FY25 accounts
  is unmodified (statutory accounts, 31 Mar 2026). Two points from that audit: the auditor listed "the carrying value of
  the Group's net assets exceeding the market capitalisation" as an impairment indicator for the K92.8m goodwill (key
  audit matter), and the audit cut FY25 profit by K6.1m after the results release (section 2).
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$188,489 (volume times close,
  data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz; lowest session A$41,314). Two sessions at 10% of that median is about A$37,700.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv lists no
  companies yet, and Kina is a bank.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The register is spread: the
  largest holder is HSBC Custody Nominees at 7.63% and the top 20 hold 41.6% (annual report, 31 Mar 2026). The company
  says it is open to acquisitions ("inorganic opportunities"), but nothing is announced.

Nothing ends the sheet.

Return on equity: 16.6% annualised in HY26 (company figure, HY26 4D p3); FY25 16.6% on the audited profit and average
equity (114.6 / average of 666.2 and 717.9); FY24 15.4%.

Does the gap between statutory and underlying profit recur? Yes. The deferred tax write-down came in FY24 (9.4m) and again
in FY25 (5.5m) as the bank tax rate stepped down from 45% to 40% to 35%; the company says 35% applies from 2026, so this
particular charge should stop. FY25 also carried a "one-off" K19.7m extra loan provision for aged bad loans, and the
audit moved K9.1m of costs it had capitalised (anti-money-laundering and ERP projects) back into expenses. Statutory profit
is the fairer base.

What the headline numbers hide:
- Bad loans against provisions. Non-performing loans are 8.9% of the book at June 2026 (8.7% at December 2025 on the
  new method that counts suspended interest), while provisions cover 2.6% of gross loans. Loan losses were K39.0m in
  FY25 against K18.2m in FY24. The gap between bad loans and provisions is covered by security values the filings don't
  set out.
- Part of FY26's growth is the tax cut. Five points of tax on FY25's K199.1m pre-tax profit is about K10m, roughly half
  the K17m to K23m of growth in the FY26 guidance.
- New costs: the Tier 2 bond adds K17.7m a year in interest before tax (K8.9m per half), from May 2026.
- The kina is falling against the Australian dollar. The rate Kina used to convert its dividends: 1 PGK = A$0.3868
  (Dec 2024), 0.3361 (Dec 2025), 0.3161 (Jun 2026); BSP's rate for its 27 Aug 2026 dividend was A$0.3008. Book value
  per share in A$ has gone backwards while it grew in kina: K1.99 x 0.3868 = A$0.77 (Dec 2024) against K2.14 x 0.3008 =
  A$0.64 now.
- All of the business is in PNG (sovereign rating B-/B, outlook positive since 19 Jun 2026). PNG was added to the FATF
  grey list on 13 Feb 2026; the company expects no material financial impact.
- Goodwill and intangibles are K147.3m, 20% of equity.
- Payments: an outside bank's debit-card problem held back Kina's card acquiring fees in HY26; the company expects it
  fixed by the end of 2H 2026. The PNG government told departments in September 2025 to move accounts to the state-owned
  National Banking Corporation; Kina called the effect "not material".

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance: FY26 net profit after tax of K132m to K138m, up 15% to 20% on FY25's audited K114.6m (7 Jul 2026,
from management accounts). The HY26 result on 31 Aug 2026 (K59.7m) said Kina "expect[s] earnings to increase during the
remainder of 2026" but did not repeat the range. To reach K132m to K138m, the second half needs K72.3m to K78.3m, 21% to
31% more than the first half.

Changes in 2026:
- 27 Feb 2026 (FY25 result): "another year of earnings growth" expected; no number.
- 31 Mar 2026 (audited accounts): FY25 profit cut to K114.6m; outlook "unchanged".
- 7 Jul 2026: the K132m to K138m range.
- 31 Aug 2026: no change to the range, no repeat of it.

Last two years:
- FY24: guidance was underlying profit of K109.5m to K111.2m (confirmed 17 Feb 2025, after the year end). Delivered
  K109.5m underlying, K100.3m statutory: the bottom of the range.
- FY25: "further earnings growth in 2025" (28 Feb 2025). Delivered 14% growth on the audited figure, but the preliminary
  figure had to be cut by K6.1m after release.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: does Kina deliver its K132m to K138m FY26 guidance, which needs a second half 21% to 31% above the first,
  and does it do so without more provisions against a non-performing loan book of 8.9% covered 2.6%? The answer sets the
  return on equity that a bank's price rests on.
- How the value is worked: for a bank, price to book should rise with return on equity. BSP (ASX:BFL) is the listed
  peer: equity K5,217m over 467.22m shares is K11.17 a share; return on equity 23.9% annualised in HY26 (24.9% in the
  September quarter); ASX close A$7.62 on 30 Oct 2026 (price file). So the market pays A$0.682 for each kina of BSP
  book (7.62 / 11.17), about 2.2 to 2.3 times book at the August exchange rates. Working in "A$ per kina of book" means
  the exchange rate drops out of the comparison.
- If it goes our way: guidance met at the midpoint (K135m). Equity at December 2026 is about K757m (K723.8m plus a
  second-half profit of K75.3m, less the October dividend of 14.2 toea on 294.9m shares, K41.9m), so return on average
  equity is 135 / ((717.9 + 757.2) / 2) = 18.3%. Valued like BSP for each point of return: K2.459 x 0.682 x (18.3 / 23.9)
  = A$1.285 a share. Cross-check: K135m at BSP's trailing price to earnings of 9.7 times (A$7.62 over 261.3 toea at
  A$0.3008) is K1,310m, A$394m, A$1.34 a share.
- If it goes against us: the second half repeats the first (FY26 about K119m, return about 16%) and the market keeps
  pricing Kina as it has over the last five year-ends rather than like BSP. Kina's price over its net tangible assets
  (company basis, converted at each year's dividend rate) was 1.34 times (Dec 2021), 1.06 (2022), 1.11 (2023), 1.48
  (2024), 1.76 (2025); average 1.35. K2.144 x A$0.3008 x 1.35 = A$0.87 a share.
- Only now the price: A$1.06 (last trade before 8 Nov 2026, ASX company data; A$1.055 at the 30 Oct close). Gap if it
  goes our way: 1.285 / 1.06 - 1 = +21%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.87 / 1.06 - 1 = -18%. At A$1.06 Kina trades at
  1.43 times book and 7.7 times the midpoint of guidance, against BSP's 2.3 times and 9.7 times.

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive. The gap if it goes our way is 21%, under 25%. Kina is cheaper than BSP for each point of return on
equity, but only by about a fifth, and the falling kina takes some of the gain each year. The FY26 result (about 26 Feb
2027) will show the answer to the guidance question if it is reopened later.
