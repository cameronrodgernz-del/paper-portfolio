# HiTech Group Australia (ASX:HIT): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #29 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

HiTech places ICT contractors and recruits staff, mostly for federal government departments in Canberra, including
Defence; 85% to 90% of its revenue comes from federal government clients and its top 20 clients are all federal
agencies [1]. On 11 September 2026 it bought the Australian business of Hudson (recruitment, contracting and project
services) out of administration (it entered administration in April 2026), which about triples its revenue [2][3][9]. The Hazouri family controls it. Financial year
ends 30 June. All figures A$.

## 1. Kill check
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An operating staffing business.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The FY26 audit opinion is unmodified
  (scanned report, read by text recognition) [4]. On 14 Oct 2026 the company announced that ASX had found it breached
  listing rules 10.1 and 3.1 in July 2020, when the board wrote off a A$638,000 interest-free loan to the CEO, Elias
  Hazouri, without a shareholder vote or an announcement [5]. No suspension.
- Too thin to trade? No, but thin. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$15,587 a day,
  counting six sessions with no trades as zero, from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz. Two sessions at 10% of
  that is about A$3,120, above a 2.5% position (about A$2,250).
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv lists no companies.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The Hudson deal is HiTech
  buying, not being bought. The Hazouri family (Ray Hazouri 38.4%, Elias Hazouri 19.1%, Salem Hazouri 4.3% after the
  September placement, each notified separately) holds about 61.7%, but none of them has bought or sold in the last 12
  months [4]. Not for the special situations watch list.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit is small and does not recur (one employment cost in FY25).

What the headline numbers hide:
- The business being valued has changed. Hudson's acquired assets billed about A$220m in the twelve months to July 2026
  at a 9.3% gross margin, but only about A$150m a year was running by September after the administration (unaudited
  management figures) [9]. The company cut Hudson's annual cost base from A$26m to about A$15m [9]. On those figures
  Hudson's gross profit at A$150m of revenue is about A$14.0m, a little under its cost base; it needs about A$161m of
  revenue to break even before interest. The company calls the price "less than 1.0x pro forma LTM EBITDA", on unaudited
  Hudson accounts [2][9]. Pro forma figures are promised "in due course" [2].
- Price paid: A$4.80m upfront less employee entitlements (about A$3.85m paid at completion plus a A$1.25m deposit), plus
  up to A$3.95m deferred within nine months, subject to margin hurdles [2]. Funding: cash, the A$10m term loan, the
  A$4m placement and the receivables facility; A$8.1m went on refinancing a Hudson facility [12].
- HiTech's own business shrank in FY26: gross profit down 25.5% to A$9.4m as government panels were re-priced; the
  second half's profit (A$2.07m) was no better than the first (A$2.04m) despite the February promise of a second-half
  benefit [13][8].
- Customer concentration: 85% to 90% federal government [1].
- Pay and control: executive chairman Ray Hazouri was paid A$1.20m in FY26 (A$0.90m in FY25) and CEO Elias Hazouri
  A$0.73m, together a third of FY26 profit before tax; the AGM will vote on 500,000 zero-price options for each [4][11].
  The AGM notice lodged on the ASX carries the watermark "DRAFT - FOR INTERNAL REVIEW ONLY. NOT FOR RELEASE" [11]. The
  FY26 results release gives an ex-dividend date (8 Sep) after the record date (7 Sep) [13]. The new CFO and COO
  (from 5 Oct 2026) led the adviser's work for HiTech on the Hudson deal [14].

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance: none in numbers. The company says the Hudson deal is "expected to be EPS accretive on a pro forma basis
pre synergies" and that service agreements "are expected to contribute to stronger margins in FY27" [9][13].

Changes this year:
- 23 Feb 2026 (H1 FY26): investment in staff and systems "expected to materialise in H2FY26" [8]. It did not: H2 profit
  was flat on H1.
- 23 Jun 2026: proposal to Hudson's administrators for up to A$15m [15].
- 20 Jul 2026: binding agreement, A$7m upfront, about A$190m of revenue on a pro forma FY26 basis [16].
- 4 Sep 2026: revised to A$7m less entitlements plus up to A$3m deferred [17].
- 11 Sep 2026: completed; upfront cut to A$4.80m, deferred raised to A$3.95m [2].
- 16 Sep 2026: about A$150m of annualised Hudson revenue, combined revenue about A$216m [12][9].

Record: the 2025 AGM (21 Nov 2025) already warned that first-half FY26 margins had fallen [18]. FY25 was a record (NPAT
A$6.38m); FY26 fell 36%. No numeric guidance to test.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: can HiTech rebuild Hudson's revenue from the A$150m a year it kept toward the A$220m it billed in the
  year to July 2026, on the A$15m cost base, while its own federal business recovers from FY26's margin squeeze? At
  A$150m Hudson loses money; at A$220m it earns about A$5.5m a year before interest.
- The multiple: 10 times profit. The only listed ASX staffing peer in the exchange's data, PeopleIn (ASX:PPE), made a
  loss in FY26 (A$39.3m loss on A$788.7m of revenue) and so has no earnings multiple; it trades at about 0.12 times
  revenue (enterprise value A$94.0m, data/research_data/asx_company.json, 7 Nov 2026). Ten times is a judgement, a little
  below HiTech's own trailing multiple on that data.
- If it goes our way: HiTech's own profit back to its five-year average of A$5.27m (FY22 to FY26); Hudson at A$220m and a
  9.3% gross margin earns A$20.46m gross, A$5.46m after the A$15m cost base, A$4.20m after about A$1.26m of interest
  (term loan and receivables facility), A$2.94m after 30% tax; less the after-tax interest HiTech no longer earns on its
  cash (A$0.24m). Profit A$7.98m at 10 times is A$79.8m, less the A$3.95m deferred payment: A$75.8m, or A$1.59 a share
  on 47.7m shares (46.3m, the 0.4m options issued and the 1.0m proposed).
- If it goes against us: HiTech stays at FY26's A$4.11m; Hudson stays at A$150m (gross profit A$13.95m, A$1.05m short of
  its cost base, A$1.62m loss after interest and tax); less the lost interest. Profit A$2.25m at 10 times: A$22.5m, or
  A$0.47 a share. Cross-check at PeopleIn's 0.12 times revenue on A$216m, less A$3.5m net debt: A$22.3m, A$0.47.
- Only now the price: A$1.06 (Norgate close 29 Oct 2026, the last close in the file; the ASX's last trade on 6 Nov was
  A$1.11). Gap if it goes our way: 1.59 / 1.06 - 1 = +50% (+43% at A$1.11). Gap if it goes against us: 0.47 / 1.06 - 1
  = -55%. At a middle case (Hudson at A$185m) the value is about A$1.11 to A$1.19, close to the price.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is +50%, but the answer needs the event itself: today's
filings hold only unaudited Hudson management figures and no pro forma accounts. The half-year result to 31 December
2026 (about late February 2027) will be the first to show Hudson's revenue and margin under HiTech, and the AGM on 20 Nov
2026 may give an early read. The value if it goes against us (-55%) rules out a value pick. Governance points to weigh in
any deep dive: family control, executive pay against profit, the 2020 loan write-off breach and a draft-marked notice of
meeting.
