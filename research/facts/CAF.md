# Centrepoint Alliance (ASX:CAF): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #19 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Centrepoint is a financial advice licensee (576 advisers on its licence, the number two licensee by ASIC register count), with salaried advisers, managed accounts and the IconiQ platform. Net revenue A$43.0m in FY26 [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No [4].
- Too thin to trade? No, but close to the floor. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 1 to 30 October 2026 was A$37,181 a day (lowest day A$1,900), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz. At 10% of daily volume over two sessions the position limit is about A$7,400.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No holder is over 50% and no approach is announced. But the register is moving and is noted for the special situations watch list (section 4): The Australian Wealth Advisors Group (WAG) has built to 18.26% with a voting agreement over another 4m shares and talks about an option to buy them; Sequoia sold out in August 2026; Pilgrim Private has bought 9.54% since 25 August 2026; TIGA (Thorney) holds 20.83% [10][11][12][13][14][15][16].

The gap between statutory and underlying recurs. "One-off" costs have been A$0.9m, A$0.6m and A$0.6m in FY24, FY25 and FY26, and long-term incentives A$0.3m, A$1.0m and A$1.1m [2]; both are left out of the normalised EBITDA that guidance is set on. In the other direction, statutory profit has been lifted three years running by recognising previously unrecognised tax losses (A$0.7m in FY25, A$1.9m in FY26) [1]. A$6.3m of revenue losses remain unrecognised, so that help is finite [1].

What else the headline numbers hide:
- The "net cash about 10% of market value" in the ranking is gone once the July book purchase, the October dividend and the SEQ deal are counted: about A$1.4m of net debt (section 2 row).
- Advisers fell in the second half: 571 at June 2025, 588 on 12 Dec 2025 (with "an additional 40 advisers currently being onboarded"), 576 at June 2026 [2][17][18]. FY27 guidance needs net revenue growth from a base that went backwards in H2.
- Book value is 72% goodwill and intangibles (A$25.1m of A$34.8m), and much of the rest is deferred tax assets [1].
- Dividends (A$6.1m) were paid from operating cash of A$6.8m, with lease payments (A$1.0m) and loan repayments (A$1.6m) covered by new borrowing (A$3.0m) [1].
- Licensee risk: advisers on the licence can leave, and the licensee carries client claims (A$0.4m provision; ten claims paid in FY26) [1][2].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: does Centrepoint deliver the FY27 step-up to A$14.5m to A$15.5m from an adviser base that shrank in H2 FY26, and once it does, is it still worth only a licensee multiple when its one listed licensee peer trades at about twice that? A deep dive would rebuild FY27 from adviser numbers, fee per adviser, salaried advice and platform flows, strip out the tax-loss credits, and read the WAG, Thorney and Pilgrim build-ups.
- If it goes our way: FY27 guidance midpoint, A$15.0m of normalised EBITDA. Less the recurring LTI (A$1.1m) and one-offs (A$0.6m), depreciation and amortisation of about A$3.9m (A$3.5m in FY26 plus the new books) and A$0.5m of interest: profit before tax A$8.9m, after 30% tax A$6.2m, less about A$0.4m for the 49% of SEQ it doesn't own: A$5.8m. At the trailing earnings multiple of Count (ASX:CUP, the other listed licensee in Centrepoint's own peer chart [19]), 20.2 times in the ASX company data: A$117.6m. Less pro forma net debt of A$1.4m: A$116.2m, or A$0.553 a share on 210.2m shares.
- If it goes against us: FY26 profit without the tax-loss credit (A$6.4m less A$1.9m, A$4.5m) is the run rate, at the 10.0 times average forward multiple for licensee services in Centrepoint's own AGM chart [19]: A$45.0m, less A$1.4m: A$43.7m, or A$0.208 a share.
- Price: A$0.38 (close 30 Oct 2026, and the last trade in the ASX data on 6 Nov 2026), market value A$80m. Gap if it goes our way: 0.553 / 0.38 - 1 = +46%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.208 / 0.38 - 1 = -45%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is +46%; the earnings question can be worked from the filings (adviser counts, the revenue lines and the EBITDA walk), and the AGM on 13 Nov 2026, a likely December trading update and the H1 FY27 result in February 2027 will show whether FY27 is on track, inside six months. Two cautions for the deep dive: most of the gap comes from the multiple (at the company's own 10 times licensee average, even guidance met is worth about A$0.27), so it needs a reason for a re-rating, and the position limit is only about A$7,400. Not a value pick: the "against" value is 45% below the price. The register build-up (WAG near 20% with a voting agreement, Pilgrim at 9.54%, Thorney at 20.83%) also belongs on the special situations watch list.
