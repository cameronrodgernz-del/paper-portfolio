Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. An operating lender and second-hand goods retailer: 200 corporate stores (124 in Australia, 10 in New Zealand, 66 in the UK) doing retail, pawn lending and buying, the Cashies Loan line of credit, run-off payday and vehicle loan books, and fees from 164 franchise stores [1][3].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Deloitte's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified; the key audit matters include the A$32.5m expected credit loss allowance on loans [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$108,903 a day (lowest A$12,303; no session under A$10,000), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries, and consumer lending and pawnbroking are not gaming.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. EZCORP Inc (US) holds 43.53%, below 50%; it took up its full entitlement in the November 2025 raising and has filed no substantial holder change in the last 12 months. No approach or review is announced [1][7].

The gap between statutory and operating profit recurs: transition and acquisition costs were left out in both years (A$0.6m after tax in FY25, A$3.5m in FY26), and the company plans 20 or more franchise store purchases in FY27 [2][3].

What the headline numbers hide:
- The lending business is being replaced. Payday lending (small amount credit contracts) stopped being written on 30 September 2025 and vehicle loans in June 2024; the run-off books were A$72.8m of the A$236.6m gross loan book at 30 June 2026. Personal Finance profit before tax fell 61% to A$9.4m and Vehicle Financing's 25% to A$5.8m [3].
- The new Cashies Loan book grew almost five times to A$114.1m. Expected losses are provided for when loans are written, so fast growth holds profit down early; the net loss rate fell from 16.0% to 11.1% [2][3].
- Second-half profit was lower: operating NPAT of A$13.2m in 1H26 and A$10.0m in 2H26 [2][4].
- Store profits now carry the group: Australian stores' operating EBITDA was A$46.8m (up 50%) and the UK's A$21.7m (up 54%), helped by 47 store purchases and openings and, in the first half, by higher gold prices [3][4].
- Book value includes A$82.2m of goodwill and other intangibles (32% of equity), up from A$47.2m a year earlier as franchise stores are bought at about 4 to 5 times their EBITDA [1][3][8].
- Return on total equity was 8.2% statutory and 9.6% operating in FY26; on tangible equity, 11.2% and 13.2% [1].
- The securitisation facility costs 11.3% and its drawing period ends in August 2027, so it has to be refinanced in the next year [1][2].

## 3. Guidance and record
- No profit or revenue guidance in FY25 or FY26: the results announcements give an outlook and priorities only [2][9]. The FY26 presentation sets out a three-year path: FY26 "strategic reset", FY27 "investment and scale", FY28 onward "earnings growth" [3].
- The one dated promise: the 29-store CCIG purchase (A$37.1m, completed 4 December 2025, at 4.5 times FY25 EBITDA) was forecast to add to earnings per share in its first full year of ownership, which is FY27 [7][8].
- Dividend: 2.0c a year for six years running [2].
- Record: nothing to check against numbers.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is the second half's lower profit (operating NPAT A$10.0m against A$13.2m in the first half) the temporary cost of providing up front for a fast-growing Cashies Loan book while payday loans run off, or the new level once payday income is gone? If the first, return on tangible equity heads back to about 15%; if the second, it sits near 11%. The 1H27 result in February 2027 is the first half-year with no payday lending at all.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: lenders trade on price to tangible book in line with return on equity. The named peers, from their FY26 results and the 30 October 2026 closes [13][14]:
  - Credit Corp (ASX:CCP): FY26 profit A$105.5m on average tangible equity of about A$885.8m (NTA A$13.18 and A$12.86 a share, 68.07m shares), 11.9%; price A$15.01, 1.14 times NTA, so the market asks about 10.5% (return divided by price to book); 9.7 times earnings.
  - Solvar (ASX:SVR, Money3): normalised FY26 profit A$36.1m (statutory A$29.5m) on average NTA of about A$322m (A$1.66 and A$1.72 a share), 11.2%; price A$1.395, 0.84 times NTA, so the market asks about 13.3%; 7.3 times normalised and 9.0 times statutory earnings.
  The mechanism is reported returns: the half-year results show whether the return on tangible equity recovers.
- If it goes our way: operating profit at the 1H26 rate, A$26.4m a year, on average tangible equity of A$176.2m is a 15.0% return. Priced like Credit Corp (10.5% asked), that is 1.43 times NTA of 24.1c: A$0.346 a share.
- If it goes against us: operating profit at the 2H26 rate, A$20.0m a year, is an 11.4% return. Priced like Solvar (13.3% asked), 0.85 times NTA: A$0.205 a share.
- Cross-check on earnings: FY26 operating earnings of 3.24c a share at Credit Corp's 9.7 times is A$0.314; second-half earnings of 2.79c a share at Solvar's statutory 9.0 times is A$0.251.
- Only now the price: A$0.265 at the 6 November 2026 close (bid A$0.265, offer A$0.27), from data/research_data/asx_company.json. Gap if it goes our way, 0.346 / 0.265 less 1 = +31%. Gap if it goes against us, 0.205 / 0.265 less 1 = -23%.

## 7. Grade
B, deep dive when its event is close. The gap if it goes our way is 31%, above 25% but short of the 40% an A needs, and the answer needs the event itself: the 1H27 result (about 23 February 2027) is the first clean half without payday lending. Not a value pick: the value if it goes against us is 23% below the price. On the bad answer the shares are priced about where Solvar is; the case for a gap rests on the new loan book earning like Credit Corp's.
