## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. PGW is a New Zealand rural services business, which it calls the country's leading one: farm and orchard supplies (Rural Supplies, Fruitfed), water, agrichemicals (Agritrade, and Nexan, bought in FY26), livestock agency with GO-STOCK livestock finance, wool and rural real estate; FY26 operating revenue NZ$1,074.4m [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The FY26 audit opinion is unmodified [3].
- Too thin to trade? No. NZX's own daily trading data: median on-market value over the 20 sessions 9 October to 6 November 2026 was NZ$24,880 a day, lowest NZ$710; 13 of the 20 sessions above NZ$12,500 [17]. At 10% of the median for two sessions the largest position would be about NZ$5,000, above the NZ$2,500 smallest order but well under the 2.5% cap.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No holder is over 50%. Agria (Singapore) holds 44.33% and Elders Limited (ASX:ELD) 12.47% [3]. The two are moving: at the October 2025 meeting they voted out the independent chair and deputy chair without giving reasons [12], and Elders' managing director, Mark Allison, joined the board on 1 July 2026 as a non-independent director before retiring from Elders around 30 September 2026 [13]. Noted for the special situations watch list in the report, not a kill.

There is no recurring gap between statutory and underlying profit to explain: PGW reports statutory profit as its headline and its non-operating items were small in both years (NZ$0.2m and NZ$1.1m) [1].

What the headline numbers hide:
- The earnings are cyclical, and FY26 to FY27 is a good part of the cycle. Profit after tax, NZ$m: FY22 24.3, FY23 17.5, FY24 3.1, FY25 10.7, FY26 15.6; five-year average 14.2. Operating EBITDA: 67.2, 61.2, 44.2, 56.1, 64.3 [3]. FY26 was "underpinned by strong red meat and dairy prices and a buoyant rural Real Estate market"; livestock earnings, the second-largest unit, are weighted to the second half [2][5].
- The year is seasonal: the first half (spring) carries the profit and the second half loses money, so half-year numbers mislead [4].
- GO-STOCK is a balance sheet business: PGW lends against farmers' livestock (NZ$88.3m of receivables at June 2026) on borrowed money [1].
- Leases are large: NZ$100.5m of lease liabilities and NZ$28.5m a year of lease principal and interest, all inside operating EBITDA's add-back [1].
- Customers are spread across thousands of farmers and growers, but the business is concentrated in one economy and its farm incomes.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is FY27's guided NZ$70m of operating EBITDA (about NZ$19.7m of profit) a level PGW can hold, through Nexan, a larger GO-STOCK book and growth in Retail & Water, or is it the top of a farm-income cycle that has averaged NZ$14.2m of profit over five years? The December guidance update and the 1H27 result show how the strong spring and livestock prices carry through.
- How the market prices this kind of business, and the mechanism: the only listed rural services peer is Elders (ASX:ELD), which runs the same mix of farm supplies, livestock agency, wool and real estate in Australia. At A$6.40 (ASX company data, 6 Nov 2026) it trades at 13.8 times its FY25 underlying earnings of 46.5c a share (25.2 times its statutory 25.4c, which carries large one-off costs) [20][21]. The 13.8 times is used for both cases. The price would get there through results and guidance updates; the Agria and Elders holdings are a second route through a bid, but no bid is in view.
- If it goes our way: FY27 guidance is met and held. FY26 profit of NZ$15.57m plus the NZ$5.69m rise in EBITDA after 28% tax, with depreciation and interest unchanged, is NZ$19.67m. At 13.76 times, NZ$270.7m, or NZ$3.59 a share on 75.48m shares.
- If it goes against us: profit falls back to its FY22 to FY26 average of NZ$14.24m. At 13.76 times, NZ$196.0m, or NZ$2.60 a share.
- Only now the price: NZ$2.19, the close on 6 November 2026 (bid NZ$2.06, offer NZ$2.19), a market value of NZ$165.3m [17]. Gap if it goes our way: 3.59 / 2.19 - 1 = +64%. Gap if it goes against us: 2.60 / 2.19 - 1 = +19%.
- For scale: at NZ$2.19 the market pays 10.6 times FY26 profit, 8.4 times the our-way profit and 1.34 times net tangible assets. The answer depends on the multiple. At 11.3 times, the median trailing multiple of eight profitable NZX consumer and food companies (Briscoe 16.5, Warehouse 20.7, Hallenstein Glasson 14.4, Delegat 11.4, Sanford 8.6, Scales 11.2, Comvita 8.0, Seeka 5.7) [22], the values are NZ$2.94 (+34%) and NZ$2.13 (-3%). On Elders' price over its own FY21 to FY25 average underlying earnings (69.5c, 9.2 times), the values are NZ$2.40 (+10%) and NZ$1.74 (-21%).

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now, and it meets the value pick test (rulebook section 5a) on the peer multiple: the gap if it goes our way is +64%, and the value if it goes against us is 19% above the price. The question can be worked from filings (segment results across the cycle, GO-STOCK's funding and margin, Nexan's contribution), and the December guidance update and the 1H27 result about 24 February 2027 should show whether FY27 is holding. The grade rests on Elders' 13.8 times its latest underlying earnings; at the NZX consumer and food median the grade would be B, and on Elders' through-the-cycle multiple it would be C, so the deep dive's first job is to settle the multiple, including how much of Elders' adjusted profit is really one-off. The Agria and Elders holdings (56.8% together, with Elders' former chief executive on the board) also belong on the special situations watch list; a deep dive should set out what the Takeovers Code lets each of them do.
