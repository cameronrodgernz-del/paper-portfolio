# Investore Property (NZX:IPL): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 4 points, #4 in the NZX table of reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. A listed company that owns its buildings directly: $1,128m of large format retail at valuation, mostly Woolworths supermarkets, Bunnings stores and the Silverdale Centre, 99.5% let [1]. It has no staff; Stride Investment Management (SIML) manages it under a management agreement, and SIML's stapled partner Stride Property Ltd (SPL) owns 18.8% [1]. Externally managed, but not a fund or unit trust.
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. PwC's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified, with no emphasis of matter [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. NZX's own daily trading data: median on-market value over the 20 sessions 9 October to 6 November 2026 was NZ$107,584 a day, lowest NZ$42,951; all 20 sessions above NZ$12,500 [13].
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is SPL at 18.8% [1]. The September 2025 package (Silverdale purchase from SPL, a wider management mandate, convertible notes) was approved on 20 October 2025 and is complete [5].

The gap between statutory profit and distributable profit recurs. There is a revaluation every half year (a $12.1m gain in FY25, $0.3m in FY26, losses of $98.8m and $185.3m in the "other" line in FY24 and FY23) and a gain on property sales in each of the last two years. Neither is cash, and distributable profit leaves both out.

What the headline numbers hide:
- Debt falling due soon. $250m of bonds, half the drawn debt, matures in the next ten months: $125m at 4.00% on 25 February 2027 and $125m at 2.40% on 31 August 2027 [1]. Replacing both at 4.5% to 5.5% adds $3.3m to $5.8m a year of interest before tax, 0.6c to 1.1c a share after tax at 28%, against 8.13c of distributable profit and a 6.5c dividend.
- The dividend was above AFFO in FY26 (6.50c against 5.81c), because maintenance spending rose from $2.4m to $8.0m [1].
- Tenant concentration. Woolworths pays 51% of contract rent after the Greenlane sale (62% a year earlier); anchor tenants pay 87% [2][3].
- The manager's fees. SIML was paid $7.97m in FY26 (asset management $5.49m, plus building, project, leasing, disposal and other fees); total corporate costs were $8.2m, 12.5% of net rental income. No performance fee was paid; the performance fee calculation carries a 5.6% deficit [1].
- The convertible notes convert on 26 September 2029 at the lower of $1.56 and 98% of the market price, unless Investore pays cash instead [1][5]. At a low share price that issues many shares below NTA (see section 6).
- Book value is all property. No goodwill or intangibles; 99.5% occupancy, average cap rate 6.26%, lease term 5.9 years [1].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: do Investore's book values and its 6.5c dividend hold through the 2027 refinancing of $250m of bonds now costing 2.40% and 4.00%, and with maintenance spending at FY26's $8.0m rather than FY25's $2.4m? Sales support the values: Browns Bay sold 4.9% above book, New Brighton 6.2% above, Greenlane 4.1% above [2][3]. The open points are earnings once the cheap debt is gone, and how much the outside manager's costs take.
- If it goes our way: property at its stated value less debt, the reported NTA: equity $613.1m over 377.6m shares, $1.62. Net of the yearly corporate costs of $8.2m capitalised at the 6.26% cap rate ($131m, 34.7c a share), $1.28.
- If it goes against us: cap rates 0.5 points higher across the portfolio (6.26% to 6.76%, 7.4% lower, $83.4m or 22.1c a share) gives a stressed NTA of $1.40. Valued at the peer median of 0.75 times NTA (below), that is $1.05. Taking the corporate costs off the stressed NTA instead gives the same answer, $1.06.
- Only now the price: the close on 6 November 2026 was $1.01 (bid $1.01, offer $1.025) [14]. Gap if it goes our way, 1.62 / 1.01 less 1 = +60.8% (+26.4% net of corporate costs). Gap if it goes against us, 1.05 / 1.01 less 1 = +4.0%.
- Against its peers, not only its book: Investore trades at 0.62 times NTA. The seven other NZ listed property owners trade at 0.55 to 0.89 times, median 0.75: Precinct 0.89 (NTA $1.13 at 30 June 2026 [15]), Kiwi Property 0.83, Property for Industry 0.77, Vital Healthcare 0.75, Stride 0.66, Argosy 0.65, Asset Plus 0.55 [14]. So on book Investore is cheaper than its peers, 17% below the median and second-lowest of eight, but it sits with the cheap group (Argosy, Stride, Asset Plus), not alone. Kiwi Property, the other listed retail landlord (shopping centres and mixed use), is at 0.83 times; at that multiple Investore would be $1.35 (+33%); at the peer median, $1.22 (+20%). Goodman Property's page returned no data and is left out.
- On cash earnings it is not cheaper. Investore's FY26 AFFO of 5.81c is a 5.75% yield at $1.01, against Stride 6.85% and Argosy 6.62% (from their annual reports [16][17]) and, as read from their FY26 results in the Argosy deep dive, Kiwi Property 6.57%, Precinct 6.38%, Vital 5.87% and PFI 4.92% [18]. At the median of those six, 6.48%, FY26 AFFO is worth $0.90 (-11%); FY25's 6.58c, before maintenance spending tripled, is worth $1.02 (+1%). Whether $8.0m a year of maintenance is the new level matters as much as the refinancing.
- Dilution at today's price: if the notes converted at 98% of $1.01, about 63m new shares would be issued (17% more), and NTA per share would fall from $1.62 to about $1.53. Investore can pay cash instead, and conversion is not until September 2029.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now, as a value pick. The gap if it goes our way is 61%, and the "against" value, $1.05, is above the price, so it meets rulebook section 5a's value pick test without needing a forcing event. That test is marginal on cash: valued on FY26 AFFO at the peers' AFFO yield, Investore is 11% below the price, just outside the 10% limit. The question can be worked from the filings, and two events inside six months bear on it: the 1H27 result in late November and the refinancing of the February 2027 bond. The peer check is the caution: Investore is cheaper than the sector median, but no cheaper than Argosy or Stride, and on AFFO it is priced like its peers, so a deep dive has to explain why an externally managed supermarket landlord should trade like Kiwi Property rather than like its manager, and whether FY26's maintenance spending repeats.
