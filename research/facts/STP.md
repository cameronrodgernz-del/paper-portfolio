## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Step One sells its own underwear online, mainly direct to customers in Australia and the UK, with a small US business and a growing share through third-party retailers [1].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. The auditor's opinion on the June 2026 accounts is unmodified [1]. Step One left the S&P/ASX 300 on 22 Sep 2025 [2].
- Too thin to trade? No, but close. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 2 to 30 October 2026 was A$14,320 a day (two sessions traded under A$110), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz. Two sessions at 10% of the median is about A$2,860, which only just covers the smallest research-pick position.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No moves. Founder and CEO Greg Taylor's Dallard Road Pty Ltd holds 107,327,144 shares (57.91%) [1]. There has been no substantial holder notice, director trade or proposal from him in the last 12 months. He controls any vote, so a take-private is possible at any time, but nothing points to one.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit doesn't recur so far: FY26 is the first year with a large stock provision (FY25's provision movement was A$0.3m) [1]. In December 2025 the company said the old stock was "now fully provisioned, and no further material provisions are anticipated" [7].

What the headline numbers hide:
- Cash is about the whole market value, but it is the only buffer for a shrinking business. Revenue fell 26.7%; Australia fell 36% to A$35.0m (55% of sales), the UK 11% to A$26.2m, the US was A$2.5m [1]. Advertising was A$21.1m, 33% of revenue [1].
- Gross margin after the provision was 61.6% (adjusted 75.9%); the provision is 40% of stock on hand (A$10.4m on A$26.2m) [1][5].
- Dividends are switched off until retained earnings are positive again; accumulated losses are A$10.8m, so about A$11m of profit must come first. A$8.6m of franking credits are available [1].
- One controlling holder (57.9%) who is also CEO.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: has the reset stopped the cash going out? With discounting cut back and the old stock written down, can Step One hold revenue near A$60m at about a 75% gross margin and earn again, or will revenue keep falling and slowly use up the A$25.8m of cash that is about the whole market value?
- If it goes our way: revenue holds near A$60m and EBITDA recovers to A$6m (10% of sales; FY25 was 20%), valued at 6 times, A$36.0m, plus net cash A$25.8m: A$61.8m on 185.3m shares, A$0.334 a share.
- If it goes against us: no recovery. Cash A$25.8m, plus half the A$15.8m book value of stock (A$7.9m), less trade payables A$5.9m and leases A$0.8m, less two more years of losses at A$2m a year (A$4.0m): A$23.1m, A$0.124 a share. Net tangible assets of 21.8c a share are above this.
- Price: A$0.135 (last trade in the ASX company data fetched 7 Nov 2026; Norgate close 30 Oct 2026 A$0.13), market value A$25.0m. Gap if it goes our way: 0.334 / 0.135 - 1 = +147%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.124 / 0.135 - 1 = -8%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now, as a value pick (rulebook section 5a). The gap if it goes our way is +147% and the value if it goes against us is 8% below the price, inside the 10% allowed, so no forcing event is needed; the 1H FY27 result (about 18 Feb 2027) is the first read on whether the reset holds. The deep dive should test the bad case harder: the cash burn if revenue falls another 25%, how much of the provisioned stock turns into cash, and what the controlling holder intends for the cash. Liquidity is the binding limit: at A$14,320 a day the position can be no larger than about A$2,860.
