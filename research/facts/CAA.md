# Capral (ASX:CAA): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 5 points, #22 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Australia's largest aluminium extruder and distributor: six extrusion plants (eight presses, 65,000 tonnes a year of capacity), nine distribution centres and 15 trade centres; about 27% market share. FY25 (year to 31 December 2025) revenue A$686.0m on 65,000 tonnes [1][2][4].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. KPMG's opinion on the FY25 accounts is unmodified; its key audit matters are impairment testing of non-financial assets and the recognition of deferred tax assets [3]. KPMG's review of the half to 30 June 2026 raises no matters [4].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$217,363 a day (lowest A$36,323; every session above A$10,000), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz [24]. The 60-session median is A$90,448.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder is Allan Gray Australia with 25.05% (14 July 2026); no approach or review has been announced [10]. The company is buying back up to 1,617,000 shares on market until 18 December 2026 [14].

Whether the gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs:
- The deferred tax benefit is the same kind of item most years: A$3.0m (FY20), A$9.4m (FY21), A$8.4m (FY22), nil (FY23), A$3.6m (FY24), A$2.5m (FY25), nil (1H26) [1][20][21][22]. It is a book entry for past tax losses, not cash, and it recurs.
- The LME revaluation (stock marked to the aluminium price) also recurs every year, in either direction: +A$2.8m (FY21), -A$2.2m (FY22), -A$1.1m (FY23), +A$0.3m (FY24), -A$0.5m (FY25), +A$1.5m (1H26) [1][4][20][21][22]. Over six periods it nets to about nil, so leaving it out is fair.
- The A$3.0m insurance settlement in FY25 is a true one-off [1].

Underlying pre-tax profit (statutory profit before tax, less significant items) has been steady for five years: A$30.5m (FY21), A$34.6m (FY22), A$32.9m (FY23), A$28.8m (FY24), A$30.9m (FY25); average A$31.5m. The twelve months to June 2026 are A$31.7m (1H26 A$14.6m plus 2H25 A$17.1m) [1][4][5][20][21][22]. FY20 was A$22.9m before tax [20].

What the headline numbers hide:
- No tax. Capral pays no income tax because of past losses: gross tax losses of A$133.1m at 31 Dec 2025 (A$63.9m booked as an asset, A$69.2m not booked), worth A$39.9m at 30% [1][2]. 1H26 used A$5.3m of that [4]. At about A$9m a year, the losses run out in about four years, from roughly 2030, subject to the tax recoupment rules [1]. Statutory profit is in effect a pre-tax figure: taxed at 30%, the twelve months to June 2026 would be about A$22.2m, not A$31.7m.
- Leases are large. Rent paid (lease principal and interest) was A$22.3m in FY25, against underlying EBIT of A$35.8m. Lease interest (A$4.3m) is inside profit before tax; lease principal (A$18.0m) is in financing cash flow [1].
- Metal prices drive revenue and working capital, not volume. Volume has fallen from 76,300 t (FY21) to 65,000 t (FY25); revenue rose on aluminium prices. The LME price plus regional premium went from A$4.09/kg (June 2025) to A$5.61/kg (June 2026); Middle East smelter supply was interrupted and freight under-recovery cost A$1.0m in 1H26 [4][5][21]. The company expects working capital to lift in 2H26 as higher-cost stock is paid for [5].
- Cash conversion: free cash flow on the company's measure (after capital spending, acquisitions, lease principal and interest received) was A$21.5m in FY24, A$10.2m in FY25 and A$10.3m in 1H26, against untaxed profit of A$29m to A$33m a year [2][5].
- Imports are the main competitor. Anti-dumping measures on Chinese extrusions were extended to 2030 (October 2025) and on Malaysian and Vietnamese extrusions to 2031; a case on imported windows and doors is expected to be decided late in 2026 [5][17]. Window fabricators report losing share to imported windows [4].
- No customer concentration is disclosed. About half of volume is industrial (transport, marine, solar, fabrication) and half residential and commercial building; all of it is Australian [5].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is Capral a steady earner worth a normal industrial multiple on its taxed profit, plus its net cash and four more years without tax, or a low-return business (about 9% after tax on its equity, against 20% or more at its peers) that is worth only its tangible book? Its pre-tax profit has stayed between A$29m and A$35m for five years, and FY26 guidance is flat. A deep dive would test the peer comparison, the second-half margin squeeze from metal costs, and how much of the profit turns into cash.
- How the market prices this kind of business: on taxed earnings, net of cash. Peers, on their FY26 filings and Norgate closes of 30 Oct 2026 (the same date as Capral's price below), with shares on issue from data/research_data/asx_company.json:
  - Bisalloy Steel (ASX:BIS), an Australian steel plate maker and distributor: market value A$198.1m (A$4.10 x 48,310,603 shares), less net cash A$1.5m (cash A$4.43m, loans A$2.93m), over profit to owners of A$16.77m = 11.7 times. Return on equity 21.2%, 2.6 times book [25].
  - Korvest (ASX:KOV), galvanising and cable supports: A$187.3m (A$15.70 x 11,927,617), plus a A$1.54m overdraft, over A$12.41m = 15.2 times. Return on equity 19.8%, 2.9 times book [26].
  Capral's taxed return on equity is 9.0% (twelve-month taxed profit A$22.2m including interest, over average equity of A$247.7m).
- The mechanism: results and the buy-back. The board buys back about 10% of the shares a year below net tangible assets, which lifts value per share; there is no controlling holder, so a bid is possible but none is under way.
- If it goes our way: twelve-month underlying pre-tax profit A$31.74m, less A$1.53m of interest received, is A$30.21m; taxed at 30%, A$21.15m. At Bisalloy's 11.7 times: A$247.9m. Add net cash of A$56.7m and the tax losses: A$34.6m of tax not paid, used at A$9.1m a year (30% of A$30.2m) over 3.8 years, worth A$27.6m discounted at 10%. Total A$332.2m on 15,424,876 shares = A$21.54 a share. (At Korvest's 15.2 times: A$26.32.)
- If it goes against us: Capral is valued at its tangible book because it earns about its cost of capital. Net tangible assets A$13.43 a share at 30 June 2026 (company figure; buy-backs below that since then lift it slightly). The harsher version leaves out the deferred tax asset: A$213.4m less A$29.0m less the A$5.33m spent on buy-backs since June, over 15,424,876 shares = A$11.61. For comparison, FY24's lower profit (A$28.8m) taxed and put on Bisalloy's multiple would still give A$19.87: the answer turns on the multiple, not the earnings.
- Only now the price: A$13.02 (Norgate close 30 Oct 2026; the ASX's last trade on 6 Nov was A$13.16, ASX company data). Gap if it goes our way: 21.54 / 13.02 - 1 = +65% (+64% at A$13.16). Gap if it goes against us: 13.43 / 13.02 - 1 = +3% (+2%); the harsher case, 11.61 / 13.02 - 1 = -11% (-12%). At A$13.02 the market values Capral at about 7 times its taxed profit, net of cash.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now, as a value pick (rulebook section 5a). The gap if it goes our way is +65%, and the value if it goes against us (tangible book) is 3% above the price, inside the 10% allowed, so no forcing event is needed; the FY26 result on 26 Feb 2027 is the next check. The question can be worked from filings. The deep dive must test three things: whether Bisalloy and Korvest, with twice Capral's return on equity, are fair comparisons at all (if not, the "our way" value falls sharply); the harsher bad case without the deferred tax asset (A$11.61, -11%), which would fail the value pick test; and the second-half margin squeeze from metal and freight costs, which led to the May guidance cut.
