# Gentrack Group (NZX:GTK): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 1 point, #13 in the NZX table of reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07); earlier quick look rejected. Also listed on the ASX.
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Billing and customer software for energy and water retailers (the Utilities business, mainly the UK, Australia and New Zealand, now moving customers to its g2 cloud platform) and airport operations and billing software (Veovo). FY25 (year to 30 September 2025) revenue NZ$230.2m, of which NZ$155.4m recurring [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. EY's opinion on the FY25 accounts is unmodified, with no material uncertainty paragraph [2]. No borrowings; an undrawn NZ$25m BNZ facility runs to 17 December 2027 [2].
- Too thin to trade? No. NZX's own daily trading data: median on-market value over the 20 sessions 9 October to 6 November 2026 was NZ$37,466 a day (lowest NZ$5,075, highest NZ$194,674; 18 of the 20 sessions above NZ$12,500) [20]. The 60-session median is NZ$69,714 and the 250-session median NZ$120,074, so trading has thinned as the price fell. The shares also trade on the ASX.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. No approach, review or sale has been announced, and no holder is near 50%: the largest substantial holder is Regal Partners at 7.64% (section 4). The board announced on 5 May 2026 that it intends an on-market buyback of up to NZ$20m (no more than 5% of shares) over up to 12 months after the half-year result [3]; no buyback notice had been lodged on the NZX or the ASX by 8 November 2026, and shares on issue rose from 112,458,975 to 112,472,167 between April and October 2026 through director fee shares only [16][21].

Index note: Gentrack joined the S&P/NZX 20 in June 2025 [19]; the repo's Norgate index file records it leaving the S&P/ASX 300 on 21 September 2026 (data/norgate/index_changes.csv). Its NZX 20 membership now was not checked against S&P's own lists.

The gap between statutory and underlying profit. The company reports EBITDA rather than an "underlying profit", so little is left out of its headline measure except acquisition costs (NZ$0.6m in H1 FY26). Two items move NPAT and recur in substance: tax credits from shares vesting under the long-term incentive scheme (NZ$0.6m in FY25 and NZ$3.9m in H1 FY26, the same kind two periods running), and currency gains on cash held in sterling (NZ$3.2m in FY25) [1][4].

Pre-tax cash earnings (operating cash flow before tax, less equipment, lease payments and lease interest; no development is capitalised): NZ$24.3m in FY25 and NZ$37.9m in FY24, the difference being mostly working capital (receivables rose NZ$9.1m in FY25) [2]. On EBITDA less equipment and leases, treating share-based pay as a cost: NZ$22.3m in FY25 [2].

What the headline numbers hide:
- Share-based pay is large and dilutes holders: NZ$6.3m expensed in FY25 and NZ$10.2m in FY24; shares on issue rose 4.1% in FY25 and another 4.4% in the six months to March 2026 as performance rights vested [2][5].
- Non-recurring project revenue swings the result: two new-client delays cut H1 FY26 non-recurring revenue by 30% and EBITDA by 39% [4]. In July 2025 an Australian customer dropped Gentrack from its platform replacement process [9].
- Book value is mostly goodwill (48% of equity at March 2026), almost all of it in Utilities (NZ$115.8m) [5].
- About NZ$41m of the NZ$73.2m March cash has gone on two acquisitions, with up to about NZ$18.5m of earn-outs to come [6][7].

## 3. Guidance and record
FY26 (year to September 2026):
- 24 Nov 2025, FY25 result: "revenue growth will be higher in FY26 than in FY25" (FY25 growth was 8%), software and support revenue to grow about 10%, no numbers for EBITDA; medium-term target of revenue growth above 15% a year and a 15% to 20% EBITDA margin after expensing development [1][10]. The 25 Feb 2026 annual meeting said there was "no change to the guidance previously provided" [11].
- 5 May 2026: cut to revenue NZ$229m to NZ$238m (below FY25's growth, so a cut), recurring revenue about NZ$174m, EBITDA NZ$13.5m to NZ$20m excluding acquisition costs; the board chose "to prioritise growth and global leadership over short term EBITDA" [3]. Repeated at the half-year result on 18 May 2026, which said it was too early to guide on FY27 [4].
- Delivered: due 23 November 2026 [12].
FY25: guidance on 19 May 2025 of revenue at or above NZ$230m and an EBITDA margin above 12% [13]; delivered NZ$230.2m and 12.1% [1].
FY24: guidance raised on 20 May 2024 to revenue of about NZ$200m and EBITDA of NZ$23.5m to NZ$26.5m [14]; delivered NZ$213.2m and NZ$23.6m, the bottom of the EBITDA range [2].
Record: met FY24 and FY25, each at the edge of the range; FY26 cut by about half on EBITDA. The 15% to 20% margin target has never been reached.

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: is FY26's halving of EBITDA a delay (two late new-client projects and a deliberate shift to more recurring g2 pricing) that FY27 reverses, or has the utilities pipeline stalled so that FY25's earnings were the peak? The 23 November result and any FY27 guidance are the first test.
- How the market prices it: listed software is priced on enterprise value over cash earnings, with the multiple set by growth. The desk's peer multiples (from filings, prices at the 6 Nov 2026 close): Objective Corporation (ASX:OCL) at 21.3 times pre-tax cash earnings after development, equipment and leases, for durable growth; hipages (ASX:HPG) at 7.6 times, for no growth [23]. The mechanism is the results: the price moves when a result shows EBITDA and cash earnings back at or above FY25's level.
- If it goes our way: FY27 cash earnings return to FY25's NZ$24.3m and the business earns Objective's 21.3 times: NZ$516.7m, plus about NZ$32.2m of cash after the acquisitions, plus Amber at its NZ$12.9m carrying value, less NZ$18.5m of earn-outs paid in full = NZ$543.3m, on 112.47m shares = NZ$4.83 a share. Cross-checks: on the FY24 to FY25 average of NZ$31.1m it would be NZ$6.12; treating share-based pay as a cost (NZ$22.3m) it would be NZ$4.47; only at the never-reached 15% margin target on FY26 revenue (NZ$35.0m EBITDA, NZ$35.9m cash earnings) would it reach NZ$7.03.
- If it goes against us: cash earnings stay at the FY26 guidance midpoint (EBITDA NZ$16.75m, plus FY25's NZ$6.3m of share-based pay, less FY25's NZ$1.7m equipment and NZ$3.7m leases = NZ$17.6m) at hipages' 7.6 times: NZ$133.9m, plus NZ$32.2m cash and NZ$12.9m Amber = NZ$179.0m, or NZ$1.59 a share.
- Price: NZ$4.36 (close 6 Nov 2026, NZX), market value NZ$490.4m, about 18 times FY25 cash earnings after taking out the cash. Gap if it goes our way: 4.83 / 4.36 - 1 = +11%. Gap if it goes against us: 1.59 / 4.36 - 1 = -64%.

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive: the gap if it goes our way is 11%, under 25%. At NZ$4.36 the price already pays close to Objective's multiple for FY25's earnings, so it is pricing the recovery the question asks about; only management's 15% margin target, never yet delivered, gives a gap above 25%. Not a value pick: the value if it goes against us is 64% below the price. Worth a fresh look if the 23 November result shows FY26 EBITDA at the top of its range and FY27 guidance back above FY25, or if the buyback starts.
