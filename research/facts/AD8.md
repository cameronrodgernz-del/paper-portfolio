## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. Audinate sells Dante, the audio and video networking technology built into other makers' equipment (chips, cards, modules, embedded software, adaptors and platform software). Revenue US$46.0m in FY26 [1].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. A$65.1m of cash and term deposits, no borrowings [2].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$416,947 a day (lowest day A$193,553), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. Largest holders are AustralianSuper (10.93%) and Yamaha Corporation (7.44% on the register; 8.12% at its last notice in July 2023), a long-standing industry holder [3]. No approach or review announced.

The items left out of underlying EBITDA are linked to one deal and one restructure, but the Iris payments continue: shares and up to US$4m of cash go to Iris staff over three years if they stay and targets are met [9][10], so a similar charge will appear in FY27 and FY28. The bigger point is what EBITDA leaves out entirely: A$12.5m a year of development spending is capitalised, not expensed [2]. Amortisation of past development (A$14.2m in FY26 [3]) now exceeds the amount capitalised, so statutory profit is not flattered, but EBITDA is.

Cash runway and the path to profit:
- Free cash flow (operating cash flow less capitalised development, equipment and lease payments) was minus A$13.4m in FY26, before the A$31.0m Iris purchase [2]. At that rate the A$65.1m lasts about 4.9 years.
- FY27 plan: US dollar gross profit growth in line with or slightly ahead of FY26 (14.7%), gross margin about 82%, Australian dollar operating costs flat at FY26's A$59.1m [1][7]. On FY26 exchange rates that is gross profit of about A$63.8m, underlying EBITDA of about A$4.7m, and free cash flow of about minus A$9.6m before interest income. Free cash flow would be about nil once gross profit reaches A$73.4m on today's cost base, 32% above FY26, which at 15% a year is FY28.
- Restructure: 10% of roles cut across the group in FY26 despite adding Iris [1]. FY26 underlying employment costs still rose 23%, including A$3.9m of higher short-term incentives [1].
- Supply: the AGM flagged memory and storage chip shortages from AI data centre building, with price increases passed on to customers [7].
- History of sharp swings: gross profit was US$44.5m in FY24, fell to US$32.9m in FY25 as equipment makers worked through stock, and recovered to US$37.7m in FY26 [1][11][12]. FY26 gross profit is still below FY24.

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance (FY27), from the FY26 result (17 Aug 2026) and repeated at the AGM (20 Oct 2026): US dollar gross profit growth in line with or slightly ahead of FY26; gross margin in line with FY26; Australian dollar operating costs flat against FY26, excluding restructuring and Iris payments [1][7]. No quarterly update since.

Changes this year (FY26):
- 18 Aug 2025: US dollar gross profit growth of 13% to 15%; operating costs up 25% [13][14].
- 16 Feb 2026: gross profit range kept; operating cost growth cut to 20% [5].
- Delivered (17 Aug 2026): gross profit up 14.7%, operating costs up 17% [1].

Record:
- FY26: gross profit guidance met (top of the range); cost guidance beaten.
- FY25: 6 Aug 2024, US dollar gross profit "slightly lower" than FY24's US$44.5m [11]; withdrawn on 22 Oct 2024 as "unlikely" [15]; delivered US$32.9m, down 26% [12]. Missed badly. The ASX sent an aware letter in February 2025 [16].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: does the FY26 cost reset plus 15% gross profit growth carry Audinate to positive free cash flow by about FY28, or does growth slip back and the cash get spent? A deep dive would rebuild the break-even from the filings (gross profit against expensed and capitalised costs), test whether 15% growth is likely from design wins, forward orders and the equipment makers' stock levels, and check the size of the remaining Iris payments.
- If it goes our way: US dollar gross profit grows 15% a year for five years from A$55.5m; operating costs stay flat in FY27 (company guidance) then rise 3% a year; capitalised development rises 3% a year from A$12.5m. Free cash flow runs minus A$9.6m (FY27), minus A$2.2m, A$6.6m, A$17.0m and A$29.2m (FY31). Discounted at the company's own 11.0% pre-tax rate with 3% terminal growth (its goodwill test [3]), the business is worth A$246.3m. Add A$65.1m of cash and deduct up to A$5.9m of Iris cash payments: A$305.5m, or A$3.61 a share on 84.54m shares.
- If it goes against us: gross profit grows 5% a year and costs are held flat; free cash flow stays negative every year to FY31 (minus A$15.1m falling to minus A$2.6m). Treat the business as worth nothing beyond its cash: A$65.1m less A$5.9m Iris less A$35.7m present value of five years' burn: A$23.5m, or A$0.28 a share.
- Price: A$2.08 (close 30 Oct 2026), market value A$176m, A$111m after cash, about 2.0 times FY26 gross profit. Gap if it goes our way: 3.61 / 2.08 - 1 = +74%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.28 / 2.08 - 1 = -87%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is 74%; the break-even, the cost base and the cash runway can be worked from the filings; and the H1 FY27 result on about 16 February 2027 will show whether gross profit is growing near 15% with flat costs, inside six months. The "against" figure is low because without growth the business burns cash for years; the deep dive should weigh FY25's 26% fall in gross profit, which came with little warning.
