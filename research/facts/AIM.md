# AI-Media Technologies (ASX:AIM): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 6 points, #6 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. AI-Media sells captioning, transcription and translation: LEXI software subscriptions, caption encoders (hardware) and a shrinking human captioning service, mostly to broadcasters and governments [1].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. Grant Thornton's opinion on the June 2026 accounts is unmodified; the key audit matters are technology revenue (subscription and usage-based billing) and the A$43m of North American goodwill [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. Median value traded over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 was A$23,100 a day (lowest day A$2,045), from data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz. Two sessions at 10% of that is about A$4,600, so a position would be small.
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries. Sports broadcasters are customers, but the company is not in gaming, wagering or racing.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest holder, co-founder and CEO Tony Abrahams with Pearlirose, has 16.9% [1]. The company itself announced an on-market buy-back of up to 9.9% on 30 Sep 2026 [2].

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs. Restructuring was taken out in both FY25 (A$1.3m) and FY26 (A$0.2m). The A$2.3m of share-based payments taken out in FY26 is the first year of a five-year plan, so it is a running cost, not a one-off; on the company's FY27 guidance midpoint of A$4.0m, adjusted EBITDA after that cost is about A$1.7m [1][2].

What the headline numbers hide:
- Book value is mostly goodwill. Goodwill is A$43.5m of A$70.3m of net assets, A$43.0m of it in North America; the impairment test assumes revenue growth of 16% a year for five years at a 15% pre-tax discount rate [1].
- The recurring revenue measure changed. On 28 Aug 2025 FY25 ARR (annualised recurring revenue) was reported as A$17.0m, with FY26 guidance of "~35% to ~$23.0M" [7]. The FY26 results show FY25 ARR as A$24m and FY26 ARR as A$36.0m, "+50%" and "in line with guidance" [3][5]. The documents read don't explain the restatement. FY26 SaaS revenue was A$34.1m, up from A$24.0m [5].
- Customer and regional concentration. "The top 20 customers contribute 80% of revenue" (September 2025 AGM); no customer is above 10% [1][8]. North America is A$41.4m of the A$60.2m (69%) [1].
- Shrinking lines. Services revenue fell 35% to A$15.4m and hardware fell A$6.4m as customers waited for new encoders; from April 2026 encoders are sold as a subscription (hardware as a service), which moves revenue later [4][5].
- Promises not yet shown in the numbers. In September 2025 the company said it anticipated "meaningful revenue from LEXI Voice to flow from H2 FY26" [8]; the FY26 results say only that LEXI Voice "progressed into commercial deployments" [3].

## 3. Guidance and record
Current guidance (30 Sep 2026): FY27 year-end ARR A$44m to A$46m; revenue A$61m to A$63m; adjusted EBITDA A$3.5m to A$4.5m; ARR gross margin about 85% [2].

Changes this year:
- 26 Feb 2026: FY26 ARR growth guidance raised from 35% to more than 50%; the February 2024 "aspirational" FY29 revenue and EBITDA targets withdrawn [4].
- 27 Aug 2026: FY26 ARR A$36.0m, up 50% on a restated FY25 base of A$24m [3][5].
- 30 Sep 2026: FY27 guidance as above [2].

Record: FY26, ARR guidance of about A$23.0m (August 2025) against A$36.0m delivered on the new basis [3][7]; LEXI Voice revenue from 2H FY26 not shown. FY25: no numeric guidance (the FY24 result gave an outlook only), and the FY29 aspirational targets set in February 2024 were later withdrawn [4][9].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: does the A$36m of recurring revenue at an 85% margin turn into earnings and cash in FY27, as guided (ARR A$44m to A$46m, adjusted EBITDA A$3.5m to A$4.5m), or do the shrinking services and hardware lines and AI competition leave a business worth little more than its A$16m of cash?
- If it goes our way: ARR reaches the A$45m midpoint and the recurring business is valued at 1.5 times ARR (a stated assumption, not a peer figure): A$67.5m, plus net cash A$16.0m, is A$83.5m on 210.2m shares, A$0.397 a share. Cross-check: 10 times the A$4.0m EBITDA guidance midpoint plus cash is A$0.267 a share.
- If it goes against us: ARR stalls at A$36m and is valued at 0.5 times, A$18.0m, plus A$16.0m of cash: A$34.0m, A$0.162 a share. Net tangible assets of 10.1c a share are a lower floor.
- Price: A$0.195 (last trade in the ASX company data fetched 7 Nov 2026; Norgate close 30 Oct 2026 A$0.225), market value A$41.0m. Gap if it goes our way: 0.397 / 0.195 - 1 = +104%. Gap if it goes against us: 0.162 / 0.195 - 1 = -17%.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now. The gap if it goes our way is +104%. The question can be answered from filings: ARR, its margin and adjusted EBITDA are reported each half, and the 1H FY27 result (about 26 Feb 2027) falls inside six months. It is not a value pick: the value if it goes against us is 17% below the price, more than the 10% allowed. The deep dive should pin down what changed in the ARR definition between August 2025 and August 2026, since the growth claim depends on it, and should find a real peer multiple for the recurring business instead of the stated 1.5 times.
