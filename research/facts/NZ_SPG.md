# Stride Property Group (NZX:SPG): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 3 points, #10 in the NZX table of reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-07).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice.

## 1. Kill check
Any "yes" ends the sheet here, with the fact and its link. Nothing else ends it.
- A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet? No. A stapled security of two operating companies: Stride Property Ltd (SPL), which owns $872m of office and town centre property directly plus 18.8% of Investore, 49% of the Industre industrial joint venture and 2.2% of Diversified NZ Property Trust; and Stride Investment Management Ltd (SIML), which manages $3.3bn for SPL and those three vehicles [1][2].
- Delisting, suspension, going-concern doubt or an auditor's qualification? No. PwC's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified, with no emphasis of matter [1].
- Too thin to trade? No. NZX's own daily trading data: median on-market value over the 20 sessions 9 October to 6 November 2026 was NZ$202,962 a day, lowest NZ$74,154; all 20 sessions above NZ$12,500 [10].
- On Cam's excluded list, or in the gaming, wagering and racing hold? No. config/judgement_excluded.csv has no entries.
- A takeover, scheme, strategic review, sale process or a holder over 50% making moves? No. The largest substantial holder is ACC at 11.70% [1][8].

The gap between statutory and underlying profit recurs, and one part of it is a trend rather than noise. SPL's own property has lost value two years running ($29.5m in FY25, $34.1m in FY26), almost all of it office ($24.4m, then $37.8m), and the Investore stake has been written down two years running ($8.8m, $2.1m; $29.3m in all) [1]. Distributable profit leaves all of it out.

What the headline numbers hide:
- Office is most of SPL's own property: $663m of $872m (76%), at an average cap rate of 6.18% and 85.7% occupancy. Two Wellington buildings are half empty: 1 Grey Street 56% let ($49.5m) and 215 Lambton Quay 65% let ($66.2m). 20 Customhouse Quay, Wellington, $212m, is fully let to 2035 on average but valued at a 5.50% cap rate [1]. Argosy's office portfolio averages a 7.05% market yield at the same date [14].
- The Investore stake is carried at $87.1m, about $1.225 a share, by adding a 17.5% "strategic investment premium" to Investore's market price at 31 March [1].
- Diversified NZ Property Trust: after its FY26 accounts, any unitholder can ask for a vote to wind it up (75% of votes needed). None had asked when Stride's accounts were signed. Stride's outlook puts the longer-term cost of losing Diversified at 5% to 6% of distributable profit per share [1][2].
- The fee business pays for the group's overheads. Outside fee income was $22.9m; group corporate costs were $22.1m ($16.7m overhead and $5.4m administration) [1]. SIML's own profit after tax was $9.6m (FY25 $8.6m), part of it from fees SPL pays [1].
- The dividend was above AFFO in both years (105% and 106%) [1][2].
- Commitments: $15.8m still to pay for a 125-year pre-paid ground lease at North Wharf, Auckland (conditional), and $8.8m of office upgrades [1].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: are SPL's office values right? Office is $663m at a 6.18% cap rate and 86% occupancy, written down two years running, with two half-empty Wellington buildings. If the values hold, the stapled group trades below its NTA with the fee business, which pays all group overheads, thrown in. The full test is the 31 March 2027 valuation, reported in late May 2027, just after the six-month window; leasing news on the Wellington buildings and the board's half-year review come sooner.
- If it goes our way: property at its stated value less debt, the reported NTA: equity $945.7m over 559.5m shares, $1.69. SIML is counted at its $23.6m book value and nothing more, because its outside fees ($22.9m) only about cover the group's corporate costs ($22.1m).
- If it goes against us: office revalued to Argosy's 7.05% average office yield (12.3% lower, $81.8m), town centres 0.5 points higher (7.48% to 7.98%, $11.0m), Industre's income-producing property 0.5 points higher (5.6% to 6.1% on $712.2m, SPL's 49% share $28.6m): $121.4m, 21.7c a share. Losing Diversified at 5.5% of the 8.78c of distributable profit, capitalised at 10 times, is 4.8c more. Stressed NTA, $1.43. Valued at the peer median of 0.75 times NTA (below), $1.07. The Investore stake is left at its carrying value in both cases.
- Only now the price: the close on 6 November 2026 was $1.11 (bid $1.09, offer $1.11) [12]. Gap if it goes our way, 1.69 / 1.11 less 1 = +52.3%. Gap if it goes against us, 1.07 / 1.11 less 1 = -3.8% (stressed NTA with no discount, +28.4%).
- Against its peers, not only its book: Stride trades at 0.66 times NTA. The seven other NZ listed property owners trade at 0.55 to 0.89 times, median 0.75: Precinct 0.89 (NTA $1.13 at 30 June 2026 [15]), Kiwi Property 0.83, Property for Industry 0.77, Vital Healthcare 0.75, Argosy 0.65, Investore 0.62, Asset Plus 0.55 [12]. So Stride is 12% cheaper than the median, but level with Argosy and Investore, the other two office or retail landlords at the cheap end. At the peer median it would be $1.27 (+14%).
- On cash earnings it is in line, a little cheaper. Stride's FY26 AFFO of 7.60c is a 6.85% yield at $1.11, against Investore 5.75% and Argosy 6.62% (from their annual reports [13][14]) and, as read from their FY26 results in the Argosy deep dive, Kiwi Property 6.57%, Precinct 6.38%, Vital 5.87% and PFI 4.92% [16]. At the median of those six, 6.13%, Stride's AFFO is worth $1.24 (+12%).
- At today's prices the Investore stake is worth $71.8m (71.1m shares at $1.01), $15.3m or 2.7c a Stride share below its carrying value.

## 7. Grade
A, deep dive now, as a value pick. The gap if it goes our way is 52%, and the "against" value, $1.07, is 3.8% below the price, inside the 10% that rulebook section 5a allows, so no forcing event is needed; the results and dividends inside 24 months are its dated events, with the 31 March 2027 valuations the main one. The peer check is the caution: Stride is cheaper than the sector median but no cheaper than Argosy or Investore, so the discount is largely what the market charges every office-heavy or externally managed NZ landlord, and on AFFO it is only a little cheaper than the sector; a deep dive has to show why Stride's discount should close.
