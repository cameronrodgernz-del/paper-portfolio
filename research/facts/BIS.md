# Bisalloy Steel Group (ASX:BIS): fact sheet

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk, from primary sources. Ranking: 4 points, #46 in reports/research_rank.md (2026-11-08).
Stage 3 of the research funnel (rulebook section 5a). A fact sheet replaces the old quick look: it records the facts in a
standard form and asks one question. It does not estimate an expected gain, and it doesn't drop a company on judgement.
This is a simulation and not financial advice. The quick look of 1 Nov 2026 no longer counts as coverage; this sheet is
written afresh.

Bisalloy is Australia's only maker of quenched and tempered (Q&T) steel plate: it buys plate ("greenfeed") mainly from
BlueScope next door in Wollongong and heat-treats it at Unanderra into wear-resistant, structural and armour plate. It
also owns 85% of a Thailand distributor, 60% of an Indonesian distributor (PT Bima) and 50% of a Chinese joint venture with
Shandong Steel (equity accounted), and is starting a wear-sensor business, OptiWear [1][2]. All A$; year ends 30 June.

Five-year record (company table) [1]: EPS 32.2c (FY22), 27.0c (FY23), 33.0c (FY24), 40.9c (FY25), 34.9c (FY26), an
average of 33.6c; profit to members A$15.0m, A$12.8m, A$15.7m, A$19.6m, A$16.8m. Return on equity 20.6% in FY26; no net
debt for three years.

Does the gap between statutory and underlying profit recur? There is no reported underlying figure. FY25's profit was
lifted by items the company itself calls one-off (the AUKUS qualification contract and insurance recoveries), and FY26
carries redundancy costs in Indonesia. The five-year average is the fairer base than either year.

What the headline numbers hide:
- Customer concentration: three customers took 28% (FY25 30%), 15% (17%) and 6% (2%) of external revenue; the first two
  are in Australia [1].
- Supplier concentration: greenfeed comes mainly from BlueScope; "disruption to, or over-concentration of, critical
  greenfeed supply" is a named key risk, and an unplanned shutdown of BlueScope's plate mill in October 2025 forced early
  buying [1][8].
- Imports: Chinese Q&T plate is taking domestic share at prices the board calls "unsustainably low"; the company intends to
  apply to the Anti-Dumping Commission (not yet lodged in the documents read). A previous anti-dumping review (Case 638)
  was pending in August 2024 [2][5][9].
- About 18% of FY26 profit is the Chinese joint venture's share (A$3.0m), which paid A$2.6m in cash dividends [4].
- Indonesia: import licences withheld, revenue down 29%, workforce cut 22%, joint venture terms renegotiated, future under
  review [2][4].
- Safety: a "very serious accident" late in FY26 at Unanderra; no regulator action is disclosed [5].
- The 8.8% "yield" in the ranking includes specials; ordinary dividends are 21.0c, 5.0% at A$4.24.

## 3. Guidance and record
Bisalloy gives guidance in words, not numbers.
- FY27 (28 Aug 2026): "a year of continued investment"; "focus in the year ahead is on execution against clear milestones
  rather than on near-term earnings growth" [2]. AGM, 6 Nov 2026: "sales revenue and volumes this year to date have also
  increased from the prior year" in Australian wear and structural plate and the Indonesian joint venture, and a 13.5c
  special dividend [5]. No change otherwise.
- FY26: "continued solid results albeit softer than FY25" (28 Aug 2025, repeated at the 6 Nov 2025 AGM; second-half
  outlook in February 2026) [8][10][3]. Delivered: profit down 16%, which the company calls in line with guidance [2].
- FY25: "optimistic to deliver another strong year" (30 Aug 2024) [9]. Delivered: record profit of A$20.0m, up 23% [1].

## 6. The question and the gap
- The question: will the domestic wear and structural plate business, most of Bisalloy's profit, hold up against cheap
  Chinese Q&T plate while defence steel (33% of Australian revenue in FY26, from 25%) grows, or is FY26's profit a step
  down toward FY23's level? A deep dive would read the segment results, the anti-dumping case, the defence order flow and
  the dependence on two customers and one supplier.
- How the market prices it, and the mechanism: small industrial makers and distributors trade on earnings multiples.
  Named peers, each on statutory basic EPS for the year to June 2026 and its 30 Oct 2026 close (price file):
  - Korvest (ASX:KOV), galvanising and steel cable supports, the closest in business, size and return on equity (about
    A$187m and 19.8% against Bisalloy's 20.0%, per the Capral deep dive's table): A$15.70 over 104.9c, 14.97 times [19].
  - GWA Group (ASX:GWA), bathroom and plumbing products: A$2.05 over 18.4c, 11.14 times [20].
  - MaxiPARTS (ASX:MXI), truck parts: A$1.985 over 15.67c, 12.67 times [21].
  - The middle peer, MaxiPARTS at 12.67 times, is used. BlueScope Steel (ASX:BSL), Bisalloy's greenfeed supplier and the
    only listed Australian steelmaker, is far larger and cyclical: A$33.29 over FY26 underlying EPS of 194.2c is 17.1
    times, but 34.7 times on FY25's 95.9c, and 7.73 times EBITDA after adding A$600m net debt [16][17]. It is shown as a
    check, not the base.
  - The research desk's Capral deep dive (research/picks/CAA.md) used the same peer group and found price to NTA across
    these peers runs at about return on tangible equity divided by 8.5%, with Bisalloy (2.43 times) on the line.
  - The price would move through half-year results and the anti-dumping decision.
- If it goes our way: profit holds at its five-year average EPS of 33.6c (FY26 was 34.9c). At 12.67 times, A$4.26. At
  Korvest's 14.97 times, A$5.03. At BlueScope's 17.1 times, A$5.76; on BlueScope's EBITDA multiple (A$27.7m x 7.73 plus
  A$1.5m net cash, over 48.31m shares), A$4.46.
- If it goes against us: EPS falls back to the five-year low of 27.0c (FY23). At 12.67 times, A$3.42; at Korvest's
  multiple, A$4.04.
- Only now the price: A$4.24, the last trade on 6 Nov 2026 (ASX company data; up from A$4.03 the session before, the day
  the special dividend was announced). Market value A$204.8m; 12.2 times FY26 earnings; enterprise value 7.3 times FY26
  EBITDA; 2.6 times NTA. Gap if it goes our way: 4.26 / 4.24 - 1 = +0% (Korvest +19%; BlueScope's P/E +36%, its EBITDA
  multiple +5%). Gap if it goes against us: 3.42 / 4.24 - 1 = -19% (Korvest -5%).

## 7. Grade
C, no deep dive: the gap if it goes our way is under 25%. Bisalloy already earns its five-year average, and at 12.2 times
earnings it is priced within the 11.1 to 15.0 times range of small industrial peers; even at Korvest's multiple the gap is
+19%. Only BlueScope's earnings multiple, lifted by a cyclical recovery in its own profit, gives a gap over 25%. Not a value
pick. If the desk looks again, the 1H FY27 result (about 27 Feb 2027) and the anti-dumping application are the events.
