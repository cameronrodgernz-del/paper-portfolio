Until then this is a guess on a policy number on which I have no edge, so wait.

## The company in plain words
EDU owns two colleges. Ikon Institute is a private higher education provider (FEE-HELP approved, CRICOS registered) teaching nine degrees in education and human services: five bachelor's degrees (counselling and psychotherapy, arts therapy, early childhood education, community services, social work) and four master's degrees (early childhood education and teaching, counselling, social work) [E17]. Australian Learning Group (ALG) is a vocational college teaching only international students, with 12 certificate and diploma courses in the same fields [E17]. Both teach from campuses in Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane and Adelaide; Ikon also teaches online [E17].

Where the money comes from: tuition fees. In the half to June 2026, international students paid A$49.0m of A$54.3m of revenue (90%) and domestic students A$5.3m [E17]. In FY25 Ikon earned A$65.9m (A$57.9m international, A$8.1m domestic) and ALG A$16.5m [E15]. Agents recruit most international students; Ikon paid them A$9.7m of commission in FY25, 17% of its international revenue [E15]. 68.5% of international enrolments come from the Indian subcontinent [E18].

Who runs it: Adam Davis has been CEO since 2015 (he founded Tribeca Learning, sold to Kaplan in 2006); Lyndon Catzel is CFO [E18]. The board: Peter Mobbs (interim chair since Gary Burg resigned for health reasons on 20 February 2026 [E34], classed as not independent, 5.8%), Jonathan Pager (classed as independent, 5.1%) [E15], John Eales (independent, appointed 1 September 2026, no shares) [E34][E29] and Davis.

Who owns it: 680 holders at 16 February 2026; the top 20 held 71.6% [E15]. Substantial holders now: Davis 12.2% [E32], DMX Asset Management 9.8%, Microequities 8.2% [E18], Wilson Asset Management 7.7% [E33], Mobbs 5.8%, Pager 5.1% [E29], Regal Partners 5.0% (from 15 October 2026) [E33].

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
They mostly aren't overlooking it; they are wary of it. It is small (A$110m) and far below the S&P/ASX 300 cut-off (I didn't check index lists directly). Until 2025 it barely traded: 50,212 shares a day in the six months to April 2025, which the board itself gave as a reason to delist [E21]. That has changed: the 20-session median to 30 October 2026 was A$295,129 a day [D1]. The register is concentrated (directors 23.1%, top 20 71.6%) but small-cap specialists now own a third of it (DMX, Microequities, Wilson, Regal). The sector is out of favour for a plain reason: federal policy is aimed at cutting international student numbers, and the listed bellwether, IDP Education, is planning for market volumes down 20% to 30% in its 2027 year [P1]. The history is messy: a 2016 relisting after a recapitalisation [E34] and a 2025 attempt to delist at A$0.165 [E21]. I found no broker research in the filings and didn't search for it.

What it means for Ikon, in plain terms. The rules now favour students moving up a level after finishing a course (diploma to bachelor's, bachelor's to master's), which is a large part of what Ikon teaches, and they shut the door on students who switch part-way through a cheaper course and need a new visa. The company says transfers part-way through were "material" but "declining" [E7]. Offshore recruitment has more room (900 priority places in 2027), but offshore visa refusals for South Asian applicants are high [S5], and Ikon's offshore base is tiny (1Q26 offshore enrolments equalled the whole of FY25 [E20]).

## Valuation

### The fact sheet's anchor, tested head on
The directors' value-in-use test at 31 December 2025 put Ikon at A$159.9m and ALG at A$9.4m, using a 5-year forecast, pre-tax discount rates of 18.9% and 16.4% (including a 7.0% premium for the commission ban), Ikon tuition revenue growth averaging 20%, and 3% growth after year five [E15]. Their own sensitivity: a 10% lower revenue cuts Ikon's value by A$34.2m (21%). Would a buyer pay it? It is an accounting test on the board's forecasts, made before the October rules; it leaves out corporate costs and rent. With those taken off and on the diluted share count, it is A$1.00 a share (correction 2). The only routes to that value inside two years are earnings the market can see or a bid; no bid is in view, and the board's own last proposal ran the other way (a delisting at A$0.165). I treat it as a check, not a method.

### Method 1: the cohort rebuild and a discounted cash earnings model
Model (python3 -I; the arithmetic is set out here so it can be checked):
- International students are tracked by intake cohort. Each trimester 2.75% leave without finishing (the fit to the T1'24 to T3'26 history is best between 2.5% and 3%). Bachelor's students graduate after 9 trimesters (85%) or 12 (15%, the four-year courses and part-timers); master's students after 5 or 6 (half each). The 2022 to 2023 cohorts graduate evenly through 2025 and 2026. This reproduces the international totals within 1% to 2% (model 5,651 against about 5,724 at T3'26).
- Domestic students are one pool: 18% leave each trimester; 2026 intakes of about 249, 194 and 215.
- Postgraduate share of the intake 45%.
- Fees per student per trimester, set from FY25 and 1H26: international A$6,269 and domestic A$5,025 in 2026 (up 6.3% and 4.7% on FY25's A$5,898 and A$4,801); ALG A$2,760 a term. The first-half split (T1 plus 42% of T2) reproduces Ikon's 1H25 revenue of A$27.4m.
- ALG: 18% leave each term (fits T3'26 and T4'26); new students 25, 45 or 65 a term.
- Costs: teaching and commission 37% of revenue (1H26); other operating costs A$33.6m in FY26 (A$15.6m in 1H26, A$18.0m assumed for 2H26), 60% fixed and 40% moving with revenue, plus 3% a year inflation; corporate costs A$3.4m. In the bad case 60% of operating costs move with revenue, rent falls 25% from 2029 (campus consolidation) and capital spending is A$1.5m a year.
- Rent paid A$6.0m in 2026 and A$6.3m in 2027, rising 3%; capital spending A$4.5m, A$3.5m, then A$2.5m; tax 30%; interest income A$0.8m; fees paid in advance move at 6.4% of the change in revenue (December 2025 ratio).
- Cash earnings = EBITDA less rent paid, capital spending and tax, plus interest income and the change in fees paid in advance. Value = cash earnings 2027 to 2031 plus a terminal value, discounted at 12% and 14% (the directors' 18.9% pre-tax is about 13% after tax), plus A$12.5m of free cash, over 134.1m shares.

Middling sensitivities at 12% / 14%: dropouts at 4% a trimester A$1.36 / A$1.15; at 2% A$1.53 / A$1.29; domestic leavers at 25% a trimester A$1.30 / A$1.11; domestic intake flat at 2026 levels A$1.11 (14%); all bachelor's degrees three years A$1.18 (14%); 30% of bachelor's students finishing in two years with credit A$1.11 (14%); postgraduate share 60% A$1.14 (14%); no fee growth after 2026 (scholarships replacing commission in the price) A$0.90 (14%). The single input that moves the answer most, after intake, is fee growth against cost growth.

### Method 2: earnings multiples against listed peers
The market prices education companies on earnings (P/E and EV/EBIT). The two listed peers exposed to international students:
- IDP Education (ASX:IEL), the placement agent and IELTS owner: A$2.21 on 30 October 2026 [D1], 278.2m shares, A$614.9m; net debt A$118.6m; FY26 adjusted net profit A$57.1m; FY27 adjusted EBIT guidance A$95m to A$115m while planning for market volumes down 20% to 30% [P1][P2]. That is 10.8 times trailing adjusted profit and 7.0 times forward EBIT on enterprise value.
- Academies Australasia (ASX:AKG), a loss-making vocational and higher education college group: A$0.10 on 27 October 2026 [D1], 127.6m shares, A$12.8m; FY26 revenue A$44.3m, net loss A$1.4m, adjusted EBITDA A$6.6m [P3]. That is 0.29 times revenue.

Applied to EDU (a smaller, net-cash provider; I use 6 to 8 times net profit, around IDP's forward multiple, plus free cash, over 134.1m shares): on FY28 net profit, good A$1.31 to A$1.72 (FY28 profit A$27.2m), middling A$0.98 to A$1.28 (A$19.8m). The bad case can't be valued on FY28 profit (A$12.7m) because FY29 falls to about A$4m; on AKG's basis, 0.3 to 0.4 times FY28 revenue of A$96.1m plus free cash, it is A$0.31 to A$0.38 today.

### What the price implies
A$0.88 is 4.9 times my FY26 earnings a share (about 18c). On 134.1m shares, less A$12.5m of free cash, it is an enterprise value of about A$105m: 2.9 times middling FY27 EBIT and 3.7 times middling FY28 EBIT. Re-running Method 1 with international intake cut once and held flat (domestic +20% then flat, no terminal growth), the value equals A$0.88 at a cut of about 33% (14%) to 38% (12%). The market is pricing a one-third permanent fall in international intake, a little worse than my middling case and well short of the vocational path.

### Range
About A$0.35 (the vocational path) to A$2.10 (good), with the middling case at A$1.10 to A$1.45 a share today. What each depends on: the bad case on intake falling the way ALG's did and costs cut only partly; the middling case on Ikon replacing about 1,750 international graduates a year with about 1,750 new students, domestic intake growing, and fees rising at least as fast as costs; the good case on the moving-up channel and offshore recruitment replacing most of the lost transfers.

### Does the buy-back change the value per share? (question 3)
The resolution on 26 November only lets the already-announced buy-back of up to 12,456,735 shares (10%) start before 17 February 2027; if it fails, the same buy-back can start from about 17 February [E28][E29]. So the vote moves timing by about 11 weeks. The board says the cash is "surplus" [E29]; the cost at A$0.845 would be about A$10.5m [E29], most of the A$12.5m I count as free in December.

Bought at A$0.88, a full buy-back changes value per share by: good A$2.11 to A$2.24 (+6%); middling A$1.24 to A$1.28 (+3%); bad A$0.36 to A$0.30 (-15%) (14% basis; (value less A$11.0m) over (134.1m less 12.46m)). It adds a little if the middling case is right and takes a lot if the bad case comes, because it spends the cash cushion that matters most when fees paid in advance shrink. It doesn't change the expected value much; it makes the same bet bigger. It also lifts the CEO from 12.2% to 13.5% and the directors from 23.1% to 25.7% (no takeover threshold is near). The CEO's older rights have hurdles already met; the 6 July 2026 grant carries EPS and shareholder-return hurdles [E17], which buy-back accretion helps.

## Why the market is wrong
On my numbers it may be a little too gloomy: it prices a one-third permanent fall in international intake, against my middling case of a quarter with modest growth after, and it gives no credit for FY27, which rises in every case because of students already enrolled. But I can't say why the market is wrong about the one number that decides the value. Nobody outside the company knows how much of Ikon's intake came from students part-way through another course, the group the October rules shut out, and the company doesn't disclose it. The price already sits between my middling and bad cases. The case will be shown right or wrong by T1'27 new international enrolments, reported in the week of 1 March 2027 [E9]. Until then there is no edge, only a guess.

## What would make this wrong
The things most likely to break a long case:
1. Ikon's international intake falls the way ALG's did. The October rules shut out students part-way through a course who need a new visa, the transfer lock runs to 30 June 2027, and the July 2027 stream will test every transfer at A$2,500 a visa. If transfers part-way through a course were most of the "material" share [E14], T1'27 could fall 35% or more. The bad case is worth about A$0.36 to A$0.45.
2. More students leave than the model allows. My dropout rate (2.75% a trimester) comes from a period when onshore visa extensions were easy. Students whose visa runs out before their course ends now generally have to apply offshore unless they need no more than 12 extra months with the same provider [G1]. Dropouts at 4% take the middling value down about 7%; fees that stop rising (if scholarships replace commissions) take it down about 27%.
3. The board. In a bad case, a renewed delisting or low-priced exit offer is a real risk given 2025 (see the capital record), and the buy-back lifts insiders past 25%.

Wait for: EDU's T1'27 enrolment release in the week of 1 March 2027, read with the FY26 result (about 26 February 2027). Reopen as an ordinary pick, with T2'27 enrolments (week of 21 June 2027) and the 1H27 result (about 27 August 2027) as its dated events, if all of these hold:

2. FY26 net profit before one-off items is at least A$20m (my estimate A$22.5m), and the FY27 outlook is for higher revenue.
3. The price is no higher than A$1.05.
4. No new federal measure since 13 October 2026 aimed at onshore transfers or private higher education, beyond the July 2027 transfer stream as announced.

If T1'27 international new enrolments fall 35% or more, the file becomes No case (the vocational path). Between 20% and 35%, keep waiting for T2'27. Even after a good T1'27 this won't be a value pick: the 2028 graduation wave and the July 2027 transfer stream keep a bad case well over 10% below any likely price.
