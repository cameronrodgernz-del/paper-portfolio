On its own figures KMD earns little cash: before working capital it used NZ$30m in FY25 and NZ$10m in FY26, and FY27's guidance (NZ$52m to NZ$55m of EBITDA after rent) would leave about NZ$16m of cash for holders if it is met. ## The company in plain words
KMD Brands owns three brands: Rip Curl (surf clothing and wetsuits, NZ$571.2m of FY26 sales, 54% of the group, sold through 166 of its own stores, wholesale and online, plus the Ozmosis chain of multi-brand surf shops in Australia), Kathmandu (outdoor clothing and gear, NZ$402.3m, 38%, almost all through its own stores and website in Australia and New Zealand) and Oboz (US hiking shoes, NZ$79.5m, 8%, mostly wholesale) [K9, K11]. It runs more than 300 stores and sells through more than 8,000 other shops [K11]. It is a New Zealand company listed on the NZX, with an ASX listing as a foreign exempt entity, so the NZX rules and the NZ Takeovers Code govern it [K21]. The financial year ends on 31 July.

Five years of sales: NZ$979.8m (FY22), NZ$1,103.0m (FY23), NZ$979.4m (FY24), NZ$989.0m (FY25), NZ$1,053.0m (FY26) [K11]. Underlying EBITDA, which KMD reports after rent (it removes the IFRS 16 lease accounting) and before restructuring costs, was NZ$50.0m in FY24, NZ$17.7m in FY25 and NZ$42.0m in FY26 [K9, K20]. FY26 had a statutory loss of NZ$414.4m after a NZ$462.7m write-down of all goodwill and most brand values [K9]. No dividend has been paid for FY25 or FY26 [K11].

The CEO is Brent Scrimshaw. Philip Bowman (also chair of Sky Network Television) became chair on 1 May 2026, replacing David Kirk after 12 years, and the board has been partly renewed since [K5, K9]. In March it had confirmed hiring Goldman Sachs for its "treasury and capital management strategy" while it renegotiated its bank debt [K4]. In April 2026 KMD raised NZ$65.3m at NZ$0.06 a share (NZ$1.50 after the later 1-for-25 consolidation) in a placement and a 1 for 0.73 entitlement offer underwritten by Goldman Sachs and Forsyth Barr, at a 69% discount to the last price [K3]. The consolidation cut the shares from 1,799,415,022 to 71,976,827 on 1 July 2026 [K7, K8].

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
It isn't overlooked so much as unloved. The market value is NZ$151m, well outside the NZX 20 and the S&P/ASX 300. Liquidity is thin: NZ$23,544 a day on the NZX and A$19,084 on the ASX (20-session medians, see Checks). Three years of losses, an April raise at a 69% discount, a write-down of NZ$463m, a share consolidation and a chair change have pushed generalist funds away, and NZSF has been a steady seller. It is a turnaround story in an out-of-favour sector (discretionary apparel). None of that hides the situation: the 2 November announcement was covered widely in the trade press [W1, W2].

## What the filings show
**What the board has said, in full.** The FY26 result (23 September) closed the business review begun in May, run with Deloitte and Barclay & Co (financial) and Chapman Tripp (legal). Its outcomes were to sell the Southeast Asian wetsuit factory, keep and shrink Ozmosis after testing outside interest ("No proposal emerged that offered greater value"), and move some shared services offshore. On approaches it said: "Throughout the review process, the Board has received and considered a number of indicative approaches from external parties and has determined that further engagement with a limited number of those parties is appropriate. ... The approaches are indicative, non-binding and incomplete, no decision has been made, and there is no certainty that any proposal or transaction will result." The chair added that "these discussions remain preliminary in nature" [K9].

The 2 November announcement, in full apart from contacts: KMD "entered into an exclusivity agreement with Bluestar Alliance LLC, a US-based consumer brand group, to permit it to undertake confirmatory due diligence and to continue discussions with KMD regarding a possible transaction to acquire all of the shares in KMD. Those discussions are ongoing with exclusivity able to be extended if specified conditions are met, subject to a long stop date of 23 December 2026. There is no certainty at this time that this process will result in receipt of a binding proposal, or that any transaction will ultimately be consummated. The Board has not made, and the granting of exclusivity should not be taken as, a recommendation of any transaction. Accordingly, shareholders do not need to take any action at this time." [K1] The ASX copy has the same text under the title "KMD update on non-binding indicative proposal received" [K22]. Nothing has been announced on either exchange since, other than NZSF's 4 November notice [K1, K22].

So: no price or range has been disclosed or credibly reported (the trade press reports none [W1, W2]); the exclusivity terms (any break fee, matching right, no-shop or no-talk) are not published; the first exclusivity period's length and the "specified conditions" for extending it aren't disclosed; and "confirmatory" due diligence normally means the price and main terms are agreed in principle and the bidder is checking them. "Indicative approaches" here means non-binding expressions of interest received during the review. The Australian reported on 24 July 2026 (via Ragtrader, a secondary source) that the offers included bids for the whole business and for single brands, that Rip Curl drew the most interest, and named Brett Blundy as one interested party; KMD called this "highly speculative" [W3]. Earlier, in March, KMD rejected a plan from Stokehouse Unlimited (a US surfwear company) to demerge Rip Curl and merge it with Stokehouse [K2].

### Method 2: enterprise value on cash EBIT at peer-based multiples
Accounting EBIT is distorted: FY27 depreciation (NZ$40m to NZ$41m) is far above capital spending (NZ$15m to NZ$16m), so EBIT after rent would be only about NZ$13m. Using EBITDA after rent less NZ$20m of sustaining capital spending (roughly the FY25 to FY27 average) as cash EBIT, and multiples below Nick Scali and Beacon Lighting, which trade at about 9.3 to 9.6 times EBIT after rent but have net cash and steady profits (worked from their FY26 filings in research/picks/ADH.md, not rechecked here [P1, P2]):

```
High:   (58 - 20) = 38.0 x 8 = 304.0 - 65 = 239.0 / 71.977 = NZ$3.32
```

### Method 3 (a ceiling, not used in the outcomes): the board's value in use
NZ$394.6m for the three brands. The note doesn't say whether lease liabilities sit inside the brand units. They probably do: the July balance sheet's brand-level assets less leases (intangibles 197.4 + property and equipment 60.7 + working capital 196.4 + right-of-use assets 234.0 - lease liabilities 275.3 = NZ$413.2m) is close to NZ$394.6m, while leaving leases out gives NZ$688m. Read that way, equity is (394.6 - 65) / 71.977 = NZ$4.58 a share. It rests on management's five-year plan, with Kathmandu earning far more than it has recently (its FY26 underlying EBIT was NZ$1.1m on NZ$402.3m of sales [K9]). It shows the board's own view of value is well above any likely offer, which helps explain why it has neither rushed nor recommended.

## The players and what the rules allow (special situations only)
**Bluestar Alliance LLC** (US, founded 2006 by Joseph Gabbay and Ralph Gindi). VF describes it as a brand management firm whose brands sell more than US$10bn at retail, managing more than 500 licensees and a branded retail platform of more than 500 stores [W4]. Its model is mainly to own brand rights and license them to operators. Recent deals: Dickies from VF for US$600m cash (announced 15 September 2025, expected to close by the end of 2025) [W4]; Off-White from LVMH (2024) and Palm Angels (2025), prices undisclosed [W8]; Scotch & Soda, rescued from administration [W11]; and Hurley, which it manages and co-owns [W9] and which competes directly with Rip Curl. How it pays: in October 2024 Hilco Global and TPG Angelo Gordon set up a joint venture "to identify, acquire, and finance consumer brands and IP in partnership with Bluestar Alliance"; it co-invested in Off-White and put about US$190m into a securitisation backed by brands Bluestar manages, including Hurley and Scotch & Soda [W9]. So it borrows against licence income and brings in partners. It has no public accounts, so I can't say what it can afford beyond that it paid US$600m cash for Dickies.

Does it want all of KMD? The announcement says "all of the shares" [K1]. Bluestar normally buys brand rights, not shops and factories, so it may plan to keep the brand rights (Rip Curl beside Hurley in surf), license the trading businesses out, or sell Kathmandu or Oboz on. That could explain the time taken and the extension conditions, which may be about funding. A sale of brands instead of a share bid would need holders' approval as a major transaction under the NZX rules if it is more than half of KMD's value, and the cash would reach holders only later as a capital return or special dividend, after separation costs; it would not finish inside this window, so I don't value it as an outcome here. KMD's tax losses and the write-downs make a big tax bill on a brand sale unlikely, but I haven't modelled it.

**Allan Gray (15.76%)** is the key holder. Under a scheme, approval needs 75% of the votes cast in each interest class and more than half of all voting rights [R1, R2]. If Allan Gray votes no, the yes vote must be at least three times its 15.76% (47.3% of all shares) and at least 50% of all shares, which needs most of ACC, FirstCape, NZSF, New Gravel and the smaller holders to vote yes. Allan Gray plus New Gravel (21.9%) voting no would almost certainly block a scheme. Under a full takeover offer, compulsory acquisition needs 90% [R2], which Allan Gray's stake alone prevents. So Bluestar can't get 100% without Allan Gray. Allan Gray's cost on its last block is NZ$1.50 (the April raise) but much higher on its older shares; it is a contrarian value manager and may hold out for more than a normal premium.

**Other holders.** ACC (9.35%) and FirstCape (7.97%) are NZ institutions likely to follow the independent adviser's value range. NZSF (6.17%) has been selling at about NZ$1.71 to NZ$1.74, so it would likely accept. New Gravel and Dosh (6.18%, about NZ$1.70) are newer and could go either way; I haven't identified who controls them. Briscoe (2.67%) once held more than 5%.

**The board** has run a formal process with advisers and given exclusivity to one party, but hasn't recommended anything and published no price [K1, K9]. Its own value in use is well above any likely offer (Method 3).

**The rules.** KMD is a Code company. No one may go above 20% except by a full offer, a partial offer, a holder-approved allotment or acquisition, or a scheme [R2]. A scheme needs the two votes above, an independent adviser's report, a Takeovers Panel "no-objection statement" (usually after a letter of intention, at least three weeks after draft documents go in), the High Court's approval, and a meeting at least 20 working days after the scheme booklet [R1]. A takeover offer stays open 20 to 60 working days, and a partial offer can give control at over 50% [R2]. Overseas investment: since 6 March 2026 the OIO's new national interest test has a 15 working day first stage, but deals where an overseas investor will own 25% or more go straight to the second stage, for 70 working days in all, with no fixed long-stop [R3]. I haven't confirmed the monetary threshold that applies to a US buyer; with NZ$920m of assets KMD is likely to need consent. Australia's foreign investment approval may be needed for the Australian businesses, which I haven't checked. A binding scheme signed in late December would likely complete in May to July 2027.

**Comparable cases.** Dickies: Bluestar announced a US$600m cash deal in September 2025 and has since completed it [W4, W10]. Accent Group (ASX:AX1) shows the other side: an opposed on-market bid from a strategic holder that moved the price little (research/deals/AX1.md). I don't have a database of NZ or ASX approaches with exclusivity and how often they became binding; my 40% below is judgement, written down with its reasons.

## Why the market is wrong
I can't say it is. At NZ$2.10 the market implies about a 39% chance of a binding offer (taking the deal price at NZ$2.47, the bad case at NZ$1.80 and the middling case at NZ$2.05 with 15%: (2.10 - 0.15 x 2.05 - 0.85 x 1.80) / (2.47 - 1.80) = 0.39). My own chance is 40%. The only edges would be a higher offer than a normal premium (Bluestar valuing the brands on licence income) or a better chance of signing than 40%, and the filings support neither.

```
Gain before costs: 2.1055 / 2.10 - 1 = +0.26%

                  plus brokerage of up to NZ$25 on each NZ$2,500 order once the month's NZ$5,000 plan cover is used (0 to 2.0%)
                  = 1.4% to 3.4%
Gain after costs: -1.1% to -3.1%
Minimum (rulebook 5a): (2.13% cash rate + 5%) x 66 / 365 = +1.29%
```

By outcome: bad -14.3%, middling -2.4%, good +17.6% (before costs). No case at NZ$2.10.

What it would take: at the high-cost end, an expected price of 2.10 x (1 + 1.29% + 3.4%) = NZ$2.198. With the same chances, that needs an offer of NZ$2.85 or more (0.40 x 0.95 x offer = 2.198 - 1.1175). With a NZ$2.60 offer, it needs a 54% chance of signing (0.15 x 2.05 + P x 2.47 + (0.85 - P) x 1.80 = 2.198 gives P = 0.54). Or a price of NZ$2.00 or less with the same chances (2.1055 / 2.00 = +5.3% before costs, +1.9% to +3.9% after). Sensitivities: with the triage's NZ$1.87 as the bad case, the expected price is NZ$2.137 (+1.8% before costs, -1.6% to +0.4% after), still short; with a NZ$3.00 offer, NZ$2.258 (+7.5%, +4.1% to +6.1% after), which would pass.

## What would make this wrong
The verdict is "don't buy now", so the risks are to that call:
1. Bluestar offers well above a normal premium because it values the brands on licence income: it paid about 1.1 times sales for Dickies, and KMD's whole enterprise is 0.2 times sales. An offer of NZ$3.00 would make NZ$2.10 a buy with hindsight.
2. The chance of signing is higher than 40%: "confirmatory" due diligence after a run-off between several bidders often ends in a signed deal, and a strong annual meeting update could make that clearer.

For anyone who buys anyway, the case breaks if Bluestar walks (the price likely goes to NZ$1.75 to NZ$1.85, or lower if the trading update is weak, given Ozmosis down 12.1% and Rip Curl up only 1.0% early in FY27) or if Allan Gray says it won't support the price. Exit rule for any holder: sell on an announcement that talks have ended, or on 15 January 2027 if there is no binding offer.
