## The company in plain words

EVZ is a Melbourne-based engineering contractor with two divisions [E1, notes 3; E2]:
- Energy & Resources (FY26 revenue A$77.3m): Brockman Engineering designs and builds large steel tanks for fuel terminals, water and mining, and does shutdown and maintenance work; TSF Power services gas engines and standby power plants and sells parts.
- Building Products (A$45.8m): Syfon Systems designs and installs syphonic roof drainage in Australia, Malaysia, Vietnam and Indonesia; Tank Industries makes panel and stainless tanks, recently eight bulk water reservoirs for STACK Infrastructure's MEL02 data centre in Melbourne.
- 85% of FY26 revenue was construction contracts recognised as work progresses (A$104.4m), 7% service work (A$8.7m) and 8% parts sales (A$9.9m, up from A$5.4m) [E1, note 3]. 94% of revenue is Australian.

## Valuation

### From profit to operating earnings

I value the operating business on profit before tax less interest income, taxed at 32%, and add free cash separately. Lease interest and bank guarantee fees stay in as operating costs. On that basis operating earnings after tax were A$0.90m (FY23), A$1.21m (FY24), A$1.20m (FY25) and A$3.69m (FY26) (`python3 -I`: FY26 5.715 - 0.288 = 5.43, x 0.68 = 3.69). FY26 was three times any of the three years before it.

FY27 cases, built from the segments (EBITDA less A$3.0m of depreciation and A$0.75m of finance costs, then 32% tax):
- Bad: Building Products keeps slipping to A$4.8m; Energy & Resources falls to A$4.2m (about 6% on about A$70m of revenue, a gap year between the finished jobs and any fuel security work); corporate A$3.0m. EBITDA A$6.0m, operating earnings A$1.53m. FY25 was A$5.3m of EBITDA.
- Middling: Building Products A$5.2m (its last two halves); Energy & Resources A$6.1m (FY26 less about 10%, in line with management's Brockman budget and TSF Power's growth); corporate A$3.0m. EBITDA A$8.3m, operating earnings A$3.09m.
- Good: Building Products A$5.5m, Energy & Resources A$7.0m (the defence jobs and new terminal work replace the finished jobs at FY26 margins). EBITDA A$9.5m, operating earnings A$3.91m.

### Method 2: cash earnings at a required return

Cash earnings are operating earnings less the A$0.41m a year by which plant and lease spending has run above depreciation. Capitalised at a 12% required return less 2% growth (4% in the good case, for fuel security work), plus the same free cash:
- Bad: (1.53 - 0.41) / 0.10 + 9.0 = A$20.2m, A$0.155.
- Middling: (3.09 - 0.41) / 0.10 + 10.5 = A$37.4m, A$0.287.
- Good: (3.91 - 0.41) / 0.08 + 10.5 = A$54.3m, A$0.417.

Net tangible assets (A$26.0m at June, about 20 cents a share diluted) are a check, not a floor: they are mostly receivables, work in progress and plant, and the stock traded at or below them through 2025 when profit was about A$1.2m [E1][D2].

### Range

Midpoints of the two methods: bad A$0.16, middling A$0.30, good A$0.46. The bad case depends on whether Energy & Resources falls back towards its pre-FY26 margins once the big jobs are gone; the middling case on management's own FY27 budget being about right; the good case on new terminal or defence work replacing the finished jobs at FY26 margins and the market paying 14 times for it.

### The fact sheet's anchors tested

- "Our way" A$0.62 is FY26's profit at 17.5 times plus all A$17.0m of cash. With free cash instead of all cash it is A$0.57. FY26's operating earnings at 12 times with free cash give A$0.42, and at 15 times A$0.51. Would a buyer pay 17.5 times peak-year earnings for a A$50m contractor? I can find no sign of it, and the mechanism the fact sheet names (half-year results showing profit holding) would at best confirm the middling-to-good earnings, not the multiple. A$0.62 is beyond my good case.
- "Against us" A$0.35 (five-year average profit at 17.5 times) is close to my middling case, because the multiple offsets the lower profit. At the multiple a fallen contractor gets, the same profit is worth about A$0.16 to A$0.20.

## Why the market is wrong

I can't say that it is. At A$0.40 the market values the operating business, net of free cash, at A$41.6m on the diluted count: 11.3 times FY26's operating earnings, 13.4 times my middling FY27 earnings, and 7.7 times FY26 EV to EBIT. That already assumes FY26 is close to the base and pays a multiple between EVZ's own history and the larger peers'. The fact sheet's gap came from three things: the larger peers' multiple, FY26 taken as the base, and committed cash counted as free.
