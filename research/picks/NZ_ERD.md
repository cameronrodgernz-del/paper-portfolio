Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk. Price at writing: NZ$0.79 (NZX close on 6 November 2026, FinFeedAPI, data/prices/NZX/ERD.csv; the NZX quote page shows the same last price, with a bid of NZ$0.79 and an offer of NZ$0.81 [S43]). Market value: NZ$149.4m (189,082,683 shares on issue, NZX [S43]).
Status: finished. Verdict: wait for a named event, the H1 FY27 result and operational and strategic update on 24 November 2026 (reopen conditions under "Dated events"). Not a value pick: the bad case (NZ$0.55) is 30% below the price and the middling case (NZ$0.85) only 8% above it. Looked at as a special situation (holder build-up) and as a value question. This is a simulation and not financial advice.
Fact sheet: research/facts/NZ_ERD.md, grade A. Its question: with the 4G unit replacement finished, do cash earnings recover to the normalised NZ$12.1m a year and does North America stop losing cash? Its "if it goes our way" anchor was NZ$1.89 (NZ$17.1m of cash earnings at Objective Corporation's 21.3 times).

> The desk's view in one line: EROAD isn't cheap on its own cash. After the 4G replacement is taken out, cash earnings (after hardware, development and leases, before interest) were NZ$15.9m in the half to September 2025 and minus NZ$3.8m in the half to March 2026, when running costs rose (cash paid to suppliers and staff up NZ$16.1m half on half, about half of it development now expensed instead of capitalised); on that latest half the business earns nothing. The value is in what a trade buyer would pay for NZ$174m of recurring revenue and New Zealand's road user charges franchise once costs are cut: about 1.2 times revenue, NZ$1.10 to NZ$1.20 a share, which is the multiple Volaris (Constellation Software) offered in 2023 and roughly what telematics deals since 2022 imply. Ampfield, a small New York fund now at 17.98% whose three board nominees all came from the Constellation group, is pushing that way, but it lost its board vote by about 35 to 65 in June, the board then hired a growth-minded chief executive, and Ampfield is too small to bid alone. On 40% bad (NZ$0.55), 45% middling (NZ$0.85) and 15% good (NZ$1.15) the expected price by 7 May 2027 is NZ$0.775, about 2% below today before costs and 4% to 6% below after them. The fact sheet's NZ$1.89 needs a buyer to pay 2.1 times ARR for a business whose North American ARR is shrinking 20% a year; no deal since 2022 supports that.

Order of work: the price was in the brief, so it was in view from the start. ## The company in plain words
EROAD sells in-cab units and subscription software to truck, van and government fleets in New Zealand, Australia and North America: road user charges collection, hours and safety compliance, tracking and cameras. It rents the hardware as part of the subscription, so it pays for units and their installation itself. In New Zealand it is the market leader in electronic road user charges and collects about 83% of RUC transactions for the Crown [S10]. FY26 (year to 31 March 2026) revenue was NZ$195.2m: New Zealand NZ$102.0m, North America NZ$74.4m, Australia NZ$18.8m [S1, S3]. Annualised recurring revenue (ARR) was NZ$174.3m: New Zealand NZ$93.5m (up 5%), Australia NZ$21.9m (up 73%), North America NZ$58.9m (down 20%) [S2].

Who runs it: David Leach (former chief executive of Cin7) became chief executive on 12 October 2026 [S13, S15]. John Scott, a director since early 2025, was executive chair from October 2025 to 12 October 2026 and is now non-executive chair, not independent [S7, S15]. Ciara McGuigan is chief financial officer [S10]. The board after 1 December 2026 will be Scott, David Green (lead independent director), Susan Paterson (chair 2023 to 2025), Ryan Brosnahan (part-time chief transformation officer until mid-2026, not independent) and Ian Whiting (Ampfield's nominee, independent) [S11, S12, S15, S17].

Who owns it (latest notices; "the players" below has every change): Ampfield and Moore 17.98% [S25], Australian Ethical about 15.0% [S34], Regal 12.60% in voting shares plus 3.16% in swaps [S27], Anthony Kandziora 8.41% [S31]. Accident Compensation Corporation fell below 5% on 2 September 2026 (4.955%) [S32]. Those four hold about 54% of the voting shares.

Five years: EROAD bought Coretex (North America and Australia) in December 2021 with a capital raise [S10]. A cost-out in 2022 and 2023 and the NZ$50m raise at NZ$0.70 in September 2023 followed the rejected Volaris proposal [S41]. Free cash flow to the firm (the company's measure, before leases and interest) was minus NZ$29.9m in FY23, NZ$1.3m in FY24, NZ$16.0m in FY25 and NZ$0.1m in FY26 [S2]. After the government said on 6 August 2025 that it intends to move all vehicles to electronic road user charges [S6], the share price rose to NZ$2.90 in September 2025; in October 2025 the company cut its guidance, refocused on Australia and New Zealand and flagged an impairment of up to NZ$150m in North America, and the price fell to NZ$0.81 by March 2026 [S7, S44].

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Size: NZ$149m of market value, outside the NZX's 20 largest companies (config/nzx_top20.csv). S&P added it to the S&P/ASX All Technology Index in September 2025 and removed it in June 2026 [S42].
- Liquidity: median NZX trading of NZ$39,195 a day over the 20 sessions to 6 November 2026 [S43]; on the ASX, a median of A$15,245 a day over the 20 sessions to 30 October (Norgate).
- A messy recent history: a NZ$134.7m write-down, a guidance cut and then a miss of the cut range, a co-chief executive and a chief executive gone within nine months, a proxy fight, and a new chief executive in his fifth week [S1, S7, S18].
- A concentrated register: four holders own about 54% of the votes, and two of them (Ampfield and Regal) are hedge funds that trade.
- Analyst coverage was not checked from primary sources.

None of this means the price is wrong. Most of it is why the price is where it is.

### Cash earnings, properly
Cash earnings here are operating cash flow with interest and tax added back, less everything spent on units and equipment, capitalised development and contract costs (all in investing), and less lease payments. Installation costs of units under contract (NZ$11.7m in FY26) are already inside operating cash flow [S2, S3]. Worked in python3 -I (scratch script erd_dd.py):

Sources: [S2, S3, S4, S6]. The H1 4G spend is the company's own normalisation (FCF NZ$6.2m reported, NZ$16.7m normalised) [S4].

What changed between the halves: receipts from customers fell NZ$2.9m (NZ$97.3m to NZ$94.4m), cash paid to suppliers and staff rose NZ$16.1m (NZ$65.8m to NZ$81.9m), and installation payments rose NZ$2.5m [S2, S6]. About NZ$7.5m to NZ$8m of the cost rise is development that used to be capitalised and is now expensed (spending on intangibles fell from NZ$7.5m in H1 to about nil in H2), which leaves cash earnings unchanged; the rest is a higher running cost: the new regional sales and service teams, the new executive team and the transformation [S2, S10]. Excluding working capital, H2 cash earnings without the 4G spend were about minus NZ$7.5m. Australia's hardware build for Cleanaway explains part of it: Australia's free cash flow was minus NZ$5.2m for FY26 [S2].

Interest: borrowings cost a weighted 6.63% [S3, note 13]; net interest paid was NZ$2.3m in FY26 [S2]. Tax: EROAD paid none in FY26 (a NZ$0.3m refund) and carries a net deferred tax asset of NZ$9.6m [S2, S3].

So the starting point for FY27 is a business whose cash earnings ran at NZ$15.9m in one half and minus NZ$3.8m in the next. The 24 November result is the first look at whether H2 was a transition (Cleanaway hardware, transformation costs, the proxy fight) or the new run rate.

### The 4G replacement: cost and whether it's finished
- Cost: NZ$9.5m in FY24, NZ$7.6m in FY25 and NZ$14.3m in FY26, NZ$31.4m in all for about 73,000 units, about NZ$430 a unit [S2].
- Finished: the FY26 release says the program is "complete", "replacing 73k units in New Zealand over the last 3 years", with "approximately 88% renewal rate over the life of the project"; the CFO told the annual meeting it "has now closed" [S1, S2, S10]. In November 2025 the company had expected NZ$2.5m to NZ$4.5m more in H2 FY26 [S4]; H2 spent NZ$3.8m. Nothing in the FY26 accounts or presentation points to further 4G spending in FY27.
- What it means for the future: the units are new, so New Zealand's replacement spending should be low for several years. But units do wear out and networks do change, so a fair long-run figure spreads the NZ$31.4m over about eight years: NZ$3.9m a year (desk assumption). On that basis FY26 cash earnings were about NZ$8.2m.

### North America: its cash, and what closing or selling it would save or cost
- Revenue NZ$74.4m (down 7.1%); ARR NZ$58.9m (down 20%) after a large customer did not renew in February 2026; asset retention 80.1% [S1, S2]. Its head told the annual meeting the company expects "further reduction in our customer base, particularly in large enterprise" [S10]. So FY27 North American revenue should be near NZ$56m, and group revenue about NZ$183m to NZ$186m (desk estimate: New Zealand up 2%, Australia up 35% with Cleanaway, North America at 95% of its ARR).
- Cash: segment EBITDA NZ$15.0m before shared costs; free cash flow after its own hardware and its share of shared platform and corporate costs minus NZ$5.0m [S2, S3]. Target: free cash flow neutral [S10].
- Book value: the October 2025 impairment wrote off all NZ$104.9m of its goodwill plus NZ$22.3m of intangibles and NZ$7.5m of equipment; value in use was US$29.5m (about NZ$51m) at 30 September 2025 at a 13.2% discount rate, and at 31 March 2026 headroom was only US$1.0m at 14.2% [S3, note 11; S6].
- Selling it: a buyer of a shrinking, loss-making US telematics book might pay 0.5 to 1.0 times ARR, NZ$29m to NZ$59m (15c to 31c a share), against management's own NZ$51m. That would clear the NZ$16.0m of net debt.
- What a sale or closure saves: the NZ$5.0m loss only goes if the shared costs charged to North America go too. North America contributes about NZ$5m a year before them (NZ$15.0m EBITDA less roughly NZ$10m of its own hardware and installation, using its NZ$8.2m of depreciation and NZ$2.2m of contract amortisation as a stand-in [S3, note 1]); it is the allocation of about NZ$10m of shared platform and corporate cost that makes it lose money. Selling without cutting those costs would leave New Zealand and Australia carrying them. Closing would add severance, lease exits and contract wind-down costs the filings don't quantify, and would throw away the NZ$29m to NZ$59m a buyer might pay, so selling beats closing.
- A complication: the Coretex platform behind North America isn't fully integrated with EROAD's own (the chair called the remaining integration "significant" and the technology chief described consolidating the 360 and MyEROAD platforms), so separating North America would take work [S10].

### Debt, covenants and maturities
- At 31 March 2026: term loan NZ$17.5m (fully drawn), revolving facility NZ$42.5m (NZ$8.9m drawn), working capital facility NZ$5.0m (on demand); borrowings NZ$26.1m, cash NZ$10.1m, net debt NZ$16.0m, lease liabilities NZ$6.0m [S2, S3 notes 12 and 13].
- Both main facilities mature on 1 October 2027, and each has its commitment cut by NZ$1.25m a quarter from 30 June 2026 [S3, note 13]. Total limits: NZ$65.0m at March 2026, NZ$60.0m at September 2026, NZ$55.0m at March 2027 (arithmetic).
- Lenders: ANZ, BNZ and Kiwibank, secured by general security agreements and cross-guarantees from the group companies in favour of BNZ as security trustee [S2, S3].
- Covenants: net leverage at most 1.25 times by September 2025, falling to 1.00 times by June 2026; interest cover at least 4.00 times; and an obligor assets test [S2, S3, S5]. Compliant at 31 March 2026, when net debt was 0.41 times reported FY26 EBITDA [S3].
- The test that matters next: at 30 September 2026 the trailing EBITDA includes H2 FY26's NZ$10.3m. If H1 FY27 EBITDA was similar and net debt rose by another NZ$5m to NZ$8m (as it rose NZ$8.3m in H2 FY26), net leverage would be about 1.0 to 1.1 times on reported figures. The banks' own definition of EBITDA isn't published and may add items back, so this may be fine, but the headroom is thin. The board said in May that EROAD had "sufficient liquidity... without the need for further capital" [S2].
- Going concern: KPMG's opinion on the FY26 accounts is unmodified; the accounts note a net working capital deficit of NZ$3.8m, mostly customer prepayments [S3]. At 31 March 2027 the facilities will fall due within twelve months, so a refinancing should be agreed before the FY27 accounts are signed (about May 2027).

### What the board has said about a review, an approach, North America and capital
- Review or approach: none announced. Every 2026 announcement on the NZX was read [S43 list]. The 17 September notice says the November briefing will bring together "financial performance, leadership transition progress, strategic priorities and operational execution", after "a majority of investors contacted" asked for a broader update [S14]. It doesn't mention a review, a sale or capital management.
- North America: "managed for cash generation and churn reduction, not for growth investment" (notice of meeting); "our focus is to be free cash flow neutral" (chair at the annual meeting); a "low friction model to reduce the NA cost to serve" (FY26 presentation). The chair also told Ampfield's nominees that keeping a US presence helps EROAD compete against "well-resourced global competitors (such as Samsara)" entering Australia and New Zealand [S2, S9, S10].
- Capital: no dividend, no buyback, and "sufficient liquidity to execute on strategic initiatives without the need for further capital" [S2]. The board said it "shares shareholders' desire for greater visibility of financial targets and operating metrics" [S9].
- The new chief executive: "build EROAD's AI-native platform, accelerate growth, and create long-term value... ensuring we invest our time and capital in the areas that produce the highest returns for customers and shareholders" [S13]. The New Zealand head told the annual meeting the New Zealand business "has been under-invested relative to its strategic importance" [S10]. Both point to more spending, not a sale.
- What the board says Ampfield wants: Ampfield argued the executive chair's "strategic orientation prioritises growth over shareholder returns", citing "competing with Samsara, AI transformation and re-entering the United States", and blamed the North American write-down and "the equity raise that followed the rejection of the Volaris bid" on the old board [S9]. That reads as a call for cost cuts, cash and openness to a sale, though Ampfield itself has published nothing the desk could find.

## Valuation
EROAD pays no dividend and its accounting profit is distorted by write-downs and a capitalisation change, so the measures are cash earnings (what a listed investor gets), EBITDA and revenue (what telematics buyers and listed peers are priced on), and a sum of the regions. Claims ahead of shareholders: net debt NZ$16.0m and leases NZ$6.0m, NZ$22.0m in all, at 31 March 2026 (H1 FY27's figure comes on 24 November). 189.08m shares. All arithmetic in erd_dd.py.

### Method 4: sum of the regions (what a break-up could realise)
- New Zealand: normalised free cash flow NZ$24.7m in FY26 [S2], less NZ$3.9m of long-run unit replacement and about NZ$1m of leases; take NZ$16m to NZ$20m (lower end for the extra spending management plans) at 10 to 12 times: NZ$158m to NZ$237m. Management's own value-in-use test puts New Zealand NZ$301.7m above its book value, on 8.6% revenue growth a year (which assumes electronic road user charges spread) at an 11.7% discount rate [S3, note 11]; the desk doesn't rely on that.
- Australia: 1 to 2 times ARR of NZ$21.9m plus about NZ$6m for Cleanaway: NZ$28m to NZ$56m.
- North America: 0.5 to 1.0 times ARR: NZ$29m to NZ$59m.
- Total EV NZ$215m to NZ$352m, NZ$1.02 to NZ$1.75 a share. This assumes the shared costs now charged to Australia and North America disappear in a break-up; until someone does that, the group's cash earnings are what Method 1 shows.

### The fact sheet's NZ$1.89 tested
NZ$1.89 needs an EV of about NZ$379m: 1.94 times FY26 revenue, 2.2 times ARR, 31 times FY26 cash earnings without 4G and about 14 times the H2 EBITDA run rate. ## The players and what the rules allow

### The rules (New Zealand Takeovers Code)
EROAD is a New Zealand company listed on the NZX, so it is a code company [S45]. Its ASX listing doesn't change that.
- The fundamental rule (rule 6(1)): a person with less than 20% may not increase its voting control unless, afterwards, it and its associates hold or control no more than 20%; a person with 20% or more may not increase at all, except under rule 7 [S46].
- The ways past 20% [S45, S47]:
  - a full offer for all the shares; if the bidder doesn't already control more than 50%, it must be conditional on reaching more than 50%, and the minimum can be set up to 90%;
  - a partial offer for a stated percentage; it must take the bidder past 50% unless the shareholders approve a lower percentage (approval by holders with more votes than those who object, the bidder's votes excluded);
  - buying an existing parcel, or being issued new shares, with the approval of an ordinary resolution of shareholders, the seller, the buyer and their associates not voting;
  - creep (rule 7(e)): only for a holder of more than 50% and less than 90%, who may add up to 5 percentage points over its lowest holding in the previous 12 months; associates can't use it [S46];
  - a holder of 90% or more can buy by any means and can compulsorily acquire the rest under Part 7 [S45, S46].
- Schemes of arrangement (Companies Act Part 15) are the other route: approval by 75% of the votes in each interest class and a simple majority of all the voting rights, the High Court's approval, and a no-objection statement from the Takeovers Panel [S45]. Volaris said in 2023 it preferred a scheme, which needs the board [S39].
- Associates: holders who cooperate towards a shared goal over control of votes can be associates, and their holdings are added together. Forming an association isn't itself a breach; it becomes one when associates together go past 20% and one of them increases [S48, para 4.3]. Shareholders may discuss the company freely (para 4.6(c)), and agreeing to vote the same way "does not necessarily make them associates" (para 8.49). In the NZME case the Panel found an association where an activist held strategy meetings and sounded out voting intentions (para 8.53).
- Board changes need no Code process: directors are elected and removed by ordinary resolution, as at the June 2026 meeting [S9]. A holder with 5% can require the board to call a special meeting (Companies Act 1993, section 121; not opened by the desk).

## Why the market is wrong
On the evidence, it mostly isn't. At NZ$0.79 the market pays 0.88 times FY26 revenue, 0.98 times ARR, 4.0 times FY26 EBITDA and 14 times FY26 cash earnings without the 4G replacement (21 times after a long-run unit allowance), for a business whose cash earnings were negative in the latest half. On its own cash, that is a fair price or a full one.

The gap is the control value: a trade buyer could pay about NZ$1.15 because it would cut costs that the current board is choosing to spend (development at 18% of revenue, new regional teams, a consumer app for electronic road user charges, a US presence). The market isn't wrong to price that gap at a low chance in the next six months: the board just beat the holder pushing for it, hired a chief executive who talks about growth, and the one buyer with a known appetite (Volaris) sold its stake in May 2025. If the 24 November update changes any of that, the market would be wrong; until then the desk sees no edge.
