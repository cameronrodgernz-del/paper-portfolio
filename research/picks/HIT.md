## The company in plain words
HiTech places ICT contractors and recruits staff, mostly for federal government departments and agencies in Canberra, including Defence; 85% to 90% of its own revenue is federal and its top 20 clients are all federal agencies [H1]. It earns a margin on each contractor's hourly or daily rate; permanent placement fees were almost nil in FY26 (A$24,000) [H1]. It is moving some work from labour hire panels to fixed-term service agreements, which it says will lift margins in FY27 [H6]. On 11 September 2026 it bought the Australian operations and selected assets of Hudson Global Resources (Aust) Pty Ltd out of administration: recruitment, contracting, project services and permanent recruitment for state and federal government and private clients, under the Hudson and UpperGround brands, adding offices beyond Sydney [H2][H1][H10].

## Why the market is wrong
I can't say it is wrong in our favour. At A$1.11 the market values the group at about A$61.5m including debt and all of the deferred price. On 30 June it valued HiTech alone at A$28.1m. The A$33m difference is what it now pays for Hudson (including about A$8m of receivables) or for a re-rating of HiTech itself, against about A$10m HiTech paid for Hudson's business (A$5.1m cash, about A$0.95m of entitlements, up to A$3.95m deferred). That price is about right if Hudson earns A$3m to A$4m a year, which needs A$185m of revenue at its own 9.3% margin or A$150m at 12% (see the table). It is also about what management's pro forma accretion claim is worth at today's trailing P/E of 11.4. My central case, which already gives Hudson a better margin than it earned, has the group's EPS falling to about 7.3c. If anything, the price is ahead of the business.

## Corrections to the fact sheet
1. Net debt: the company's A$3.5m is at 11 September, before the A$1.69m final dividend paid on 22 September, and leaves out about A$0.95m of employee entitlements taken on and up to A$3.95m of deferred price. Like for like it is about A$5.2m, and about A$10.1m with the deferred price in full [H1][H2][H4].
2. The A$8.1m was not a cost of the deal: it bought Hudson's receivables book ("the acquired receivables book" [H1 p7]). It is working capital the business needs, funded in part by the A$15m receivables facility.
3. "Less than 1.0x pro forma LTM EBITDA" uses gross profit from A$220m of billing and the cost base cut for A$150m. On its own figures Hudson lost about A$5.5m before interest in the year to July (A$20.5m of gross profit against A$26m of costs), and the Victorian regulator says it traded at a loss for several years [H1][R1].
4. A$220m is not a level to rebuild toward but a trailing figure of a shrinking business: federal billings fell 38% from December 2022 to 2025 and Queensland work 73% from 2023 to 2025 (secondary [S1]), and revenue ran at A$190m a year in May and A$150m in September [H10][H1]. The fact sheet didn't give the May figure.
5. Victoria cancelled Hudson's labour hire licence from 10 July 2026 [R1], and ASIC convicted Hudson in July 2026 for not lodging accounts for 2022 to 2024 [R2]. Neither is in the fact sheet. HiTech's own footprint list leaves out Victoria [H1].
6. The "our way" anchor puts A$220m of revenue on the A$15m cost base. Hudson needed A$26m of costs to bill that; rebuilding revenue needs consultants. It is a 24-month good case, not reachable in six months.
7. Peers: Ignite (ASX:IGN), a federal ICT contractor, and Ashley Services (ASX:ASH) are listed peers with profits, at about 4.5 to 4.7 times EV to EBIT(DA) and 6.6 to 8.8 times earnings [P1][P2]. The fact sheet used only PeopleIn, which has no earnings multiple, and a judgemental 10 times.
8. The undisturbed price was A$0.89 to A$0.92 (30 June to 17 July 2026) [M2], when HiTech alone was priced at 5.1 times FY26 EBIT, like its peers. The fact sheet didn't give it.
9. The second half of FY26 was worse than the first at the gross margin line (14.0% against 14.7%) [H5][H7].
10. Elias Hazouri's salary rose 26% to A$864,208 for FY27 [H13], and the proposed options vest on continued employment only.
11. The term loan amortises over 4.5 years on page 16 of the presentation and over seven years on page 18 [H1].
12. Hudson's administrators recommended a management buyout over HiTech's and WorkPac's offers on 24 June (secondary [S1]); HiTech's deal came after that proposal fell through [H1 p6].
