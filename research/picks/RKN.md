In short:
1. The fact sheet's anchor doesn't survive the after-tax test. It put hipages' pre-tax multiple (7.6 times) on Reckon's pre-tax group cash earnings (A$11.06m). But hipages pays almost no tax and is growing; Reckon pays full Australian tax and its main business is shrinking slowly. On the same after-tax basis hipages trades at about 10.9 times (15.1 times if its share-based pay is counted as a cost) and Xero at about 33 times; those multiples are for growth Reckon doesn't have.
2. The Business Group's cash earnings, after all development (expensed and capitalised), all head office costs, all leases, equipment and 28% tax, were A$8.40m in the 12 months to June 2026 (A$8.55m in FY25). Before the January 2025 purchase of Cashflow Manager they were about A$5.6m a year in FY23 and FY24. Organically, the Business Group's EBITDA less capitalised development has been flat for five years (A$10.5m to A$12.2m), but H1 FY26 was the first half to fall (2.6%).
3. Legacy is shrinking and Reckon One only just replaces it. Legacy Intuit-code subscriptions (Reckon Accounts desktop and Hosted) fell from 44,000 at December 2024 to 39,000 at June 2026, about 9% to 10% a year at the current pace, while Reckon One subscriptions stayed at 40,000 to 42,000. Legacy revenue fell about 7% in H1 FY26 (A$1.2m) and Reckon One rose A$1.0m (23%), almost all through price (average revenue per subscription up 19%). A legacy subscription brings A$625 to A$840 a year; a Reckon One subscription about A$260. Churn by product and payroll on its own are not disclosed.
4. The Legal Group (Zebraworks, 78%) has used about A$19m of the Business Group's cash since FY21 (EBITDA less capitalised development: minus A$3.8m, A$5.1m, A$4.0m, A$3.7m, A$1.8m in FY21 to FY25, minus A$1.4m in the 12 months to June 2026). The Dec 2024 round (US$4.5m of preference shares at a US$20m pre-money value, with a 3.5 times liquidation preference and a 7% dividend, 83% taken by Reckon) was said to fund it to the end of 2026. Its document-workflow annual recurring revenue fell 3% in constant currency in H1 FY26 and the cloud product's stayed at A$1.4m. I value Reckon's share at minus A$2m to A$15m, A$5m in the middle. A sale at about 1 times revenue would be worth about A$0.09 a share and stop the burn; closing it would not add value, but stopping the cloud spending would.
5. Value: about A$0.53 a share in the middle (range A$0.30 to A$0.80), from a cash-flow model and an after-tax peer multiple that agree within 2c. At A$0.395 the market prices the Business Group at about 5.2 times after-tax cash earnings (Legal at A$5m), as if those earnings shrink 6% to 7% a year for ever; the record is flat with a 2.6% dip.
6. The executive cash plan (CDIP) pays the CEO and CFO only if dividends, distributions and takeover consideration paid from 24 May 2023 to 31 December 2029 reach A$150m, grossed up for franking. So far A$11.3m has been paid in cash, A$16.2m grossed up. Allowing for three more ordinary dividends, it needs about A$122m more, about A$1.07 a share, roughly 2.7 times the price. The board books it on the assumption that "returns to shareholders will be similar to previous transactions" (A$356k accrued). It signals intent to sell and pay out by 2029, but no sale or review has been announced, and a buyback earns the executives nothing under it.
7. Two holders have been selling into a thin market: MA Financial sold 2.70m shares between February and June 2026 at an average A$0.445, and Microequities sold 1.17m between January and October 2026 at A$0.454. Between them they still hold 25.5%, about 640 days of the median trading volume.

## The company in plain words
Reckon sells accounting and payroll software to small businesses in Australia and New Zealand (the Business Group) and document and billing software to law firms, mainly in the US (the Legal Group, trading as Zebraworks, 78% owned) [R1].

The Business Group (FY25 revenue A$48.6m, EBITDA A$25.4m) has four product lines [R1, R5]:
- Reckon Accounts (desktop) and Reckon Accounts Hosted (the same desktop program run on Amazon's servers). Both are built on Intuit source code, used under a royalty-free licence that "has some restrictions and is non transferable in certain circumstances" [R4]. FY25 revenue A$12.5m and A$18.7m, 64% of the Business Group.
- Reckon One, Reckon's own cloud product: A$8.9m in FY25, A$5.4m in H1 FY26 (22% of subscription revenue) [R3, R5].
- Cashflow Manager, a desktop product bought on 6 January 2025 for A$8.66m: A$6.1m revenue and A$3.3m EBITDA in FY25 [R2, R5, R17].

The Legal Group (FY25 revenue A$13.8m) sells scanning, print and cost-recovery software ("Document Workflows") to large law firms, including 12 of the 20 largest in the world, and a newer cloud billing and collections product ("Billing Workflows") [R1, R3].

Who runs it: Sam Allert, group CEO since 2018 (joined 1999); Chris Hagglund, CFO since 2004; chair Clive Rabie (chief executive from 2006, group managing director to March 2022, chair since January 2023); non-executive directors Greg Wilkinson and Phil Hayman, both co-founders in 1987. Three things the totals hide:
- Cashflow Manager provides about A$3.3m of the EBITDA, so before it the Business Group earned about A$5.6m a year after tax. Cashflow Manager is itself a desktop product, and its revenue fell 3% in H1 FY26 (A$3.0m against A$3.1m) [R4].
- H1 FY26 held EBITDA flat (A$14.04m against A$13.97m) on revenue down 2.9%, by cutting costs: group employee costs fell from A$11.27m to A$10.17m, and the company cites "approximately 30% efficiency gains in our product development processes" from AI [R2, R4].
- The company's own impairment test assumes the Business Group grows 2.5% a year at a 13.52% pre-tax discount rate [R1]. I assume it shrinks (below).

### 3. The Legal Group
- Revenue A$13.8m in FY25 (95% subscription), A$6.6m in H1 FY26 (down 4%; up 5% in constant currency) [R3, R5].
- Annual recurring revenue A$12.4m at June 2026: Document Workflows A$11.0m (down 3% in constant currency) and Billing Workflows A$1.4m (up 5%; it was A$1.4m at December 2025 and A$0.6m at December 2024). Billing Workflows had 6,400 attorneys sold and 3,000 installed [R4, R5].
- Cash use: EBITDA less capitalised development was minus A$3.8m to minus A$5.1m a year in FY21 to FY24, minus A$1.8m in FY25 (which included a A$0.5m one-off US COVID payment) and minus A$1.4m in the 12 months to June 2026 [R4, R5, R8, R11, R13]. US leases and any US tax come on top; the group does not split them.
- Funding: in March 2023 Reckon committed about US$4m and lifted its stake from 70% to 76% [R15]. On 18 December 2024 Zebraworks' US holding company issued US$4.5m of Series B preference shares at US$1.5766 each, a US$20m pre-money value, carrying a 7% annual dividend and, on a sale or wind-up, 3.5 times the issue price plus unpaid dividends before anything goes to ordinary shares. Reckon committed US$3.75m (83.33% of the preference shares; US$1.6m by converting existing loans, the rest on call "anticipated to be fully called by the end of 2026"), taking it to 77.51% overall. The company said the money "is expected to support the business through to the end of 2026" [R14]. Minority holders include Zebraworks' CEO Bill Bice and its sales head Ken Bassham [R14].
- Exit terms already set: if Reckon sells the Legal Group for more than US$70m, management co-investors get "top-up" securities; above US$100m Reckon transfers some of its own securities to them, up to US$7.5m [R15].
- Goodwill of A$3.2m is carried against it; the company's test assumes 3% long-term growth at a 12.6% pre-tax rate [R1].

What it is worth to Reckon. The US$20m round was set by Reckon and management, with a large preference attached, so it is not a market price. On a sale for a value V (US$m), Reckon takes 83.33% of the first US$16.4m or so (the preference plus about two years of dividends) and 76.19% of anything above. A sale at 1 times revenue (about US$9.3m) would give Reckon about US$7.8m, A$11m at A$1 = US$0.69 [D3], before costs and tax; at 1.5 times, about A$17m. If nobody buys and it keeps burning A$1.4m a year for two more years with Reckon funding about 80%, it costs Reckon about A$2m. My figures for Reckon's share: bad minus A$2m, middling A$5m (break-even by FY28 and a modest value after), good A$15m (a sale at about 1.3 to 1.5 times revenue).

Would selling or closing it add value? Selling at around 1 times revenue or better would: about A$10m, roughly A$0.09 a share, compared with a market that appears to give it little or nothing, and the A$1.4m annual burn would stop. Closing it would not: Document Workflows is profitable at EBITDA (the Legal Group's EBITDA was A$3.4m in the 12 months, before A$4.8m of capitalised development), and a closure would throw away about A$11m of recurring revenue. The middle course, stopping the Billing Workflows spending, would make it cash-positive at once; the company is doing the opposite, with a new US sales team [R3].

A 25 November 2022 extraordinary meeting approved "special incentive performance shares" (66.1m votes for, 0.9m against), and FY22 executive pay included "make whole" payments of A$716,571 (CEO), A$250,800 (CFO) and A$214,971 (company secretary) [R10, R22].
- Since then: one 2.5c fully franked dividend a year (A$2.83m), paid 2023, 2024, 2025 and 31 August 2026 [R1, R10, R25]. ### 5. The executive cash plan (CDIP), exactly
Approved by holders at the 2023 AGM (including under section 200E of the Corporations Act) and announced on 4 August 2023 [R21]. Only the CEO and CFO take part; there will be no further offers [R1].
- Measured from 24 May 2023 to 31 December 2029. Paid only if the executive is still employed at 31 December 2029 (the board has discretion on other departures, and can test early on a takeover or similar event).
- "Shareholder Return" is the cumulative total of dividends, distributions and, if 100% of Reckon is acquired, the takeover consideration. It is "adjusted upwards for the effect of franking credits" and ignores the share price. Buybacks are not in the list.
- Bands: below A$150m nothing; A$150m to A$200m: A$770,000 CEO and A$230,000 CFO; A$200m to A$250m: A$1.3m and A$0.4m; A$250m to A$300m: A$2.6m and A$0.8m; A$300m or more: A$5.7m and A$1.5m (pro rata within bands) [R1, R21].
- Accrued so far: A$49.5k (2023), A$84.9k (2024), A$221.5k (2025), A$355.9k in total, booked "on the assumption that returns to shareholders will be similar to previous transactions", raised pro rata over the life of the plan [R1, R10]. With 39% of the period gone, that accrual implies an expected payout of about A$0.9m, close to the bottom band: the board's accounting assumes it gets to about A$150m.

The arithmetic: four 2.5c dividends since May 2023 are A$11.33m in cash, A$16.18m grossed up for franking. Three more by 2029 add about A$12.14m. That leaves about A$121.7m, A$1.07 a share in a takeover (or about A$0.85 a share if paid as a 60%-franked special like 2022's). What it signals: management is paid to sell businesses (or the company) for cash and pay it out by the end of 2029, at about 2.7 times today's market value, and is not paid to buy back stock, however cheap. The amounts are small next to the sums involved, so it steers rather than costs.

### Method 2: discounted cash flow of the Business Group (after-tax cash earnings of A$8.40m)
Five years at a stated change, then a constant change for ever (python3 -I, inputs as shown):
- Bad: down 8% a year, then down 4% a year, at 12%: A$43.0m (5.1 times). Legacy keeps falling 10% a year, Reckon One doesn't keep up, and costs can't be cut as fast.
- Middling: down 3% a year, then down 2%, at 11%: A$60.8m (7.2 times). Roughly the H1 FY26 rate of decline, with Reckon One growing A$1.5m to A$2m a year.
- Good: flat, then flat, at 10%: A$84.0m (10.0 times). The five-year record.
Per share, with the same Legal values and net debt: bad A$0.325, middling A$0.543, good A$0.836.

The single input that moves the answer most is the rate of decline: each point of permanent decline is worth about A$4.9m, 4c a share, in the middle case. At 30% tax instead of 28% every figure falls about 3%.

### Cross-check: what a buyer of the whole would pay
The 2022 sale fetched 8.4 times EBITDA for a smaller, stickier business; Cashflow Manager went for 2.6 times. At 3 to 4 times its A$25.4m of EBITDA (5.0 to 6.7 times EBITDA less capitalised development), the Business Group would be worth A$76m to A$102m to a buyer, who would also save most of the head office. A takeover of Reckon on that basis, with the Legal Group at A$10m to A$15m, less net debt and about A$1m of CDIP payout, is worth about A$0.71 to A$0.98 a share; a sale of the businesses followed by a payout would leave less, after company tax on the gain. That is the CDIP world. It needs a buyer, probably Intuit's consent on the legacy code or a finished migration, and for an asset sale a holder vote; it is the good case at most, not the middle.

## Why the market is wrong
At A$0.395, with the Legal Group at A$5m, the market pays about 5.2 times the Business Group's after-tax cash earnings. At an 11% discount rate that price assumes those earnings shrink 6% to 7% a year for ever (at 12%, about 6%). The record is five flat years and a 2.6% dip in the last half; management expects 2026 to be "much the same as 2025" [R1, R6]. The likely reasons for the gap: two funds holding a quarter of the company have been selling into a market that trades about 45,000 shares a day, so each results-day rise (6% in February, 9% in August) has been sold back; small-business software has been marked down across the board in 2026; and the group's statutory numbers (EPS 6.5c, negative net tangible assets) hide the Business Group's cash behind the Legal Group's burn and capitalised development.

Why that should change: the selling must end some time (Microequities still holds about 390 days of trading, MA about 260), and the next result will show whether the decline is the 3% I assume or the 6% to 7% the price assumes. But nothing in the next six months forces it, and the price has fallen 30% this year on unchanged numbers, so I can't show the market is wrong by a date. That is why this is a wait.
