## The company in plain words

- Commercial (Flexicommercial in Australia and New Zealand): loans and leases to small businesses for vehicles, trucks and equipment, written through brokers. A$3.3bn of assets under management at 30 June 2026, of which A$0.5bn was sold to a third party under a "forward flow" arrangement and is managed for a fee [H2].

A$1.9bn [H2]. The Board decided on 2 November 2026 to exit Canada, which loses money [H5].

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Index: Humm left the S&P/ASX 300 on 20 September 2021 and the ASX 200 in December 2017 [M4].
- Size and trading: A$246m market value; median A$160,228 traded a day over the 20 sessions to 30 October 2026 [M1].
- Register: one family controls 31.4%, and 71% sits with the top 20 [H1, S3].
- A messy two years: two failed control proposals, a Takeovers Panel declaration that the company's December 2025 announcement was misleading, a board fight, a High Court and a Federal Court challenge by the founder, three ASIC investigations, a A$24.5m legal settlement (Forum Finance) and a claim by a former CEO [P1, H3, H19, H20, H24].
- Sector: non-bank consumer and small-business lending, out of favour after the buy now, pay later losses (Humm lost A$170.3m in FY22 [H1]).
- I found no broker research among the primary sources; I didn't search for it.

None of this means the price is wrong. The register and the history are also the reasons a buyer pays less.

## What the filings show

### Profit, and how far it fell in the second half
FY26 statutory profit was A$15.7m (3.1c a share diluted), down from A$39.6m [H1]. The company's "underlying statutory profit after tax", which adds back Board-approved irregular items (A$19.1m before tax in FY26: Forum Finance legal costs A$9.9m, EGM and Panel A$2.6m, the two takeover proposals A$2.5m, restructuring A$3.6m, ASIC inquiries A$1.4m, remediation A$1.1m, less a A$2.0m onerous contract release), was A$29.0m [H1, H3]. The headline "underlying net profit adjusted for non-cash items" of A$44.2m [H2] also adds back the growth in loan-loss provisions and the amortisation of capitalised software; for a lender both are real costs, so I don't use it.

The halves tell the story (worked in python3 -I, scratch script value.py [M6]):
- 1H26 statutory profit A$13.9m [H4]; with its A$8.3m of irregular items added back after 30% tax, about A$19.7m underlying.
- 2H26 statutory profit A$1.8m (A$15.7m less A$13.9m); underlying about A$9.3m.
- Commercial's statutory profit went from A$28.4m (1H25) to A$15.2m (2H25), A$13.4m (1H26) and A$9.8m (2H26) [H4, H2]. For FY26 its net credit losses rose 29% to A$40.6m and its loss provision grew by A$10.9m [H3].
- In February 2026 management said Commercial "net credit losses peaked in the first half ... with losses forecast to trend down in the second half" [H4]. They didn't: the FY26 report blames "softer SME conditions in 2H FY26" and a fuel supply shock that hit borrowers who run diesel vehicles and machinery [H2, H3], and the 1Q27 update says the rolling loss ratio "continues to capture elevated losses from prior periods, including 2H26" [H5].

Humm's halves are seasonal (1H25 A$27.3m against 2H25 A$12.3m [H4]), so 2H26 can't simply be doubled. But FY26 underlying (A$29.0m) is the base, and it is 30% below FY25's.

### The loan book
- Net credit loss to average net receivables: 2.0% in FY26 (1.8% in FY25); Commercial 1.5% (1.1%), point of sale 2.7% (2.3%), Australian cards 2.3% (2.7%), NZ cards 3.5% (3.4%) [H2].
- Provisions on the balance sheet cover 2.7% of receivables, above the 2.0% loss rate [H3].
- 1Q27 (to 30 September 2026): volumes up 13.7% on a year earlier to A$957.7m; assets under management A$5.28bn, down 1.5% (A$87.2m of it the weaker NZ dollar); net interest margin 5.3%, the same as 1Q26; rolling loss ratio 1.9%, the same; underlying cost-to-income 53.5% against 57.0% in 1Q26 [H5]. No profit figure was given.
- ASIC closed three investigations on 29 June 2026 without enforcement, but recorded concerns about unfair terms or unconscionable conduct in small-business loan agreements written since January 2021, and about breach reporting and the use of an AI model in card credit decisions [H19].

The book isn't broken. Losses of 2% on a book that earns 5.3% over its funding cost leave a thin but positive margin. What changed is that Commercial, which earned two thirds of the profit in FY25, now earns much less.

### Capital and cash: the cash is committed
- Net assets A$535.5m (A$1.05 a share); intangibles A$122.6m (A$77.5m of it goodwill on NZ cards); net tangible assets (NTA) A$412.9m, which the Appendix 4E gives as 82c a share (80.7c on today's 511.7m shares) [H1]. The NTA includes A$48.1m of deferred tax, and the A$36.0m rise in NTA during FY26 came from the after-tax mark-to-market of interest rate hedges, not from profit [H3]. Without the deferred tax, NTA is 71.3c a share.
- The A$100.5m of unrestricted cash is spoken for: A$40.0m minimum liquidity under the corporate debt covenants, A$30.0m risk appetite buffer, A$25.4m working and settlement capital, leaving A$5.1m of "strategic capital"; and the company notes it "does not consider drawn corporate debt of A$63.9m" [H3]. The other A$146.3m of cash is restricted inside the funding vehicles [H1].
- The equity sits mostly as the first-loss slice under each warehouse and securitisation. It comes back only as the book runs off or is sold (point of sale Australia and Canada are running off), so a large capital return would need the book to shrink. In February the then independent directors said the activists' plan (a special dividend, a 10% buy-back, a 75% payout) risked "lender, merchant and partner confidence" and "covenant headroom" [H21].
- Dividends: 1.5c interim (paid March 2026) and 0.5c final (paid 7 October 2026), fully franked [H1, H26]; the final went out with a dividend reinvestment plan priced at A$0.40 [H22].

### Why Credit Corp cut and walked (question 1)
What the announcements say, in full:
- Credit Corp, 22 June 2026: "Following a period of commercial due diligence, Credit Corp raised a number of matters which it was unable to gain comfort on following further discussions with Humm. Consequently, it informed Humm on the evening of Friday 19 June that its bid was materially reduced relative to its non-binding indicative offer" [C2].
- Humm, 22 June 2026: the IBC had sought confirmations from Credit Corp on value, and those confirmations, "including after the conclusion of substantive diligence, did not indicate that any updated proposal would, in aggregate, depart materially" from the original; then Credit Corp proposed a scheme "at a price materially lower than the proposal previously advanced, with no indication of form of consideration", which the IBC found not capable of recommendation and ended talks [H15].
- Credit Corp, 7 May 2026: due diligence began mid-March and was "nearing completion of work to validate strategic fit, valuation, synergies"; its motivation was "consumer POS distribution" and "solid franchises (e.g. NZ and commercial)" [C3].
- Credit Corp's chair, 27 October 2026: "While we didn't proceed with the Humm acquisition it demonstrates a willingness to look for bolt on opportunities but tempered with a strong discipline around the price we are willing to pay" [C4].

Neither side disclosed the reduced price or the "matters". The filings Humm published two months later show what a buyer reading the March to May 2026 accounts would have seen: a second half earning about A$9m underlying against A$19.7m in the first, Commercial losses still rising after management had said they had peaked, a A$10.9m Commercial provision top-up, open ASIC investigations into small-business loan terms (closed only a week after Credit Corp walked), and the Forum Finance settlement. That is my inference from the timing, not something either party said.

What it says about the "nothing happens" value: the only outside party to finish due diligence valued Humm below A$0.77 by a margin it called material, and TAG, which had four months of diligence in 2025, withdrew at A$0.58 rather than raise [H11, P1]. Two informed buyers both stopped at or below about A$0.65. The board's adviser (Flagstaff) had put the bottom of its control value range above A$0.77 in October 2025, and the board told Credit Corp holders saw value "closer to $1" [P1, P3]; that was before the second-half fall.

### Method 3: what control buyers offered
- TAG A$0.58 (June 2025): 0.72 times NTA, 10.2 times FY26 underlying profit; withdrawn after diligence [H10, H11].
- Credit Corp A$0.77 scheme or A$0.72 bid (November 2025, before diligence): 0.95 and 0.89 times NTA, 13.6 and 12.7 times FY26 underlying profit; cut materially after diligence [H12, C1, C2].
- Holder bids on the ASX where the bidder already held 25% to 50% (eight in the deal database, 2016 to 2026): six with a stated premium, from 8% to 48%, median 16%; seven completed, one (LDR Assets for ECF, A$0.70) lapsed [M5].

Is A$0.58 to A$0.77 a fair guide to control value? No. A$0.77 was a price offered without diligence, and the bidder cut it materially once it had the books; it can't be used. A$0.58 is what an insider with diligence offered, a 35% premium to an A$0.43 price, and it failed for lack of holder support [H11]. On my valuation a buyer gets an 8% to 10% return on NTA only if profit recovers towards A$33m to A$40m, so a control price of about A$0.58 to A$0.65 (0.72 to 0.80 times NTA) is the evidence-backed range. A bid would most likely come from TAG, at a modest premium to an undisturbed price that is itself about A$0.42 to A$0.48.

### The range
Standalone value A$0.42 to A$0.53 (A$26m to A$33m of underlying profit, the mean of methods 1 and 2), central A$0.46 at FY26's A$29m. Control value A$0.58 to A$0.65. The single input that moves it most is the FY27 profit level, which the 1H27 result will show.

What the rules let it do:
- Creep (item 9 of section 611): up to 3 percentage points more than its voting power six months earlier. Its voting power on 20 May 2026 was about 29.55%, so until late November it can go to about 32.55%, room for about 5.8m shares; from about 9 January 2027 (six months after it reached 31.84%) it can go to about 34.84%, room for about 17.5m shares (A$8.4m at A$0.48). Reaching 50% by creep alone would take about three years. The Panel's ban on creeping ended on 1 June 2026 [P2]. Its October 2026 inaction is telling: it had room for about 3.6m to 5.8m shares when the price was A$0.375 to A$0.42 and filed no notice.
- A takeover bid at any time, for any price: the bid must be at least the highest price it paid in the four months before, and its last purchase (A$0.481, 9 July) fell outside that window on 9 November 2026.
- A scheme of its own: it would be excluded from voting, so it needs 75% of the votes cast by the others and a majority by number; that is where A$0.58 failed in 2025.
- A block: at 31.4% of the shares, with the other holders turning out as they did at the 2025 AGM (about 210m votes), TAG would cast about 43% of the votes on a third-party scheme, so it can veto one alone. A third-party takeover bid can't reach 90% compulsory acquisition without TAG's shares.
- The Panel's orders have run out: the voting restriction on 15m shares lapsed about 1 November 2026, and the order to accept a Credit Corp bid for 15m shares ended with the talks [P2]. Abercrombie's judicial review in the Federal Court was heard on 15 June 2026 with judgment reserved [P4].

## Why the market is wrong
I can't say that it is. At A$0.48 the market is paying 8.5 times FY26 underlying profit and 0.59 times NTA for a lender earning about 7% on that NTA, which is where the peers' pricing puts it (A$0.46 at FY26's profit). The price rose from A$0.415 on 30 October as Rangeley bought 22.9m shares in four days, about 4.5% of the company [S1, M1]. The gap the triage saw (0.46 times book) is mostly intangibles, deferred tax and a low return; on tangible assets and earnings the price is fair. What would make it wrong in our favour is a control bid or a clear recovery in profit, and neither is more likely than the market already allows.

Why bad is the most likely: the company's own record (underlying return on equity of 1.5%, 7.8% and 5.5% in three years), management's February forecast that Commercial losses had peaked, which proved wrong, and the two buyers who did diligence and walked. Why middling isn't far behind: on a like-for-like basis 1Q27 held its margin and losses and cut costs 3.5 points, and Canada is going. Why good is only 15%: TAG can gain control by creeping without paying a premium, it didn't buy at A$0.375 to A$0.42 in October, Credit Corp walked on price, and no other buyer has appeared.

Expected price: 0.45 x 0.426 + 0.40 x 0.543 + 0.15 x 0.62 = A$0.502, +4.5% before costs.

Round-trip costs at our size (2.5% of NZ$100,000 is NZ$2,500, about A$2,016 at NZ$1 = A$0.8064 on 6 November [M3]):
- half the spread each way: the Corwin and Schultz estimate over 60 sessions is 1.0% wide, the 6 November quote 2.1% wide; about 1.5% in all;
- size cost about 0.27% each way (daily volatility 2.6% over 20 sessions, times the square root of A$2,016 over the A$185,995 average day), 0.5% in all;
- brokerage A$15 each way (0.74%) unless the plan's monthly cover applies, 0% to 1.5%;
- 0.5% each way to convert NZ dollars, 0% to 1.0%.
That is 2.0% to 4.5%; I use 3.0%.

**Expected gain after costs: about +1.5% (+0.0% to +2.5% across the cost range)**, over 116 days from 9 November 2026. The minimum (rulebook section 5a) is the 2.13% cash rate plus 5%, for 116 days: 2.27%. Below it. With the chances shifted five points from bad to middling (40/45/15) it would be +2.8%, just over; at 50/40/10 it is -0.5%. A case that passes or fails on a five-point change in chances I can't pin down, with no reason the market is wrong, is not a case. The price at which the central case clears the minimum is A$0.477.

Value pick test: the bad case (A$0.43) is 11.3% below A$0.48 (10% allowed) and the middling (A$0.54) 13.1% above (15% needed). It fails both.

## What would make this wrong
The two things most likely to make "wait" the wrong call:
1. TAG moves for control sooner than I allow. It acted three times in twelve months (the A$0.58 proposal, 3% in three days in December 2025 at A$0.73, and 2.3% in two weeks after Credit Corp walked), it can now bid at any price, and a spill meeting would hand it influence over the board. A TAG bid at A$0.58 or more would be +21% or better.
2. The second-half fall was a one-off. If the fuel shock has passed and 1Q27's cost and volume gains carry through, 1H27 underlying profit could beat 1H26 by a quarter or more, worth A$0.55 to A$0.64.

And the two things that would hurt anyone holding: Commercial losses keep rising (the warning in Credit Corp's cut); or TAG creeps towards control 3 points at a time, and any buy-back speeds that up, so that minorities never get a control price.

Exit rule, if a card is ever written: sell after the 1H27 result or by 5 March 2027, whichever is first; re-decide at once on a control proposal; sell on a close below A$0.40 that follows company news.
