# Stride Property Group (NZX:SPG): deep dive. Not a value pick: on cash earnings (AFFO) it yields only about 5% more than its office peers, the bad case (NZ$0.96) is 14% below the price and the middling case (NZ$1.18) only 6% above it

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk. Price at writing: NZ$1.11 (close on 6 November 2026, FinFeedAPI, data/prices/NZX/SPG.csv; the NZX quote page shows the same close, bid NZ$1.09 and offer NZ$1.11 [T8]). Market value: NZ$622.1m (560,420,186 stapled shares, NZX [T8]).
Status: finished. This is a simulation and not financial advice.
Fact sheet: research/facts/NZ_SPG.md, grade A as a value pick on NTA. The question it set: are SPL's office values right, given NZ$663m of office at a 6.18% cap rate and 86% occupancy, written down two years running, with two half-empty Wellington buildings?

Value pick (rulebook section 5a): no. The bad case is 13.5% below the price (the limit is 10%) and the middling case 6.3% above it (it needs 25%). Counting the seven quarterly dividends to 30 June 2028, the middling case is 18.9% above the price, still short.

Order of work. The price was in view from the start (the brief and fact sheet quote it). The AFFO work and the sum of the parts use Stride's filings and the prices the market pays peers; Stride's own price enters only in the yields, "Why the market is wrong", the outcomes and the checks. This file is kept short because the first test kills the case. Investore, in which SPL owns 18.8%, is worked in research/picks/NZ_IPL.md.

> In short: Stride's FY26 AFFO of 7.60c is a 6.85% yield at NZ$1.11, against 6.38% to 6.62% for Precinct, Kiwi Property and Argosy: about 5% higher, not the 15% that would make it clearly cheap, and only 2% higher on a stricter AFFO of 7.38c. FY27 should be about the same (7.6c), and FY28 lower (about 7.1c to 7.2c) if Diversified is wound up as management expects, which would leave the 8.0c dividend at about 111% of AFFO. The sum of the parts at book is NZ$1.66 a share and about NZ$1.53 with the office marked to sales evidence, but the fee business is worth roughly its book value once it carries the group's overheads, and the property value only turns into cash through sales or a bid. Valued the way the market values peers (SPL's cash earnings at office-peer yields plus SIML at 10 to 15 times its profit) Stride is worth NZ$1.06 to NZ$1.18, which is where it trades. The board has neither done nor flagged a buyback or capital return; it is pointing at growth. No card.

Corrections to the fact sheet, for the red team:
1. The "against us" case compared SPL's office cap rate (6.18%) with Argosy's office market yield (7.05%), which is a different measure (research/picks/NZ_ARG.md, correction 1). Argosy's office cap rate is 6.51%. At 6.51%, SPL's office would be 5.1% lower (NZ$33.6m, 6.0c a share), not 12.3%.
2. The two half-empty Wellington buildings are moving in opposite directions. 215 Lambton Quay rose from 53.3% to 65.4% let and gained 3.0% in value; 1 Grey Street fell from 89.4% to 56.0% let and lost 16.4% (NZ$59.2m to NZ$49.5m) [T1 note 3.2]. Since the annual result, leasing lifted office occupancy from 85.7% to about 88% (annual meeting, 8 July 2026 [T4]); the fact sheet doesn't have this.
3. "The fee business pays for the group's overheads" depends on Diversified. Of the NZ$22.9m of outside fees, NZ$5.9m came from Diversified [T1, investment management section]; without it, outside fees would fall about NZ$5m short of the NZ$22.1m of group corporate costs. Management expects the wind-up "to commence soon" [T4].
4. Distributable profit adds back NZ$1.2m of SIML project and disposal fees charged to SPL and eliminated on consolidation (NZ$0.6m in FY25) [T1 note 4.3]. Taken off, as capitalised interest is on the Argosy basis, FY26 AFFO is 7.38c, not 7.60c.

## The company in plain words
Stride is two companies whose shares trade stapled together. Stride Property Ltd (SPL) owns NZ$871.8m of property directly at 31 March 2026: six office buildings (NZ$662.9m: three in Auckland, three in Wellington), two town centres (NorthWest and NorthWest Two, NZ$175.0m) and NZ$33.9m of development and other property (including 55 Lady Elizabeth Lane, Wellington, valued on a residual basis because seismic work is needed and its office tenants have left) [T1 note 3.2]. SPL also owns 49% of the Industre industrial joint venture (book NZ$264.7m; partners are institutional investors advised by JP Morgan Asset Management), 18.8% of Investore (book NZ$87.1m) and 2.2% of Diversified NZ Property Trust (NZ$4.5m) [T1 note 7.1, T4]. Stride Investment Management Ltd (SIML) manages NZ$3.3bn, including all of the above [T4]. Philip Littlewood is chief executive; David Green became chair on 2 June 2026 [T7]. Substantial holders at 31 March 2026 were ACC 11.70%, Forsyth Barr Investment Management 8.10%, ANZ New Zealand Investments 5.44% and Westpac 5.02%, at their last notices [T1, holder pages]. NTA a share was NZ$1.72 at March 2025 and NZ$1.69 at March 2026 [T1]; the dividend has been 8.0c for two years [T2].

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
They aren't, much: the register is institutional (above) and it trades a median NZ$202,962 a day (Checks). The sector is out of favour, and Stride has fallen more than most: from NZ$1.465 a year ago and NZ$1.38 at 30 September 2025 to NZ$1.11 (data/prices/NZX/SPG.csv), -24% in a year against -14% to -21% for Kiwi Property, Argosy and Investore.

The evidence for the marks is in research/picks/NZ_ARG.md: industrial selling at or above book, prime Auckland office 0% to 5% below, Precinct's Wellington office values "declined modestly", and no sizeable Wellington office sale. 20 Customhouse Quay (NZ$212m, fully let, 9.1-year lease term) is valued at a 5.50% cap rate, the firmest in the portfolio and below Argosy's office average of 6.51% [T1 note 3.2]; a 10% Wellington mark is NZ$32.8m, 5.8c a share.

SIML on its own fee earnings after the costs it covers. Two consistent ways to count it, with the same answer:
- Group view (SPL's property counted at value, which carries no management cost): outside fees NZ$22.9m less all group corporate costs NZ$22.1m is NZ$0.8m before tax; NZ$3.0m if share-based pay and one-off costs are added back. At 10 to 12 times after tax that is NZ$6m to NZ$26m, 1c to 5c a share, about SIML's book of NZ$23.6m (4.2c), which NTA already counts [T1]. After Diversified's NZ$5.9m of fees go, it is negative until costs are cut.
- Stand-alone view: SIML earned NZ$33.2m of fees including NZ$10.2m from SPL, spent NZ$20.6m and made NZ$9.6m after tax [T1 note 2.0]. At 10 to 12 times that is NZ$90m to NZ$108m (16c to 19c). But then SPL must carry its NZ$8.3m a year of management and corporate costs, worth about NZ$92m capitalised at 6.5% after tax, which takes the same amount off SPL's side. The two cancel.

So the fee business is not a hidden asset on top of NTA; its value is that Stride's overheads cost holders roughly nothing today, which is already in its AFFO.

### Buybacks and capital returns
None done or flagged. The 2025 and 2026 announcement lists, the FY26 annual report, results release and presentation and the 8 July annual meeting address contain no buyback or capital return, only the standard capital risk wording [T1, T2, T3, T4, T5]. The dividend reinvestment plan has been suspended since the FY26 fourth quarter [T2]. The board is pointing the other way: "actively exploring options that would accelerate the growth of our funds and asset management activities", the conditional North Wharf ground lease (NZ$15.8m still to pay; resource consent submitted) and Industre's ~NZ$70m Patiki Road development [T2, T4]. Precinct, by contrast, dropped a development on 30 October 2026 and announced a NZ$103m capital return and a NZ$50m buyback, saying its shares trade below NTA "despite recent transaction evidence at or around book value" [P4]. Stride has room to do the same (NZ$166m of headroom [T2]): an illustrative NZ$50m buyback at NZ$1.15, funded at 5.5%, would lift AFFO a share by about 3% (7.58c to 7.84c) and NTA from NZ$1.69 to NZ$1.73 (python3 -I). That would be a reason to look again, not a forecast.

## Valuation
Method 1, sum of the parts on NTA (above): NZ$1.40 to NZ$1.66, central NZ$1.53 with the office marked to evidence. This needs buildings sold or the company bought.

Method 2, AFFO at what the market pays the office peers: 7.6c at 6.38% to 6.62% is NZ$1.15 to NZ$1.19; the strict 7.38c gives NZ$1.12 to NZ$1.16; FY28's 7.1c to 7.2c at 6.52% gives NZ$1.09 to NZ$1.10.

Method 3, the market's way of pricing the two halves: SPL's AFFO after paying SIML (NZ$32.9m, 5.88c) at the office peers' 6.38% to 6.62%, plus SIML's NZ$9.6m profit at 10 to 15 times (an assumption; listed fund managers' multiples weren't checked): NZ$1.06 to NZ$1.18, NZ$1.11 at 6.52% and 12 times.

Cross-check, multiples of NTA: Stride's own price to NTA at its last three balance dates was 0.65, 0.79 and 0.64, average 0.69 (data/prices/NZX/SPG.csv; NTA NZ$1.72, NZ$1.75 and NZ$1.69 from T1 and the HY26 release [T10]), NZ$1.15 on NZ$1.66; the peer median of about 0.75 gives NZ$1.25.

Range: as a listed company NZ$1.06 to NZ$1.25, central about NZ$1.15, depending mostly on AFFO holding near 7.5c after Diversified and on office landlords staying at about a 6.5% cash yield. NZ$1.40 to NZ$1.66 only if assets are sold and the money returned, or someone buys the group.

## Why the market is wrong
On book value it may be: NZ$1.11 is 0.66 times NTA, and nothing in the sales evidence supports a 34% discount. On what the market actually prices, cash earnings, Stride yields what its office peers yield, pays out more than its AFFO, and faces a loss of about a quarter of its outside fees. I can't name an event in the next 24 months that should make the market price it against NTA rather than AFFO, unless the board copies Precinct. No trade.

## What would make this wrong
"No case" is wrong if the price rises well above NZ$1.18. The two likeliest ways:
1. The board copies Precinct: an asset sale (Wellington office at or near book, or a partnership in an office building) with a buyback or capital return. Precinct rose 7.4% on its announcement (research/picks/NZ_ARG.md). Stride has the headroom; nothing in its filings points that way yet.
2. Rates fall and the sector re-rates. Stride was NZ$1.38 at 30 September 2025 and NZ$1.465 a year ago; a return there is +24% to +32% with nothing Stride-specific. A sale of SIML, or of a stake in it, at a price above its book would add to that.

The case for anyone who did buy gets worse if Diversified is wound up with few cost savings, 1 Grey Street needs a large repositioning spend, or the 8.0c dividend is held well above AFFO.

Exit rule: no position. If one were taken, sell at the next open after FY28 dividend guidance below 8.0c, NTA below NZ$1.55 at a balance date, or Diversified's wind-up without an offsetting new mandate, and otherwise on 30 June 2028.

Reopen with a fresh deep dive if: Stride announces a buyback or capital return, or sells Wellington office near book with money returned; it sells all or part of SIML, or wins a new management mandate large enough to replace Diversified's NZ$5.9m of fees; any holder goes above 10% with a stated intention (ACC is at 11.7% without one) or there is an approach; or the price falls to about NZ$0.94 or below while FY27 and FY28 guidance stays at 8.0c and NTA at NZ$1.60 or more.
