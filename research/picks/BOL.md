## The company in plain words
Boom hires out cranes (20 to 800 tonnes) and travel towers with their operators ("wet hire"), and plans and runs heavy lifts for mines, infrastructure, wind farms and industrial plants across Australia, from 17 depots with about 295 lifting and support machines whose value-weighted average age is 5.9 years (target 5 to 7) [B2]. FY26 revenue by sector: resources 55% (47% in FY25), infrastructure 20% (12%), renewables 16% (28%), industrial 9% (13%) [B2, slide 6]. About half of FY26 revenue came from contracted or recurring work [B1, p. 5]. The largest contract is BHP's Olympic Dam, a customer since 2012, renewed on 30 June 2026 for five years at about A$40m a year (about 15% of FY26 revenue), with a two-year option for BHP and "customary termination rights on reasonable notice" [B13]. Costs are mostly wages (A$130.9m in FY26), servicing and supplies (A$59.7m), cranes hired in from others (A$12.8m), and depreciation and lease interest (A$40.3m, nearly all on the fleet) [B1, income statement].

Who runs it: Lester Fernandez, managing director and chief executive since 12 December 2025 (interim since the former chief executive was stood down on 29 August 2025); Pieter Le Roux, chief financial officer, appointed in March 2026; Kieran Pryke, chair; Damian Banks, Phil Canning and Chris O'Neill, who replaced James Scott on 16 October 2026 [B1][B2][B20][B23].

Who owns it (6 August 2026 [B1, p. 68 to 69]): Collins St Value Fund (held by Sandhurst Trustees) 6,458,005 shares, 17.47% then and 18.0% of today's shares; Greig & Harrison 5.67% (the other substantial holder); HSBC Custody Nominees 5.99%; Citicorp Nominees 4.48%; J P Morgan Nominees 4.34%; Trafalgar Custodians (Forfar account) 2.84%; Boom's own employee share trust 1.93%. ## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Size and liquidity: A$78m of market value and a median of about A$157,000 traded a day [D2]. At that size it sits far below the smallest S&P/ASX 300 members (my inference; not checked against the index list).
- Little coverage: FNArena's broker call page of 2 December 2025 listed two brokers, with only Taylor Collison's note shown (Outperform, target A$2.13, FY27 EPS forecast 30.2c) [X1]; TradingView shows one analyst's target of A$3.06 [X2]. Both are secondary sources, not checked. Taylor Collison is also the broker running Boom's buyback [B17].
- A messy history: A$168.3m of accumulated losses on the balance sheet [B1], a loss as recently as FY23, a former chief executive stood down in August 2025 over A$1.143m of misused company money (recovered in full by December 2025, per the fact sheet), and a fatality under regulator investigation since July 2025 [B1, note 19].
- A concentrated register in flux: one value fund holds 18%, and a 15% holder sold out on market in mid-2026.
- A cyclical, capital-hungry trade: crane hire to miners and builders, where earnings have swung from losses to profits within a few years.

### Cash earnings after leases and fleet replacement
- Where the leases sit. Under the lease accounting standard (AASB 16) both owned and leased cranes are on the balance sheet. Lease interest (A$7.0m in FY26) is in operating cash flow and lease principal (A$29.3m) in financing [B1, cash flow statement]. Cranes taken on new leases never pass through the cash flow statement: in FY26, A$30.3m of rental equipment and A$7.6m of vehicles were added on leases, against A$3.8m of owned additions [B1, notes 7 and 12]. The company's own gross capital spending figure is A$21.3m, net A$16.7m after A$4.6m of sales [B2, slide 10], which suggests part of the lease additions were extensions or refinancings of cranes it already ran. Either way, "operating cash flow less lease repayments less cash capital spending" (the company's A$18.3m of free cash flow, the fact sheet's A$22.2m) leaves out the fleet bought on new leases.
- The clean test is net debt with leases. FY26 left A$16.6m for holders, but A$9.1m of that was working capital released as receivables fell from A$55.2m to A$42.1m and payables from A$16.0m to A$12.1m [B1]. FY25 left -A$15.4m while working capital absorbed A$16.0m and the fleet grew A$11.2m. Over FY22 to FY26 together the business left -A$20.7m: net debt rose A$39.2m while A$18.6m went to holders. In the same five years the fleet at book grew A$47.9m (with revenue up 26% from FY22), and working capital A$2.7m. Adding those back gives about A$30m, against A$25.6m of profit before tax: so, through a cycle, profit before tax has matched the cash the business produced before it grew its fleet (python below).
- Is depreciation enough to stand still? FY26 depreciation on the fleet was A$30.1m (owned rental equipment A$10.5m, leased rental equipment A$16.2m, vehicles A$3.5m; buildings a further A$2.9m) against A$41.7m of fleet additions, owned and leased, less A$4.6m of sales [B1, notes 7 and 12]. The fleet's average age held at 5.9 years [B2]. Assets sell at about book: FY26 sale proceeds A$4.64m against a carrying amount of A$4.66m; FY25 A$9.93m against A$9.02m [B1][B9]. So depreciation is a fair cost of keeping the fleet as it is. Growing it, even just with crane prices, costs more: about A$5m a year for 2.5% growth on A$206m of capital, of which about 60% is equity's share at the board's gearing (that is in Method 2 below).
- Conclusion: cash earnings after leases and fleet replacement, before tax, are about underlying profit before tax: A$12.8m in FY26. Taxed at 30% that is A$8.96m, 25c a share.

## Valuation
Worked from the filings. I saw the fact sheet's A$2.21 and the 6 November price while checking liquidity and the share count, as the brief required; no input below comes from Boom's price. Peers' prices are used, as the market's measure for this kind of business.

Inputs (python3 -I, A$m unless stated; scripts in the desk's scratchpad, figures repeated here):
- Shares 35.775m (35,821,551 less 46,062 bought back and not yet cancelled). The 669,794 performance rights are covered by the 714,829 shares the employee share trust already holds [B18][B1].
- Net debt with leases A$85.05m at 30 June 2026 (7.947 + 101.817 - 24.711). Distributions since (A$1.67m) are roughly offset by four months of cash earnings; I hold it at A$85m.
- Interest A$7.97m (bank A$0.94m, leases A$7.03m). EBIT = profit before tax + 7.97.
- Tangible equity A$119.4m (121.08 less the dividend and buyback since).
- Tax losses: A$26.744m used at 30% of profit before tax a year, profit growing 2.5%, discounted at 12%: A$15.6m if profit is A$9.0m, A$17.7m at A$12.8m, A$18.7m at A$15.5m.
- Three profit levels: A$9.0m (back to FY25's level, the bad case), A$12.8m (FY26 holds), A$15.5m (+21%, on the way to the board's A$18m FY29 hurdle).

### Method 1: EV/EBIT against listed equipment hire peers, leases counted as debt
This is how the market prices fleet-heavy hire companies: on operating profit after the fleet's depreciation, with leases as debt, since their price to earnings ratios mostly reflect how much each one has borrowed.
- Emeco (ASX:EHL, rental and maintenance of mining equipment): FY26 operating EBIT A$148.0m, net debt with leases A$127.1m (0.43 times EBITDA), return on capital 16.9%, FY27 guided to "stable earnings" [P1][P2]. At A$1.29 on 513.6m shares [D1], EV A$790m: 5.3 times EBIT; 7.4 times operating profit after tax; 0.85 times net tangible assets of A$1.51.
- Acrow (ASX:ACF, formwork, scaffold and industrial access hire): FY26 underlying EBIT A$51.3m, net debt with leases A$178.8m at June [P3][P4]; at A$0.88 (market value A$375.8m [D1]) EV about A$555m, 10.8 times trailing EBIT. It guides FY27 EBITDA up 37% after two acquisitions and an A$86m raising; on my rough FY27 EBIT of A$74m that is about 7.5 times.
- Boom earns less on its capital (EBIT A$20.8m on A$206m, 10.1%, against Emeco's 16.9%) and carries more debt (1.6 times EBITDA). I use 5.4 to 7.5 times, central 6.5.

| A$9.0m | 17.0 | A$0.62 | A$1.14 | A$1.62 |

(EBIT x multiple, less A$85.05m, plus the tax losses, over 35.775m shares.) What it depends on: the multiple. With debt equal to the market value, each turn of EBIT moves the share by about 58c.

### Method 2: taxed cash earnings to holders, capitalised
Cash earnings after leases and fleet replacement, taxed at 30%, less what the fleet needs to keep up with 2.5% a year of growth in prices (g divided by the return on tangible equity, times earnings), capitalised at the return holders should ask of a small, indebted crane company, plus the tax losses. Emeco's holders accept 13.4% (the inverse of 7.4 times) with a quarter of Boom's debt; I use 12% to 13%.

| Profit before tax | Taxed earnings | Return on tangible equity | 13%, no growth | 13%, 2.5% growth | 12%, no growth | 12%, 2.5% growth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A$9.0m | 6.30 | 5.3% | A$1.77 | A$1.30 | A$1.90 | A$1.41 |
| A$12.8m | 8.96 | 7.5% | A$2.41 | A$2.07 | A$2.58 | A$2.25 |
| A$15.5m | 10.85 | 9.1% | A$2.84 | A$2.60 | A$3.05 | A$2.84 |

What it depends on: the required return first, then whether growth is paid for. Boom earns 7.5% after tax on its tangible equity, below what holders should ask, so growing the fleet at today's returns doesn't add value; that is why the market prices it below book.

### Asset backing (a ceiling, not a middle)
Net tangible assets of about A$3.34 a share, supported by an independent valuation of the cranes and by asset sales at about book [B1]. Nobody earning 10% before tax on these assets would pay book for them, and selling about 295 machines at once would cost redundancies, lease break costs and lower prices. So this is what a bidder or a wind-up might pay at most, not what the market will pay for the shares as they are. The market's own record: Boom traded at 0.49 times net tangible assets in mid-2025 (A$1.36 against A$2.77) when profit was A$9.3m, 0.57 times at December 2025 and 0.65 times now; Emeco trades at 0.85 times on a 16.9% return on capital [D2][B2][P1].

### Range
One rule for every case: the average of Method 1's central value (6.5 times) and the midpoint of Method 2's four values.
- If FY26 holds (A$12.8m): Method 1 A$1.89, Method 2 A$2.07 to A$2.58 (midpoint A$2.33): A$2.11. Range A$1.89 to A$2.33.
- Bad case (A$9.0m): Method 1 A$1.14, Method 2 A$1.30 to A$1.90 (midpoint A$1.60): A$1.37.
- Good case (A$15.5m): Method 1 A$2.41, Method 2 A$2.60 to A$3.05 (midpoint A$2.83): A$2.62. Emeco's 0.85 times net tangible assets would be A$2.84; a bid near book, A$2.70 to A$3.34.
- The board's FY29 hurdle (A$18.0m): Method 1 A$2.88, Method 2 A$3.09 to A$3.48: A$3.08. That is two and a half years away, if it comes.

How well the rule tracks the market: applied to mid-2025 (FY25 profit A$9.3m, 41.27m shares, net debt A$93.3m, A$29.1m of tax losses) it gives A$1.16, against a June 2025 close of A$1.36; applied today it gives A$2.11 against A$2.18. The market has paid at or a little above it, so my bad case could land nearer A$1.60 than A$1.37 (that changes the expected price by 5c and the verdict not at all).

### The fact sheet's anchors, tested
- "If it goes our way", A$2.81 (FY26 profit taxed, at Emeco's statutory price to earnings ratio of 9.1, plus A$19.9m for tax losses): Emeco's ratio on its own operating profit is 7.4, and Emeco has net debt of 0.43 times EBITDA against Boom's 1.6. Putting a lightly indebted company's earnings multiple on a heavily indebted one overstates the value: A$2.81 is 8.0 times Boom's EBIT with the leases counted, against Emeco's 5.3. No mechanism gets the price there in six months unless the half shows real growth, which is my good case. It is the good case.
- "If it goes against us", A$1.29 (the five-year average profit, including FY23's loss, at the same multiple): a return to that average within a year is a tail inside my bad case. My bad case is a fall back to FY25's level, A$1.37, close to the sheet's figure because the debt weighs so heavily when profit falls.

## Why the market is wrong
I can't say it is. At A$2.18 the market pays 7.9 times underlying EBIT with the leases counted (8.6 times statutory), against Emeco's 5.3; 8.7 times taxed earnings, or 6.7 times after taking out the A$17.7m of tax losses, against Emeco's 7.4; and 0.65 times net tangible assets for a business earning 10% on its capital, against Emeco's 0.85 times for 16.9%. The price is up about 54% in a year (A$1.42 on 6 November 2025 [D2]) on the profit recovery, so FY26 holding is already paid for, and the only fresh evidence since the result (a weak fourth quarter, wind work only now restarting, no first-quarter update) points down rather than up.

## What would make this wrong

## Corrections to the fact sheet
1. Net debt is A$85.1m on the company's own count (A$88.3m in the annual report's review), not "about 65m". All A$101.8m of lease liabilities belong in it: A$105.8m of the A$120.6m of right-of-use assets is rental equipment, and the asset finance facility is drawn in "both finance and operating leases" [B2, slide 9][B1, notes 9 and 12]. The "net cash A$16.8m" line misleads.
2. "After leases and capital spending, cash generation was about 22.2m" leaves out A$37.9m of rental equipment and vehicles taken on new leases in FY26, which never pass through the cash flow statement, and A$9.1m of working capital released. Counting every fleet addition, FY25 and FY26 together left A$1.2m for holders; FY22 to FY26 left -A$20.7m while the fleet grew A$47.9m.
3. The sheet misses the weak end to FY26: fourth-quarter revenue of A$59.2m, 12.6% below a year earlier; renewables down from 28% to 16% of revenue; and the 8 October announcement that Narrogin "launches" FY27 wind revenue.
4. The "our way" anchor puts Emeco's statutory price to earnings ratio (9.1; 7.4 on Emeco's operating profit) on a company with nearly four times Emeco's debt relative to earnings. On EV/EBIT, the measure that allows for debt, Boom at A$2.18 (7.9 times) is already dearer than Emeco (5.3 times). A$2.81 is the good case.
5. Shares on issue are 35,821,551 at 4 November 2026 [B18], not 36.16m. The FY27 buyback had bought 379,512 shares for A$835,031 by 5 November [B17].
6. Tax losses: the total fell A$2.3m in FY26, not A$3.8m, because taxable income runs below accounting profit. My value is A$17.7m at 12% (the sheet's A$19.9m at 8%).
7. Not in the sheet: Boom released a first-quarter update in each of 2023, 2024 and 2025 and none this year; the board's FY29 incentive hurdles start at A$18m of profit before tax, while its FY28 hurdles start below FY26's actual; the only broker note found (secondary) is from Taylor Collison, which also runs the buyback.
