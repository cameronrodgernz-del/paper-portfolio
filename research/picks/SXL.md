> In short. The fact sheet's "goes our way" value of A$0.667 comes from two choices that don't hold up. First, it uses Nine's multiple, 4.44 times EBITDA after lease payments, but Nine expects about 70% of its FY27 EBITDA from Stan, outdoor advertising (QMS) and digital mastheads, and guides to EBITDA growth; Nine's own free-to-air TV business is carried at about 2.7 times its EBITDA after a A$426m write-down. The market has priced this group itself at 3.9 to 4.2 times: 4.2 times at the merger in January 2026, about 4.1 times for Southern Cross alone before the merger, and 3.86 times today. At 4.0 to 4.2 times, FY27 EBITDA held at A$191.9m is worth A$0.55 to A$0.62 a share, and a cash flow valuation gives A$0.46. Second, "holds at A$191.9m" isn't the middling case. Since the August result, first-quarter trading got worse in every division (TV from "tracking flat" to down, audio from up to flat, publishing from stable to down), Google's news payments stopped, the first half carries one-off Commonwealth Games and Rugby League World Cup costs, and the company now says the full benefit of the cost program lands in FY28, not FY27. The program itself is a gross figure that has to cover cost inflation of up to 4% on an A$1.42bn cost base (about A$57m a year) before it adds to profit. The price, A$0.48, is what the market would pay at 4.0 times for FY27 EBITDA of about A$184m to A$186m, a fall of 3% to 4%, which is close to my middling case. The case opens if the half-year shows EBITDA held and costs falling faster than revenue.

## The company in plain words
Seven Media Group is the merger of Southern Cross Media (SCA: the Hit and Triple M radio networks and the LiSTNR app) and Seven West Media (the Seven Network, 7plus, and The West Australian, The Nightly and PerthNow in publishing). SCA issued 238,879,990 shares to Seven West Media's holders, valued at A$189.9m (A$0.795 each), and the scheme was implemented on 7 January 2026 [A1]. Holders approved the new name on 5 November 2026 and the ASX code becomes SMG on 10 November 2026 [A7].

Where the money comes from (FY26, as if merged for the whole year, "pro forma") [A2]:
- Television: revenue A$1,251.5m, EBITDA A$109.6m (down 32.3%). Seven had a 42.5% audience share and 41.6% of TV advertising revenue, but the TV advertising market fell 9.9%. It carries the AFL and cricket; the AFL rights fee stepped up in FY26 and rises with inflation from here [A3].
- Audio: revenue A$429.9m (up 1.4%), EBITDA A$100.4m (up 15.5%); digital growth outpaced the fall in broadcast radio for the first time.
- Publishing: revenue A$187.0m, EBITDA A$26.2m.
- Corporate costs: A$44.3m.
- Group: revenue A$1,869.6m (down 4.5%), EBITDA A$191.9m before an A$8.1m non-cash release of onerous contract provisions (A$200.0m after it), down 15.8%.

EBITDA means earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. Under the lease accounting standard it is also before lease payments (A$38.3m in FY26), so the market compares companies on EBITDA less lease payments.

Who runs it: Rohan Lund, managing director since 1 May 2026 (previously chief executive of the NRMA); Scott Butterworth, CFO; Ryan Stokes, chief executive of SGH, chair since 7 September 2026, with Cathy O'Connor as lead independent director and six of eight directors independent [A5][A9].

Who owns it: substantial holders in the annual report and later notices [A1][H1][H2][H3][H5]: the SGH group and Stokes associates 23.19% (111,039,545 shares); Bruce McWilliam 9.73% (Rinehart-financed, see correction 1); 19 Cashews (Alex Waislitz's Thorney group) 8.56%; Samuel Terry Asset Management 7.69%; Spheria 6.09%; Sandon Capital 5.49%. Top 20 holders own 76.27%; 4,570 holders have less than a marketable parcel [A1].

Five years: the share price was A$2.35 on 1 November 2021 and is A$0.48 now, down about 80% (Norgate [D2]). SCA lost A$224.6m in FY24 (write-downs) and earned A$9.2m in FY25 [D1]. The last dividend was a 4.0c fully franked final for FY25, paid 7 October 2025; there has been none since [A1].

## Valuation
Done from the filings and from prices the market has paid for comparable businesses. The single input that matters most is FY27 EBITDA. All figures are before the non-cash contract releases. Lease payments are taken at A$37m a year (A$38.3m in FY26, A$43.1m in FY25, falling as office space shrinks [A3]). Arithmetic in python3 -I.

### Method 1: what the market pays, EV divided by EBITDA less lease payments
Enterprise value (EV) is the market value of the shares plus net debt (leases left out on both sides).
- Nine Entertainment (ASX:NEC): 1,584.1m shares (FY26 weighted average [P2]) at A$0.67 (30 October 2026 close [D2]) is A$1,061.4m, plus net debt A$657.9m, is A$1,719.3m. Nine's net debt is 1.7 times "EBITDA less cash lease payments" [P1], so that EBITDA is about A$387.0m and the multiple 4.44 times (4.31 to 4.57 for 1.65 to 1.75 times). But Nine expects more than 60% of FY27 revenue and about 70% of FY27 EBITDA from Stan, outdoor (QMS) and digital publishing, and expects FY27 EBITDA to grow [P1]. Its free-to-air TV business is carried at about A$360m after a A$426m write-down, against TV EBITDA of A$133.5m: about 2.7 times [P1]. That is a book value, not a sale, but it is Nine's own view of what its TV business is worth.
- This group at the merger: 478.8m shares at A$0.795 (the price used for the share issue on 7 January 2026 [A1]) is A$380.6m, plus pro forma net debt of A$338.2m at 31 December 2025 [A8], is A$718.8m. On the FY26 guidance given in February (midpoint A$210m less A$38.3m of leases, A$171.7m), that is 4.19 times.
- SCA alone before the merger: A$0.84 (29 September 2025, the day before the merger announcement [D2]) times 239,899,149 shares is A$201.5m, plus net debt of A$67.6m at 30 June 2025 [A8], is A$269.1m. On SCA's FY26 EBITDA guidance of A$78m to A$83m less its FY25 lease payments of A$14.5m [A1], that is about 4.1 times.
- So the market has paid 3.9 to 4.2 times for this group and its audio half on the earnings it expected at the time; Nine's 4.44 times is the ceiling for a business with better growth. I use 4.0 times in the middle, 3.8 times when earnings are falling and debt is rising, and 4.2 times when the cost program is visibly working.

Net debt at 31 December 2026: about A$345m to A$360m. The first half usually generates cash (pro forma net debt fell A$16m in 1H FY26 after a A$9.6m dividend [A8]), but this half pays redundancies from the June program and carries one-off sport costs.

### Method 2: discounted cash flow before interest
Inputs, each from the filings unless stated:
- EBITDA, E.
- Less other non-cash items inside EBITDA: A$20m a year (A$21.5m in FY26 once the A$8.1m release is taken out, A$20.0m in FY25 [A3]; the company doesn't explain them, so I treat them as recurring).
- Less working capital A$3m, capital spending A$30m (A$27.5m in FY26, A$35.4m in FY25), lease payments A$37m.
- Less restructuring cash A$15m a year (A$21.2m in FY26, A$19.0m in FY25; this group and Nine both book "one-off" restructuring every year [A3][P1]).
- Less tax at 22.6% (the FY26 effective rate [A2]) on E less A$64m of underlying depreciation and amortisation [A3], less about A$12m of lease interest [A1], less restructuring.
- At E = A$191.9m: tax A$22.8m, cash flow before interest A$64.1m.
- Discount rate 10% after tax: the company's own impairment tests use 9.58% for TV and 10.5% for audio [A1].
- Long-run growth minus 1% a year: the TV advertising market fell 9.9% in FY26 [A2]; the company's TV model assumes revenue growth of 0.6% a year and its audio base case 0% [A1]; I take a blend below both because TV is two-thirds of revenue.
- Value: 64.1 / (0.10 + 0.01) = A$583m, less net debt A$362.8m at 30 June 2026, is A$220m, or A$0.46 a share. Growth of 0% gives A$0.58; minus 2% gives A$0.36. A discount rate of 9.5% or 10.5% moves the A$0.46 to A$0.52 or A$0.41.
- At E = A$180m the same method gives A$0.28; at A$170m, A$0.14; at A$205m, A$0.65.

The cash flow method falls faster than the multiple method because about A$105m of cash costs (leases, capital spending, restructuring and the non-cash items) come out before tax whatever EBITDA does. On these inputs, cash to equity after A$27m of interest is about A$43m (9.0c a share) at E = A$191.9m and about A$26m (5.5c) at A$170m.

### A check: the parts
Audio at SCA's own pre-merger 4.1 times on A$87.4m (A$100.4m less about A$13m of leases), TV at Nine's 2.7 to 3.0 times on A$89.6m (A$109.6m less about A$20m), publishing at 3.5 times on A$20.9m, and corporate costs of A$44.3m at the blend, less A$362.8m of net debt: A$0.33 to A$0.38 a share. The lease split is my estimate. The combination isn't worth more in parts than as a whole.

### Range
If FY27 EBITDA holds at A$191.9m, the business is worth A$0.46 (cash flow) to A$0.62 (4.2 times), about A$0.55 in the middle. If it falls to A$180m, A$0.28 to A$0.46. Each A$10m of EBITDA is worth about 8.4 cents a share at 4.0 times. The value depends first on FY27 EBITDA, second on the multiple (4.0 against Nine's 4.44 times is 7 cents a share at A$191.9m), third on the recurring cash costs below EBITDA.

What the rules allow:
- Creep (Corporations Act section 611, item 9): a holder that has had at least 19% for six months may add up to 3 percentage points every six months without a bid. Wroxby used it on 17 August 2026, so the group may next buy up to 3% (about 14.4m shares, about A$7m at A$0.48) from about 17 February 2027, to 26.19%, then 29.19% from August 2027. In August the 3% came as one block (16.3m shares traded that day [D2]), not as buying spread over weeks, and the price kept falling afterwards.
- Above 20% otherwise needs a takeover bid or a scheme (75% of votes cast and a majority of holders voting, with the bidder's associates excluded). Compulsory acquisition follows at 90%.
- Media ownership: the Stokes group already holds 23% and the chair, and SGH appears on ACMA's register for this group (I couldn't open the register entry; see "What I couldn't verify").
- Comparable case: the same family's group did this to Boral. SGH crept to 23%, then bid A$6.50 on 11 May 2021, the previous close (no premium); the independent directors called it opportunistic; SGH raised it to A$7.40 (14% above the first offer) and passed 50% in July 2021 [S6][S7]. Holders who waited got 14% more than the opening offer, about two months after it.
- Stated intentions: in January 2026 SGH sources called reports of a sale "nonsense" and said SGH would keep its stake; the report found no plans to buy more or bid [S1]. Seven months later the family bought 3% through a private company [H1][S2]. Nothing filed says what the family intends.

What it is worth if nothing happens: the middling value, A$0.46, with the bad case A$0.30 if the half-year is weak. Creep at these prices doesn't need to pay a premium, so slow creep with no bid is a poor outcome for other holders: the family gets closer to control without paying for it. I put a bid before 31 March 2027 at about 5%, inside the good case, at A$0.55 to A$0.65.

## Why the market is wrong
On today's evidence I can't say it is. At 4.0 times, A$0.48 prices FY27 EBITDA at about A$184m to A$186m, down 3% to 4%, which is close to my middling case; the cash flow method says the price is already generous unless EBITDA holds. Two things could make the price too low, and both will show at the half-year result:
1. The costs. The company says first-quarter costs fell despite one-off sport costs, the program is ahead of plan and will beat A$150m [A5]. If the half shows operating costs down 4% or more while revenue falls less than 3%, EBITDA holds and the market moves towards 4.2 times.
2. The holders. Every informed buyer in 2026 paid A$0.54 to A$0.62, and the Stokes group can buy another 3% from February. That supports the price but doesn't set it.
Neither is enough to buy before the result.

## What would make this wrong
The two things most likely to make the wait verdict wrong (the price rises before the result):
1. The cost program is landing faster than I assume. The company says first-quarter costs were down despite the one-off sport costs and that it will beat the target [A5]. If TV advertising also steadies, the half could hold EBITDA and the price could reach A$0.60 to A$0.66 before we buy.
2. The Stokes group bids rather than creeps. The Boral case shows the pattern: creep to 23%, then a bid at the market price, raised by 14% [S6][S7].

The two things most likely to sink a buy at today's price:
1. TV advertising keeps falling at FY26's pace while Google's payments are gone and wagering limits start in January; at 4.0 times, every A$10m of lost EBITDA takes about 8 cents a share.
2. Debt. Net debt is 1.6 times the market value of the shares; the equity takes the whole of any fall in business value, and the recurring restructuring and non-cash items keep cash to equity low.

What reopens it, and at what price:
- At the 1H FY27 result: EBITDA before contract releases of at least A$104m (no more than about 3% below 1H FY26's A$106.9m), operating costs (excluding revenue-related costs) down at least 4% on the previous half, net debt at 31 December 2026 of A$350m or less, and no outlook that implies FY27 below A$190m. Then a new deep dive resolving at the FY27 result (late August 2027) would carry outcomes of about A$0.47 (20%), A$0.62 (50%) and A$0.81 (30%): expected A$0.647, which clears the minimum (3.6% over about 183 days, plus 2.7% costs) at A$0.60 or below. Reopen if the price after the result is A$0.60 or less.
- At any time: a price of A$0.44 or below with nothing else changed (the watch trigger), or A$0.30 or below as a value pick.
- A takeover bid or scheme proposal from anyone: write it up as a special situation or, once binding, in the deal sleeve.

What closes it: the half's EBITDA before contract releases below A$96m (down more than 10%), net debt above A$370m, or net debt above 2.25 times EBITDA at the covenant test. Then the bad case dominates and the stock isn't worth watching above about A$0.30.
