In short. The kill check is a judgement here, because PAC passes every mechanical test: its name doesn't match the ranking's fund pattern, its industry group is "Financial Services", it doesn't publish monthly net asset figures, and ASX hasn't treated it as an "investment entity" under the Listing Rules. On substance it is an investment company. Since 2024 it has called itself "an owner of a portfolio of investment holdings", and until May 2026 an outside manager ran that portfolio for 0.75% a year of its fair value. At 30 June 2026, 56% of the portfolio was cash, bonds, listed shares and loans, and the other 44% was minority stakes in asset managers it doesn't control. Interest made up 67% of underlying income, and revenue from operations was nil. The company measures itself on net assets per share, and one option in the review is to delist and sell everything. It looks like Bailador (ASX:BTI), which the ranking drops as an investment company. So the deep dive stops here, as the brief directs. Two corrections to the triage, and the leads already found, are recorded below in case Cam or the desk overrules the classification before the AGM.

## Kill check: operating holding company or investment company?

The test in research/facts/TEMPLATE.md is "A fund, trust or investment company, or no business to value yet?". A "yes" ends the work. The ranking (src/research_rank.py) applies it mechanically: a name pattern, a "Listed investment" industry group, or at least three monthly net asset or portfolio updates in six months. PAC passed those, and the ranking sent it to the special situations watch list. Nothing in the ranking spares a fund because it is under review: the fund test runs before any special-situation routing. The question for a person is what PAC is in substance.

**Why it is an investment company**

1. **Its own description.** On 27 August 2026 it said: "In early 2024, Pacific Current took the decision to transition from an active investor in global boutique asset management firms to an owner of a portfolio of investment holdings, with management of those investments externalised for an interim period of time ... the Company's asset base now comprises a combination of investments in asset managers, financial assets and cash" [S1]. The directors' report gives its principal activity as to "invest in asset managers, private advisory, placement and investment related firms" [S4].
2. **External management on a fee.** From May 2024 to 17 May 2026 an affiliate of GQG Partners managed PAC's portfolio for "0.75% of the fair value of PAC's investment portfolio (excluding cash)". Most of PAC's US staff moved to GQG [S10, S11]. That is the standard listed investment company setup. Since May 2026 a Managing Director (Michael Clarke) and a CFO run it with a small team: employment costs were A$1.7m in FY26 [S3].
3. **What it owns.** Of A$460m of gross assets at fair value on 30 June 2026, 34% was cash (A$157.7m), 22% was Abacus Global Management bonds (A$76.9m) and shares (A$7.8m) plus loans to boutiques (A$15.4m), and 44% was stakes in boutiques [S3]. Every boutique stake is a minority interest: 9.2% of Victory Park's equity, 7.5% of Pennybacker, 30.0% of Roc, 24.9% of IFP, 44.5% of Astarte, 23% of Northern Lights, and a preference stake in Global IMC [S3, S4]. No operating business is consolidated. After Aether was sold in June 2026, "revenue from operations" was nil [S3].
4. **Where the income comes from.** FY26 underlying income was A$25.1m: interest A$16.8m (67%), boutique income A$7.1m (28%) and a one-off legal settlement A$1.2m [S3]. Most of what PAC earns is interest on cash, bonds and loans, not profit shares from asset managers.
5. **How it measures itself.** Results lead with "fair value NAV per share" (A$16.18 at 30 June 2026). PAC rejected Roc's proposal because it was "below Pacific Current's assessment of fair value" [S2, S3]. The 2024 strategy reset was explained by "the continued gap between PAC's share price and its estimated fair value net asset value" [S10].
6. **Where it is heading.** The review's three options are River Capital's proposal (which would turn PAC into "an active listed equities and private markets investment manager" investing third-party money), a sale, or "delisting the Company from the ASX and undertaking an orderly realisation of holdings" [S1]. Two of the three are about realising net assets.
7. **Like-for-like.** Bailador Technology Investments (ASX:BTI) holds minority stakes in unlisted companies with board seats, has had an outside manager, and is valued on net assets. The ranking drops it as an investment company (reports/research_rank.md, "Dropped on rules"). PAC is the same shape, holding asset managers instead of technology companies.

**Why it might not be**

1. **ASX's own test.** The Listing Rules define an "investment entity" as one whose main activity is investing in securities and whose "objectives do not include exercising control over or managing any entity, or the business of any entity, in which it invests". ASX weighs "the extent of board representation, the size of the holdings, the investment period and the amount of cash held" [S13]. PAC's stated strategy includes "identifying, investing, and managing investments in investment management or related firms" [S4]. It holds 23% to 45% of four firms as associates with significant influence, and it has never filed the monthly net asset reports investment entities must give (none in the 2024 to 2026 announcement lists [S12]).
2. **Its business model.** The boilerplate still calls PAC "a multi-boutique asset management firm" that applies "capital, institutional distribution capabilities and operational expertise to help our partners excel" [S1]. Some loans carry approval rights over a boutique's budgets and distributions (Northern Lights) [S4]. Before 2024, PAC was an operating multi-boutique group, like Affiliated Managers Group in the US.

**The call.** The first two counter-points describe form and history, not what PAC is today. ASX's classification has never been tested since PAC changed in 2024. Board seats and 23% to 45% stakes are normal for a private equity style investment company, and Bailador has them too. Half the assets are cash, bonds and loans, the income is mostly interest, and the review is about turning net assets into cash. That is an investment company. The exclusion exists because an investment company's price can sit below its net assets for years without anything forcing the gap shut. PAC shows that pattern. At each 30 June from 2021 to 2026, its close was 31%, 38%, 36%, 18%, 30% and 29% below its own fair value net assets, and 27%, 33%, 25%, 7%, 27% and 18% below statutory book. The net asset figures come from the chart in [S3]. The closes are A$5.81, A$6.92, A$7.41, A$11.08, A$10.82 and A$11.46 from Norgate. Excluded.

This is a judgement on a borderline case, and it is the only thing stopping the deep dive. If Cam reads the rule as covering only funds, trusts and listed investment companies in the ASX sense, PAC is not excluded and the deep dive should go ahead at once: the review update is due at or before 12 November 2026. The leads below would let it start where this one stopped.

## Corrections to the triage

1. **River's price is A$12.72, not A$13.00.** The A$13.00 for both the new shares and the bookbuild floor is "reduced by the cash amount of any dividend or distribution declared or paid by Pacific Current after 30 June 2026 and prior to completion" [S1]. The 28c final dividend was declared on 27 August 2026 and paid on 8 October 2026 [S3, data/research_data/asx_company.json]. That takes both prices to A$12.72 (13.00 - 0.28). Against A$11.02, that is 15.4% above the price, not 18%.
2. **What River holds.** The 12,207,237 shares are River Capital's relevant interest. The 11 June 2026 notice shows 10,066,964 held by River-managed funds through JP Morgan Nominees (6,559,857 and 3,507,107), 1,160,161 by Bellwether Investments and 980,112 by River Capital Nominees [S8]. The "roughly 10 million" shares in the proposed bookbuild are therefore the fund-held block, not River's whole interest [S1].

Checked and correct in the triage: the review began on 27 August 2026, with the three options as stated [S1]. Roc's May 2026 proposal was declined because it was "largely funded from PAC's own cash assets" and gave "differential consideration" [S2]. Any River deal needs a vote with River and its associates excluded, plus an independent expert's report [S1]. River's 43.69% matches the annual report, where the substantial holders are Perpetual 10.17%, Michael Fitzpatrick 8.60% and DS Capital 5.56% [S7]. Fair value net assets were A$16.18 a share and statutory net assets A$13.96 [S3]. The WHSP facility was repaid in October 2025, so there is no debt [S3, S4]. The price was A$11.02 [data]. Median daily value traded was A$14,745 [Norgate, below].

## Leads already found (for a restart if the classification is overruled)

None of these is a finished valuation. Arithmetic in python3 -I.

- **Hard assets.** At 30 June 2026, cash was A$157.7m (A$5.64 a share), Abacus bonds and shares A$84.7m (quoted prices), and loans to boutiques A$15.4m. Together that is A$258.0m, or A$9.23 a share, before the 28c dividend (about A$7.8m) [S3].
- **The soft part of A$16.18.** Fair value net assets exceed statutory book by A$2.22 a share. That gap is management's own unaudited uplift on three equity-accounted stakes: Roc from A$7.9m to A$60.1m, IFP from A$7.8m to A$35.8m, and Astarte from A$9.1m to A$14.4m. Those add A$85.5m gross, less a A$24.8m tax allowance, or A$2.17 a share [S3]. Roc Group, all of it, earned A$3.0m after tax on A$38.7m of revenue in FY26 and paid PAC A$2.0m [S4]. A$60.1m for PAC's 30% is about 30 times the cash Roc paid PAC. IFP had net liabilities of A$4.8m and earned A$0.27m after tax [S4]. Its value rests on a sale waterfall (100% of the first US$8m, then 44.9%) and on growth funded by PAC's own US$25.1m loan [S3].
- **A change of valuation method.** In FY26 PAC replaced the perpetuity-growth terminal value in its unquoted valuations with "a trailing FRE (management-fee EBITDA) exit multiple" of 4.5 to 15 times. It said the old method gave values "materially below observable market benchmarks". Discount rates were 15.54% to 45.00% [S4].
- **Roc on both sides.** Roc is PAC's largest boutique by fair value and the party whose May 2026 proposal PAC declined. PAC also lent A$2m at 10% to a Roc affiliate in December 2025 [S2, S3, S4].
- **What River's deal means for outside holders.** PAC would issue about 6.3m shares at A$12.72 (about A$80m) for River Capital, a manager of about A$1b, with a two-year escrow on the shares. River's funds would sell their 10.07m shares in a bookbuild at no less than A$12.72 [S1]. Outside holders get no cash. Issuing shares below fair value net assets dilutes them unless River is worth more than A$80m. For example, at A$15.90 of net assets (A$16.18 less the dividend) and River worth exactly A$80m, net assets per share fall to about A$15.30 (python: (27.94 x 15.90 + 80) / (27.94 + 6.3)). The bookbuild's A$12.72 floor is the only price an outside holder would see, and that only on the market.
- **Board independence.** The 7 September update was "authorised by the independent directors" [S2], which suggests at least one director is not independent of the River or Roc proposals. This was not checked.
- **Liquidity.** The median value traded over the 20 sessions to 30 October 2026 was A$14,745 (Norgate close times volume). Section 3's limit (two sessions at 10% of daily value) caps a position at about A$2,949, not far above the A$1,500 smallest ASX order. Two of the 20 sessions traded a single share, and closes ranged from A$10.42 to A$11.50.
- **Not yet read:** the AGM notice's resolutions [S9], the 30 September 2026 FUM update [S6], substantial holder notices before 2026, and Perpetual's and Fitzpatrick's histories. River's April 2026 notice is a scanned image with no text [S12].
