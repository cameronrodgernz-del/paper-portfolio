## The company in plain words
Zimplats mines platinum group metals (PGMs) on Zimbabwe's Great Dyke: underground mines at Bimha, Mupani and Mupfuti, pillar reclamation at Ngwarati (the South Pit open pit closed at the end of FY26), concentrators at Ngezi and Selous, and a smelter at Selous that turns concentrate into matte [Z1, Z4]. All its matte and concentrate is sold to one customer, Impala Platinum Limited, a sister company in South Africa, which refines it; payment is due three to five months after sale and carries no interest [Z1, note 33]. "6E" means six metals: platinum, palladium, rhodium, ruthenium, iridium and gold. Nickel, copper and a little cobalt and silver come with them.

Where the money comes from (FY26, to 30 June 2026) [Z2, note 4]: platinum US$474.2m, palladium US$315.1m, rhodium US$211.6m, gold US$122.3m, nickel US$74.5m, copper US$40.1m, iridium US$39.1m, ruthenium US$20.0m; revenue US$1,298.8m. Zimplats reports in US dollars.

Structure: Zimplats Holdings is registered in Guernsey. The operating company, Zimbabwe Platinum Mines (ZPM), is 90% owned; the other 10% was issued in 2017 to the Zimplats Employee Share Ownership Trust for a stated US$95m [Z1, note 16]. The trust is consolidated, so the group accounts show no minority interest and attribute all profit to Zimplats shareholders.

Who runs it: Dr Stanley Segula became CEO on 1 November 2026; he has been managing director of ZPM since 2015. Alex Mhembere, CEO since 2007, retired and stays chairman of ZPM [Z6]. CFO Patricia Zvandasara; chairman Prof Fholisani Mufamadi. Implats' CEO Nico Muller and CFO Meroonisha Kerber sit on the board [Z1, Z7]. Two independent directors with Zimbabwean careers, Gugulethu Ngwenya and Clara Sadomba, joined on 1 November 2026 [Z14].

Who owns it [Z1, shareholder information, 30 June 2026]: Impala Platinum BV 93,644,430 shares (87.00%); Citicorp Nominees 6,633,373 (6.16%); HSBC Custody Nominees 2.02%; J P Morgan Nominees 1.52%; BNP Paribas nominee accounts about 0.75%; the top 20 hold 98.41%; 3,029 holders. At the 27 October AGM 102.8m shares voted (95.5% of the company); the only proxy outside Implats was one for 9,186,954 shares, given to the chairman, voting for everything [Z7]. The outside holders are a few custodians and are not agitating.

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Index: never a member of the S&P/ASX 200 or 300 on Norgate's history from 2010 to 30 October 2026 (data/norgate/index_members.csv).
- Free float: 13%, about A$200m, held mostly by a handful of custodians.
- Trading: median A$85,792 a day over the 20 sessions 5 to 30 October 2026 (Norgate); the company itself says volume has "remained at a low level" because of Implats' 87% [Z1].
- Coverage: I found no named broker. An aggregator (alphaspread, a secondary source) shows an undated "average 1-year price target" of A$15.92 with no analyst count [S3].
- Country: Zimbabwe, with currency surrender rules, an open tax audit and a new "critical minerals" policy whose details are still to come [Z2, Z8].
So size, index, register, coverage and an out-of-favour country all apply. The plainer reason it is cheap on earnings is that its cash doesn't reach outside holders unless Implats decides it should.

## What the filings show

### Where FY26's cash went
Profit was US$276.7m but cash fell US$40.3m to US$58.3m [Z2]. From the cash flow statement and note 14 (arithmetic in python3 -I):
- Cash from operations before working capital: about US$538.8m.
- Working capital took US$323.8m: inventories up US$87.3m (concentrate stockpiled during smelter maintenance), prepayments up US$28.3m, receivables up US$226.6m, payables up US$18.4m.
- Of the receivables: the Implats trade receivable up US$136.2m (US$273.7m to US$409.8m); the "statutory receivable" (export proceeds surrendered for local currency, ZWG) up US$32.8m to US$87.1m, plus US$43.9m used to pay tax by set-off; VAT owed by the tax office up US$20.0m [Z2, notes 12 and 14; Z1, notes 21 and 22].
- Then interest US$8.9m, tax paid US$25.7m, capital spending US$150.7m, US$69.3m of bank debt repaid [Z2].
- Implats' measure of Zimplats' free cash flow after growth spending: US$31m [I2].

### Is the Implats receivable cash held back?
Not in any way the board can choose month to month. It is the sales pipeline under the contract. As months of the previous half-year's sales: 3.45 at June 2025, 3.42 at December 2025, 3.74 at June 2026 (US$273.7m on H2 FY25 sales of US$476.4m; US$365.8m on H1 FY26's US$641.8m; US$409.8m on H2 FY26's US$657.1m) [Z1, Z3]. It grows when prices and volumes rise and shrinks when they fall. The terms do favour the parent: the receivable carries no interest (about US$16m to US$20m a year at 4% to 5%), while Zimplats paid Implats US$2.9m of interest on sales paid in advance [Z1, note 33; Z2, note 7].

### What does keep cash from holders
- Capital spending is not falling much. The US$1.8bn programme (Mupani, the smelter expansion, solar) is largely built [Z6]: Mupani US$368m of US$386m, the smelter US$480m of US$544m, solar phase 2A US$45m of US$54m at September 2026 [Z4]. But Implats guides Zimplats' FY27 capital spending at R2.4bn to R3.0bn, US$145m to US$182m at its R16.50 assumption, against US$153.5m in FY26 [I1]. Stay-in-business spending was US$122.2m in FY26, up from US$90.8m [Z1]. The base metals refinery (US$154m left of US$190m) was deferred outside the five-year plan [Z3]; a new mine, Portal 10, is under study [I2]; Mupfuti's reserves end in FY2030 [Z1].
- The board chose debt and resilience. With US$145.7m of cash and US$66.2m of bank debt at 31 December 2025, and H1 profit of US$143.7m, no interim was declared [Z3]. The FY25 annual report's audit committee said "with the completion of the capital projects, management has one of its key focus areas, the payment of dividends to shareholders" [Z9]. A year later, after US$276.7m of profit, the FY26 report says decisions are "informed by the Group's funding requirements, liquidity position, capital programme, risk outlook and the objective of preserving long-term financial resilience", and the dividend line is gone from the committee report [Z1].
- The listed company has run out of offshore cash. Zimplats Holdings' own cash was US$291.6m at June 2022, US$131.6m at June 2023, US$15.9m at June 2024, US$14.4m at June 2025 and US$11.7m at June 2026 [Z10, Z1]. The FY23 and FY24 dividends (US$320m) were paid largely from that pile. ZPM has owed the holding company US$53.55m of declared dividends since at least June 2023; in FY26 it declared another US$9.0m, of which none was paid in cash (US$1.35m of withholding tax was set off and the rest added to the debt, now US$61.2m) [Z10, Z1, notes 21 and 33.2, company cash flow]. So a dividend to ASX holders now needs ZPM to send US dollars out of Zimbabwe, which it hasn't done in size for three years.
- The leakage on the way out. ZPM's dividends to the holding company carry 15% Zimbabwe withholding tax (US$1.35m on US$9.0m in FY26) [Z1, tax note], and 10% of every ZPM dividend goes to the employee trust. Of each dollar ZPM pays out, an outside ASX holder's 13% share is worth about 9.9 cents (13% x 90% x 85%); Implats' is worth about 66.6 cents.
- Zimbabwe's local currency. The 70:30 rule surrenders 30% of export receipts for ZWG [S1]. US$76.9m was surrendered in FY26 and US$44.1m used, mostly against taxes [Z2, note 12.1]. Implats reclassified circa US$76.9m held at the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe from cash to statutory receivables, and leaves Zimbabwe gold balances out of its own adjusted cash [I1]. Implats' CFO said in September that since a March agreement Zimplats receives 50% of surrender proceeds in cash, the rest by set-off, about US$99m having been offset against taxes, royalties and duties (Reuters via Kitco, a secondary source) [S2]. In March, Implats' CEO said its Zimbabwe risk had "materially shifted upwards" over the last year or two (Sunday Times, a secondary source) [S1].

### What a quick reader would miss
1. The 10% employee trust in ZPM and the 15% withholding tax on ZPM's dividends: about 23.5% of anything paid out never reaches the listed company's holders.
2. The listed company's offshore cash is US$11.7m, and ZPM has not paid it US$53.55m of declared dividends in three years.
3. The Implats receivable is a steady 3.4 to 3.7 months of sales, not a growing hoard.
4. FY26 cost of sales includes a US$61.2m credit for stock built up; cash costs were US$706.4m (the sum of the four quarters [Z4, Z5]).
5. FY27 capital spending is guided at US$145m to US$182m, not down.
6. At spot the basket is held up by minor metals that are at about twice their H1 FY26 averages, while palladium is at a 16-month low.
7. The FY25 report's promise on dividends was dropped from the FY26 report.

## Valuation
All in US dollars, converted at A$1 = US$0.6900. Shares 107.6m. Arithmetic in python3 -I; the formulas are set out below so each figure can be rebuilt.

Common inputs:
- Volume 630,000 oz a year (bottom of Implats' FY27 range [I1]).
- Royalty and export commission 5.89% of revenue (FY26: US$76.5m on US$1,298.8m [Z2, note 5]).
- Operating cash costs US$741.7m, FY26's US$706.4m plus 5% (the September quarter ran 7% above a year earlier [Z4]); other cash costs, including the recurring currency losses and credit losses, US$15m.
- Capital spending US$150m a year to keep production level (FY26 US$153.5m; FY27 guidance US$145m to US$182m [I1]).
- Tax 25.75%, the operating company's rate [Z1, tax note], on cash earnings less capital spending and interest (about the same as on earnings less depreciation when the two are close).
- To reach an ASX holder: x 90% (employee trust) x 85% (withholding tax) [Z1].
- Discount rate 14.19% real: the company's own pre-tax real rate of 19.11% [Z1, note 15] after 25.75% tax.
- One-off cash: net cash US$28.3m at June plus about US$40m of the stockpile turning back into cash in FY27, after leakage US$0.49 a share. The US$87.1m statutory receivable is left out, as Implats leaves Zimbabwe gold balances out of its own cash [I1].

Free cash flow of the operating company at each basket (US$ an ounce): 1,800: US$118m; 1,900: US$162m; 2,000: US$206m; 2,030: US$219m; 2,150: US$272m; 2,171: US$281m. The gearing is the point: a 10% fall in the basket from US$2,000 takes away 43% of the free cash.

### Range
Midpoint of methods 1 and 2, at a US$2,000 basket: A$16.75 if a dividend comes now, A$14.71 after a year, A$13.79 after 18 months, A$12.93 after two years. At US$1,850 with a two-year wait, A$9.26; at US$2,150 with an 18-month wait, A$17.71.

### The fact sheet's anchors retested
- "Our way", A$18.05 (US$2.12 x 6 at A$1 = US$0.7062): it counts 100% of ZPM's earnings and ignores the 15% withholding tax. With only those two corrections and today's exchange rate the same arithmetic gives A$14.10 (US$2.12 x 6 x 0.90 x 0.85 / 0.69); with the trust alone, A$16.59. Its cost base also used FY26 cost of sales, which carries a US$61.2m credit for stock built up; on cash costs its earnings would be US$1.72 a share, not US$2.12. The mechanism inside the window would have to be a dividend, and at a US$2,000 basket a dividend declared now is worth A$16.75 on my methods. So the fact sheet's "our way" is my good case at best, and its corrected arithmetic is the price.
- "Against us", A$6.33 (basket US$1,800, costs up 5%, 4 times): close to my two-year bad case at US$1,800 (A$8.04) and below it, because 4 times is lower than the cash the business makes at that basket supports.

## Why the market is wrong
I can't say it is at A$14.19. The price matches my value at a US$2,000 basket if outside holders wait a little over a year for cash, which is what the record says: six straight decisions without a dividend, including two after large profits, an offshore holding company with US$11.7m, and a parent whose CEO says Zimbabwe risk has gone up. A dividend in February would move the value at a steady basket from about A$13.8 to A$16.75, but on the evidence it is about a one-in-five chance, and in the four months to March the metal prices will move the share more than the dividend decision will.

## What would make this wrong
The two things most likely to make this verdict wrong:
1. The board declares an interim dividend in February. The cash could be there: on my estimate net cash at 31 December 2026 is about US$100m to US$150m (central about US$130m, with the US$30m loan repaid) if the basket stays near US$2,000, against US$79.5m net a year earlier, because the receivable shrinks as the furnace rebuild cuts first-half sales. Debt is nearly gone, the CEO is new, and the FY25 report called dividends a key focus. A dividend of US$0.30 a share or more at a steady basket takes the value from about A$13.8 to A$16.75.
2. Metal prices. A 7% move in the basket moves my value by about 25% to 30%. Platinum and palladium back towards FY26 averages would give A$18 or more without any dividend. Equally, iridium at US$9,650 and ruthenium at US$1,950 are more than twice their H1 FY26 averages; their reversal alone takes the basket to about US$1,910.
A third, less likely: Implats bids for the 13%. It could afford it (about US$180m at a 30% premium against its own net cash), and as a Guernsey company Zimplats is, as I understand it, outside Chapter 6 of the Corporations Act, so the 3% creep limit doesn't bind Implats. But its CEO has said Zimbabwe risk has risen, and nothing in its filings points to buying more.

Reopen conditions:
- Watch trigger A$13.30, with the basket still at or above about US$2,000 and no dividend news: send to the red team as an ordinary pick resolving 5 March 2027, on the outcomes above. If the price gets there because the basket fell, rerun the values first: at US$1,900 the middle value drops to about A$11.2.
- The half-year declares an interim dividend of at least US$0.30 a share, or sets a payout policy, with the basket at or above about US$1,950: with payments under way the outcomes become a basket bet around A$16.75 (A$11.97, A$16.75, A$21.53 at 30%, 40%, 30%). Reopen as an ordinary pick resolving after the FY27 result if the price is at or below about A$15.4.
- Kill: the half-year passes again with net cash above US$100m; or Implats approves Portal 10 or the refinery to be paid from Zimplats' cash; or Zimbabwe raises the surrender share or royalties, or adds a levy on matte exports. Then the wait is two years or more and the value at US$2,000 is about A$12.9; no case above about A$12.
- Value pick: only at about A$8.9 or below, where the two-year bad case (A$8.04) is within 10% and the dividend case (A$16.75) is far above.

Exit rule, if a card is ever written: sell at the next open after the half-year if no dividend is declared, or after a month with the basket below US$1,850; otherwise sell on or after the resolve date unless a fresh deep dive renews it.

- The holding company's US$9.0m of FY26 dividend income was not received in cash: US$1.35m of withholding tax was set off and the rest added to the US$61.2m owed by ZPM, US$53.55m of which has been owed since at least June 2023 [Z10, Z1].

- Why ZPM hasn't paid the holding company the US$53.55m; the filings don't say whether exchange control or choice is the reason.
- The contract terms between Zimplats and Implats beyond the payment period and no interest: payability and refining charges are not published. My realised-price ratios come from FY26 revenue divided by ounces sold.
- Metal prices from primary exchanges: the prices above are from Umicore's posted prices, Trading Economics and ISA Bullion; palladium's latest posted Umicore price was 26 October.
- Implats' latest share price: the 9 October figure is from a secondary site.
- Any statement by Implats on Zimplats dividends. The FY26 booklet and presentation say nothing about it; the CEO and CFO comments are from press reports.
- Whether Zimplats has a written dividend policy: the FY25 report mentions one but doesn't set it out [Z9].
- The cash cost of the furnace rebuild in the December quarter.
- Guernsey's compulsory acquisition threshold, and whether Chapter 6 of the Corporations Act reaches a Guernsey company listed on ASX; I haven't checked either against the law itself.
