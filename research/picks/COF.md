# Centuria Office REIT (ASX:COF): deep dive

Written 2026-11-02 by the research desk. Price at writing: A$0.875 (close on 30 October 2026, Norgate, data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz; the ASX company data shows the same last price [S30]). Market value: A$522.7m (units on issue 597,336,931, FY26 financial report note C7 [S3] and ASX company data [S30]).
Status: finished. This is a simulation and not financial advice.
Fact sheet: research/facts/COF.md, grade A. The question it set: would the buildings sell for book value (7.04% cap rate, NTA A$1.66)? Its two pressures: distributions exceed cash earnings after leasing costs, and interest cover is 2.0 times against a 1.75 times covenant as hedges roll off.

> The desk's view in one line: the buildings would probably sell for close to book, a few per cent below it, but that isn't what the market is pricing. After rent-free periods, fit-out incentives and upkeep, COF keeps about 4 cents a unit a year and pays out 9 cents, and on that cash it's priced more richly than any listed office peer. It trades at the same discount to NTA as Abacus and GDI. The December valuations are more likely to take NTA down than up. The expected price by 5 March 2027 is about 0.4% below today's before costs, so there's no edge and no card.

Order of work: the fact sheet that set the question already quoted the price, and so does the ASX header in the repository's company data, so the price was in view before I read the filings. The valuation below is worked from COF's filings, other REITs' filings and the prices the market pays for those peers. COF's own price is used only in "Why the market is wrong" and "Outcomes and chances".

## The company in plain words
COF owns 18 office buildings (two of them as half-shares) with a book value of A$1,842.0m at 30 June 2026, plus a ground-lease asset of A$32.3m [S1, S2]. By state: Victoria A$486.5m (four buildings), Sydney fringe and Wollongong A$413.8m (four), Brisbane fringe A$407.5m (four), Perth A$263.0m (three), Canberra A$235.3m (two) and Adelaide A$36.0m (one) [S1]. The money is rent: A$152.6m of rent and recoverable outgoings in FY26, and A$128.1m of net property income on the trust's FFO basis [S2, S3]. The ten largest tenants by income are the Commonwealth (13.9%), the WA Government (5.2%), the NSW Government (3.5%), Target (2.8%), ResetData (2.4%), IAG (2.4%), Stantec (2.3%), Pacific Magazines (2.2%), Central House (1.9%) and Costa (1.7%) [S1].

COF is a registered managed investment scheme with no employees. Centuria Property Funds Limited, a subsidiary of Centuria Capital Group (ASX:CNI), is its responsible entity; the directors are Matthew Hardy (chair), Darren Collins, Elizabeth McDonald and Peter Done, and the fund manager is Belinda Cheung [S3, S4]. The manager is paid 0.55% of gross assets a year (the constitution allows 0.60%), A$10.9m in FY26, and Centuria companies also charge for property and facility management, leasing, custody, projects and administration, A$20.2m in all after A$1.3m of rent they paid the trust [S3].

Who owns it, at 24 July 2026 [S4]: substantial holders Centuria Capital Group with 112,721,666 units (18.9%) and LOR Assets, the Lederer family company, with 101,001,333 (16.9%). The top 20 registered holders have 367.2m units (61.5%): HSBC Custody Nominees 173.6m (the Lederers' units are held through it [S17]), Centuria Investment Holdings 85.4m, Citicorp Nominees 42.0m, JP Morgan Nominees 15.5m, then a long tail under 7m each. There are 8,721 holders. The responsible entity's directors and Centuria's two founders hold between 47,646 and 328,614 units each, unchanged through FY26 [S3].

Five years. Year-end closes were A$2.34 (2021), A$1.53 (2022), A$1.34 (2023), A$1.10 (2024) and A$1.15 (2025), and A$0.875 now (Norgate). The portfolio was A$2,018m at a 5.52% cap rate in June 2022 and A$1,842m at 7.04% in June 2026, after A$230m of sales [S2].

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Index: COF left the S&P/ASX 300 on 23 March 2026 (Norgate, data/norgate/index_changes.csv). On 20 March, the last session before, 29.8m units traded, 5.0% of the trust (Norgate). Vanguard went from 7.34% to 6.29% (16 February), 5.19% (3 March) and below 5% at 3.39% (20 March) [S19].
- Size and trading: A$523m market value and a median of A$376,558 traded a day over the last 20 sessions (Norgate).
- Register: Centuria and the Lederers hold 35.8% between them and rarely trade.
- Sector: office owners are out of favour. Total returns in 2026 to 30 October were COF -16.0%, Dexus -14.9%, Growthpoint -15.4%, Abacus -27.0% and Cromwell -24.1% (Norgate), while the RBA raised its cash rate four times, to 4.60% on 30 September [S40].
- An external manager paid on gross assets that also owns 18.9%.
- Coverage: I didn't check broker databases.

So COF is less owned by index funds than it was, but its discount to NTA is the sector's: Abacus and GDI trade at the same 0.53 times NTA (see Valuation).

What the table shows:
- Valuation dates. Eleven buildings worth A$1,102.6m (59.9%) were valued by outside valuers at 30 June 2026; seven worth A$739.6m (40.1%) carry directors' values and were last valued externally at 31 December 2025 [S1, S3]. The two groups moved differently in the June half. The eleven fell 1.67% (A$1,121.3m to A$1,102.6m) and their weighted cap rate rose 18 basis points (6.97% to 7.16%), with seven of the eleven moving out. The seven directors' values rose 0.61% (A$735.1m to A$739.6m) with not one cap rate changed [S1, S6]. Had the seven moved like the eleven, values would be A$16.9m lower, 2.8c a unit. The seven include three of the four lowest cap rates in the trust (8 Central Avenue and 57 Wyatt Street at 6.50%, Nishi at 6.64%). Every building's last external valuation is either December 2025 or June 2026, which suggests each is valued externally at least once a year; the filings don't state the policy. If that holds, these seven are the ones due in December 2026.
- 818 Bourke Street lost its biggest tenant. In December 2025 it was 75.0% let, with the Commonwealth at 36% of its income and ResetData at 25%, valued at A$215.0m on 6.70% [S7]. By June 2026 the Commonwealth had gone: 60.9% let, ResetData 32% and Costa 23%, valued at A$210.0m on 7.01% [S1]. A 39% vacancy in a market where the agents put prime vacancy at 19.5% and incentives at 48% [S23] took only 2.3% off the value. This is the trust's largest vacancy: 10,894 sqm and 4.5% of income [S2].
- Nishi, the third-largest building, has 66.2% of its income expiring in 2028, and the Commonwealth is 59% of it [S1].
- The largest tenants' lease ends, inferred from where each building's income expires: the Commonwealth at Nishi mostly in 2028 and at William Square mostly 2031 or later; WA Government at 144 Stirling Street in 2030 (93.4% of that building expires then); Target at Williams Landing in 2029 (74.2%); Stantec and Warner Bros. at 203 Pacific Highway in 2029 (57.6%); IAG at Colin Street in 2030 (65.9%). - Across the trust, by income: vacant 9.0%, FY27 4.7% (cut to 2.7% by leasing in the September quarter [S5]), FY28 16.9%, FY29 12.4%, FY30 17.7%, FY31 12.9%, FY32 and later 26.4% [S2].
- Two tenants are tied to the manager. Centuria Capital owns 50% of ResetData [S38], COF's fifth-largest tenant and a third of 818 Bourke Street's income, and cirque by Centuria, which carries the manager's name, takes 14% of 154 Melbourne Street [S1]. ResetData is building GPU "AI factories" and expects its first customer revenue in the second half of FY27 [S38], so it's a young tenant to have a third of a building that is already 39% empty.

What the evidence says:
- Office buildings are selling, to private and offshore buyers as well as listed ones, and big CBD towers have sold at their latest valuations. But those valuations had just been cut about 4%, and the seller gave deferred payment at 6.0% to 6.25% when three-month bank bills are 4.92% [S41]; a buyer would pay more than that to borrow, so the true price is perhaps 1% below the headline.
- Smaller metro buildings like COF's have sold from 9% below to 12.5% above book, mostly a few per cent below.
- Valuers' cap rates sit well below the agents' yields. Growthpoint's own office cap rate is 7.14% [S24], close to COF's 7.04%, even though the JLL and CBRE yields Growthpoint quotes for its fringe markets are 8.13% to 8.50% [S23]. Marking COF's buildings to those yields, and holding at book the two with no published figure, takes 9.0% off the portfolio (calculation in the Valuation section).
- Peers' cap rates at 30 June 2026: Dexus office 6.22% (up 3 basis points in the half) [S27], Abacus 6.70% [S29], GDI 6.8% [S35], Growthpoint office 7.14% [S24], Cromwell 7.20% [S33].
- Since the June valuations the 10-year government bond yield has risen from 4.73% to 5.21% (30 June to 28 October 2026) [S42], and the RBA raised its rate again on 30 September [S40].

My answer to the fact sheet's question: one building at a time, COF's buildings would probably sell close to book, more likely a few per cent below it than above. The December valuations have a known drag (the seven directors' values) and a likely one (yields up 0.48 points since June). Nothing I found supports the 25% discount to book the unit price implies, but the agents' yields say the book could be 9% too high.

### Debt and the covenants
- Facilities of A$1,012.5m, with A$832.5m drawn and A$9.2m of cash: net debt A$823.3m [S3]. After the March 2026 refinancing (five lenders, margins about 0.30 points lower, covenants unchanged) [S11], nothing falls due before FY29. Read from the bar chart in the FY26 results [S2]: A$310m falls due in FY29, A$242.5m in FY30 and A$460m in FY31. The note gives the first and last dates as 31 March 2029 and 31 March 2031 [S3]. Headroom A$180.0m [S2].
- Loan to value 44.8% against a 60% covenant; gearing 43.7%; interest cover 2.0 times against 1.75 [S2]. Interest cover was 2.8 times in FY24, 2.4 in FY25, 2.1 at December 2025 and 2.0 at June 2026 [S6, S9]. The filings don't say how the covenant measure is worked out. It is stricter than FFO earnings before interest over FFO interest, which comes to 2.4 times (A$115.7m against A$48.7m [S2]), so it probably leaves out the rent-free add-back.
- Hedges at 30 June 2026 [S3]: A$630m of swaps (75.7% of drawn debt). A$75m at 4.0% had about 0.2 years left, so it ended around September 2026, with a A$75m swaption at 4.0% on the same date that the bank could exercise. A$555m at 3.7% has an average of 1.9 years left, so it runs out around May 2028 on average. The results show average hedging of 79% in FY26, 69% in FY27 and 62% in FY28, all at about 3.7%, and show nothing for FY29, which fits the 1.7-year average hedge life [S2]. The filings give no individual swap dates.
- All-in cost of debt was 5.2% in FY26 [S2]. FY26 three-month bank bills averaged 3.91% [S41], so margins and line fees came to about 1.46%, and about 1.23% after the refinancing.

Debt doesn't stay flat, though. At FY26's cash flows and the 9.0c distribution, the trust spends about A$39m a year more than comes in (operating cash A$46.4m, less A$31.7m spent on the buildings, less A$53.8m of distributions) [S3]. With no sales, debt would be about A$950m by June 2029 and cover about 1.56 to 1.62 times even with earnings up 3% a year: a breach. Selling 154 Melbourne Street at book (A$88.25m, held for sale [S3]) and repaying debt only lifts that to about 1.61 to 1.67 times. The covenant doesn't bind inside the window, but on present settings the trust has to sell more buildings, cut the distribution towards cash earnings, or both, before the hedges are gone.

Sensitivities asked for:
- A 1% fall in values: loan to value goes from 44.8% to 45.25% and gearing from 43.66% to 44.09%. Interest cover doesn't move. Values can fall 25.3% before loan to value reaches 60%.
- A 0.5-point rise in rates: no effect on loan to value. On interest cover, today about A$277.5m is unhedged (assuming the bank didn't exercise the September swaption), so it adds A$1.39m a year (2.8% of interest) and cover falls from 2.0 to 1.94 times. Once the hedges are gone it adds A$4.16m (8.5%), and cover at flat earnings falls from 1.69 to 1.56 times in FY29.
- Breach points from 2.0 times: earnings before interest down 12.5%, or interest up 14.3% (about A$7.0m a year).

- Is it at risk? The 2.25c December quarter payment is safe: it's guided, was reaffirmed on 15 October [S5], and a cut inside the year would be a break from three years (FY24 to FY26) of paying exactly the guided amount [S9, S21, S2]. The December quarter should be declared about 4 December 2026, go ex about 30 December, record 31 December, and be paid about 29 January 2027 (last year: declared 5 December, ex 30 December 2025, paid 30 January 2026 [S13]). That is the only distribution in the window. The March quarter will be declared about 5 March 2027 but goes ex about 30 March (last year 30 March 2026 [S14]), after the resolve date, so it's not counted. The distribution reinvestment plan is suspended [S12].

### The manager and the big holders
- Fees: A$10.9m base fee in FY26, 0.55% of gross assets [S3]. The fee rises with gross assets, so every A$100m of buildings sold to repay debt or buy back units costs the manager A$0.55m a year. That's a reason the trust hasn't shrunk faster. In all, A$20.2m went to Centuria companies in FY26, A$21.7m in FY25 [S3]. Some of the property and facility fees are recovered from tenants [S3], and an internally managed REIT would have its own head-office costs, so not all of this is extra cost.
- Centuria Capital's 18.9% didn't change in FY26 [S3]. Centuria itself is under strain: its units fell 43% in 2026 to 30 October (Norgate); it raised A$300m in June 2026, partly for ResetData [S39]; its Centuria Bass credit funds are recovering money from Bathla Group, which went into voluntary administration in August 2026 [S39]; and John McBain hands over to Jason Huljich as sole chief executive on 27 November 2026 [S39]. Its group gearing is 5.1% [S39], so it isn't a forced seller, and nothing it has filed says it plans to sell its COF units.
- The Lederers have been substantial holders since at least September 2021 [S20]. They held 11.25% to 11.43% in January 2023, 12.21% in September 2023 and 13.25% by late 2023. They bought 10.9m units for A$13.2m (about A$1.21) on 30 August 2024, reaching 15.08% [S20]. Between 13 February and 20 March 2026 they bought 8,209,587 units on market for A$8.11m (average A$0.988), reaching 16.45% [S16], and about 2.7m more by 30 June, below the 1% that needs a notice (101,001,333 less 98,283,687). On 30 June 2026 all 101.0m units moved from PEJR Investments and PELSF Nominees to LOR Assets, a Lederer company, for A$92.4m (A$0.915 a unit) [S17, S18]. That is a transfer within the family, not new buying. No notice states an intention.

## Valuation
Worked in python3 -I. Units 597.337m; NTA A$990.7m, A$1.659 a unit [S3]; 1% of property value is A$18.4m, 3.08c a unit.

### Method 1: NTA marked to the sales evidence
| Step | Change in values | NTA a unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Book, 30 June 2026 | | A$1.66 |
| Mark the seven directors' values to the external trend (-2.28% on A$739.6m = -A$16.9m) | -0.92% | A$1.63 |
| Then a sales discount of 2% (the middle of the evidence above: 0% to 4% below book) | -2.92% in all | A$1.57 |
| Instead, every building at the agents' yields (two held at book; no building marked up) | -9.0% | A$1.38 |
| The fact sheet's bad case (cap rates up another 1.52 points) | -17.8% | A$1.11 |

The agents' figure is a stress test, not the central case. Growthpoint's valuers sit about a point inside the same agents' yields, and Dexus's July sales cleared at valuers' figures, not agents'.

NTA is what holders would get if the buildings were sold and the trust wound up, less selling costs (about 1.5% to 2% of values, 5c to 6c a unit). A holder who can't force that keeps paying the manager. The base fee of A$10.9m capitalised at 10 times is 18.2c a unit. Only the excess over what an internally managed REIT spends on its own head office (perhaps A$4.5m a year, which I haven't been able to check against a peer) is a true extra cost: 7.5c. On that basis Method 1 gives A$1.20 to A$1.55 a unit, central about A$1.44 (A$1.57 less 13c).

### Range
A$0.60 to A$1.44 a unit. The low end is what the cash COF actually keeps is worth at the yields the market gives listed office peers. It depends on rent-free periods staying near A$25m a year. The high end is the buildings marked to sales evidence, less the manager's cost. It depends on someone, the manager or a bidder, turning book value into cash, and nothing in the filings says that is planned. Peers at 0.53 to 0.64 times NTA put the market's pricing between the two, at A$0.85 to A$1.03.

## Why the market is wrong
I can't say that it is, within this window. At A$0.875 the units imply the buildings are worth about A$1,376m, 25% below book: a cap rate of about 9.4% and A$5,186 a square metre. That is A$522.7m for the units, plus A$823.3m of net debt, plus A$41.6m of other liabilities, less A$11.5m of other assets [S3]. No sale I found supports a discount that deep, so on the buildings alone the price looks too low.

But the market isn't pricing the buildings. It's pricing about 4c a unit a year of cash, paid out at 9c with borrowed money and sale proceeds, by a manager paid on gross assets that owns 18.9%, with interest cover heading for the covenant once the hedges end. On that cash COF yields 4.6% at A$0.875, below Abacus (5.4%), Growthpoint (6.8%) and Dexus (7.2% to 7.8%). On NTA it trades exactly where Abacus and GDI do. So the gap to NTA is real, but it's the sector's gap, and it has a cash reason. The one dated event that might close it, the February result with December valuations, is more likely to lower NTA than raise it. What would close the gap is a sale of the trust or of a large part of it, with the money returned, and nothing on the record points to that.

## What would make this wrong
"Don't buy" is wrong if the price rises well above A$0.88 by March. The likeliest ways:
1. A control move. The Lederers (16.9%, buying since at least 2021), or a buyer for Centuria's 18.9% while its parent is under pressure, would put NTA of about A$1.57 to A$1.66 in play. There's no notice or statement pointing to one.
2. Rates falling. The 10-year yield is 0.48 points above June. If it falls back, every listed office owner rises, and COF's higher gearing would make it rise more.
3. Rent-free falling faster than I assume. Each A$5m a year less lifts the cash value 12c to 16c.

And the case against buying gets stronger if December values fall more than 4.5%, ResetData struggles to pay, or the Commonwealth doesn't renew at Nishi.

Exit rule: no position is taken. If one were, it should be sold at the next open after December values come in more than 3% below June, a cut to FY27 FFO or distribution guidance, or cover reported below 1.9 times, and otherwise on 5 March 2027.

Reopen with a fresh deep dive if any of these happen: the trust announces a sale of a building or the portfolio at or above book with money returned to holders; a substantial holder goes past 20% or states an intention; or the February result shows rent-free under A$11m for the half (against A$12.5m and A$13.1m in the two FY26 halves [S45, S3]) with December values within 1% of June and the price still under A$0.90.
