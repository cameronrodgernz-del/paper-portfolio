# Kaiser Reef (ASX:KAU): deep dive (not a value pick)

Verdict: wait for a named event, the December quarter production and cash update (about 8 January 2027), read with the December quarterly report (about 30 January 2027). No card. Not a value pick (rulebook section 5a): the bad case (A$0.09) is 40% below the A$0.15 price, far outside the 10% the rule allows, and the middling case (A$0.17) is only 13% above it. As an ordinary pick the expected price is A$0.147, about 2% below the price before costs and about 9% below after them.

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk. Price at writing: A$0.15 (last trade on 6 November 2026, ASX company data fetched 7 November 2026 [D2]; Norgate's last close in the repo is A$0.155 on 30 October 2026 [D1]). Market value: A$90.8m (605,322,399 shares on issue, annual report 2026 [K1]).
Status: finished. This is a simulation and not financial advice.
Fact sheet: research/facts/KAU.md, grade A. The question it set: can Henty lift from about 22,500oz a year (its last two quarters) to its 30,000oz target at close to its FY26 cost of about A$4,250/oz?

Order of work. The brief and the fact sheet both state the A$0.15 price, so it was known before the work began. Kaiser's own price series was opened partway through, while pulling peer closes from the same Norgate file. No input to the cash flow valuation comes from Kaiser's share price: production, grade, costs and capital come from the quarterly reports, the gold price from the Perth Mint, and the peer multiples from the peers' own prices and filings.

In short: the fact sheet's A$0.265 "if it goes our way" figure is the good case, not the middling one. It needs Henty at 30,000oz a year at about A$4,200/oz all-in sustaining cost (AISC), gold near A$6,000, Maldon spending stopped, and a 6.6-year life that forgets about 8 to 13% of the gold is lost in the plant. Management plans to keep spending on Maldon (A$17m a year at the September quarter's rate), and the reserve's recoverable gold supports about six years at 30,000oz, not 6.6. Rebuilt on that basis at today's Perth Mint price (A$5,920/oz), the plan is worth A$0.24 to A$0.26 a share. At Henty's current rate it is worth A$0.09 to A$0.12, and at the FY26 rate about A$0.17 to A$0.18. Three things the filings show that the fact sheet doesn't:
1. Production fell because grade fell. Henty milled 62,000 to 69,000t every quarter, but head grade fell from 4.12g/t (September 2025) to 2.94 to 3.00g/t for three quarters running, below the 3.22g/t reserve grade [K2 to K6]. Reaching 30,000oz needs about 315,000t a year at reserve grade against about 258,000t now. Capital development, which opens new stopes, fell from 537 to 691m a quarter to 104 to 151m in the last two quarters, the opposite of February's plan to increase it [K10, K2, K3].
2. The reserve kept its size by lowering the bar. The 2026 reserve "replaced depletion" after the cut-off grade was cut from 2.0g/t to 1.5g/t and the cost inputs from A$211/t to A$197/t, while site cash costs actually ran at A$290 to A$306 per tonne milled [K8, K9, K3]. On the reserve's own inputs, a 1.5g/t cut-off needs about A$5,000/oz gold, not the A$4,000/oz stated.
3. The cash has stopped growing. Cash and bullion went from A$45.6m (31 March 2026) to A$40.0m (30 September) while about A$5.7m of gold went to repay the gold loan and Catalyst [K14, K2]. In the September quarter the group's all-in cost was A$6,034/oz against a realised A$6,067/oz [K2].

At A$0.15 the market already prices a partial recovery: about 3 times Kaiser's sustaining cash margin, between the current-rate and FY26-rate values. The December quarter is the first real test of the 30,000oz target: Henty needs about 8,100oz a quarter for the rest of FY27 to reach it, against 5,692oz in September.

## The company in plain words
Kaiser mines gold underground at Henty, near Queenstown on Tasmania's west coast, and mills it in Henty's own 300,000t a year plant. It bought Henty from Catalyst Metals on 15 May 2025 for A$21.6m cash, 118.6m shares, 3,000oz of gold paid at 50oz a month, and A$4.1m of bond repayments, a final consideration of A$55.6m [K1 note 1, K20]. Henty has produced more than 1.4Moz since 1996 [K11]. In Victoria, Kaiser owns the Maldon project: a 200,000t a year plant processing low-grade stockpile and small bought-in parcels, the Union Hill mine (an Inferred Resource of 1.31Mt at 4.44g/t for 187koz, from 2022), and an exploration program. The A1 mine has been on care and maintenance since 2025 [K1, K2]. Henty produced 25,812oz of the group's 27,766oz in FY26 [K3]. All gold is sold unhedged [K11].

Who runs it. Brad Valiukas, a mining engineer, has been managing director since 7 October 2025, when founder Jonathan Downes resigned [K32]. Steve Formica is chair. Stewart Howe is an executive director and also Head of Corporate Development at Catalyst Metals; Craig Dingley is Catalyst's nominee director [K11, K1]. Tony Muir became CFO in April 2026 [K3].

Who owns it. At 25 September 2026: Catalyst Metals 118,649,875 (19.60%), Jack Yetiv 45,000,000 (7.43%), Ragnar Metals 33,400,000 (5.52%), then nominee accounts (BNP retail 6.46%, Citicorp 5.26%, HSBC 5.17%, BNP 4.47%) and the chair's company, Steven Formica Group Holdings, 1.76%. The top 20 hold 62.59%; 3,030 holders [K1]. The company's own chart puts Yetiv at 8.1% and board and management at 2.5% at 30 September 2026 [K11]. Catalyst also holds an option to take 50% of the Maldon processing plant for A$1, exercisable from May 2030 (or earlier if Kaiser breaches the deferred gold deed) for 12 years, a pre-emptive right over the plant, and a 0.5% royalty on Henty production beyond the current mine life [K20, K1].

Some signs of an overlooked share apply (size, a concentrated register, thin trading). But the 63% fall since March lines up with real news: the A$ gold price fell from over A$7,000 to about A$5,920, and Henty's grade and production fell at the same time. It looks cheap for a reason as much as overlooked.

What held Henty at about 22,500oz:
- Grade, not tonnes. Milled tonnes have been flat at 62,000 to 69,000t a quarter (about 258,000t a year, below the 300,000t nameplate; the plant was tested at a 400,000t a year rate in late 2025 [K5]). Grade fell from 4.12g/t to about 2.95g/t. The March quarter blamed "lower-than-expected stoping grades" and a review of grade control [K4]; the June quarter said grade control was resolved but "stoping delays associated with remnant mining" pushed ounces back [K3]; in October the managing director said "Our production at Henty is not yet where we want it to be. There have been some personnel changes on site" [K12]. The first stope from the high-grade block freed up by moving the underground magazine was fired in July 2026 [K2]; Kaiser said in 2025 that the move would open about 12,800oz [K7]. September's stoping grade was still 3.1g/t.
- Not enough stopes. In February 2026 the company said the plant could take more than the mine supplies and that "over the next 12 months, there will be a strong focus on increasing aggregate development to open up more stopes" [K10]. Total lateral development fell from 924 to 1,006m a quarter (September 2025 to March 2026) to 815 to 858m in the last two quarters, and capital development, which reaches new areas, fell to 104 to 151m.
- Costs rose. Site cash cost per tonne milled was A$282 in September 2025 and A$275 to A$313 in the last four quarters (A$306 in September 2026); with flat tonnes and lower grade, site cash cost per ounce rose from A$2,383 to A$3,484 over the year [K6, K2].

What 30,000oz needs: about 315,000t a year at the 3.22g/t reserve grade and 92% recovery (30,000 x 31.1035 / (3.22 x 0.92) = 314,983t), or 340,000t at today's 2.95g/t. That is 22 to 32% more ore than the mine has delivered in any recent year, at a higher grade than the last three quarters.

### The reserve and the mine life
The 2026 statement (effective 30 June 2026): Probable Ore Reserve 1.91Mt at 3.22g/t for 198koz; Mineral Resource 4.23Mt at 3.16g/t for 429koz (Indicated 3.49Mt at 3.27g/t for 366koz, Inferred 0.74Mt at 2.61g/t for 62koz) [K8]. The reserve was set at A$4,000/oz gold, 87% recovery, 5.9% royalty and a 1.5g/t cut-off, with cost inputs of A$142/t mining, A$37/t processing and tailings and A$18/t administration, A$197/t in all [K8].

Against the October 2025 reserve (1.89Mt at 3.28g/t for 199koz) [K9]:
- The cut-off fell from 2.0g/t to 1.5g/t; minimum stope length fell from 10m to 5m and maximum stope height rose from 15m to 20m [K9, K8].
- The cost inputs fell from A$211/t (A$139 mining, A$43 processing, A$29 administration) to A$197/t, while FY26's actual site cash cost was A$290 per tonne milled (mining A$214 per tonne mined, processing A$62/t, site services A$20/t) and the September quarter's was A$306/t [K3, K2].
- The 2025 inputs give a 2.0g/t cut-off at A$4,000/oz exactly (211 / (4,000/31.1035 x 0.87 x 0.941) = 2.0). The 2026 inputs give 1.87g/t, not 1.5g/t. A 1.5g/t cut-off on the 2026 inputs needs gold at about A$4,990/oz (197 / (1.5 x 0.87 x 0.941) x 31.1035). The statement says the cut-off "has been derived from current mining and processing costs" [K8]; it may be a stope-only (variable) cost, but the figures as published don't produce it.
- About 28,900oz were milled in FY26 [K3], and the reserve went from 199koz to 198koz. The second drill rig helped; so did the lower cut-off. The reserve was not externally audited [K8].

Royalties: Tasmania charges 1.9% of net sales plus a profit-based part (4.07% in total in the September quarter, at most 5.35%); Triple Flag takes 3% and Franco-Nevada 1% [K8, K2]. The reserve assumes 5.9%. Royalties actually booked were 9.0 to 10.6% of the value of Henty's gold produced in each of the five full quarters [K2 to K6].

What is economic, reworked. The question is what grade pays for itself at actual costs. Breakeven head grade = cost per tonne / (gold price per gram x recovery x (1 - royalty)), with 92% recovery and royalties of 5.9% at A$4,000, 7.5% at A$5,000 and 9.5% at about A$6,000 (the profit-based part shrinks as the price falls). The all-in sustaining cost per tonne (site cash cost, sustaining capital and Henty's corporate share, less silver credits) over the last four quarters was A$376/t: (76.19 + 19.35 + 2.98 - 1.8) / 257,402t [K2 to K5].

So:
- At A$4,000 the reserve does not pay its all-in sustaining cost at Kaiser's actual costs, on average, before any growth capital, corporate cost or tax.
- At A$5,000 it covers sustaining cost with a thin margin: A$29 to A$65 a tonne, A$7m to A$17m a year on 258,000t, roughly what Henty spends on growth capital (A$12.6m over the last four quarters [K2 to K5]). Henty would be about cash neutral.
- At A$5,920 the average reserve grade clears all-in breakeven (2.37g/t) comfortably. But ore between the 1.5g/t cut-off and about 1.8 to 1.9g/t does not pay its own cash cost even at today's price. The statement doesn't give a grade-tonnage curve, so I can't say how many of the 198koz sit in that band.
- At higher prices more of the 429koz resource could become reserve, which is the main route to a longer life. That is upside, not something to count on: the reserve has held at about six years for the last two statements only with a lower cut-off.

Mine life. The 198koz is gold in the ground. At 87 to 92% recovery it gives 172koz to 182koz of gold poured, about 5.7 to 6.1 years at 30,000oz a year (the company says "~6 years"). After the September quarter's 67,787t of ore mined, about 1.84Mt of reserve remains; at the current 258,000t a year that is about 7.1 years, but at 2.95g/t it would yield about 162koz, not 198koz. Tailings set a limit too: Henty's dam has approval for one more 6m lift, an expansion being permitted would allow production "through to 2030", and work on capacity beyond 2030 has started [K8]. The reserve runs to about 2032.

## Valuation

### Method 1: cash flow from the mine plan
Inputs common to all cases:
- Gold flat at A$5,920 (Perth Mint, 7 November) or A$5,000. Royalties 9.5% of revenue at A$5,920 and 7.5% at A$5,000 (actual 9.0 to 10.6%; lower at lower prices because Tasmania's profit-based part shrinks).
- Site cash cost A$79m a year at today's 258,000t (the last two quarters ran at A$80m a year [K3, K2]), 55% fixed and 45% moving with tonnes.
- Sustaining capital A$19m a year (A$19.4m over the last four quarters [K2 to K5]); silver credit A$1.8m a year.
- Henty growth capital A$12m, A$12m, A$9m, A$9m, A$4m, A$4m, then A$2m: today's rate (A$13.4m a year in the September quarter) for two years for the development push, and A$10m within years 3 and 4 for tailings capacity beyond 2030 (my assumption; no cost has been published).
- Corporate cost A$5.5m a year (FY26 A$5.2m; A$5.8m a year in the September quarter [K1, K2]).
- Maldon, as management plans: net spend A$15m in year 1 and A$10m in year 2, then A$2m a year on care and maintenance; or cut back now: A$3m then A$2m a year. Maldon's assets (the Inferred Resource and the plant, half of which Catalyst can take for A$1 from 2030) counted at A$10m in both.
- Tax 30%, after A$34.7m of losses and A$50m of existing tax base spread over the life.
- Reserve left at 1 October 2026: 1.84Mt. Discount rate 10%, also 8% and 12%; mid-year discounting.
- Net free cash A$28.6m, less the Catalyst gold A$12.0m (A$10.1m at A$5,000).

### The range
On today's output and today's gold price the business is worth about 9c to 12c (Methods 1 and 3) up to about 17c (Method 2's median, before Maldon). At the FY26 rate it is worth about 17c to 20c. Only if Henty reaches 30,000oz at about A$4,200/oz and gold holds near A$5,900 is it worth 24c to 26c, and 27c to 29c with one or two more years of reserve. At A$5,000 gold everything below the plan is worth 4c to 8c. The main thing each depends on: the low end on Henty staying at about 5,700oz a quarter and Maldon spending going ahead; the middle on Henty getting back to its FY26 rate; the top on the 30,000oz plan and gold.

## Why the market is wrong
I can't say that it is. At A$0.15 the market value is A$90.8m and the EV is about A$74m (90.8 - 28.6 + 12.0): about 3.0 times the September quarter's cash margin (3.4 times at spot), A$2,962 per annual ounce. The fact sheet's A$0.265 needs the plan delivered, gold held, and Maldon spending stopped; a buyer would pay that only on evidence the market doesn't have yet, and the filings point the other way (falling grade, fewer new stopes, falling capital development, guidance that needs 8,100oz a quarter). ## What would make this wrong

What would make a buy wrong, if one were made: Henty staying near 5,700oz a quarter with costs above A$5,000/oz while Maldon spending goes on, which leaves the group cash neutral at today's gold price; and a lower gold price, since at A$5,000 everything short of the plan is worth 4c to 8c.

Exit rule, if a card is ever written: sell the whole position at the next open after a Henty quarter below 5,500oz, a quarterly AISC above A$5,500/oz, a withdrawn FY27 guidance, or an equity raising not offered to all holders; otherwise sell on or after 30 April 2027 unless a fresh deep dive renews it.
