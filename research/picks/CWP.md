Fact sheet: research/facts/CWP.md, grade A. The question it set: is FY27's A$75m (mostly presold) the top of a cycle that has already turned, so that profit falls back from FY28, or do the land bank and a peak in interest rates hold profit near this level? Value pick (rulebook section 5a, from 8 Nov 2026): it doesn't qualify at A$6.33. Both legs pass at A$6.18 or below on my central bad case, and at about A$5.97 or below (A$5.95 used as the trigger) if the bad case is set at the 0.70 times book that weaker developers trade on today.

Order of work. The fact sheet shows A$6.14 and the AGM slides carry a share price chart, so the price was in view before this file was started. The filings, the cycle rebuild and the valuation methods below use Cedar Woods' own past year-end multiples and its peers' prices, never its current price. > In short: the fact sheet's A$9.15 is what a buyer of the whole company might pay, not what the market pays a minority holder in a downturn. A$9.15 is 1.44 times book and 10.4 times FY27 profit, which is about where Kroll put its control value for Peet (1.38 to 1.52 times book, 9.3 to 10.2 times FY26 profit). On a trading basis Cedar Woods is worth about A$5.60 to A$6.65 a share (mid-cycle profit of about A$48m at about 10 times, or its long-run 0.97 times book), and the price sits inside that. FY27's A$75m is safe enough, but my rebuild has FY28 profit falling to about A$43m (range A$21m to A$63m): FY28 is about 70% presold, but Q1 FY27 brought only about A$53m of net new sales, a third of FY26's quarterly average. Nothing scheduled before May 2027 is likely to close the gap between the trading value and the control value. Net debt and covenants are not the problem. No bid is in sight: the Chair and Deputy Chair hold 22%, and Peet's own bid will probably fall away.

## The company in plain words
Cedar Woods buys land, gets it rezoned and approved, builds roads and services, and sells house lots, townhouses, apartments and some strata offices and office warehouses. It has 36 projects in Western Australia, Victoria, Queensland and South Australia, with a pipeline of 9,651 lots, homes and offices at 30 June 2026: 61% land, about 30% apartments, 8% townhouses and 1% commercial [C2]. It makes money when a buyer settles: revenue and profit are booked at settlement, often a year or more after the sale contract (a "presale") is signed. It sells mostly to first home buyers and owner-occupiers (70% owner-occupiers in presales at June 2026) [C2]. It partners some projects with Tokyo Gas Real Estate (Verde, Subiaco, 51% Cedar Woods) and QIC (Robina Quarter, 50%) [C2].

Who runs it: Nathan Blackburne, Managing Director since September 2017 [C1]. The Chair, William Hames, has been a director since 23 March 1990 and chairs the architecture firm Hames Sharley, which the company engages for planning and design work on commercial terms (A$29,695 owing at June 2026) [C1]. Robert Brown is Deputy Chair [C1].

Who owns it [C1, C20]: the directors' report gives the Chair's relevant interest as 11,260,552 shares (13.2% of 85.48m) and the Deputy Chair's as 7,618,633 (8.9%), together 22.1%. The annual report's substantial holder list, which uses the percentages in each holder's last notice, also names Wilson Asset Management 6,617,816 (7.78%) and Dimensional 4,256,323 (5.00%). The top 20 hold 66.64%; there are 3,988 holders. No filing since 2024 names any Fitzhardinge interest; the founding and board interests in the filings are Hames and Brown.

## Valuation
Shares 85.48m [C21]. Equity A$544.5m (A$6.37 a share; the company's NTA figure is A$6.35) [C1]. Arithmetic in python3 -I (scratchpad scripts/val.py).

### Method 1: earnings over the cycle, at the market's own multiples (a minority holder's value)
Developer profits swing with the housing cycle, so I value mid-cycle profit, not FY27's.
- Mid-cycle profit: A$41m / A$48m / A$54m (FY23 to FY29 core average on my low, middle and high years).
- Multiple: Cedar Woods' own year-end market value over mid-cycle profit of about A$45m to A$50m was 6.7, 9.2, 8.7, 12.2 and 11.8 times for FY22 to FY26 [C1], an average of 9.7. Peet's undisturbed market value (A$795.9m on 9 July 2026 [P1]) was 13.6 times its FY25 profit of A$58.5m and 9.8 times the average of FY25 and FY26 [P3]. I use 8, 10 and 12 times.
- Value: A$41m x 8 = A$330m (A$3.86, a stress case); A$48m x 10 = A$481m (A$5.63); A$54m x 12 = A$644m (A$7.54).
- Cross-check on Kroll's basis (EV over four-year average EBITDA, about A$82.4m for Cedar Woods, net debt A$157.6m [P1]): peers excluding Cedar Woods trade at 6.6 (Finbar), 8.8 (Winton) and 12.0 (Peet undisturbed) times, median 8.8: A$567m, or A$6.64 a share.
- Method 1 range: A$5.63 to A$6.64 in the middle, A$3.86 to A$7.54 at the edges.

### Method 2: the land bank (what the assets are worth)
- Book: A$6.37 a share. Cedar Woods has traded between 0.72 and 1.20 times book at year-ends since FY22 (0.72, 0.97, 0.85, 1.20, 1.09; average 0.97) [C1]. 0.97 times December 2026 book (about A$6.65, after H1 profit of about A$45m and the A$21.4m final dividend) is A$6.45.
- Book plus the after-tax gain on older land (section 3): A$7.06 / A$7.78 / A$8.73 a share at 1.3 / 1.6 / 2.0 times its cost. This is roughly what the land would fetch if sold to other developers over time. It ignores the cost of the platform needed to sell it and the waiting time.
- Project cash flows on Kroll's method (control basis): about A$4.6bn of revenue over ten years from the existing pipeline (9,330 lots, partners' shares excluded, at FY26's average of about A$470,000 a settlement, plus modest price growth) [C3, C2], gross margin 28%, overheads A$52m a year, 30% tax, the conditional land paid, discounted at 11.5% (Kroll's Peet range 11.0% to 12.2%), less net bank debt and vendor debts: A$7.23 a share. With a 25% margin A$6.53; 30%, A$7.70; twelve years instead of ten, A$6.61; 13% discount rate, A$6.79.
- Control cross-check: Kroll's value for Peet implies 11.2 to 12.1 times four-year average EBITDA, 7.6 to 8.2 times FY26 EBITDA and 1.38 to 1.52 times book [P1]. For Cedar Woods (four-year EBITDA about A$82.4m, FY26 about A$110.8m, both derived from Kroll's own multiples for it) those give A$8.95 to A$9.82, A$8.00 to A$8.79 and A$8.79 to A$9.68. These are what a buyer paying full price for a well-run developer might pay, and they are where the fact sheet's A$9.15 sits.

### The range
- What a minority holder can expect the market to pay, through the cycle: A$5.60 to A$6.65, central about A$6.15 (Method 1 middle, 0.97 times book). It depends on FY28 and FY29 profit and on whether the market goes back to paying about book for developers.
- What the business and its land are worth to a buyer of the whole company: A$7.00 to A$8.75, central about A$7.50 (Method 2). It depends on the older land being worth well above cost and on margins holding near 28%.
- Bid-level value: A$8.00 to A$9.80 (Peet's control multiples), which takes in the fact sheet's A$9.15. No bidder, approach or route to it is in view (see below).

## The players and what the rules allow (special situations only)
Not a special situation, so left out. For the record, the brief asked whether the Peet bid makes Cedar Woods a likely target. Not inside six months, on the filings:
- The board-related 22.1% would be about 39% of the votes at a typical meeting, enough to block a scheme (which needs 75% of votes cast) unless the founders support it. The board turned away a A$5.05 approach at a 10% premium in March 2011 (secondary source: Real Estate Source, 23 March 2011).
- The Peet bid is itself likely to fail, and AVID (the buyer of Villa World and AVJennings) is busy bidding for Ingenia. Ingenia wanted Peet's large masterplanned land for land lease conversion (5,000 to 7,000 lots) [P1]; Cedar Woods has a smaller land bank, 30% of it apartments.
- Management says sites are coming to market "on reasonable terms" and some are distressed [C5], so developers can buy land without paying a premium for a listed company.
- No substantial holder notice from a developer or financial buyer has been filed. Wilson Asset Management (7.78%) is the one holder that could press for a sale; it hasn't said so.
I put the chance of an approach being announced before May 2027 at about 3%, inside the good case below.

## Why the market is wrong
On what can be shown before May 2027, it isn't. At A$6.33 the market pays 7.2 times FY27 guidance, 8.2 times FY26, about 13 times my middle FY28 (A$43m) and about 11 times mid-cycle profit (A$48m), at 1.00 times June book and 0.95 times December book. That is in line with the company's own history (0.97 times book on average) and with Peet standalone (1.01 times book, 6.8 times its record FY26).

The gap to the A$7.50 central asset value is real, but only a buyer of the whole company, or years of selling lots from cheap old land, would close it. Neither is scheduled. The fact sheet's A$9.15 is a control value: 10.4 times FY27 applies a through-cycle multiple to the top of the cycle (it is 18 times my middle FY28).

## What would make this wrong
"Wait" is wrong if the price rises well above the A$6.61 middling value without us. The two likeliest ways:
1. A fast sales recovery. Some of Q1's fall was self-inflicted (fewer releases, less marketing), Peet's sales fell only a third, and the company reports "signs of improvement in Q2 to date" [C4, C5]. If Q2 and Q3 gross sales are back above 300 a quarter and FY28 holds near A$60m, the market could pay 1.1 to 1.2 times book (A$7.30 to A$8.00) by mid-2027.
2. A bid. Cedar Woods' bid-level value (A$8.00 to A$9.80 on Peet's control multiples) is 26% to 55% above the price, buyers of listed developers exist (AVID twice, Warburg Pincus, Ingenia), and Kroll has named it the closest listed company to Peet. It needs the Chair and Deputy Chair to agree.

What would make a value-pick entry wrong, if the price falls to the reopen level:
1. FY28 falls further than my low case: sales stay near Q1's rate into 2027, buyers walk away from presold apartments when valuations fall, and margins drop with price cuts. Profit near FY24's A$24m, with the stock at 0.70 times book (about A$4.80).
2. Write-downs or a discounted share issue: land bought near the top (Fairfield VIC, Kealba, Mount Barker; about A$130m) written down, or new shares sold to fund more land once headroom falls below about A$50m.

Reopen if:
- the price closes at or below A$5.95 with FY27 guidance intact and Q2 sales no worse than Q1: send to the red team as a value pick (resolve about November 2028, events: the H1 FY27, FY27, H1 FY28 and FY28 results);
