# Servcorp Limited (ASX:SRV): deep dive

Written 2026-11-01 by the research desk. Price at writing: A$5.19 (close on 30 October 2026, Norgate, data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz; the ASX's own feed showed A$5.20 for 29 October [S9]). Market value: A$528.6m (shares on issue 101,855,088, ASX company data [S9]).
Status: finished. This is a simulation and not financial advice.

> Read the red team first: research/red_team/SRV.md (verdict: don't buy). It found these points here wrong or missing: FY26 guidance was raised once, not twice; the A$6.1m is mostly recurring losses on new floors, not impairments; the "missing A$20m" does not add up; the founder's buying has run since April 2026 at higher prices, so it is not new; and the deep dive leaves out the roughly 40 Gulf and Lebanon floors exposed to the Middle East war and the chairman's retirement at the AGM. Where the two disagree, the red team's figures stand until a new deep dive checks them.

## The company in plain words
Servcorp rents out serviced offices, coworking desks and "virtual offices" (an address, phone answering and meeting rooms) on high floors of prime towers. It leases whole floors from landlords on long terms, fits them out, and sells space and services by the month. At 30 June 2026 it ran 137 floors in 38 cities across 19 countries [S1, pp. 10-11]. The money comes from membership and office fees plus services (IT, phones, secretarial). Its biggest profit region is Europe and the Middle East (mature segment profit A$50.0m in FY26, against A$30.9m for North Asia and Australia, New Zealand and South East Asia together), then North Asia (mostly Japan), Australia and New Zealand with South East Asia, and a small USA business [S2].

The founder, Alf Moufarrige AO, is Managing Director and CEO [S4]. He and his companies (Sovori Pty Ltd and Omnioffices Pty Ltd) held 547,436 shares directly and 53,720,065 indirectly after his last notice, 54,267,501 in all, or 53.3% of the shares [S7]. I could not read the top 20 and substantial holder pages of the 2026 annual report (the copy the ASX serves ends before them), and there were no substantial holder notices filed in 2026 [S8], so beyond Mr Moufarrige the register below 5% is not known from primary sources.

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Index: Servcorp left the S&P/ASX 300 on 18 March 2019 and has not been back (Norgate index history, data/norgate/index_changes.csv). Index funds and most benchmarked funds don't have to own it.
- Register: Mr Moufarrige holds 53.3% [S7], so the free float is about 47%, roughly A$248m at A$5.19. A controlled company caps how much an outside fund can influence or sell into.
- Liquidity: the median day's trading over the last 60 sessions was about A$428,000 (Norgate). A fund wanting A$20m would need most of a year.
- Franking: dividends are only 10% franked [S1, S3], which makes the 6.5% yield less useful to Australian income funds than it looks.
- Sector: serviced offices are an out-of-favour corner of commercial property, and the company's shares fell 36% from the 52-week high of A$8.16 (January 2026) to A$5.19 while the company reported a record year (Norgate; [S1]).
- Coverage: I found no broker research referenced in the company's materials. I did not check broker databases, so treat "little coverage" as likely rather than proven.

## What the filings show
Profit and guidance
- FY26 underlying net profit before non-cash impairment and tax (the company's "underlying NPBIT") was A$87.0m, up 26% on A$69.1m; statutory profit before tax was A$80.9m and after tax A$65.6m [S1, S2]. Income tax was A$15.3m, an effective rate of 18.9% [S2].
- Management has beaten its own guidance this year. It started FY26 guiding to A$72m to A$76m, raised that to A$80m to A$84m on 21 January 2026 [S5], and delivered A$87.0m [S1].
- The first half was the stronger half. H1 FY26 underlying NPBIT was A$47.0m [S3], so the second half was about A$40.0m (A$87.0m less A$47.0m). That slowdown is a fair reason for caution, and it matters for the February result (see below).
- FY27 guidance: underlying NPBIT "between $85.0 million and $90.0 million", "subject to no worsening near-term economic conditions globally", underlying free cash of no less than A$105.0m, and dividends of 34 cents a share [S1, pp. 06-09; S2, p. 14]. The CEO calls FY27 "largely ... a year of consolidation as we complete six new locations" [S1, pp. 08-09].

Cash, leases and the balance sheet
- No external debt [S1, p. 07]. Cash and investments were A$153.1m at 30 June 2026 (FY25 A$144.4m), of which cash and cash equivalents A$137.8m [S1, S2].
- Leases are the real liability: A$337.4m at 30 June 2026 (A$89.9m current, A$247.5m non-current), down from A$363.2m [S2]. Cash rent paid against those leases was A$91.8m in FY26 [S2]. Bank guarantees and letters of credit, mainly for leases, were A$30.3m at 31 December 2025 [S4].
- Net assets were A$255.0m [S1]; net tangible assets were A$2.32 a share at 31 December 2025 [S3]. Book value says little about a business like this, whose value is in its lease book and customers.
- "Free cash" is the company's own measure: net operating cash flow before tax, less cash rent paid [S2, p. 15]. FY26 net operating cash flow was A$176.6m; adding back A$15.3m of tax and taking off A$91.8m of cash rent gives free cash of A$99.8m, and A$101.9m "underlying" after timing items [S2, p. 7]. It is before tax and before capital spending, so it overstates what is left for owners.
- Capital spending (net additions to property, plant and equipment) was about A$44.8m in FY26 against A$28.7m in FY25 [S2]. Dividends paid were A$29.9m and option exercises brought in A$4.1m [S2]. Cash and investments rose only A$8.7m in the year, so about A$20m more went somewhere: the most likely use is 333 Sussex Street, Sydney, "a flagship property acquired in FY26 ... currently under renovation" [S2, p. 3], described in the annual report as the "first owned real estate venture" with real estate assets of "at least $20 million" [S1, p. 08]. The price paid is not disclosed in what I could read. This is an inference, not a reported figure.
- Owner cash flow, my own measure (free cash less tax less capital spending): FY26 A$101.9m less A$15.3m less A$44.8m is about A$41.9m, after a year of heavy fit-out spending and before the property purchase.

Capital raisings
- None. The only new shares in 2026 came from employee and director option exercises (the "Application for quotation of securities" notices) [S8]. The company bought back no shares in FY26 [S1].

Something a quick reader would miss
- Free cash is a pre-tax, pre-capital-spending number, and the "underlying" profit leaves out non-cash impairments of floors (about A$6.1m in FY26: A$87.0m less A$80.9m) [S1]. Floor impairments recur in this business, so the statutory profit is the safer base.
- Trading volume rose sharply in September and October 2026: the median day was 29,000 to 51,000 shares in each month from April to August, then 75,600 in September and 96,700 in October, with 593,004 and 600,442 shares on 16 and 18 September (the ex-dividend date was 16 September) (Norgate). Somebody has been selling while the controlling holder buys. There is no substantial holder notice to name the seller [S8].

## Valuation
Two methods, both on the company's own guidance for FY27, plus a cross-check. Shares: 101,855,088 [S9].

Cash: the company holds A$153.1m with no debt [S1]. Some of that backs lease guarantees and customer deposits, so I count only A$100m as surplus in the central case, and show the full figure as the high end.

Method 1: price to after-tax earnings, against Servcorp's own trading range
- FY27 underlying NPBIT midpoint A$87.5m [S1]. After tax at FY26's 18.9% rate: A$87.5m x (1 - 0.189) = A$71.0m, or 69.7 cents a share.
- At A$5.19 that is 7.4 times earnings, and 6.0 times after taking off A$100m of surplus cash (5.3 times after taking off all A$153.1m).
- Servcorp's own range over the last year: A$8.16 high and A$5.13 low (Norgate), which is 12.4 and 7.8 times FY26 basic EPS of 66.0 cents. At the end of calendar 2025 it closed at A$6.80, 12.6 times FY25 EPS of 53.8 cents.
- There is no close listed peer. IWG (Regus, London) carries heavy debt and so isn't comparable on equity multiples; I use Servcorp's own history instead.
- Value at 7, 8.5 and 10 times after-tax earnings, plus A$100m of cash: 7 x A$71.0m + A$100m = A$596.9m, A$5.86 a share; 8.5 x gives A$6.91; 10 x gives A$7.95. With all A$153.1m of cash, add 52 cents a share.

Method 2: owner cash flow, valued as a growing stream
- FY27 owner cash = underlying free cash guided at no less than A$105.0m [S1], less tax of about A$16.5m (18.9% of A$87.5m), less capital spending of A$35m (between FY25's A$28.7m and FY26's A$44.8m [S2]) = about A$53.5m.
- Value = A$53.5m x (1 + growth) / (discount rate - growth), plus A$100m surplus cash:
  - 11% discount, 2% growth: A$53.5m x 1.02 / 0.09 = A$606m; plus A$100m = A$706m, A$6.93 a share.
  - 12% discount, 1% growth: A$491m plus A$100m, A$5.80 a share.
  - 10% discount, 3% growth: A$787m plus A$100m, A$8.71 a share.
- Read backwards: at A$5.19 the market pays A$428.6m for the business after A$100m of cash. At 11% and 2% growth that is a stream of about A$37.8m a year, 29% below the A$53.5m the guidance points to. FY26's owner cash, in a year of heavy fit-out spending, was about A$41.9m (see above), so the market is pricing roughly "FY26's cash after a bad year of spending, with no growth".

Cross-check: dividends
- The guided 34 cents is a 6.6% yield at A$5.19 [S1]. At the 2025 calendar-year close the FY25 dividend of 28 cents was a 4.1% yield. A 5% yield on 34 cents is A$6.80.

Range: A$5.80 to A$8.70 a share, central about A$6.90. The single input that moves it most is whether free cash holds near A$105m, which depends on occupancy across 137 floors whose rents are fixed. A 10% shortfall in free cash (A$10.5m) takes about A$1.00 a share off the central value.

## Why the market is wrong
1. The price treats a flat year as the start of a decline. FY27 guidance is roughly level with a record FY26, from a management team that raised its FY26 guidance twice and then beat it [S5, S1]. At about 6 times after-tax earnings net of surplus cash, the price assumes owner cash about 29% below what the guidance implies.
2. The person who knows the business best is buying, in size and above the market: A$1.62m of shares at A$5.60 to A$6.21 since 9 September, including an off-market block at a premium, after exercising A$3.35m of options [S11-S15]. He already owns 53%.

What should change it: the FY27 interim result in February 2027 will show whether the half is tracking the A$85m to A$90m range, and will carry the interim dividend (17 cents if the 34-cent guidance holds) [S1]. The AGM on 12 November is a smaller test: the company may restate guidance there, but the notice of meeting doesn't promise it [S6].

## What would make this wrong
1. The second half of FY26 (about A$40.0m against A$47.0m in the first half) was the start of a downturn rather than noise: new floors opening in FY27 carry rent from day one, so a slow fill hits profit at once. The interim result will be compared with A$47.0m and could look like a fall even if it is on guidance.
2. A stronger Australian dollar. North Asia revenue already fell on a weaker yen [S2]. Guidance assumes "currencies remaining constant" [S1].
3. Capital drifting into property. 333 Sussex Street is the "first owned real estate venture" [S1]; if more cash goes into buildings rather than back to owners, the surplus cash I count may not be surplus.
4. Key person: the business is run by its founder and 53% owner; nothing on succession is public [S6].

Exit rule: sell the whole position at the next open after any of: FY27 guidance cut or withdrawn; H1 FY27 underlying NPBIT below A$41m; Mr Moufarrige selling shares (other than to pay tax on options). Otherwise sell on or after 1 March 2027 unless a fresh deep dive and card renew the position. A takeover offer, including from the controlling holder, is a fresh decision.
