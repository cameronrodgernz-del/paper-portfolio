# MGX Resources Limited (ASX:MGX): deep dive, the case for buying

Written 2026-11-09 by Claude for the one-company-at-a-time process (rulebook section 5a, docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md). This is the case for buying.
Price at writing: A$0.295 (last sale, ASX company data, read 9 November 2026) [S12]. Market value: about A$348.2m (1,180,428,735 shares on issue [S13]). Cash and investments A$408.7m at 30 September 2026, about A$0.346 a share, and no borrowings [S1].
Status: finished. Wait: not a value pick at A$0.295 (see "Where it lands"). This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Order of work: chosen on 9 November 2026 from the re-ranked weekly sweep, as a company priced below its cash. The sweep's "a substantial holder left" tag was wrong: the notice was MGX's own, leaving a stake in Maronan Metals [S14]. The sweep now skips notices like that. The bad case was roughed out from the filings first, at about A$0.22, inside the 30% limit, so the work went on to the evidence outside the filings.

## Where it lands
Wait. MGX is a pile of cash with half a gold project attached, and the market values the whole company at 0.85 times the cash. That puts MGX's half of the Central Tanami gold project at about minus A$60m. Tanami Gold (ASX:TAM) owns the other half, and the market values that identical half at about plus A$58m.

The gap is real, but it isn't new and it isn't forced. At quarter ends since late 2024, MGX has traded between 0.68 and 1.08 times its cash. The people who decide what happens to the cash also control the partner on the other side of the project. And the chief executive said in July that the project won't need all the cash, which leaves room for "a range of other things".

On my numbers:
- the bad case is about A$0.20, 31.5% below the price, just outside the 30% limit;
- the middling case is about +14.6% to August 2028, just under the 15% floor;
- the expected gain after costs is about +3%, against the 12.9% minimum.

It qualifies at about A$0.255, below the June 2025 low of A$0.26. A buyback or a capital return would change the answer, because it would lift the bad case. The board let MGX's buyback lapse in February 2026 and said it might bring it back. The AGM is on 18 November.

No case against has been written, because the case for doesn't pass (process step 4, as changed on 9 November).

## What the price assumes
What MGX holds: A$408.7m of cash and investments at 30 September 2026, and no bank debt [S1]. At 30 June the total was A$412.1m [S2]:
- A$332.2m of cash and term deposits;
- about A$80m of investments: A$20.4m of corporate bonds, A$37.7m of listed shares, A$19.0m of Fenix Resources shares and options, A$2.4m of convertible notes and A$0.5m of unlisted shares.

The other assets and liabilities roughly cancel out. Koolan Island still carried about A$28m of rehabilitation provisions after the September quarter's A$7.7m of work. Crestlink, the buyer of Koolan, takes over about A$30m of rehabilitation and pays MGX at least A$20.2m over five years, with settlement targeted for late 2026 or early 2027 [S6]. Payables and staff entitlements were about A$23m at June [S2].

So the price of A$0.295 is about 0.85 times the cash and investments of A$0.346 a share. It gives nothing for MGX's half of Central Tanami, which it paid A$59.1m for in February 2026, including costs and adjustments [S4]. In effect it gives about minus A$60m.

The price assumes either that a good part of the cash will be spent for less than it's worth, or that it will stay out of shareholders' reach. That's consistent with how the market has priced MGX since late 2024 (see the test outside the filings), and with the process rule since 8 November: count cash a controlling holder keeps out of reach only through a buyback, capital return or bid [S24].

## The one thing the market is missing
Stated as the rulebook asks: the market prices MGX's half of Central Tanami at below zero, while the other half trades at about A$58m inside Tanami Gold. And Tanami Gold can't fund its half of a mine build from its own cash, so the joint venture has to be settled by 2028: either Tanami Gold is diluted in MGX's favour, or the two combine.

It doesn't clear, because the people who would settle it sit on both sides (below), and nothing makes them settle it on terms that suit MGX's minority holders.

## The test outside the filings
Done on 9 November 2026, under docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md step 2. The questions the filings can't answer:
1. Is the discount to cash new, a window where someone was made to sell, or is it MGX's normal state?
2. What does the market pay for the other half of the project?
3. Who decides how the cash is used, and what have they said?
4. How much will the project cost MGX before August 2028, and can the partner pay its share?
5. Are insiders buying?

   - Before 2026 the cash sat beside a working iron ore mine with large closure costs ahead, so those years aren't exactly like today. Writers at the time put the discount down to the closure costs and to doubts that management would spend the cash well [S21]. The closure costs are now mostly gone to Crestlink, yet the discount is about where it was.
   - No forced seller: MGX left the S&P/ASX 300 in March 2022 [S14]. No holder of MGX has lodged a substantial holding notice in 2025 or 2026; the notices in its feed are its own, about stakes it holds in other companies [S10]. Short positions are 0.05% [S13].
   - The fall from A$0.38 at the end of August to A$0.295 follows gold:

     - Tanami Gold fell by the same 21% over the same weeks, from A$0.071 to A$0.056 [S14];
     - so the market is pricing MGX like a gold developer, even though about 85% of its value is cash.
2. The other half of the project is valued at about A$58m.
   - Tanami Gold's market value is about A$141m [S12]. At 30 September it held A$63.3m of cash, Northern Star shares worth A$12.4m and half of the joint venture's A$13.6m of cash [S15]. That leaves about A$58m for its half of Central Tanami, or about A$41 an ounce of its 1.4Moz share.
   - Northern Star sold the half MGX now owns for A$50m, agreed in July 2025 [S7].
   - On Tanami Gold's pricing, MGX would be worth about A$466m, or A$0.395 a share, 34% above today's price.
3. The cash is controlled by a group that also controls the partner.
   - APAC Resources (HKEX:1104), part of Lee Seng Hui's Allied Group, holds 452.8m MGX shares, 38.4% [S4]. Shougang, which also holds a large stake in APAC, has 13.1%, and Paradice 9.7% [S4]. The top 20 holders have 82% [S4].
   - APAC holds 46.3% of Tanami Gold, counting the Metals X stake it has an interest in [S18, S19]. Tanami Gold's chair, Arthur Dew, chairs APAC and Allied Group [S17].
   - MGX's chair, Brett Smith, is an executive director of Tanami Gold and of Metals X [S4, S17]. Lee Seng Hui chaired MGX until April 2025. Another MGX director, Alan Jones, sits on the boards of Allied Group and Sun Hung Kai [S4]. MGX's annual report notes the common major shareholder [S4]. MGX's related-party note covers APAC's iron ore offtake, not the joint venture [S4].
   - Capital returns: MGX bought back 38.8m shares at an average A$0.313 in late 2024 and early 2025. With the share price higher and the project bought, the board let the buyback lapse in February 2026 and said it might bring it back later [S10]. No dividend was paid in FY25 or FY26 [S2].
   - What management has said: in July 2026 the chief executive, Peter Kerr, said the market gave no value to the gold project, that MGX won't need all its cash for Central Tanami, and that the rest opens up "a range of other things" [S20]. MGX's quarterlies say the same: it "continues to examine and invest in opportunities" [S1, S3].
4. The project needs money before 2028, and Tanami Gold can only pay for the first stage.
   - The joint venture's current programme is the Groundrush exploration decline (a A$38m Macmahon contract over about 14 months from September 2026), drilling, camp repairs and site infrastructure [S8]. Tanami Gold raised A$70.5m at A$0.06 in May 2026 for its half of that: A$37.2m for the decline, A$5.5m for drilling, A$11.4m for the camp and A$11.6m for other infrastructure [S16]. The underwriter took up A$20.2m of shortfall [S16]. So the programme is about A$130m in all, about A$65m each.
   - MGX's half cost A$14.7m in the September quarter [S1].
   - No study, capital cost or reserve has been published. Indicative scoping work points to a 1.5Mtpa plant producing about 100,000 to 120,000oz a year for about ten years [S20]. The old 1.2Mtpa plant has been idle since 2005 and may be replaced [S9]. The target is a development decision in 2027 and production within three years [S8, S20].
   - Tanami Gold's own offer document says it may not be able to fund its share, that this could dilute its interest in the joint venture, and that there's no assurance of "satisfactory arrangements" with MGX for the build [S16]. It had A$63.3m of cash at September [S15]. Its talks with Prodigy Gold about treating Prodigy's ore are on hold [S15].
   - So a build will force a choice: Tanami Gold raises a large sum (APAC paying 46% of it), it is diluted, or the two companies combine. A merger at today's prices would shift value from MGX holders to Tanami Gold holders unless the whole project were worth more than about A$270m (my working).
5. Directors' buying is token. Brett Smith bought 170,000 shares at A$0.35 and Paul Dougas 30,002 at A$0.34 in October 2025; Evian Delfabbro bought 62,998 at A$0.3175 in October 2024. That's about A$90,000 in all [S23].

What this changes: it moves the bad case down. The worse case uses the June 2025 low of 0.68 times cash, and the chief executive's comment raises the chance that cash goes on something other than Central Tanami. The Tanami Gold comparison supports the middling case, but only once the project is settled, and that's in the hands of a group with a larger share of Tanami Gold than of MGX.

## The company in plain words
MGX was Mount Gibson Iron:
- it mined iron ore on Koolan Island, off the Kimberley coast, from 2007;
- a rockfall in the Main Pit in October 2025 ended mining, and the last low-grade ore shipped in July 2026 [S1, S3];
- the island goes to Crestlink, which plans a logistics, aviation and marine base [S6].

It changed its name in December 2025 to mark the move into gold and base metals [S10]. What's left is:
- about A$409m of cash and investments;
- a 50% share of the Central Tanami Project Joint Venture in the Northern Territory, bought from Northern Star in February 2026. The joint venture has 31Mt at 2.8g/t for 2.8Moz of gold resources, of which Groundrush is 11Mt at 3.3g/t for 1.2Moz [S4];
- about 3,600 km² of exploration ground of its own nearby [S7];
- the Koolan rockfall insurance claim. One insurer has paid A$9m and the others are still in talks; the size of any further payment is unknown [S1].

So interest roughly pays for head office. The joint venture is the drain.

- provisions A$45.7m, of which closure costs are A$40.7m: Koolan A$35.7m, Tanami A$4.6m and Tallering Peak A$0.5m.

Notes on the middling case:
- A$150m for MGX's half is about A$107 an ounce of its 1.4Moz. That's my estimate for a project with a study and a decision. Tanami Gold's half trades at about A$41 an ounce today, with neither.
- It's less than the A$58m the market gives the project today plus the A$140m MGX would spend. That's deliberate: so far the market has not given MGX full credit for money it spends.

What the value depends on most: the discount on the cash. Each 0.1 of price-to-cash on about A$300m is worth about A$0.025 a share.

## Why the market is wrong
1. It still prices MGX as if the cash were at risk from an iron ore mine. That risk has gone to Crestlink.
2. It values MGX's half of Central Tanami below zero, while the market values the other half at about A$58m.
3. It ignores that the partner can't fund a build, which gives MGX the stronger hand when the joint venture is settled.

It's right that:
- the cash is controlled by a group that sits on both sides of the project and has shown no wish to return it;
- Koolan's last years ended in impairments of A$75.0m in FY25 and A$50.0m in FY26, so management's record with capital is mixed [S2];
- the chief executive is open to spending spare cash on other things;
- gold has fallen about 28% from its January peak.

## Outcomes and chances
Resolve date: 31 August 2028 (661 days away). No dividends are assumed.

| Outcome | What happens | Chance | Price by resolve date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Worse | Gold falls back and cash goes on a second purchase or a weak project; the market pays its June 2025 discount | 10% | A$0.173 |
| Bad | The decision slips; cash dwindles at 0.8 times, with nothing for the project | 25% | A$0.214 |
| Middling | A development decision on a study; the discount narrows to 0.9 and the project gets some credit | 45% | A$0.338 |
| Good | Cash comes back to holders, the joint venture is settled on fair terms, a bid, or strong gold | 20% | A$0.458 |

These chances were set after the evidence from outside the filings. The worse case is only 10% because MGX has said Central Tanami is its focus and has no debt. The good case is 20% because the joint venture has to be settled before a build, and the AGM gives the board a chance to bring the buyback back.

For the card, the first two combine into one bad case: 35% at A$0.202 (the chance-weighted average of A$0.173 and A$0.214). The card would read 35% at A$0.202, 45% at A$0.338 and 20% at A$0.458.

Expected price: 0.35 × 0.202 + 0.45 × 0.338 + 0.20 × 0.458 = A$0.314, or +6.6% before costs. After about 3.5% of costs it's about +3%. The costs are A$15 brokerage each way on about A$2,250, half the 1.7% spread each way, and the 0.5% currency fee. The rulebook minimum over 661 days is 12.9%, so that test fails.

The value test (rulebook section 5a):
- The bad case of A$0.202 is 31.5% below A$0.295, just outside the 30% limit.
- The middling case is +14.6%, below the 15% floor. Against a 31.5% fall it would need +47%.
- So the test fails.

It passes at about A$0.255. There:
- the bad case is 21% below;
- the middling case is +32.5% (it needs +31.2%);
- the expected gain after costs is about +20%.

A$0.255 would be MGX's lowest close since at least 2020; the low so far was A$0.26 in June 2025 [S14]. At 0.74 times today's cash a share, it would still be above June 2025's 0.68.

## What would make this wrong
- MGX buys a second project with its spare cash, at a premium or for shares issued below cash.
- MGX and Tanami Gold merge at a ratio that pays Tanami Gold's holders, where APAC's share is larger, more than its half is worth to MGX.
- Gold falls a long way, and the study comes back weak or late.
- The rest of the insurance claim comes to nothing, and the listed shareholdings (about A$57m) fall with small miners.

Exit rule, if it's ever bought: sell if MGX issues shares below its cash a share, or agrees a purchase outside Central Tanami of more than about A$50m before the development decision. A close 25% below the buy price means a review, not a sale.
