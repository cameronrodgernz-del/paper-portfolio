# Auric Mining (ASX:AWJ): deep dive (not a value pick)

Verdict: wait for a named event. First the Munda Ore Reserve, promised for October 2026 and still not lodged on 8 November (it should come by the December quarterly report, about 28 January 2027, at the latest). Then the funding package for the A$145m build, which can't come before the mining and plant approvals (mid-2027 at the earliest on the company's own six-month allowance). No card. Not a value pick (rulebook section 5a): over 24 months the bad case (A$0.14) is 40% below the A$0.235 price, against the 10% the rule allows, and the middling case (A$0.24) is 2% above it, against the 15% needed. As an ordinary pick, with the Ore Reserve as its event and 30 April 2027 as its resolve date, the expected price is A$0.2235: 4.9% below the price before costs and about 12% below after them, against a minimum of 3.4%. Watch triggers, nothing else changing: A$0.20 (ordinary pick, Ore Reserve still pending) and A$0.155 (value pick).

In short: the fact sheet's A$0.33 "if it goes our way" is the good case, not the middling one. It assumes holders keep half the project's value after funding and then adds all the cash on top. Worked through with a funding package of the usual shape (about A$60m of debt and A$90m of new shares at A$0.18), the study is worth about A$0.29 a share to today's holders at full value, and about A$0.27 by November 2028 at the multiple the market gives a new producer. A$0.33 needs the new shares sold at A$0.24 or more for A$70m, or a higher gold price. Five things the filings show that the fact sheet doesn't:
1. The value of the plan swings hard on grade. Mining, haulage and milling costs are per tonne, so 10% fewer ounces cuts the project's after-tax value at 8% from A$108m to A$60m at today's gold price. That is why the late Ore Reserve matters.
2. The timetable has already slipped. The study planned to lodge the Munda Mine Development and Closure Plan in August 2026 and all approval submissions by the September quarter, with about six months to approve [A1, A11]. On 15 October its preparation was "well advanced" [A2]. Approvals in "early 2027" now look like mid-2027 at best, and the board will only approve the build once all the money is raised and all permits are in hand [A1].
3. There are 33.5m nil-priced performance rights (31.75m held by directors) and 18.3m options on issue [A2]. About 11.5m rights vest along the middling path (raising the build money, the 300,000oz resource, the Burbanks permits, the camp) and about 5m more at commissioning and first gold [A4 note 14]. The fact sheet's share count leaves them out.
4. The second-hand plant bought for A$1.2m doesn't lower the A$145m. The company says the study's capital estimate already assumed comparable second-hand equipment; the "A$15 to 20 million" saving is against new equipment [A2].
5. The Union Jack vote shows nothing about the question. It is A$3m of shares (6% more shares, already in the count). Its 2 December date was the company's estimate [A7]; no notice of meeting had been lodged by 8 November [A17], and a listed company must give 28 days' notice [S2], so it can't be held before about 7 December. The company must use its best endeavours to hold it by 31 December 2026 [A7].

At A$0.235 the market values Auric at about its cash plus its 301,000 ounces at what unfunded developers' ounces fetch, plus the Burbanks mill at cost (A$0.22 to A$0.25 a share). That is close to what the plan is worth to today's holders once it is paid for. The gap the fact sheet found sits in the good case.

## The company in plain words
Auric is a small Western Australian gold company. It owns the Munda gold deposit, 5km west of Widgiemooltha, and the idle Burbanks mill, 9km south of Coolgardie and about 87km by road from Munda [A1]. It bought Burbanks for A$4.4m, settling on 30 September 2025 [A10]. Its income so far came from a profit share in the Jeffreys Find joint venture (final A$2.2m received in March 2026) and from the Munda Starter Pit, whose ore was milled by others: 8,886oz in total at an all-in sustaining cost of A$2,857 an ounce, sold at an average of A$7,178 [A2, A1, A4]. Both have finished. Nothing is producing now.

The plan, from the July 2026 scoping study: one larger open pit at Munda (2.77Mt at 1.75g/t for 155,800oz, 93.1% Indicated), mined over 32 months, trucked to Burbanks rebuilt as a 600,000t a year plant, 141,300oz recovered over 56 months [A1]. Pre-production capital A$137.6m, peak funding A$145m, first gold targeted for March 2028 [A1]. In October 2026 Auric also bought Union Jack, 15km from Burbanks: an Inferred resource of 3.1Mt at 1.02g/t for 102,000oz, for A$6.6m cash, 12.5m shares at a deemed A$0.24 (subject to the vote), up to A$7m of milestone payments and a 1% gross royalty [A5, A6]. Group resources are now 7.5Mt at 1.25g/t for 301,000oz [A6].

Who runs it. Steven Morris chairs. Mark English, the founding managing director, becomes Executive and Finance Director on 1 December 2026, when Gareth Solly, the CEO since 18 May 2026 and formerly founding managing director of Black Cat Syndicate, takes over as managing director [A11]. John Utley is technical director [A4].

### Method 1: the project's cash flow, then the cost of paying for it
Built from the study's yearly table [A1 Table 10]: revenue is ounces times the gold price, royalties scale with revenue, operating costs as stated (they don't change with the gold price). I add what the study leaves out: head office at A$5m a year (the September quarter's staff and administration were A$1.26m [A3]), during the build and the five operating years; tax at 30% after depreciating the A$137.6m of capital and A$26.3m of sustaining capital over the ounces produced; the A$2.2m of pre-development spending. Capital and the A$8.1m of net working capital are spent through 2027 (mid-point t = 0.75 years); operating year i is discounted at t = 1.6 + i; working capital comes back at the end. The model reproduces the study's EBITDA (A$437.3m against A$436.9m).

## Why the market is wrong
I can't say that it is. At A$0.235 the market pays for the cash, the ounces at what peers' ounces fetch, the mill at cost and a little of the plan, which is about what the plan is worth to today's holders once the A$145m is raised on normal terms (A$0.27 by late 2028 at the market's multiple). The price fell to its 2026 low the day the funding need was published and hasn't recovered, which is the market doing this arithmetic. There is no trade until either the funding terms turn out better than normal or the price falls to where the ounces and cash alone hold it up.

The chances. Small developers that must raise several times their own value usually do it at a discount, and Auric has already missed two of its own dates this year (approval submissions and the reserve). Gold has fallen from above A$7,000 in March to about A$5,920 [M1], and a 10% move either way in six months is ordinary. Against that, the Starter Pit outperformed its model and the new managing director built Black Cat Syndicate into a producer, which should help the raise.

Round-trip costs at our size (about A$2,015, the 2.5% cap of NZ$2,500 at NZ$1 = A$0.806): a spread of about 3.5% (the 20-session average high-low range is 3.46% [D1]; one tick of A$0.005 is 2.1% of the price; the quoted spread on 6 November was A$0.23 to A$0.245, 6.3% [D2]), brokerage at the A$15 cap, 0.74% each way, unless the monthly plan covers it, 0.5% currency conversion each way, and a size cost of 0.78% each way (4.19% daily volatility x the square root of A$2,015 over the 20-session average of A$58,088). About 7.5% for the round trip.

Against the minimum (cash rate 2.13% plus 5% a year):

- Value pick, 730 days to 8 November 2028: minimum 14.3%; expected gain after costs about -0.3%. Not a case.
A quarter-Kelly stake on either set is zero.

What the events must show to reopen the case:
1. The Ore Reserve. It supports the plan if it holds at least about 145,000oz Probable (the Indicated part of the pit) at 1.7g/t or better, a strip ratio of 11 to 1 or less, a reserve gold price of A$5,500 or less, and pre-production capital of A$145m or less. That lifts the six-month middling to about A$0.23 and the ordinary-pick trigger to about A$0.215. A reserve under about 130,000oz, a grade under 1.6g/t or capital over A$160m closes the case: each 10% of ounces lost takes about A$48m off the project value, and the share falls back to the ounces-and-cash floor.
2. The funding package. It answers the question if new equity is A$70m or less at A$0.22 or more (or A$50m or less at A$0.18 or more), with the rest from debt, a prepayment or a royalty. On the grid that is A$0.32 or more by late 2028, at least 36% above today's price, and it would be worth re-running as an ordinary pick with first gold as the event. Equity of A$110m or more, or a placement at A$0.16 or less, closes it: the grid gives A$0.23 or less even if the mill is built on time.
3. Or the price falls to the triggers above with nothing else changed.

## What would make this wrong
The view here is that the price is about fair. What would make that wrong:
1. Funding on better terms than normal. A large debt facility, a gold prepayment, or a strategic partner who pays a premium for a stake in the only mill nearby would leave holders with much more than the grid's middling row. The study itself raises "a sale, partial sale or joint venture" [A1], and a nearby mill owner could also bid for Munda's ounces to avoid the A$138m rebuild. Nothing in the filings points to either.
2. The gold price. Each A$500 an ounce moves the project value by about A$40m. Gold back at the Starter Pit's A$7,178 would roughly double it, and the raise would then price far higher.
And what would make a holding wrong, for anyone who reopens it: a short reserve (grade is the input that moves the value most), and a placement before the reserve or at a deep discount.

Exit rule, if a card is ever written: sell on a reserve under 130,000oz or capital over A$160m, on a placement at A$0.16 or less, or at the resolve date.
