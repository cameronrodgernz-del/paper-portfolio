# 360 Capital REIT (ASX:TOT): deep dive, the case for buying

Written 2026-11-09 by Claude for the one-company-at-a-time process (rulebook section 5a, docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md). This is the case for buying.
Price at writing: A$0.405 (last sale on 9 November 2026; bid A$0.405, offer A$0.41, ASX company data read 9 November 2026) [S13]. Units on issue 223,650,355 after the September distribution reinvestment issue [S13, S7], so the market value is about A$90.6m. Net tangible assets (NTA) A$0.58 a unit at 30 September 2026 (unaudited), about A$129.7m [S4].
Status: wait. It fails at A$0.405 and qualifies at about A$0.375, as an ordinary pick or as a special situation (see "Where it lands"). The case-against prompt is filed (research/against/TOT_prompt.md), to run at about A$0.375 or on Cam's call. This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Order of work: chosen on 9 November 2026 from the new holder-notice screen (tools/holder_notices.py), which flagged TT Investments, Tony Pitt's company, building from 42.8% to 51.8% in small steps. The bad case was roughed out from the filings first at about 10% to 25% below the price, well inside the 30% limit, so the work went on.

## Where it lands
It fails at today's price, though not by much. On my numbers:
- the bad case is 12.1% below the price, inside the 30% limit;
- the middling case is +16.2%, against the +18.2% the value test then asks for;
- the expected gain after costs is about +5.1%, against the 12.9% minimum. Costs are about 3.0%: brokerage on a small position, a 1.2% quoted spread and the currency fee.

It qualifies at about A$0.375, where the expected-gain test passes (about +13.7%) and so does the value test. That is the 52-week low; it last traded there on 15 October 2026 [S14]. As a special situation (the controller's build-up) the answer is the same, because the expected-gain test is the one that binds.

The evidence outside the filings changed the picture in three ways:
- The holder the screen found is the controller, not an outsider. Tony Pitt runs the fund's manager and now owns 52.5% personally [S6, S7]. Since March 2026 he has taken about 95% of every distribution reinvestment issue, at prices about a third below NTA [S6, S7, S8]. His buying explains why the price is steady, and it's the main thing holding up the bad case.
- His last public deal sets the likely price of a bid. In 2025 he took his listed fund manager, 360 Capital Group (TGP), private at A$0.31 a security, roughly 22% below its net assets after a A$0.40 special distribution, and told holders he would delist it once he reached 75% [S10, S11, S12]. A bid for TOT on the same terms would be worth about A$0.46, not NTA.
- TOT isn't cheap against its peers. Listed REITs with large office holdings trade at 37% to 47% below NTA; TOT trades at 30% below [S15]. Interest rates have risen 1.25 points this year to 4.85%, and TOT's A$82m of debt is all floating [S1, S16].

## What the price assumes
At A$0.405 [S13, S4]:
- 30.2% below NTA of A$0.58;
- an 8.9% yield on the FY27 distribution target of 3.6 cents;
- 10.7 times the FY27 earnings guidance of 3.8 cents a unit, given on 12 August 2026 [S3].

The price assumes the fund stays as it is: fully leased, paying 3.6 cents a year, with no bid and no change to the discount. Against its peers that is a generous reading, not a mean one (see question 3 below).

## The one thing the market is missing
The controller is buying every quarter and took his last listed vehicle private within months of passing 50%, and the price carries no allowance for a bid.

That is true, but the evidence caps what it's worth. Pitt's record is to take control at a discount to asset value, so a bid adds about 13% to today's price, not the 43% it would take to reach NTA. On its own it doesn't make TOT pass.

## The test outside the filings
Done on 9 November 2026, under docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md step 2. The questions the filings can't answer:
1. What will Pitt do with TOT, and at what price?
2. Are the property values right, after this year's rate rises?
3. Is TOT cheap against the other listed office and mixed REITs?
4. How much do higher interest rates cut the distribution?

What the evidence showed:

1. Pitt is building steadily and cheaply, and his record says a bid would come at a discount to NTA.
   - His notices, read in full [S6, S7]:

   - The distribution reinvestment plan was suspended in December 2024 and switched back on in March 2026, the month Pitt completed his purchase of 360 Capital Group [S8, S1]. Pitt took 2.05m of the 2.17m units issued in March, 2.08m of the 2.20m in June and 2.68m of the 2.83m in September: about 95% each time [S1, S6, S7]. Each issue is priced at a 1.5% discount to the market [S4], so in practice it works as a quarterly placement to Pitt at about two-thirds of NTA. For holders who don't reinvest, it takes about a quarter of a cent off NTA each quarter (my arithmetic).
   - The takeover rules let him add up to 3% every six months, and reinvestment issues are exempt on top of that, because the plan is open to all holders [S17]. At the pace allowed he'd reach about 65% by August 2028 and 75% around 2029, without making a bid (my arithmetic).
   - The TGP precedent [S10, S11, S12]:
     - In June 2025 TGP's net assets were A$0.80 a security. It paid a A$0.40 special distribution in September, leaving about A$0.40 (my arithmetic).
     - Days after the payment, TT Investments bid A$0.30, then A$0.31 "best and final", unconditionally, holding 50.03%.
     - The independent expert (Grant Thornton) found the offer fair and reasonable.
     - At 74.84% TT said it would have TGP delisted once it held 75%. It bid again on market at A$0.31 in December, and moved to compulsory acquisition in January 2026.
     - That is a price about 22% below net assets.
   - Pitt said in November 2025 that most of his time as an executive had gone on listing obligations rather than real estate investing [S10]. TGP's FY25 results said that "at present TOT is subscale" [S11]. TOT's chief executive resigned on 5 November 2026, and Pitt took over his duties [S5].
   - The other listed fund the group manages points the other way: 360 Capital cut its stake in 360 Capital Mortgage REIT (TCF) from 19.9% to 9.8% between December 2024 and October 2025 [S18].
   - Reading: the signs point to Pitt wanting TOT for himself, but he doesn't need to bid. Creeping and reinvestment get him to control cheaply. I put a bid before August 2028 at about 12%, most likely at about 80% of NTA (about A$0.46).
2. The valuations are at the firm end of the market evidence, and rates are rising.
   - The values at June 2026 were the directors' own; the last independent valuations were at June 2025 [S1]. The independent ones are due again by June 2027, because the fund values externally at least every two years [S1].
   - The cap rates are 6.38% for 510 Church Street, Cremorne; 6.25% for 38 Sydney Avenue, Forrest; and 7.00% for Cannon Hill [S1]. A quarter-point rise takes A$7.6m off, about 3.4 cents a unit [S1].
   - Cremorne: Kinkora paid A$94.5m for Buildings 1 and 2 at 658 Church Street, further along the same street, on about an 8% passing yield, reported 4 September 2026 [S19]. Those buildings date from 1997 and 510 Church Street from 2021, so a gap is fair, but 1.6 points is a lot of gap. TOT's half-share cost A$135.0m in 2022 [S20] and is now carried at A$101.75m.
   - Canberra: Knight Frank puts prime yields in the parliamentary precinct (which includes Forrest) at 7.00% to 7.50% [S21]. The best long-lease evidence is lower: the Australian Federal Police building at 9 Brisbane Avenue, Barton, leased to 2036, sold for A$86m at a 5.73% passing yield in 2026 [S22]. Forrest's main tenant, the Australian National Audit Office, is leased to December 2034 [S1]. So 6.25% is defensible.
   - The cash rate rose from 3.60% to 4.85% in five steps in 2026, and before the November rise markets had priced about one and a half more rises by mid-2027 [S16].
   - What this changes: the bad case. I take three-quarters of a point on cap rates as the bad case and 1.25 points as the worse case.
3. TOT trades at a smaller discount than its peers.
   - At 9 November 2026, using the latest NTA [S13, S15]:
     - Centuria Office REIT: A$0.895 against A$1.66, 46% below;
     - GDI Property Group: A$0.64 against A$1.21, 47% below;
     - Cromwell Property Group: A$0.365 against A$0.575, 37% below;
     - TOT: A$0.405 against A$0.58, 30% below.
   - Reading: the market already gives TOT credit for being fully leased with long leases and a steady buyer. There's no gap to close against peers. If anything, the risk is that the discount moves toward theirs.
4. Higher rates trim, but don't break, the distribution.
   - The debt is unhedged; FY26's average rate was 5.20% [S1]. Drawn debt was A$84.0m at June and about A$82m at September [S1, S4]. Each extra half-point costs about A$0.41m a year, about 0.18 cents a unit (my arithmetic). The FY27 guidance was given when the cash rate was 4.35%; it's 4.85% now [S3, S16].
   - FY27 earnings also include two things that don't repeat: Dentsu's lease surrender payment, and a 16% coupon on preference equity that is being repaid as the apartments sell (A$10.9m at June, A$8.8m at September) [S1, S4].
   - Reading: FY27's 3.6 cents is probably covered. FY28 needs new structured equity deals or rent growth to hold it. The bad cases trim it to 0.8 or 0.75 cents a quarter.

Summary: the market isn't missing an upside here. The controller's buying holds the price up, and his record caps what a bid would pay. The rate rises are the larger risk, and TOT is already priced better than its peers.

## The company in plain words
TOT owns three modern buildings and lends to property deals [S1, S2]:
- half of 510 Church Street, Cremorne, Melbourne: offices and healthcare, completed 2021, A$101.75m, leased to Dentsu, Monash IVF, Siemens Energy, Endeavour Group and others;
- 38 Sydney Avenue, Forrest, Canberra: an A-grade office building refurbished in 2018, A$68.6m, 57% leased to the Australian National Audit Office until December 2034;
- 34 Southgate Avenue, Cannon Hill, Brisbane: Michael Hill's head office, completed 2022, A$32.0m, with 6.2 years left on the lease.

The buildings are 100% leased, with an average lease of 5.7 years and no expiries until FY29 [S4]. Government tenants provide 35% of the rent [S4]. Dentsu (9.7% of rent) is expected to leave levels 7 and 8 at Cremorne in FY30, and is talking about leaving earlier [S2, S4].

It also holds A$8.8m of preference equity in 360 Capital Private Equity Fund 1, a 360 Capital fund that bought a block of 36 new apartments about 6km from the Sydney CBD for A$49.9m and is selling them one by one [S1, S4]. TOT earns 16% a year on it. The fund may put up to 25% of its balance sheet into investments like this [S2].

The fund is managed by 360 Capital, which is now Pitt's private company. In February 2025 holders approved a 10-year investment management agreement: fees of 0.60% a year of total assets (0.55% to the investment manager and 0.05% to the responsible entity), a fee of two years' fees if the manager is removed without cause, and acquisition and disposal fees of up to 1% [S9]. It passed with 80.0% of the votes cast [S9]. Fees in FY26 came to about A$1.39m [S1].

Its record: 360 Capital bought the three buildings in 2022, as part of Charter Hall's takeover of Irongate, for A$256.7m [S20]. They are now carried at A$202.35m [S1]. NTA was A$1.15 a unit in December 2022; it fell to A$0.61 by June 2024 [S23, S26]. In February 2024 the fund raised A$25.5m in a non-renounceable 0.8-for-1 offer at A$0.40 to pay down debt. 360 Capital said it would take up its share in full; only 54.6% of the offer was taken up overall, and holders who didn't take part got nothing for their rights [S24]. NTA has been A$0.58 since June 2025 [S1].

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- About A$90m of market value, and only about A$43m that the controller doesn't hold.
- About A$14,000 traded a day [S14].
- Externally managed, controlled by the manager's owner, and mostly office: the three things REIT fund managers avoid.

## The players and what the rules allow
- Tony Pitt, through TT Investments, 52.49% [S7]. He is a director of the responsible entity and, since 5 November, also does the chief executive's job [S5]. Above 50% he controls ordinary resolutions. He can add 3% every six months, plus whatever he takes in reinvestment issues, without a bid [S17].
- A bid: because he holds more than 30%, any bid needs an independent expert's report. The TGP bid shows he will bid unconditionally, at a premium to the market but below asset value, and use the threat of delisting [S12].
- Delisting: under the ASX's guidance, delisting needs a special resolution (75% of the votes cast), and a controller can usually vote unless a takeover bid closed within the past 12 months [S25]. At 52.5% that's possible only on a low turnout of other holders. It's the tail risk for minority holders.
- The board of the responsible entity: David van Aanholt (independent chair), Andrew Moffat (independent) and Pitt [S10]. Moffat holds 1.4m units and reinvests his distributions [S7].
- Greig & Harrison held 5.37% in June 2026, down from 6.50% [S6]. No other holder has filed a 5% notice [S1].
- If nothing happens: the middling case below. The bad outcome for a special situation is that value; here the card's bad case is A$0.356.

## Valuation
Worked at 31 August 2028, 661 days out. I value it on NTA at June 2028 and a discount to NTA, plus the distributions that go ex before then.

The assumptions:
- Rents rise 3.3% a year under fixed reviews, so income is about 6.7% higher by mid-2028 [S4].
- Seven quarterly distributions go ex before the resolve date (December 2026 to June 2028): 6.3 cents at 0.9 cents each in the middling case.
- Reinvestment issues continue at about 2.2m units a quarter at about A$0.39, almost all to Pitt; this dilutes NTA for everyone else.
- A A$2m allowance for Dentsu leaving in FY30, which valuers will start to deduct by 2028.
- The middling case keeps today's 30% discount, as the rules ask.

Arithmetic for the middling case: property value A$202.35m × 1.067 × 6.43 / 6.68, less A$2m, is A$3.5m higher; NTA (A$129.7m + A$6.0m of reinvestment issues + A$3.5m) on 239.05m units is A$0.582; at a 30% discount that's A$0.408; plus 6.3 cents, A$0.471.

What the value depends on most: the discount. Each 5 points of discount on a A$0.58 NTA is 2.9 cents. At a 25% discount the middling case is about A$0.50, and it would pass the value test at today's price.

## Why the market is wrong
Mostly it isn't. The market prices TOT better than its office peers, because the controller buys steadily and the fund is fully leased on long leases. What the market may be underweighting cuts both ways:
- For: Pitt's likely end-game is to take it private, as he did with TGP, and the price allows nothing for that.
- Against: his record says a bid would come about 20% below NTA, the reinvestment plan transfers a little value to him every quarter, and the June 2027 independent valuations land after a year of rate rises.

For the card, the first two combine into one bad case: 35% at A$0.356. The card would read 35% at A$0.356, 45% at A$0.471 and 20% at A$0.507.

Expected value: 0.35 × 0.356 + 0.45 × 0.471 + 0.20 × 0.507 = A$0.438, or +8.1% before costs. After about 3.0% of costs it's about +5.1%. The costs are A$15 brokerage each way on about A$2,250 (1.3%), crossing a 1.2% quoted spread, and the 0.5% currency fee. The minimum over 661 days is 12.9%, so that test fails.

The value test (an ordinary pick):
- The bad case of A$0.356 is 12.1% below A$0.405, inside the 30% limit.
- Against that fall the middling case needs +18.2%, and it's +16.2%. It fails narrowly.
- It qualifies at about A$0.375, where the expected-gain test passes (+13.7%), the bad case is 5.1% below and the middling case is +25.5%.

As a special situation (the controller's build-up), the value test doesn't apply, and the expected-gain test still sets the price at about A$0.375.
