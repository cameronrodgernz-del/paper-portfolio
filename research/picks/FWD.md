> In short. Fleetwood is now two businesses: worker accommodation in the Pilbara (Community Solutions, A$50.0m EBIT in FY26, almost all from the 1,250-room Searipple Village in Karratha) and modular building (Building Solutions, an A$8.7m underlying loss in FY26). Rio Tinto takes 800 Searipple rooms on take-or-pay until 30 April 2027, about half of the division's revenue on the company's own contract figures. The land tenure question has a clear answer, and it matters. Searipple sits on three lots: one freehold lot holding about half the village, and two Crown lots leased from the State. The City of Karratha's planning approval for workforce accommodation on all three runs only to 31 January 2029, after a tribunal-mediated extension in January 2022, and the City's policy allows extensions of at most five years at a time and requires a plan for removing the village 18 months before expiry (so by about 31 July 2027). A new five-year Rio Tinto contract from May 2027 would run to April 2032, past that date. The Red Dog Village purchase (2,169 rooms, A$20m, completion now expected January 2027) brings tenure to October 2032 if its conditions are met, and management's own AGM slide says the aim is long-term tenure at Red Dog "to enable Searipple Village residential conversion". So these earnings have a planning horizon of two to six years, not forever. That undoes the fact sheet's "if it goes our way" anchor of A$3.56, which put FY27-level earnings on a perpetual multiple and also counted A$28m of cash that is either early customer receipts or committed to restructuring. On a tenure-limited cash flow valuation the Rio-win outcome is worth about A$2.60, the middle about A$1.80 and a Rio loss about A$1.15. The market price is close to the weighted value.

### Searipple's land and planning approval (the open point)
From the City of Karratha's council minutes [C1, C2]:
- "Searipple Village was originally developed as a caravan park in the 1970s. Fleetwood Limited (Fleetwood) purchased the caravan park in 1987 and converted it into a Workforce Accommodation facility in 2003."
- "Searipple Village extends across three separate lots with a total area of 15.42ha ... Lot 250 is the northernmost lot and contains about half of the development, including the Bayview Road entry and central facilities buildings. Lot 250 is a freehold lot ... Both Lot 4471 and Lot 4931 are Crown lots leased by the State Government to Fleetwood. The current lease for Lots 4471 and 4931 expires on 31 January 2022." (report of 14 June 2021)
- History of the time-limited approval: December 2013, the Council extended it to 1 February 2018; in October 2014 the State Administrative Tribunal extended freehold Lot 250 to 1 February 2024; June 2016, the Crown lots were extended to 31 January 2022 "to align with the Crown lease term", on Fleetwood showing an 800-room contract; late 2017, Fleetwood applied to convert the Crown lots to freehold and the Council objected in January 2018, preferring "base-level supply over the freehold portion, with the ability to accommodate peak demands over the leasehold portion", which "allowed for the leasehold portion to be closed if demand fell".
- 30 June 2021: Fleetwood asked for 1 February 2034; the Council granted 31 January 2027 (resolution 154829), the five-year maximum in its workforce accommodation policy. Fleetwood sought a tribunal review.
- 31 January 2022: on the tribunal's invitation the Council set that aside and approved the extension "valid until 31 January 2029" for Lots 250, 4471 and 4931 (resolution 154939), with a condition that "A Transition Plan being submitted to the City of Karratha at least 18 months prior to the expiry of this approval indicating how and when the development will be removed and the site rehabilitated or developed for a different use".
- The policy (DP10): workforce accommodation is "a temporary and transitional use"; new approvals up to ten years, "Extensions of time may be permitted for a maximum period of five years", and need must be shown with contracts or bookings [C3].
- Since then the City has approved small works on Lot 250 (a laundry building, 18 June 2025; a shower building, 5 January 2026) [C4]. I found no later decision changing the 31 January 2029 date.

What this means: the village has had its approval extended every time since 2003, each time on evidence of contracts, so a Rio Tinto contract would be the evidence for another extension. But the approval ends 21 months into a new five-year Rio contract, the next extension is a fresh application under a policy that prefers permanent housing, and the transition plan falls due in mid-2027. Management's AGM slide lists "Secure long-term tenure at Red Dog Village to enable Searipple Village residential conversion" [F4]. The Red Dog conditions (development approval and Crown lease both extended by five years to October 2032 [F5]) cover a five-year Rio contract from May 2027 almost exactly. My reading: Fleetwood's plan is to serve Rio Tinto from Red Dog beyond 2029 and redevelop Searipple, which ties the Rio outcome to Red Dog's completion.

Not verified: the current term of the Crown lease over Lots 4471 and 4931; whether Fleetwood has applied to extend the approval beyond 31 January 2029; the zoning of Lot 250 for housing. The WA land register (Landgate) is a paid search I didn't run.

### Method 2: the market's measure for accommodation owners, against a listed peer
Worker accommodation owners are priced on enterprise value to EBITDA. The one listed peer I could check, Civeo (NYSE: CVEO; villages in Canada and in Queensland's Bowen Basin, services in WA), raised 2026 EBITDA guidance to US$89m to US$92m with net debt of US$196.0m at 30 September 2026; at US$30.55 (30 October) on about 10.3m shares, its EV is about US$511m, 5.6 to 5.7 times EBITDA (Civeo's third-quarter release of 29 October 2026, read through finviz, a secondary source [P1]).

At 5.6 times, Fleetwood's FY27 EBITDA before lease accounting (Community Solutions 48 + 3.3, Building Solutions -5 + 3.7, corporate -7.0 + 0.3 = A$43.3m) gives A$256.8m with net cash and the Red Dog loan, **A$2.84** a share; a through-cycle EBITDA (Community Solutions at its FY22 to FY26 average of A$23.8m EBIT plus 3.3, Red Dog 10 + 3.3, Building Solutions 4 + 3.7, corporate -6.7 = A$41.4m) gives **A$2.73**. At A$2.04 Fleetwood's EV including the Red Dog loan is A$169.9m, 3.9 times the FY27 figure.

These are upper bounds. Civeo's owned villages sit on long-term tenure; Searipple's approval ends in 26 months and Red Dog's would end in under six years, and the FY27 base is near peak occupancy. A trade buyer doing its own sums would pay for the approved years, which is Method 1.

### Testing the fact sheet's "if it goes our way" anchor (A$3.56)
It put Community Solutions at A$45.3m plus Red Dog A$10m, less corporate costs, on 8.33 times after-tax profit forever, and added A$62.4m of net cash less the Red Dog loan.
- The cash is about A$28m too high (31c a share): A$16.5m of early receipts, A$13.9m of restructuring still to pay, and Camec's A$7.8m net rather than A$9.5m, offset by A$4.0m of tax assets.
- The perpetual multiple ignores tenure. Searipple's approval ends 31 January 2029; Red Dog's would end in October 2032; management itself plans Searipple's residential conversion [C2, F5, F4]. No buyer would pay a forever multiple on that.
- Mechanism: "There is no current on market buy-back" (annual report, page 122 [F1]), no bid is in view, and 100% of profit already goes out as dividends. The price can only get there by the market putting a perpetual multiple on a Rio Tinto win.
On my figures a Rio win is worth about A$2.60 (A$2.84 on the peer multiple). A$3.56 is beyond the good case.

## Why the market is wrong
On my figures it isn't, at A$2.04. The price is close to the probability-weighted value, and the market has fallen 30% since 31 August as the Red Dog delay, the Building Solutions loss guidance, the CFO's exit and the index removal came out. The one thing the market might be getting wrong is the chance of a Rio Tinto win: with Red Dog, Fleetwood would own about 45% of Karratha's worker rooms and the closest village to town, and Rio Tinto has stayed since 2012. If that chance is nearer 55% to 60% than my 45%, the case clears at today's price (see the sensitivity below). That is exactly what the award will show.

## What the award must show, and at what price
1. Fleetwood wins Rio Tinto's next contract: at least about 700 rooms, five years from 1 May 2027, take-or-pay, revenue of at least about A$40m a year (A$200m over the term; the current contract runs at A$43m to A$53m a year), and which village serves it after January 2029 (Red Dog, or Searipple with a stated plan for extending its approval). Then the outcomes become A$2.00 / A$2.24 / A$2.58 (chances about 20/45/35), and the case reopens as an ordinary pick to the next result at or below about **A$2.25**, or as a value pick at or below about **A$1.95** (middling test; the bad-case test gives A$2.22). The price traded at A$2.92 in late August, so it may well jump above these on a win; if so, no case.
2. Fleetwood keeps Rio Tinto on a smaller or cheaper contract (say 300 to 500 rooms or under about A$25m a year): value about A$1.6 to A$2.0. Reopen only at or below about A$1.60.
3. Rio Tinto goes elsewhere: outcomes A$0.63 / A$1.12 / A$1.42. Reopen as an ordinary pick at or below about **A$1.04**, or as a value pick at or below about **A$0.70**.
4. Whatever the award: Red Dog must complete by the end of January 2027 with both the development approval and the Crown lease extended to October 2032 and the Pluto Train 2 restriction removed [F5]. If the agreement lapses, take about 25c off the good case.
5. The half-year result (about 25 February 2027) should show Community Solutions EBIT of about A$24m or more for the half (fully booked to December), a Building Solutions loss no worse than about A$7m as guided, net cash after the final dividend and restructuring of about A$25m to A$35m before the Red Dog loan, FY28 contracted occupancy across both villages, and the plan for Searipple's approval after 31 January 2029 (the transition plan is due by about 31 July 2027).

## What would make this wrong
The verdict is "wait", so these are the ways it could be wrong:
1. The Rio Tinto chance. If Fleetwood's control of about 45% of Karratha's rooms makes a win nearer 60%, today's price already clears the minimum (+10% after costs at 10/30/60), and waiting costs the re-rating that would follow a win.
2. The tenure haircut. The City has extended Searipple's approval every time since 2003 when Fleetwood showed contracts, and Red Dog's lease may run past 2032. If a buyer treated the earnings as lasting, the good case is nearer the peer multiple's A$2.8 to A$3.0 than A$2.60.
On the other side, the things that would make a purchase today wrong: Rio Tinto leaving (bad case 44% below the price), Red Dog failing just as Searipple's approval runs out, and Building Solutions losses running past the first half (a legacy project dispute is still open: A$2.2m of defect claims paid and disputed [F1, note 14]).

Exit rule (for any later card): sell if Rio Tinto's award goes elsewhere, if Red Dog's agreement lapses without completion, or on the resolve date.
