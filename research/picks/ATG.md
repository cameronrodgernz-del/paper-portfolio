## The company in plain words
Articore (Redbubble until 2023) runs two online marketplaces, Redbubble and TeePublic, where more than three million independent artists sell designs printed on demand on T-shirts, stickers, wall art and other products. It holds no stock: a customer pays first, one of 44 third-party printing sites makes and ships the item, and the artist is paid later, monthly once their balance passes $10 [A1 notes 1 and 18][A3]. Articore keeps marketplace revenue (sales less the artists' share): A$354.5m in FY26 (year to 30 June 2026), down 6.5% (3.8% in constant currency) [A2]. By brand: Redbubble A$170.1m (down 12.7%, 11.1% in constant currency), TeePublic A$182.0m (down 1.1%, up 2.8% in constant currency), and Dashery, a storefront service for creators launched in January 2025, A$2.4m [A1, operating and financial review]. In May 2026 it bought Frankly Wearing, a small Indian print-on-demand marketplace, for US$0.9m (75% paid, 25% an earn-out in about 18 months) [A11][A1 cash flow]. North America is 73% of sales, Europe and the UK 19%, Australia and New Zealand 8%; 72% of revenue and 75% of costs are in US dollars [A3][A4]. Sales peak in the December quarter: 39% of FY26 marketplace revenue and 73% of its operating EBITDA came from it [A6][A2].

Who runs it: Vivek Kumar, the former head of TeePublic, Group CEO since June 2025 and Managing Director since August 2025; Derek Yung, Group CFO from January 2026 [A12][A21]. The board: Robin Mendelson (chair since June 2025), Robin Low, Bob Sherwin, John Lewis (of Osmium Partners) and Naseema Sparks (from March 2026) [A12].

Who owns it [A1, shareholder information, 29 July 2026]: substantial holders Martin Hosking (founder) and his entities, cut from 41,725,573 (14.20%) to 33,706,581 (11.56%) by selling 8,008,992 shares on 28 August 2026 at A$0.39 to A$0.40 [A16]; Osmium Partners (director John Lewis) 25,994,781 (8.92%); Spheria Asset Management and Pinnacle Investment Management, each 24,281,138 (8.27%), at least 14,170,656 of them counted in both; Jencay Capital 14,489,383 (5.09%). ## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Size and trading: A$109m market value and A$16,421 a day traded (median, 20 sessions to 30 October 2026) [M2].
- A concentrated register: the top 20 hold 83.7% and four holders hold about 34% [A1].
- A messy history: five years of falling revenue and four years of losses; a founder-led attempt to remove four directors and elect four others, defeated on 22 August 2025 with 59.7% to 67.0% of votes against [A17]; a seven-month strategic review with Citizens Bank that "included engagement with a range of potential counterparties" and ended on 24 March 2026 with no deal [A11]; and the founder selling 8.0m shares on the day of the FY26 result [A16].
- An out-of-favour sector: online consumer retail sold mostly to US shoppers, reported in Australian dollars.
- But it isn't unknown. Analysts from RBC (Wei-Weng Chen) and Canaccord (Owen Humphries) asked questions on the FY26 results call [A4], Canaccord is the buy-back broker [A15], and the company moved its US over-the-counter quote up to the OTCQB tier (ticker ATGRF) on 15 September 2026 to reach US investors [A20]. Short selling is negligible: 1,022 shares reported short on 23 October 2026 [M4]. I didn't check index membership.

So it is small and thinly traded, but the main reason it is cheap on its headline numbers is that the headline cash isn't spare and sales are still falling.

## Valuation
**Cash earnings.** I use operating EBITDA less share-based pay, capitalised development, equipment and lease payments, before interest and tax (other income, mostly one-off write-backs, left out). FY26: 16.44 - 1.88 - 3.05 - 0.52 - 3.29 - 0.36 + 0.13 (sublease receipts) = A$7.48m (A$5.98m if share-based pay hadn't been cut by the A$1.5m reversal) [A1]. For FY27 onward I deduct a normal A$9.08m from operating EBITDA: share-based pay A$2.5m, development A$3.58m (FY26 times 1.175, the CFO's 15% to 20% rise), equipment A$0.5m, leases net of sublease A$2.5m. So FY27 operating EBITDA of A$15m, A$19.5m and A$23m gives A$5.92m, A$10.42m and A$13.92m. Cash tax 10%. Per share on 308.8m shares.

**Cash.** Free cash was minus A$5.2m at 30 June 2026 (table above). The float stays while sales hold, so it isn't worthless to holders, but it can't be paid out without leaving the company short at a weak moment, with no facility to fall back on. I count the A$40.5m less a A$15m working buffer (about two weeks of payments to printers and artists, A$8.7m, plus a month of operating costs, A$7.1m [A1 cash flow and Table 1]), and I put the float into the cash flows: if sales shrink at 2% a year the float shrinks with them, costing A$45.7m x 0.02 / 0.13 = A$7.0m in today's money; if they grow 3% a year it adds A$45.7m x 0.03 / 0.08 = A$17.1m. Counting only free cash would take about A$0.10 a share off every value below; counting all A$40.5m would add about A$0.05.

**Method 1: discounted cash flow.** Value at 1 July 2026 = after-tax cash earnings / (11% less growth) + cash less buffer, less float run-off or plus float build; then carried forward eight months at 11% to the resolve date. 11% is my cost of equity for a small, seasonal, US consumer business with shrinking sales; it is between the rates the two peers' prices imply (about 13% for hipages and 9.5% for Kogan, with 5% growth). ## Why the market is wrong
At A$0.37 I can't say it is. The price counts all the cash and values the rest at about 6.5 times my FY27 middling cash earnings, or 10.9 times once the cash is treated as the float it is, which is about what a flat business is worth at 11% and below the two growing peers. The share has already moved from A$0.27 in June to A$0.425 on the day of the FY26 result and back to A$0.37 [M2]. A gap would open only if the price fell without news (to A$0.345 or lower), or if the December quarter showed sales growing again, which would show that the 2% to 3% a year of growth in my good case is real. Both are named below.

- 0.5% each way to convert the money: 0% to 1.0%.
That is 3.9% to 6.4%; I use 5.0%. Expected return after costs: about -4.7%. The minimum (rulebook section 5a) is the 2.13% cash rate plus 5% a year over 117 days, +2.3%. Not a case for review.

## What would make this wrong
The two things most likely to make this "wait" wrong:
1. Another beat and raise. Management raised guidance at the half in FY25 and FY26 and beat it both times. If the December quarter shows sales growing again, the price could jump 25% to 35% in a day (28 August 2026: +29%), and waiting misses it.
2. The float working the other way. If Redbubble's decline (-11% in constant currency) pulls group sales down again, the float shrinks with them, cash falls even while the company reports a profit, and the market may start to price free cash, which was negative. The share would then fall below my A$0.22 bad case, which still counts most of the cash.
Also: paid marketing (21% of marketplace revenue) depends on Google and Meta prices and on search traffic shifting to AI tools, which the company is courting [A4]; and a founder still holding 11.6% who sold on the result day [A16].

Exit rule, if a position is ever opened: sell after the half-year result unless the deep dive is re-written; sell at once if the December quarter meets the "weak" conditions above.
