## What the filings show

### From FFO to the cash GDI keeps
FFO counts rent at face value through rent-free periods: GDI books the forgone rent as an incentive asset and amortises it, and FFO adds the amortisation back (A$18.7m in FY26) [G1, note 9 and FFO reconciliation]. Fit-outs GDI builds on spec are owned by GDI and capitalised as property improvements, not incentives [G1, note 1]. So the cash GDI keeps is FFO less rent-free given, fit-outs and subdivisions, maintenance and leasing fees.

The FFO excludes the consolidated trusts' own results and counts only what GDI receives from them ("contribution from consolidated trusts" is taken out; "distributions/funds management fees received from consolidated trusts" go back in [G7]). So I deduct only GDI Property Trust's rent-free and maintenance, not the consolidated trusts'; the fact sheet deducted both, a small difference.

What a quick reader would miss:
- Fit-out spending is falling fast. The second half of FY26 ran at A$6.9m a year against A$25.8m in FY24. GDI says the spec fit-out and subdivision programme at Mill Green has "resulted in reduced tenant incentives and earlier lease commencement dates" [G1, directors' report].
- Rent-free given is not falling yet, and the amortisation charge (A$17.9m for GDI Property Trust in FY26, up from A$10.8m in FY24) shows the incentive bill already sitting in the leases [G1, G9].
- GDI's FFO counts the Co-living JV before tax: the A$1.7m that is GDI's share of the JV's income tax is added back ("other non-operating costs") [G1]. The JV's contribution is A$10.1m in the segment table and A$9.45m after the JV's own interest of about A$0.6m (GDI's documents describe it both ways) [G1, G2]. Only A$5.2m came back in cash in FY26, and the JV's profits "cannot be distributed without consent from the two joint venture partners" [G1, note 14].
- The FFO includes one-off fees. Funds Management FFO of A$6.2m included disposal fees on the Autoleague and Bassendean sales; I take about A$2.5m a year as the base (remaining fees on A$260.4m of funds), so FY26 FFO without one-offs was about A$40.8m, 7.56c. FY27 will include about A$6.2m of Autoleague disposal and performance fees from the 19 August 2026 settlement [G1, note 36; G19], about 1.15c, once.
- The distribution has been paid out of capital for four years. FY23 to FY26 distributions totalled 20c [G1, G9]; cash kept in FY24 to FY26 was about 1.7c in all. The gap came from asset sales and debt. GDI says FY27's "may be paid wholly or partly out of capital" [G1].

- Leasing: over 21,000 sqm of office space in FY25 [G10] and 20,937 sqm in FY26 (including heads of agreement and the funds' buildings), about 13,000 sqm of it in the first half [G1, G8]. By November 2026, WS1, WS2 and the office part of 197 St Georges Terrace were 100% leased and negotiations were under way for the rest of 5 Mill Street [G5]. Much of 197's remaining vacancy is in its retail precinct [G1].
- Expiries by income, read from the bar charts in the FY26 presentation [G2, pages 27 and 28] (approximate, my reading): WS1, about 6% in FY28 and 14% in FY29, the rest FY32 or later; WS2, about 9% in each of FY29, FY30 and FY31, the rest FY32 or later; 197 St Georges Terrace, vacant 8%, FY27 3%, FY28 27%, FY29 12%, FY30 20%, FY31 18%, FY32+ 13%; 5 Mill Street, vacant 7%, FY27 13%, FY28 21%, FY29 34%, FY30 24%. 1 Mill and 180 Hay are wholly vacant; Townsville has about 40% expiring in FY28.
- So FY27 has few expiries anywhere, FY28 brings about a quarter of 197 St Georges Terrace and a fifth of 5 Mill Street, and FY29 to FY30 bring WS1's first meaningful block, a third of 5 Mill and more of 197. Rent-free should ease in FY27 to FY28 as the FY25 to FY26 leasing wave passes, then rise again.
- The market: Knight Frank puts Perth CBD prime net incentives at 47.0% and secondary at 50.0%, flat through the first half of 2026, prime net face rents at A$737 and A$749 a square metre (Q1, Q2), and prime vacancy at 15.6% [M1, M2] (secondary source). GDI cites CBRE's prime net face rent growth of 4.3% a year to June 2026 [G2]. Its managing director expects "very strong rental growth over the next four to five years" and lower incentives [G4].

### The values against rent actually paid
The lesson from Peter Warren (research/picks/PWR.md): test book values against the rent actually received. For the four income-producing towers (WS1, WS2, 197 St Georges Terrace, 5 Mill Street), worked in python3 -I [G1, G2]:
- Book A$794.4m, at a weighted cap rate of 6.70%. FY26 FFO A$54.9m: a 6.91% yield on book, so on face rent the valuations look consistent.
- On the rent actually paid (FFO less GDI's A$16.2m of rent-free, A$0.9m of leasing fees and A$0.8m of maintenance): A$37.0m, 4.65% on book. At the valuers' own 6.70% that supports A$552m, 30% below book. After fit-outs as well, 3.47%.
- On the through-cycle charge (FFO less the amortisation of incentives and leasing fees): A$34.1m, 4.30%.
- Valuers capitalise face rent and deduct incentives as a capital cost, so these are not like-for-like with the cap rate. But they show book value is supported only if cash rent climbs toward face rent and stays there through the FY28 to FY30 re-leasing. With incentives at 47%, it won't fully.
- Market evidence: Knight Frank's Perth prime "core market yield" was 7.58% in Q1 2026 (flat for seven quarters) and 7.90% in Q2; recent Perth sales were 1 Nash Street (A$79.4m, 8.3%, May 2026) and 66 St Georges Terrace (A$75.0m, 8.2%, October 2024) [M1, M2]. The JLL and CBRE Perth CBD prime range quoted in Growthpoint's FY26 results is 6.50% to 8.75% (research/picks/COF.md [P4]). WS1 and WS2 at 6.50% sit at the tightest end of every published range. WS1's 6.0-year WALE and the State Government's lease to FY34 justify a tighter yield than Knight Frank's 5-year-WALE benchmark, but not the tightest in the city.
- Marking the four towers' FY26 face FFO at 7.5%, 7.9% and 8.3% gives A$732m, A$695m and A$661m (-8% to -17%), and NTA of A$1.10, A$1.03 and A$0.97. A rise of 0.25, 0.5 and 1.0 points on the existing cap rates gives NTA of A$1.17, A$1.12 and A$1.03.
- WS1, WS2 and the car parks were last valued at 31 December 2025, and GDI's policy is independent valuations "usually at least annually" [G1, risks]. So the 31 December 2026 valuations, reported with the 1H FY27 result, are likely to test WS1 and WS2 against Knight Frank's 32-basis-point rise in Perth prime yields since Q1. 180 Hay Street's A$18.7m is a June 2025 valuation of an empty building [G2].

## Why the market is wrong

Partly, and slowly. The market prices GDI on the cash it kept in FY26 (5.0% at A$0.64, inside the peer range) and on its distribution yield (7.8%, already lower than peers). What it may be underpricing is that FY24 to FY26 were the expensive years of a lease-up: fit-out spending has already fallen by two-thirds, few leases expire in the core towers before FY29, and so cash kept should rise from about 3c to 4c to 5c in FY27 to FY28, bringing the 5.0c close to covered. Meanwhile holders are paid 7.8% a year to wait, with a 10% holder who has been buying at about A$0.615 and a buy-back as soft support near A$0.60.

What the market is right about: Perth incentives are still 47%, the WS1 and WS2 book values sit at the tightest yields in the city, and FY28 to FY30 bring a new round of re-leasing at 197, 5 Mill and WS1. So the rise in cash earnings is partly a lull in leasing costs, not a new level, and the NTA gap is not a mispricing anyone is about to correct. The edge is small: the expected price in August 2028 is about 3% above today's; the rest of the expected return is the distribution.
