# Centrepoint Alliance (ASX:CAF): deep dive (not a value pick)

Verdict: wait for a named event, the half-year trading update (mid-December 2026, if the company repeats last year's) and the H1 FY27 result (about 24 February 2027). No card. Not a value pick (rulebook section 5a): the bad case (A$0.32) is 15% below the A$0.375 price, outside the 10% the rule allows, and the middling case (A$0.40) is only 7% above it. But WAG has about A$4m to A$6m of its own cash and can't bid, Thorney (20.6%) sold 10m shares at A$0.35 in 2025, and no approach has been announced. On 25/60/15 chances the expected price is A$0.392 plus a 1.25c interim dividend, +7.9% before costs. Reopen at A$0.35 or below with guidance unchanged, or after a half that shows normalised EBITDA of A$7.5m or more before SEQ with advisers back above 585, or on any control event.

## The company in plain words
Centrepoint is the licence behind about 576 independent financial advisers (authorised representatives, the number two licensee in Australia by ASIC register count) and a support service for another 201 self-licensed firms with about 1,072 advisers [S2, S4]. Advisers pay it a fixed monthly licence fee (A$25.4m in FY26) and buy research, compliance, education, paraplanning and software through it [S1]. All client advice fees pass through Centrepoint's books (A$332.8m in FY26) and almost all of it is paid straight back to the advisers (A$320.6m), which is why "gross revenue" of A$365.6m becomes net revenue of A$43.0m [S1, S2]. On top of the licence it runs a salaried advice business (Financial Advice Matters, about 23 employed advisers after the Cairns and Pinnacle books bought in July 2026, A$10.3m of revenue in FY26), managed portfolios and the IconiQ platform (combined A$638m of funds at June 2026, still loss-making), a small lending service and an XPLAN consulting arm (Enzumo) [S1, S2, S17]. In September 2026 it bought 51% of SEQ Advice Group, a Queensland advice firm with about A$3.0m of revenue [S14, S15].

Chief executive John Shuttleworth (4.28m shares) and chief financial officer Brendon Glass run it; the board is four non-executives: chair Georg Chmiel, Martin Pretty, Linda Fox and Thorney's Anthony Vogel [S1, S2]. The board and key management hold 3.1% [S2].

Register at 31 July 2026: 1,750 holders; top 20 hold 65.6% [S1]. Substantial holders today (from the notices, on 210.54m shares): Thorney (TIGA Trading and related entities) 43,418,564 (20.6%); WAG 34,201,831 of its own (16.2%) plus a voting agreement over Bondia's 4,000,000 (18.2% together, if the agreement is still in force); Pilgrim Private 20,055,271 (9.5%) [S24, S26, S27, S33]. Sequoia Financial Group ceased to be substantial on 27 August 2026 but on its own notices still held about 9.26m shares (4.4%) then [S30].

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Size: A$79m market value. Not in the S&P/ASX 300 at the start or end of the repo's index file (2010 and October 2026), and not added to the All Ordinaries in the September 2026 rebalance that brought Count back [S40, S47].
- Liquidity: median A$26,483 a day over the last 20 sessions to 30 October 2026 counting the two sessions with no trade, A$37,181 counting only days that traded (Checks).
- Register: Thorney, WAG and Pilgrim hold about 48% between them; with the board, management and Sequoia's remainder, the free float is under half the company, about A$35m to A$40m.
- Coverage: I found no broker research in the filings. The peer chart in the 2025 AGM deck was charted by Ventura from Bloomberg data, not by a broker [S10].
- Sector: licensee services are seen as a low-growth, regulation-heavy business, and 2025 to 2026 brought the Shield and First Guardian failures, ASIC's suit against InterPrac and the near-wipeout of Sequoia's licensee (sold for A$200,000) [S44, S45].
- Payout: the company pays out about all of its cash earnings as a fully franked dividend (8% yield), so it is held for income rather than growth.

What a quick reader would miss:
- Licence fees grew 11.9% in FY25 and only 3.8% in FY26 [S1, S18]. The company's "licensee services revenue" (licence fees, virtual services and partner programme) was A$27.7m, up 2.3% [S1, S2].
- Net product revenue has fallen three years running while funds under management and administration rose 48% in FY26: the platform and managed accounts cost more to run than they earn so far. The funds management segment's EBITDA went from a A$0.5m loss to a A$1.3m loss in FY26, even though FY26 no longer carries allocated indirect costs [S1]. The annual report says Investment Solutions "experienced headwinds that affected the commercialisation of IconiQ" [S1].
- Platform rebates, the old "product rebate" income of licensees, have ceased: the H1 FY26 deck says other revenue fell 10% a year since FY23 "due to cessation platform rebates" [S6]. What remains is A$1.5m of partner programme sponsorship.
- Lending isn't reported on its own. The lending aggregation business was sold to Astute for no cash (expected completion 31 March 2026) in exchange for the full licence margin on Astute-aligned advisers, expected to add A$0.4m of EBITDA a year from FY27 [S8].
- About A$0.48m of FY26 revenue (A$0.93m in FY25) was "customer acquisition revenue", a non-cash entry: first-year licence fee rebates given to new advisers are booked as revenue and capitalised as an asset (A$1.75m at June 2026), then amortised below EBITDA [S1 note 4(a) and 6(a)]. It inflates normalised EBITDA by that amount each year.

### Advisers: the number the guidance rests on
- 571 at 30 June 2025; 588 on 12 December 2025 "with an additional 40 advisers currently being onboarded" [S9]; 587 at 31 December 2025 (41 appointments, 27 resignations in the half) [S6, S7]; "AR numbers as at 20 February exceeded 600" [S6]; 576 at 30 June 2026 [S3, S4].
- So the network lost at least 24 advisers between late February and June 2026, after the company had promised growth in the second half. The ASIC register shows 74 appointments and 69 cessations for FY26, so the second half had about 33 appointments and 42 cessations [S1, S6]. The annual report blames practice sales and brought-forward retirements, and says net growth "was below the Group's original expectations" [S1].
- Revenue per adviser kept rising (A$541k a year, up 22% over four years; average client fee A$3,624), which is what has carried licence fees [S2].

### Industry risk the filings don't mention
Centrepoint's FY26 filings say nothing about the Shield and First Guardian failures or the compensation scheme of last resort, and carry a client claims provision of only A$0.4m (ten claims paid in FY26, A$0.05m) [S1, S2]. The same events cost Sequoia its licensee: platforms (Macquarie, then Netwealth) reviewed their dealings with InterPrac's advisers, advisers resigned, A$4.7m of intangibles were written off, ASIC sued InterPrac, and Sequoia sold it for A$200,000 in October 2026 [S44, S45]. I found no Shield or First Guardian link to Centrepoint's licences in its filings or in a news search, but I did not search ASIC's court records licence by licence. For any licensee this is the tail risk: losing platform access or a wave of adviser exits can take the licence fees away quickly.

## Valuation

### Testing the fact sheet's anchor: is Count really at 20 times?
The fact sheet's A$0.553 takes FY27 earnings at the guidance midpoint and Count's 20.2 times, the trailing price-to-earnings in the repo's ASX data (Count's statutory EPS of 8.41c at A$1.70) [S47]. From Count's own filings:
- FY26: statutory profit to shareholders A$15.18m (boosted by a gain when WSC Group became a subsidiary, a lease variation gain and a contingent consideration write-back); underlying profit A$13.86m; underlying profit before amortisation (NPATA) A$17.65m; underlying EBITA A$33.4m [S36].
- At A$1.70 on 216.5m shares (A$368m): 24.2 times statutory, 20.9 times underlying NPATA. But Count issued shares in April and May 2026 to buy Oracle, completed on 20 July 2026 and forecast to earn about A$10m of EBITA [S39]. Count's own pro forma FY26 EBITA including Oracle is A$42.5m [S38]. Adding Oracle's earnings less interest, pro forma NPATA is about A$23.3m: 15.8 times at A$368m. With Skybridge (75% of A$8.8m EBITA, paid with a A$25m placement at A$1.57 and 2.5m scrip shares) it is about A$27.5m on 234.9m shares: 14.5 times [S38].
- On enterprise value (market value A$399m, about A$48m of pro forma net debt, A$27m of deferred and earn-out payments for Oracle and Skybridge, and minority interests at book A$24m), about 9.7 times FY26 pro forma EBITA of A$51.3m.
- Q1 FY27 underlying EBITA was A$12.7m, up 66.6%, which Count says is ahead of broker consensus on an annualised basis, so its forward multiple is lower still [S37].

What earns Count its premium: it is five times Centrepoint's size; it has A$6.7bn of funds under management (Centrepoint A$0.64bn) and its Wealth segment alone earns A$15.1m of EBITA on A$45.8m of revenue; before corporate costs, about 44% of its earnings come from wealth (licence, financial planning and investment services), about 31% from part-owned accounting firms and about 24% from actuarial and other services; group EBITA has more than tripled since FY23 (A$10.4m to A$33.4m); it buys businesses at 7.2 to 7.5 times EBITA with shares priced at 15 times or more; it rejoined the All Ordinaries on 21 September 2026; and it raises equity through brokers who cover it [S36, S38, S39, S40]. None of this applies to Centrepoint, whose growth is 16% a year and whose platform loses money. So the re-rating that makes up most of the fact sheet's gap has no mechanism inside six months, and the gap itself is smaller than stated: on the same pro forma bases Centrepoint is at about 10.4 times cash earnings and 8.3 times EBITA, Count at about 14.5 to 15.8 times and 9.7 times.

### What the rules allow (Corporations Act chapter 6)
- The 20% line (section 606): nobody may go from 20% or below to above 20%, or increase above 20%, except through an exception: a takeover bid, a scheme, approval by the other holders, or creep.
- Creep (section 611 item 9): a holder that has had at least 19% for the whole previous six months may buy 3% every six months. Thorney qualifies: about 6.3m shares (about A$2.4m) per six months. WAG and Pilgrim do not (WAG has never been at 19% for six months).
- Headroom to 20% (42,107,834 votes): WAG about 3.9m more shares with Bondia counted, about 7.9m if the agreement has lapsed; Pilgrim about 22.1m shares (about A$8.3m).
- Association: if WAG and Pilgrim (or either with Thorney) agreed to act together on control or the board, their votes would add up and the 20% rule would be breached. Thorney, WAG and Pilgrim together hold about 48%. There is no evidence of an association; both WAG and Pilgrim are based in Collins Street, Melbourne, which proves nothing.
- Partial bids: the renewal of the proportional takeover approval clauses failed at the November 2025 AGM (48.4% for, 51.6% against, a special resolution needing 75%) [S11]. A bidder can now make an offer for a fixed share of each holder's shares without a holder vote.
- Schemes: need board support and 75% of votes cast. With about 118m votes cast at the 2025 AGM, 25% is about 30m votes, so Thorney alone or WAG alone can block a scheme.
- Board pressure: any 5% holder can call a meeting (section 249D). A vote of 25% or more against the remuneration report on 13 November 2026 would be a first strike; a second in November 2027 would put a board spill to a vote.
- Approvals for any control deal: ACCC notification under the mandatory regime is likely (Count's Skybridge and Apiary deals are both subject to ACCC approval) [S37, S38]; licence (ASIC) change-of-control matters would also apply.

### Is a control transaction likely?
Not inside six months on present evidence. For: WAG's stated aim, Pilgrim's steady buying, the lapsed proportional takeover clauses, a fragmented licensee market where Count is buying, and InterPrac's collapse pushing advisers to look for new licensees. Against: no approach, no board statement, WAG can't fund a bid, Thorney last sold, Sequoia still has shares to sell, and the obvious buyer (Count) has just committed to Skybridge (completion in Q3 FY27) and pays 6.6 to 7.5 times EBITA, which is about A$0.36 to A$0.41 a share for Centrepoint before synergies.
