### The ACCC case
What is alleged [C1, C2, D6]. On 21 July 2026 the ACCC sued Dusk Australasia Pty Ltd in the Federal Court (NSD1332/2026). It alleges that between 17 May 2023 and 12 December 2024 dusk supplied 54,635 units across 25 product lines (MoodMist diffusers and LED Christmas, Easter and Halloween figures) before they were tested to the mandatory button battery safety standard (they passed when tested between 12 August and 14 September 2024), and 11,828 units of four Christmas candle lines without the mandatory warnings between 24 October and 12 December 2024. The ACCC's release says 66,453 units in all; the schedules add up to 66,463. It also alleges each supply breached dusk's 2023 undertaking. Relief sought: declarations, penalties, costs, an order to pay any financial benefit from the breaches, and other orders [C2]. dusk says the four candle lines were recalled in December 2024 and it knows of no harm [D6].

dusk's record [C3, C1]. On 1 May 2023 the ACCC announced that dusk had paid eight infringement notices (A$106,560) for four Halloween novelty products, 7,720 units supplied from 30 August to 27 October 2022, which hadn't been tested and lacked the warnings, and that it had accepted a court-enforceable undertaking (dated 27 April 2023) in which dusk admitted the products failed both standards and undertook to run a compliance program for three years. The first sale in the ACCC's new schedule is 17 May 2023, three weeks later [C1].

The precedent [C4, C5, C6]. City Beach (Fewstone Pty Ltd), sued on about 2 April 2025, was ordered on 22 December 2025 to pay A$14m: one penalty for 54,819 supplies in breach of the safety standard and 56,974 in breach of the information standard, from 22 June 2022 to 24 October 2024, across 63 and 67 products. Most of its products were also simply untested: of 63 not tested before supply, 22 were tested later, and 8 failed while 14 passed [C4, para 58]. City Beach earned only A$542,314 of revenue and A$34,368 of profit from them; its own profit was A$26.6m, A$21.5m and A$17.6m in FY22 to FY24 on revenue of about A$315m to A$331m [C4, paras 62 to 64, 114]. The ACCC sought A$14m; City Beach proposed A$3.15m (A$4.5m less 30%) [C4, para 150]. The judge weighed no compliance system, slow and "condemnable" recall, sales after the ACCC's notices and children's products, against admissions, cooperation, no reported harm and no prior record [C4, paras 54, 120 to 122, 142 to 148, 157]. The Full Court dismissed every ground of appeal on 19 August 2026: general deterrence is the main purpose; the penalty should not be "watered down" because little profit was made; a ratio to profit is not the test; prior contraventions can weigh more than the seriousness of the conduct itself (citing Pattinson); and the maximum for each contravention after November 2022 is about A$50m [C5, paras 8, 37, 57 to 64, 93, 151]. All of dusk's alleged conduct is after November 2022.

How big, for dusk (A$m) [my arithmetic in python3 -I from C4 and D1]:
- City Beach's A$14m was 4.4% of its revenue, 0.64 times its three-year average profit (A$21.9m) and about A$244 a product.
- Scaled to dusk's revenue in the breach years (FY24 and FY25 average A$132m): A$5.8m. Scaled by profit at City Beach's ratio (dusk's FY24 to FY26 average underlying profit A$5.9m): A$3.8m. Per unit, then scaled by revenue: A$6.7m.
- Lower than City Beach for: every tested line passed; a quick recall once the ACCC called; a fifth as many information-standard units; homewares and novelties rather than children's toys (though several are LED snowmen, reindeer and bunnies).
- Higher than City Beach for: a prior record of the same kind, an undertaking breached within weeks, and a second, different breach (missing warnings) weeks after the testing breach was fixed. The Full Court's reading of Pattinson makes this record count heavily.
- My estimate: A$7m central, with A$4m (20%), A$7m (45%), A$10m (25%) and A$14m (10%): expected A$7.85m. Penalties are not tax deductible (section 26-5 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997). Add about A$0.5m for the ACCC's costs and any financial-benefit order (dusk's own costs were provided for in FY26). I use A$8m in total. Each A$1m is 1.6c a share.
- Timing: City Beach took under nine months from filing to penalty with liability admitted [C6, C4]. At that pace dusk's would land about mid-April 2027, but no hearing dates are public and dusk hasn't said whether it admits liability. I put about a 10% chance on a penalty before 5 March 2027, and about 20% on a provision or agreed figure appearing in the half-year accounts. Directors said in August that an outflow is "probable" but can't yet be measured [D1, note 36].

## Corrections to the fact sheet
1. The position cap is the other way round: 2.5% of the portfolio is about A$2,016, which binds before the A$3,690 liquidity limit. The fact sheet says the liquidity cap is "well under the 2.5% sizing limit".
2. City Beach's products did not "actually fail" as a rule: like dusk's, most were untested; of 22 lines tested later, 8 failed and 14 passed [C4, para 58]. The difference between the cases is smaller than the fact sheet says.
3. The fact sheet leaves out dusk's own record: eight infringement notices (A$106,560) and the 27 April 2023 undertaking, with the alleged new conduct starting on 17 May 2023 [C3, C1]. 4. Performance rights: 4,823,028 are on issue (502,173 lapsed on 9 September, not only the CEO's 414,953), and the 12 August reset allows up to 7.5 million in all for seven executives, about 11% of the fully diluted shares [D9, D11, D12]. Underlying profit leaves their cost out.
5. Katherine Ostin left at the close of the 16 October 2026 AGM, not "on or before 31 December 2026" [D7].
6. The penalty could come inside six months: City Beach went from filing to penalty in under nine months, which for dusk would be about mid-April 2027. It is still unlikely before the February result.
7. Operating cash flow of A$23.3m is after A$2.7m of lease interest; total lease cash was A$20.0m, not A$17.4m [D1].
8. The 10.5 times multiple in the fact sheet's anchors is Shaver Shop's; dusk has traded at 5.5 to 9.2 times underlying profit at its June dates and after its last result, and at 10 times or more only for days after strong halves (February 2025 and the February 2026 peak).
