## Revised after the debate
The case against (research/against/MAD.md) found four things this case for got wrong or undersold. Round 1 of the debate (research/debate/MAD.md) accepted them:
- The board has no independent director (Mader's own governance statement). After Burton leaves on 24 November it will be three executives. That makes a poorly priced acquisition more likely, so the worse case goes from 10% to 12% and the good case from 20% to 18%.
- Burton didn't sell every year: he sold in March 2022, then nothing until November 2024, then about 5m shares in ten months.
- The AGM seeks authority for up to 10.57m more incentive securities over three years, more than the buyback can absorb. Shares in 2028 are about 212m, not 209m.
- Mader is cheaper than Monadelphous and SRG, but not than Perenti or NRW on their underlying earnings. The "cheapest of its peers" claim below used statutory figures that flatter fleet-heavy contractors.

The revised card: 37% at A$4.04, 45% at A$7.39, 18% at A$9.67. The expected gain after costs is about +18%, which passes. The bad case is 26% below A$5.46, inside the limit, but the middling case is +35.4% against the +39% the test then asks for. It qualifies at about A$5.38. ## Where it lands
On the line, and on the passing side. On my numbers:
- the bad case is 24% below the price, inside the 30% limit;
- the middling case is +37.6%, against the +35.9% the value test asks for;
- the expected gain after costs is about +22%, against the 12.9% minimum.

It passes at today's A$5.46 and up to about A$5.50. That margin is smaller than my uncertainty: a small change in any input (two million shares, or half a turn on the multiple) flips the middling test either way.

The evidence outside the filings says the fall is mostly about who's selling and what management plans to do with the cash, not about the business:
- demand for mine maintenance is strong;
- Mader's growth record is unbroken;
- the selling came from a fund leaving, staff receiving shares from a five-year bonus scheme, and a long-time director who has been selling for two years and leaves the board on 24 November.

What the market fears is real but bounded. The dividend has been stopped to build cash for acquisitions, and the founder controls half the company.

## What the price assumes
The business is valued at about A$1,115m: A$1,151m of shares less A$35.7m of net cash [S1].

FY26 [S1, S2]:
- revenue A$1,001.1m, up 15%;
- EBITDA A$120.7m (12.1% of revenue, from 12.5%);
- profit after tax A$65.4m, up 15%;
- operating cash flow A$85.0m, less A$27.4m of vehicles and equipment and A$2.6m of leases: about A$55m of free cash flow;
- return on invested capital 25%.

FY27 guidance: revenue of at least A$1.13bn and profit of at least A$72.5m, up 11% [S1].

So the business is valued at:
- 15.9 times FY27 guided profit, or 17.6 times FY26's;
- 9.2 times FY26 EBITDA;
- a 4.8% free cash flow yield.

The price assumes Mader hits guidance and grows only modestly after that. Only at the market low of mid-2022 has Mader traded lower against the coming year's profit [S16]. On statutory figures the listed mining-services companies trade at 18 to 30 times trailing profit [S17], but on underlying earnings Perenti is about 10 times and NRW about 15 (research/against/MAD.md). Mader is cheaper than Monadelphous and SRG, the closest maintenance-led peers.

## The one thing the market is missing
Stated as the rulebook asks: the market treats Mader's fall as a verdict on the business, but most of the selling since August has come from holders with their own reasons to sell:
- a fund leaving;
- about 50 staff receiving A$51.8m of shares in October;
- a director who sold in 2022 and has sold about 5m shares since November 2024.

Meanwhile the business keeps meeting its targets and its customers' maintenance demand is at a record.

## The test outside the filings
Done on 9 November 2026, under docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md step 2. The questions the filings can't answer:
1. Why have the shares fallen 38% since February, and is it the business or the holders?
2. Is the finance chief's exit a warning?
3. Is demand for mine maintenance holding up?
4. How cheap is Mader against its own history and its peers?
5. How big is the acquisition risk behind the "industrial conglomerate" plan?

What the evidence showed:

1. The falls came on results days and from selling, not from missed targets.
   - The price path (Norgate) [S16]:
     - A$8.83 on 23 February 2026;
     - the 1H result on 24 February deferred the interim dividend to build cash for acquisitions. The shares fell 15% to 19% during the day (reports differ) and closed down about 4% [S18];
     - the FY26 result on 25 August met guidance but confirmed no dividend and an "opportunity warchest". The shares closed at A$6.63, from A$7.24, down 8.4% [S16, S19].
   - Capital Group, a US fund manager that held 6.1%, ceased to be a substantial holder on 3 September [S9]. The board started a buyback of up to A$30m four days later [S10]. By 6 November it had bought 609,526 shares at A$5.36 to A$5.77 [S11].
   - On 2 October Mader issued 7.965m shares, worth A$51.8m, to about 50 staff. This was the final tranche of the bonus scheme set up in 2021 for the five-year plan [S15, S20]. Staff are taxed when they receive such shares, so some of them usually sell.
   - Craig Burton, a non-executive director since 2012 and an early backer, holds 33.0m shares through Skye Alba [S3]. He has sold 982,000 at A$6.71 (November 2024), 1m at A$6.15 (February 2025), and 4m at A$8.40 (September 2025) [S21]. On 23 October he said he won't stand for re-election and will leave the board at the AGM on 24 November [S8].
   - Luke Mader, the founder, sold 10m shares at A$6.15 in June 2024 and still holds 103.8m [S3, S21].
   - Short positions are about 0.1% of the shares [S15]. Mader isn't in the S&P/ASX 200 or 300, so index funds weren't sellers [S16].
2. The finance chief's exit looks like the end of a bonus scheme, not a warning.
   - Paul Hegarty stepped down on 29 October, four weeks after the final tranche vested. He and two other executives shared 2.5m shares and A$2.5m in cash from it [S20]. Patrick Conway, a former chief executive and finance chief, is acting finance chief [S7].
   - The shares fell 11% that day on 2.4m shares, about twenty times a normal day. The ASX asked why, and Mader said it knew of nothing unannounced and the business was running as usual [S12].
   - Nothing in the accounts suggests a problem:
     - operating cash flow before tax was about the same as EBITDA;
     - receivables grew 12% against revenue growth of 15%;
     - debtor days were 67;
     - the auditor, BDO, gave a clean opinion [S1, S2].
   - This is a reading, not proof. Mader hasn't said why he left.
3. Demand for mine maintenance is at a record.
   - Monadelphous, one of the largest listed maintenance contractors in Western Australia, grew maintenance revenue 20% to a record A$1.61bn in FY26. It reported strong maintenance demand from iron ore and energy customers [S22].
   - Mader's own figures [S1, S2]:
     - headcount up 600 to more than 4,500;
     - Australian revenue up 16%;
     - North American revenue up 17% in constant currency (Canada strong, the US harder).
   - The risk is iron ore. The benchmark was about US$90 a tonne in late October, near four-year lows on the Dalian exchange [S23]. The big Pilbara producers make money at that price, and maintenance follows tonnes moved, not price. But Mader is a flexible workforce, and miners cut flexible labour first in a downturn.
4. It's cheap against its history and its peers.
   - Against its own history (Norgate and the annual results) [S16, S2]:
     - at the June 2022 low it was about 11.5 times the profit it went on to make the next year, and about 16 times the year just ended;
     - at the September 2024 low it was about 17 times the coming year's profit;
     - at the 2025 peak it was about 30 times.
   - Today it's 15.9 times the coming year's guidance.
   - The listed peers, at trailing profit [S17]:
     - Monadelphous 22.6 times;
     - SRG Global 30.1;
     - NRW 18.5;
     - Perenti 23.4;
     - Macmahon 20.0.
   - Mader has net cash and a higher return on capital than any of them.
5. The acquisition risk is real but small so far.
   - The new plan calls Mader a future "globally diversified industrial conglomerate". It targets about 15% EPS growth a year, A$30m to A$50m of capital spending a year and a "measured leverage position" [S2].
   - The chief executive told investors the group is "allergic to debt" and would fund purchases with cash [S18].
   - The only purchase so far is Wilson Hammond Group, a defence consultancy, for up to A$5.3m [S20].
   - The bigger governance points:
     - Luke Mader is executive chairman and controls about 49%;
     - the board has no independent director, and after the AGM it will be three executives (corrected after the debate);
     - the AGM asks holders to approve a new equity plan and 1.5m performance rights for the chief executive [S6].

What this changes: it supports the middling case. Demand, the targets record and the cash flow all back it. It also explains the fall without a fault in the business. It leaves the worse case where it was, because a mining downturn and a bad acquisition are both still possible.

## The company in plain words
Mader supplies skilled tradespeople, mostly heavy-diesel mechanics, to keep mining trucks, diggers and other heavy equipment running. They work on customers' sites, and Mader charges by the hour [S2]. Luke Mader started it in the Pilbara in 2005 with one ute. It listed in 2019 at A$1.00 [S2, S21].

It now has:
- more than 4,500 staff in 10 countries;
- more than 520 customers [S2].

Its newer lines (rail, road transport, infrastructure maintenance, energy, marine) are growing faster than the core: infrastructure maintenance up 30% and road transport up 63% in FY26 [S1]. North America is 18% of revenue, from a standing start in 2018 [S1].

Customer spread [S1]:
- the five largest customers are about 28% of revenue, and the top ten about 36%;
- no customer is more than 10%;
- contracts are mostly non-exclusive.

Its record against its first five-year plan, in profit after tax [S2]:

| FY23 | A$32m | A$39m |
| FY24 | A$40m | A$50m |
| FY25 | A$51m | A$57m |

## What the filings show
FY26 by segment [S1]:
- Australia: revenue A$797.7m, up 16%; EBITDA A$92.4m, up 11.4%;
- North America: revenue A$186.6m, up 12%; EBITDA A$32.8m, up only 3.1%, as the US stayed hard;

Margins slipped: group EBITDA fell from 12.5% to 12.1% of revenue, and was 11.6% in the first half [S1, S4]. FY27 guidance has profit growing more slowly than revenue (11% against 13%), because of "significant early investment" [S2].

Capital returns:

## Valuation
Worked at 31 August 2028, after the FY28 result, about 22 months out. By then the market prices the year to June 2029 (FY29).

The assumptions:
- Shares about 209m: 210.8m today, less about 4.5m from finishing the buyback, plus about 2.5m of new staff equity.
- Net cash about A$95m at June 2028 in the middling case:
  - A$35.7m today;
  - plus about A$99m of free cash flow over FY27 and FY28: operating cash flow of A$90m to A$100m a year less A$40m to A$45m of capital spending, the middle of the company's A$30m to A$50m range;
  - less about A$27m for the rest of the buyback, A$2.5m of bonus cash and about A$10m of small purchases.
- The middling case uses today's multiple, about 16 times the coming year's profit, so it assumes no re-rating.

Arithmetic for the middling case:
- Profit: FY27 about A$73.5m (guidance is at least A$72.5m, and Mader has met or beaten every target since FY22), FY28 about A$82m, FY29 about A$92m.
- Value: 16 times A$92m is A$1,475m. Plus A$95m of net cash is A$1,570m, or A$7.51 a share on 209m shares.

What the value depends on most: the multiple. Each turn of profit multiple is worth about A$0.44 a share in the middling case.

## Why the market is wrong
1. It reads the falls as a problem with the business. They came on results days that met guidance, and from holders with their own reasons to sell: a fund leaving, staff receiving bonus shares, and a director who has been selling since November 2024.
2. It prices Mader below Monadelphous and SRG, the closest maintenance-led peers, though Mader has net cash, a 25% return on capital and five years of meeting its targets.
3. It treats the finance chief's exit as a warning. It came four weeks after his five-year bonus vested, and the accounts show nothing amiss.

It's right that:
- margins slipped in FY26 and FY27 profit will grow more slowly than revenue;
- the founder controls the company and wants to buy his way into new industries with cash that used to go to dividends;
- iron ore is near four-year lows;
- Burton's 15.7% may come to market after 24 November.

These chances were set after the evidence from outside the filings. Monadelphous's record maintenance year and Mader's unbroken record against its targets are why the middling case is the likeliest. Iron ore and the acquisition plan keep the worse and bad cases at 35% together.

For the card, the first two combine into one bad case: 35% at A$4.15 (the chance-weighted average of A$3.18 and A$4.54). The card would read 35% at A$4.15, 45% at A$7.51 and 20% at A$9.81.

Expected price: 0.35 × 4.153 + 0.45 × 7.512 + 0.20 × 9.81 = A$6.80, or +24.5% before costs. After about 2% of costs it's about +22%. The costs are A$15 brokerage each way on about A$2,250, half the 0.2% spread each way, and the 0.5% currency fee. The rulebook minimum over 661 days is 12.9%, so that test passes.

The value test (rulebook section 5a):
- The bad case of A$4.15 is 23.9% below A$5.46, inside the 30% limit.
- Against that fall the middling case needs to be +35.9%, and it's +37.6%.
- So the test passes, narrowly.

It passes up to about A$5.50.

What would flip it:
- with 211m shares instead of 209m (no further buyback), it fails by about two cents;
- with a middling multiple of 15 instead of 16, it fails, and it only passes at about A$5.30.

## What would make this wrong
- Iron ore falls further, and Pilbara miners cut contract labour.
- Mader buys a large business outside its trade, at a full price.
- Margins keep slipping as the new lines and North America grow faster than their profits.
- Mader can't recruit enough mechanics, or wages rise faster than it can pass them on. Migration settings affect its overseas recruiting.
- Burton sells his whole stake in one go at a deep discount. That would be a price risk more than a value risk.

Exit rule, if it's ever bought: sell if FY27 profit comes in below A$70m, if Mader takes on net debt for an acquisition, or if a single purchase costs more than about A$150m. A close 25% below the buy price means a review, not a sale.
