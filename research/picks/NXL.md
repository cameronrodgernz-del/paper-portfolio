## Revised after the debate
The case against (research/against/NXL.md) and round 1 of the debate (research/debate/NXL.md) changed three things:
- Macquarie doesn't share the class action liability in any useful way. The co-defendant is Macquarie Capital, one of the float's lead managers, and Nuix indemnified the lead managers against losses from the offer. The only exclusions are their own recklessness, gross negligence, fraud or wilful misconduct (prospectus section 9.8.1.5). Macquarie Capital has already claimed A$9.15m of legal fees under it. So the worse case is a larger loss, about A$150m net, and a raising.
- Nuix's HSBC revolving facility can't be used for litigation costs (FY26 report, note 4.7).
- Revenue is running ahead of ACV because more deals are multi-year: they accounted for about A$32.3m of the A$41.7m revenue growth. Net cash at June 2028 in the middling case falls from about A$150m to about A$110m, after lease debt and continuing legal costs.

The revised outcomes: worse 10% A$0.90, bad 25% A$1.29, middling 45% A$2.54, good 20% A$3.70. The card would read 35% at A$1.18, 45% at A$2.54 and 20% at A$3.70. The expected gain after costs is about +17%, which passes. But the bad case is 39% below A$1.925, outside the 30% limit. It qualifies at about A$1.68. Even with no legal risk at all, it would qualify at only about A$1.84 (the debate file has the working). The sections below are the case as first written.

## Where it lands
On the line, and the best of the four looked at so far. On my numbers the expected gain is about +24% after costs to 31 August 2028, nearly twice the 12.9% minimum. The bad case is 28% below the price, inside the 30% limit. But the middling case, about +38%, falls just short of the +42% the value test asks for against a fall of that size. It passes at about A$1.89, two cents below where it trades, and in October it traded between A$1.775 and A$1.92.

The evidence outside the filings backs the business:
- Government buyers, including ASIC (the regulator still appealing against Nuix), signed much larger Nuix contracts in 2026, which fits the move to its newer platform, Nuix Neo.
- The class action that hangs over the shares looks weaker than the market fears.

What keeps it from clearing is how low the market priced Nuix only four months ago: 1.3 times revenue in July. That's my bad case.

## What the price assumes
The business is valued at about A$611m: A$661.3m of shares less A$49.9m of net cash [S1].

FY26 [S1]:
- annualised contract value (ACV, the yearly value of contracts in force) A$260.0m, up 13.9%, or 8.6% before the Linkurious purchase (11.1% in constant currency);
- revenue A$263.2m, up 18.8%;
- adjusted management EBITDA, which counts all research and development spending and share-based pay as costs, A$59.8m (22.7% of revenue, from 16.8%);
- underlying cash flow A$51.0m, and free cash flow A$37.4m after legal and other one-off costs.

FY27 guidance [S1]:
- ACV of A$285m to A$300m;
- adjusted management EBITDA similar to FY26, after a one-off A$15m spend on product development. Without it, about A$75m.

So the business is valued at:
- 2.3 times FY26 revenue;
- 2.1 times FY27's guided ACV;
- about 8 times FY27 EBITDA before the one-off spend.

Shares in Cellebrite, the closest listed peer in digital investigations, trade at roughly 4 times revenue on my estimate. It has grown faster, but it also cut its outlook in August [S20].

The price assumes Nuix delivers FY27 guidance and that the market keeps paying today's multiple, with little growth beyond that. In July, before the FY26 result, it paid only 1.3 times revenue (A$1.16 a share) [S14].

## The one thing the market is missing
Stated as the rulebook asks: the market prices Nuix as a slow-growing software company with a large lawsuit hanging over it. But the public contract records show government customers, including the regulator suing it, paying much more for Nuix Neo, and the court record shows the class action narrowing after ASIC lost its parallel case.

## The test outside the filings
Done on 9 November 2026, under docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md step 2. The questions the filings can't answer:
1. Is the move to Nuix Neo bringing in real new money from customers, or just repackaging old contracts?
2. How big is the class action risk?
3. Is the digital investigations market slowing?
4. Who has been selling, and who is left?

What the evidence showed:

1. Government customers are paying more, and the regulator suing Nuix is one of them.
   - AusTender's contract notices for Nuix, summed by the financial year they were lodged (my working from the full AusTender file in the repo) [S15]:

     | Financial year | Contract value lodged | Notices |
     | --- | --- | --- |
     | FY23 | A$12.6m | 15 |
     | FY24 | A$1.1m | 5 |
     | FY25 | A$3.7m | 7 |
     | FY26 | A$15.0m | 14 |
     | FY27 to October | A$20.7m | 15 |

   - The largest single change: on 5 August 2026 ASIC added about A$17.5m to its Nuix evidence management contract, which runs to November 2027 (it was A$5.2m when signed in 2022). ASIC is the regulator appealing its loss against Nuix [S15].
   - Others in 2026: the Australian Taxation Office (A$6.1m to 2028), the Australian Federal Police (A$3.1m to 2028), the ACCC (A$3.2m to 2028), Defence (A$2.8m) and several Attorney-General's Department notices in October [S15].
   - In the US, the Department of Justice's Criminal Division ordered "Nuix Neo Foundations" worth up to US$5.1m over two years in June 2026, through a reseller [S16]. Canada's public works department also bought Nuix Neo Discover [S16].
   - Sense check: contract notices record multi-year values when signed, so they are lumpy (FY23 was high too). But the last 16 months, A$35.7m, compare with about A$17.4m over the three years before. That fits the company's own figures: Nuix Neo ACV up 179% to A$78.5m, and a 30% to 50% uplift when a customer moves to it [S2].
2. The class action looks weaker than the price allows for.
   - The case, Lay and Batchelor v Nuix and Macquarie, covers shareholders who bought between the prospectus of November 2020 and June 2021. It went to an eight-week trial in the Supreme Court of Victoria from 3 August 2026, and judgment is reserved [S3, S17].
   - During the trial the plaintiffs dropped their claims about ACV and under consumer law [S3].
   - In April 2026 the Federal Court dismissed all of ASIC's claims over Nuix's disclosures from January to April 2021. It found Nuix had reasonable grounds for its forecasts and that a 3.5% gap to forecast wasn't material [S18].
   - Nuix's insurance covers claims above a A$10m retention (the Full Federal Court settled that amount in July 2026) [S19].
   - Macquarie Capital, a lead manager of the float, is a co-defendant [S17]. Corrected after the debate: Nuix indemnifies the lead managers under the 2020 underwriting agreement, so in most outcomes Nuix would carry Macquarie Capital's share too.
   - The risks:
     - the prospectus claims carry a stricter test than ASIC's case did, and the trial heard internal emails in which the former finance chief called Nuix's record of disappointing results "worse than terrible" [S17];
     - the first shareholder class action to win at trial in Australia (Brambles, April 2026) shows plaintiffs can win [S18].
   - I put the chance of a judgment costing Nuix around A$100m, after insurance and Macquarie's share, at about 10%. That's the worse case.
3. The market is slowing a little. Cellebrite cut its 2026 outlook in August on longer government sales cycles and smaller price rises when customers moved to its new platform, and its shares fell more than 35% [S20]. The same risk applies to Nuix Neo. Cellebrite reports again on 12 November.
4. Forced selling has passed, and the biggest holder is still there.
   - Nuix left the S&P/ASX 300 on 23 March 2026 [S14]. AMP and ECP sold out in August [S21]. Short positions are about 0.7% [S13].
   - Australian Ethical bought, from 6.14% to 7.22% in October [S21].
   - Macquarie still owns 95.65m shares, 27.8% [S21]. It has held since the float and is a co-defendant. It's either an overhang or a natural bidder.

What this changes: it backs the middling case and trims the worse case, so the chances moved a little (see Outcomes and chances). The gap it finds is real but not large. Most of the cheapness was in July, at A$1.16, before the FY26 result.

## The company in plain words
Nuix makes software that processes and searches huge volumes of messy data (emails, documents, phones, chat logs) for investigations, regulators, law enforcement, eDiscovery and privacy work [S1]. It sells:
- Nuix Neo, a platform that bundles its tools, now 30% of ACV;
- older component products, which customers are moving to Neo;
- Discover, a legal review product;
- Linkurious, a graph analysis product bought in April 2026 [S1, S4].

Customers include about 100 regulators and many police and government agencies [S16].

History in brief:
- It floated in December 2020 at A$5.31, with Macquarie selling down.
- It missed its prospectus forecasts in 2021, which led to the class action and ASIC's case [S17, S18].
- The shares recovered to about A$6.28 in December 2024 [S14], then fell through 2025 as growth came in at the low end of guidance [S5]. The chief executive left in October 2025 [S6].
- John Ruthven, interim chief executive from November 2025, was appointed permanently in May 2026 [S7].

## What the filings show
FY26 [S1, S2]:
- ACV bridge: ACV went from A$228.4m to A$260.0m:
  - Neo added A$50.4m;
  - the older components lost A$28.2m, to migration and some downsell;
  - Discover lost A$2.7m;
  - Linkurious added A$12.0m.
- Net dollar retention 105.2%; churn 6.6%.
- Multi-year deals were 35% of revenue, from 27%, which lifts revenue ahead of ACV.
- Statutory profit after tax A$16.4m, after A$13.0m of legal costs, mostly the class action.

Balance sheet at 30 June 2026 [S3]:
- cash A$69.9m;
- a A$20.1m loan for the Linkurious purchase;
- deferred revenue A$68.3m;
- net assets A$313.1m.

Contingent liabilities, with nothing provided [S3]:
- ASIC's appeal, which concerns the company only;
- the class action.

## Valuation
Worked at August 2028, after the FY28 result, about 22 months out.

The assumptions:
- Shares about 350m, allowing for share-based pay.
- Net cash builds from A$49.9m to about A$150m by June 2028 in the middling case. That's about A$40m of free cash flow in FY27 (A$60m of EBITDA less legal costs, tax and leases) and about A$65m to A$70m in FY28.

The middling case uses the same forward multiple the market pays today (about 8 times next year's EBITDA before one-offs, or 2.1 times next year's ACV), so it assumes no re-rating.

| Case | What it assumes | Value of the business | Net cash after any claim | A$ a share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Worse | Revenue about A$280m, 1.2 times; a class action costing about A$100m net | A$336m | A$20m | 1.02 |
| Bad | Growth slows to about 5% a year; revenue about A$290m, at 1.4 times (July was 1.3) | A$406m | A$130m | 1.53 |
| Middling | FY27 guidance met, ACV up about 11% in FY28; about A$95m of FY29 EBITDA at today's 8.2 times | A$779m | A$150m | 2.65 |
| Good | ACV growth of 15% or more and margins near 30%, valued at about 3.3 times revenue, or a bid | about A$1,170m | A$160m | 3.80 |

Arithmetic for the middling case:
- EBITDA: FY28 adjusted EBITDA about A$85m (a 26% margin on about A$325m of revenue), and about A$95m expected for FY29.
- Value: 8.2 times A$95m is A$779m. Plus A$150m of net cash is A$929m, or A$2.65 a share.

What the value depends on most: the multiple. Each half-turn of revenue multiple is worth about A$0.46 a share in the middling case.

## Why the market is wrong
1. It still prices in a damaging class action, after ASIC's parallel case failed and the plaintiffs dropped part of their claim.
2. It reads slower group growth as a weak product. But much of FY26's slowdown was older components moving to Neo at a 30% to 50% uplift, and government buyers are signing larger contracts.

It's right that the company's record on forecasts is poor (2021 and 2025), that Cellebrite shows government sales cycles are lengthening, and that FY27 profit will be flat because of the one-off development spend.

## Outcomes and chances
Resolve date: 31 August 2028, after the FY28 result (661 days away). No dividends are assumed.

| Outcome | What happens | Chance | Price by resolve date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Worse | The class action goes badly (about A$100m net) and growth stalls | 10% | A$1.02 |
| Bad | Growth slows to about 5% a year and the market goes back to about July's multiple | 25% | A$1.53 |
| Middling | FY27 guidance is met and growth continues at about 11%, with no re-rating | 45% | A$2.65 |
| Good | Growth of 15% or more and a re-rating, or a takeover bid | 20% | A$3.80 |

These chances were set after the evidence from outside the filings. The government contracts and the court record are why the worse case is only 10% and the middling case is the likeliest. Cellebrite's warning keeps the bad case at 25%.

For the card, the first two combine into one bad case: 35% at A$1.38 (the chance-weighted average of A$1.02 and A$1.53). The card would read 35% at A$1.38, 45% at A$2.65 and 20% at A$3.80.

Expected price: 0.35 × 1.384 + 0.45 × 2.65 + 0.20 × 3.80 = A$2.44, or +26.6% before costs. After about 2% of costs it's about +24.5%. The costs are A$15 brokerage each way on about A$2,250, half the 0.3% spread each way, and the 0.5% currency fee. The rulebook minimum over 661 days is 12.9%, so that test passes.

The value test (rulebook section 5a):
- The bad case of A$1.38 is 28.1% below A$1.925, inside the 30% limit.
- Against that fall the middling case would need to be +42.2%, and it's +37.7%.
- So the test fails, narrowly.

It passes at about A$1.89. There the bad case is 26.8% below, the middling case is +40.2% (it needs +40.2%), and the expected gain after costs is about +27%.

## What would make this wrong
- The ACV uplift from moving to Neo is a one-off. Once the older components have moved, growth falls back to single digits.
- AI cuts the price of eDiscovery and review work faster than Nuix can charge for its own AI modules.
- The class action judgment goes against Nuix and Macquarie, and the damages are well above the insurance.
- Macquarie sells its 27.8% in a block at a discount.

Exit rule, if it's ever bought: sell if FY27 ACV comes in below A$280m, if the class action costs Nuix more than A$150m net, or if net dollar retention falls below 100%. A close 25% below the buy price means a review, not a sale.
