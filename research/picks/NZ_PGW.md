## The company in plain words
PGG Wrightson calls itself New Zealand's leading rural services business. It has two operating groups [S1, segment note]:
- Retail & Water (FY26 revenue NZ$851.2m, operating EBITDA NZ$44.5m): Rural Supplies stores, Fruitfed Horticulture, Agritrade (agrichemicals and animal health wholesale, including Nexan, an animal health manufacturer bought for NZ$19.9m on 31 July 2025, and the Blue AG private-label range) and PGW Water (irrigation). It earns a margin on farm and orchard inputs, mostly in the spring.
- Agency (FY26 revenue NZ$221.5m, operating EBITDA NZ$29.0m): livestock agency (commission on sales, the bidr online auction, about 30% of saleyard bids [S2]), GO-STOCK (PGW buys livestock for a farmer, keeps title and is repaid on sale, up to 540 days, NZ$88.3m of receivables at June 2026), wool and rural real estate. It earns commissions and GO-STOCK fees, mostly in the second half.
- A corporate cost centre of about NZ$9m a year.
Everything is in New Zealand. The first half (July to December) carries the profit: in FY26 Retail & Water earned NZ$41.8m of its NZ$44.5m in the first half, while Agency earned NZ$20.3m of its NZ$29.0m in the second [S4][S1].

Who runs it: Stephen Guerin, chief executive since 1 June 2019 [S22]; Brian Harrison, chief financial officer from November 2026 after Peter Scott left at the end of October [S19]. The board has five directors: John Nichol (independent chair, reappointed by the remaining directors on 14 October 2025 after the annual meeting removed the chair and deputy chair), Dr Charlotte Severne and Wilson Liu (independent; Liu is Hong Kong resident and joined on 1 July 2025), U Kean Seng (not independent: associated with Agria) and Mark Allison (not independent: managing director of Elders until about 30 September 2026, joined 1 July 2026) [S3][S13][S14][S15].

Who owns it (registered holders at 31 July 2026 [S3, p. 133]): Agria (Singapore) Pte Ltd 33,463,399 shares (44.33%); Elders Limited 9,409,296 (12.47%); New Zealand Depository Nominee 2.58%; ACC 1.76%; HSBC Nominees 1.47%; Custodial Services 1.09%; Forsyth Barr Custodians 0.88%; JBWere Nominees 0.77%; GMH 38 Investments 0.66%; FNZ Custodians 0.42%; then individuals and H & G Limited (0.39%), each under 0.4%. There are 8,567 holders. Substantial holders: the Agria group (notices of 17 December 2018 and 10 April 2019) and Elders (notices of 14 December 2022 and 18 August 2023) [S3][S17].

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
Some of the usual reasons apply, and they are also reasons for a lasting discount.
- Size and index: NZ$165m, outside the NZX 20 (config/nzx_top20.csv). I didn't verify NZX 50 membership.
- Float and trading: 43.2% of the shares are outside Agria and Elders, about NZ$71m at the price, and the median day trades NZ$24,880 [S20].
- Register: two foreign holders control 56.8% and decide every board vote. In October 2025 they removed the independent chair and deputy chair, who got 85.5% of votes against them; the holders other than Agria and Elders who voted backed them by about 99 to 1 [S13]. In March 2024 the NZ Shareholders' Association wrote to every holder calling Agria's request to replace three independent directors "one of the worst cases of board interference by a majority shareholder in the last few years" [S16].
- Coverage: I found no broker research in primary sources and didn't look further.
- Not out of favour: New Zealand farming is having one of its best years. MPI forecast record food and fibre exports of NZ$64.3b for the year to June 2026, red meat had "an exceptional year", and dairy farmers received a capital return from Fonterra's sale of Mainland Group as well as a strong milk price [S3, p. 16].

## What the filings show

### FY26 cleaned up, and what the FY27 guidance means
FY26 operating EBITDA of NZ$64.3m included a credit loss charge of NZ$4.6m, against an average release of about NZ$0.5m a year over FY22 to FY25 [S1, notes 3 and 11][S10][S9][S8][S7]. The note says it is "a significant provision in respect of one customer which has entered liquidation / administration"; at the annual meeting the chief executive named the customer as Kiwi Crunch Group and said PGW "provisioned the entire exposure of around $5 million" [S1, note 11][S6]. Profit also included NZ$2.1m of currency gains and NZ$0.2m of non-operating gains, and tax was 26.0% of profit before tax against the 28% rate [S1]. Cleaned up, FY26 EBITDA was about NZ$68.8m to NZ$69.3m and profit about NZ$16.7m (22.2c a share) (python: (21.031 - 2.071 - 0.221 + 4.5) x 0.72 = 16.73).

FY27 guidance is "approximately $70 million" of operating EBITDA, given on 13 October 2026 on an "encouraging" first quarter, "particularly in Retail & Water", and to be reassessed "after the key second-quarter trading months" [S5]. Against the cleaned-up FY26 that is flat to +2%, not the +9% the headline numbers suggest.

Profit at NZ$70m of EBITDA. Depreciation will rise again: right-of-use assets went from NZ$81.3m to NZ$94.4m in FY26 after NZ$22.6m of lease reassessments and NZ$14.9m of new leases; software amortisation is now about NZ$4.2m a year since the Microsoft D365 system went live in April 2025; and Nexan's NZ$7.4m of product rights are amortised over seven years [S1, notes 14, 15 and 16]. I use NZ$36.0m of depreciation and amortisation (FY26 NZ$34.8m) and NZ$10.8m of net interest (FY26 NZ$10.8m: more lease interest on a larger lease book, offset by lower floating rates). That gives profit of (70 - 36.0 - 10.8) x 0.72 = NZ$16.7m, or 22.1c a share. With FY26's depreciation unchanged it would be NZ$17.6m (23.3c). The fact sheet's NZ$19.7m (26.1c) carried FY26's currency gains and lower tax rate into FY27.

### Where the cycle shows: Agency is at a peak, Retail & Water is not
Agency's NZ$29.0m in FY26 compares with a five-year average of NZ$20.5m and NZ$12.3m in FY24. The company puts it down to "historically strong sheep, cattle, and dairy prices", a "buoyant rural Real Estate market" with dairy farm sales up about 30% and horticultural property sales up about 60% [S2]. GO-STOCK fees grew to NZ$8.0m from NZ$7.2m with a larger book [S1, note 1]. Livestock earnings are weighted to the second half, so FY27's Agency number depends on prices from January to June 2027 [S5].

Retail & Water's NZ$44.5m is below its five-year average of NZ$46.9m and well below FY22 and FY23 (NZ$52.5m and NZ$54.1m), despite record revenue of NZ$851.2m [S1][S9]. Margins are thinner than they were; the company names "elevated fuel, fertiliser, financing and other farm-input costs", weakness in viticulture and arable farming, and the risk of an El Nino drought in eastern regions [S5][S6].

### Leases, debt and GO-STOCK
- Leases: NZ$100.5m of lease liabilities at June 2026 (NZ$83.5m property, NZ$17.0m vehicles), with NZ$94.5m more if all extension options were used. Lease payments were NZ$28.6m, 44% of FY26 EBITDA [S1, note 16].
- Bank debt is seasonal: NZ$85.6m at June 2025, NZ$170.7m at December 2025, NZ$88.0m at June 2026 [S4][S1]. FY26 bank interest and fees were NZ$6.8m; at about 5.5% that is an average drawn balance of about NZ$115m, which I use as average debt.
- GO-STOCK: NZ$88.3m of receivables at June 2026 (NZ$64.1m at December 2025), almost all current; only NZ$0.4m past due [S1, note 12][S4]. New facilities signed on 26 June 2026 give GO-STOCK its own NZ$115m line, funding up to 90% of the book, alongside a NZ$50m core facility and a NZ$100m seasonal working capital facility, all to 30 September 2029, with leverage covenants tested quarterly or monthly and limits on capital spending and asset sales [S1, note 9]. No refinancing falls inside any window here.
- Together, average bank debt and leases are about NZ$215m, more than the market value of the shares (NZ$165m).
- Book equity NZ$182.6m (NZ$2.42 a share); net tangible assets NZ$123.1m (NZ$1.63 a share) after NZ$53.3m of intangibles and goodwill and NZ$6.2m of deferred tax [S1, note 8]. The assets are working capital and receivables, a NZ$51.8m plant and equipment base including NZ$17.6m of land, and leased sites; none is likely to be sold for more than book.

### Cash earnings
The brief's lesson applies here. Over FY22 to FY26, operating cash flow less lease principal and capital spending was -NZ$7.2m in total. Adding back the NZ$42.5m that went into growing the GO-STOCK book (a lending decision, now 90% bank-funded), free cash flow was NZ$35.3m, or NZ$7.1m a year, against profit of NZ$71.1m (NZ$14.2m a year) and dividends paid of NZ$61.2m (NZ$12.2m a year) [S1][S7][S8][S9][S10]. Net bank debt rose NZ$81.5m from June 2021 to June 2026: the GO-STOCK book, NZ$19.7m for Nexan, and dividends paid out of debt. Two things make the record worse than the run rate: NZ$36.5m of software spending over the five years, mostly the D365 system (FY23 to FY25), and working capital built up in the inflation of FY22 and FY23. Without the excess software spending, free cash flow was closer to NZ$12m to NZ$13m a year, still below profit. FY26 alone was strong: NZ$28.9m before GO-STOCK growth, helped by capital spending of only NZ$6.6m. The dividend of 10c a share (NZ$7.5m) was covered about twice by FY26 profit; imputation credits were NZ$5.95m at June 2026 [S1, note 7].

### The record against guidance, and the market's response
Guidance was cut in FY24 (NZ$50m to NZ$43m, delivered NZ$44.2m) and met or beaten in FY25 and FY26 [fact sheet sources 6 to 11][S12]. The share price did not re-rate on the good news: it was NZ$2.17 before the December 2025 upgrade and NZ$2.16 at the end of that month; NZ$2.32 before the February 2026 half-year result and NZ$2.13 a week later; NZ$2.38 before the August 2026 full-year result and NZ$2.23 after it; NZ$2.07 before the October 2026 guidance and NZ$2.14 a week later [S20].

## Valuation
Done on PGW's filings and Elders' multiples. The key input is mid-cycle EBITDA.

### Mid-cycle EBITDA
Three ways, in FY27 terms:
- By segment: Retail & Water's five-year average (NZ$46.9m) plus Agency's (NZ$20.5m) plus about NZ$1.5m for the larger GO-STOCK book, less corporate (NZ$8.8m), plus about NZ$2.5m for Nexan: NZ$62.6m.
- By margin: the five-year average EBITDA margin (5.98% of revenue) on revenue trended at 3% a year from the five-year average (about NZ$1,070m): NZ$63.9m. This treats FY26's record revenue as trend, so it is generous.
- The five-year average itself: NZ$58.6m.
I use NZ$62m, with a range of NZ$58m to NZ$66m. That is profit of NZ$10.9m (14.5c), range 10.7c to 18.3c. The guided NZ$70m is about 13% above it, consistent with Agency at a peak and Retail & Water near normal. Mid-cycle return on book equity is 6.0%, below any reasonable cost of equity; the company's own three-year average return on capital employed is 8.1% [S2].

### Method 1: price to mid-cycle earnings
At Elders' 10 times (range 9 to 11 times) on 14.5c (10.7c to 18.3c): NZ$1.45, range NZ$0.96 to NZ$2.01. If FY27's 22.1c were the new normal: NZ$2.21 at 10 times, NZ$2.50 at 11.3 times (the NZX consumer median the fact sheet quotes).

### Method 2: enterprise value to mid-cycle EBIT, leases as debt
Mid-cycle EBIT after all depreciation, including on leased assets, is 62 - 36.0 = NZ$26.0m. At 11.4 times that is an enterprise value of NZ$296m; less lease liabilities of NZ$100.5m and average net bank debt of NZ$115m leaves NZ$80m, or NZ$1.07 a share. The range is wide because the debt and leases are large next to the equity: NZ$0.22 (NZ$58m EBITDA, 11.0 times, NZ$125m of debt) to NZ$1.97 (NZ$66m, 11.8 times, NZ$105m). On FY27's NZ$70m held for good: NZ$2.28. This method gives less than Method 1 because PGW carries about 8.3 times its mid-cycle EBIT in debt and leases, against about 4.8 times at Elders, and the P/E method charges those claims only at their interest rates.

### Method 3: cash flow to shareholders
FY27 at guidance, FY28 halfway back (NZ$66m of EBITDA), then mid-cycle NZ$62m growing 2% a year. Cash to shareholders is profit less NZ$0.5m a year for the equity share of GO-STOCK growth, with capital spending equal to depreciation on owned assets (about NZ$10.4m; FY22 to FY26 averaged NZ$14.6m including the ERP build). Cost of equity 10% (my assumption for a small, cyclical, controlled company; 9% to 11% tested): NZ$1.77 (NZ$2.01 at 9%, NZ$1.58 at 11%). If FY27's level held for good: NZ$2.59 (NZ$2.96 at 9%, NZ$2.30 at 11%).

### What a buyer of control might pay
At Delta's multiple (11.0 times EBIT before lease accounting, which charges cash rent): on FY27's NZ$70m (EBIT about 70 - 30.0 lease payments - 10.4 other depreciation = NZ$29.6m), NZ$326m less NZ$115m of debt, NZ$2.79 a share; on mid-cycle (NZ$21.6m), NZ$1.62. Delta's multiple was struck on a weak Australian year, so it is generous on FY27's strong one.

### The range
- On mid-cycle earnings (what the business is worth through a farm cycle): NZ$1.05 to NZ$1.80, central about NZ$1.45. Method 1 NZ$1.45, Method 2 NZ$1.07, Method 3 NZ$1.77, control NZ$1.62. The one input that moves it most is mid-cycle EBITDA: each NZ$1m is worth about 0.95c of earnings a share, or about 10c of value at 10 times.
- If FY27's level held for good: NZ$2.20 to NZ$2.80 (Method 1 NZ$2.21 to NZ$2.50, Method 2 NZ$2.28, Method 3 NZ$2.59, control NZ$2.79).
- At the price (for comparison only): 10.6 times FY26 reported EPS, 9.9 times cleaned-up FY26 and FY27 EPS, 15.1 times mid-cycle EPS; enterprise value with leases of NZ$381m is 11.2 times FY27 EBIT and 14.6 times mid-cycle EBIT, against Elders' 11.4 times mid-cycle; 0.91 times book and 1.34 times net tangible assets.

## The players and what the rules allow
This is a holders situation for the watch list, not a special situation pick: there is no approach, offer, review or requisition in view.

Agria (Singapore) Pte Ltd, 33,463,399 shares, 44.33%.
- History: held 50.22% until 17 December 2018, when a settlement with the Overseas Investment Office required it to sell below that level; it held 46.58% after December 2018 and on 10 April 2019 sold 17,000,000 pre-consolidation shares to H & G Limited at 49c (about NZ$4.90 after the 2019 ten-for-one consolidation, before that year's capital return) [X3][S17]. The 2019 sale needed "consent from Agria's lenders" [S17]. Its original cost and current financing I couldn't verify.
- Behaviour: on 8 February 2024 it requisitioned a meeting to remove three of four independent directors, add its own nominees and return former chair Alan Lai, then withdrew on 22 March 2024 [S16]. It complained of "inconsistent and declining business performance" and the "ongoing erosion of value", including dividends [X2]. In October 2025 it voted against the chair and deputy chair without giving reasons; Mr Lai then said it saw "an opportunity to refresh the Board" and "remains committed to its long-term investment in PGW" [S13]. It has one representative director, U Kean Seng, who may pass company information to Agria under a board protocol [S3].
- What it can do: at 44.33% the Takeovers Code's fundamental rule stops it buying any more shares except by a full offer, a partial offer conditional on taking it above 50%, or an acquisition or allotment approved by the other holders; creeping (5% in twelve months) is open only to a holder already above 50% [R1][R2]. An overseas investor going above 50% would also need Overseas Investment Office consent, and the Office made Agria sell below 50.2% after its 2018 investigation [X3]. It can sell: a buyer of its block who would go above 20% needs a Code offer or holder approval, so a block sale would come with an offer to all holders or a vote.
- What it seems to want: board influence and dividends, without paying a control premium.

Elders Limited, 9,409,296 shares, 12.47%.

"Nothing happens" value: the mid-cycle range, NZ$1.05 to NZ$1.80, central NZ$1.45.

## Why the market is wrong
I can't say it is. At NZ$2.19 the market pays about 10 times FY27's earnings, which are near the top of the cycle, and about 15 times mid-cycle earnings; Elders trades at about 10 times its own mid-cycle earnings. The price has ignored four rounds of good news, which is what a market pricing through the cycle would do. If anything the price is generous on mid-cycle earnings, and it would be cheap only if FY27's level became the new normal, which the segment record doesn't support.

## What would make this wrong
The two things most likely to make the "no case" view wrong:
1. FY26 and FY27 are a new normal, not a peak. Nexan and Blue AG lift Retail & Water margins, the GO-STOCK book keeps growing and earning fees of about 9% a year, D365 cuts costs, and tight global beef and lamb supply keeps livestock prices up for years. If mid-cycle EBITDA is really NZ$68m to NZ$70m, the value is NZ$2.20 to NZ$2.80 and the price is fair to cheap. The 1H27 Retail & Water number and FY28 guidance (October 2027) are the tests.
2. A bid. Elders (with a former Fonterra executive as chief executive and its former managing director on PGW's board) or a trade buyer buys Agria's block, bringing an offer to all holders at a control price of about NZ$2.80. Agria's 2018 history with the Overseas Investment Office, Elders' leverage after Delta and Agria's stated long-term intent all argue against it within two years; I put it at about 5%.
A third, smaller one: a cut in debt and leases (a sale of property, or vehicles moved off leases) would lift Method 2 more than the others.
Exit rule: no position. If one is opened under the trigger: sell at the resolve date, or at once if FY28 guidance is below NZ$55m of EBITDA, the dividend is suspended, or a substantial holder notice shows Agria or Elders selling.
