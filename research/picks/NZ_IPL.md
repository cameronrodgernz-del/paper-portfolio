# Investore Property (NZX:IPL): deep dive. Not a value pick: on cash earnings (AFFO) it is priced like its peers, the bad case (NZ$0.83) is 18% below the price and the middling case (NZ$1.05) only 4% above it

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk. Price at writing: NZ$1.01 (close on 6 November 2026, FinFeedAPI, data/prices/NZX/IPL.csv; the NZX quote page shows the same close, bid NZ$1.01 and offer NZ$1.025 [S10]). Market value: NZ$381.4m (377,623,361 shares, NZX [S10]).
Status: finished. This is a simulation and not financial advice.
Fact sheet: research/facts/NZ_IPL.md, grade A as a value pick on NTA. The question it set: do Investore's book values and its 6.5c dividend hold through the 2027 refinancing of NZ$250m of bonds now costing 2.40% and 4.00%, with maintenance spending at FY26's NZ$8.0m?

Value pick (rulebook section 5a): no. The bad case is 17.8% below the price (the limit is 10%) and the middling case 4.0% above it (it needs 25%). Counting the seven quarterly dividends to 30 June 2028, the middling case is 15.2% above the price, still short.

Order of work. The brief, the fact sheet and the Argosy deep dive all quote the price, so it was in view from the start. The AFFO work and the valuation use Investore's filings and the prices the market pays peers; Investore's own price enters only in the yields, "Why the market is wrong", the outcomes and the checks. This file is kept short because the first test (below) kills the case. Stride's side of the relationship (the fees, the 18.8% stake) is worked in research/picks/NZ_SPG.md.

> In short: the market prices NZ listed property on cash earnings after maintenance spending and leasing costs (AFFO), and on that measure Investore is not cheap. FY26 AFFO was 5.81c, a 5.75% yield at NZ$1.01, against 6.4% to 6.8% for Kiwi Property, Precinct, Argosy and Stride. A full year of the Silverdale Centre and a more normal maintenance bill should lift FY27 AFFO to about 6.5c (range 5.9c to 7.0c), a 6.4% yield, which is the peer median, not 15% above it. Then the 2027 bonds reprice: replacing NZ$250m at 4.00% and 2.40% with money at about 5.5% takes about 1.1c a share a year off AFFO once both are done, so FY28 AFFO is about 6.1c, below the 6.5c dividend. NTA of NZ$1.62 is real (supermarkets have sold 4% to 6% above book), but only a sale of the buildings or of the company turns it into cash, and Stride's management contract and 18.8% stake make a bid unlikely. As a listed company Investore is worth about NZ$0.92 to NZ$1.15, central about NZ$1.00 to NZ$1.05. No card.

Corrections to the fact sheet, for the red team:
1. Anchor tenants pay 81% of contract rent at 31 March 2026, not 87%. The 87% was at 30 September 2025, before Silverdale was bought [S3 page 4, S4 release].
2. FY26 maintenance (NZ$8.0m) is high but not a one-off jump from a normal NZ$2.4m. Maintenance, seismic work and incentives were NZ$6.4m in FY24, NZ$3.8m in FY25 and NZ$8.8m in FY26, an average of NZ$6.3m [S1 note 3.2, S5]. NZ$3.4m of roofing work at Pakuranga Road and Averill Street is committed for FY27 [S1 note 2.3].
3. The fact sheet's refinancing range (4.5% to 5.5%) starts below today's evidence. Kiwi Property paid 5.62% for seven-year secured green bonds on 23 October 2026 (a 4.42% base rate plus 1.20%) [P2]; a bank refinancing on Investore's swaps (about 3.3% to 3.8%) plus margin and line fees is about 5.0%. I use 5.0% to 5.9%.
4. The fact sheet's "against us" value (NZ$1.05, a stressed NTA at the peer median multiple) is above the AFFO answer. On AFFO the bad case is about NZ$0.80 to NZ$0.85.
5. Distributable profit adds back a NZ$0.5m incentive paid to an anchor tenant for an early renewal and NZ$0.33m of rental guarantee income [S1 note 3.2]. On the strictest basis (both taken off) FY26 AFFO is 5.59c, not 5.81c.

## The company in plain words
Investore owns 43 large format and convenience retail properties worth NZ$1,128m at 31 March 2026: mostly Woolworths supermarkets, Bunnings and Mitre 10 stores, and three shopping centres (Bay Central, Mt Wellington and the Silverdale Centre, bought from Stride for NZ$114m on 31 October 2025) [S1 notes 1.8, 2.2; S3]. Occupancy is 99.5%, lease term 5.9 years, average cap rate 6.3%; Woolworths pays 51% of contract rent after the June 2026 sale of Woolworths Greenlane, anchor tenants 81% [S2, S3]. It has no staff. Stride Investment Management (SIML) manages it under a management agreement, appoints two of its five directors (now David Green and Ross Buckley), and its stapled partner Stride Property Ltd (SPL) owns 18.8% [S1, S8]. Five years: NTA a share NZ$2.32 (FY22), NZ$1.84, NZ$1.57, NZ$1.60, NZ$1.62 (FY26); distributable profit a share 8.11c, 8.44c, 8.39c, 7.58c, 8.13c [S1 five-year summary]. The quarterly dividend was cut from 1.975c to 1.625c in early 2024 (7.9c to 6.5c a year) and has been 6.5c a year since [S10].

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
They mostly aren't. The top 20 holders own 82.8%, including SPL 18.8%, Forsyth Barr Investment Management, ACC and ANZ Investments [S1, holder pages]. It trades a median NZ$107,584 a day (Checks). What does apply is the sector being out of favour: the price fell from NZ$1.275 a year ago to NZ$1.01 (data/prices/NZX/IPL.csv), with Stride, Argosy, Kiwi Property and Precinct falling 14% to 24% over the same year (research/picks/NZ_ARG.md).

### The 2027 bonds

In May the manager said "options [are] proactively being considered to fund $250m of bond maturities in 2027" [S3 page 21]; nothing has been announced since [S12].

### What Stride is paid, and what a change would be worth
- FY26 fees to SIML: NZ$7.97m in all, of which the asset management fee was NZ$5.49m; the rest were building management NZ$0.53m, project management NZ$0.62m, leasing NZ$0.52m, disposal NZ$0.16m, accounting NZ$0.25m and other NZ$0.40m [S1 note 4.0]. Corporate costs in the income statement were NZ$8.21m, 0.73% of property value [S1].
- The base fee is 0.55% a year of gross assets up to NZ$750m and 0.45% above (about NZ$5.75m on NZ$1.11bn); the performance fee is 10% of returns above the NZX property index, capped; from October 2025 the building management fee became the greater of the actual building and centre management costs and NZ$10,000 a property (indexed), which the independent appraiser expected to cost Investore a net NZ$0.2m a year after tenant recoveries [S6 appraisal sections 5.3.3 and 8.3]. No performance fee was paid in FY26, but the carried-forward shortfall shrank from 31.4% to 5.6% [S1 note 4.0], so a re-rating of Investore against the property index would start paying SIML 10% of the excess.
- Internalisation. An internally managed peer runs at 0.51% of property (Argosy) to 0.53% (Vital after internalising) [P6]; at Investore's NZ$1.1bn a team would likely cost more per dollar. At 0.51% to 0.65% the saving is NZ$1.0m to NZ$2.6m a year before tax, 0.2c to 0.5c a share after tax, worth 3c to 7.5c a share at a 6.5% yield. The only NZ precedent sets the price of getting there: Vital paid its manager NZ$214m to save about NZ$20.9m a year, about 10 times the gross saving [P5]. At that multiple Investore would pay NZ$10m to NZ$27m (3c to 7c a share) for a saving worth about the same. Stride's own annual report says "there are limited grounds for termination" in its management agreements [T1, risk section]. A change of manager is not available to Investore holders on their own, and internalisation at a precedent price is roughly value-neutral. I didn't find the agreement's termination clause itself in the documents read.
- So the fee load costs holders perhaps 0.2c to 0.5c a year of AFFO against an internal model; it isn't the reason Investore trades below NTA, and there is no event that would remove it.

### SPL's 18.8% and whether Stride could or would bid
- SPL holds 71,107,744 shares (18.83%) and carries them at NZ$87.1m, about NZ$1.225 a share, after adding a 17.5% "strategic investment premium" to the March price [S1 holder pages, T1 note 7.1]. It received NZ$4.6m of dividends from them in FY26 [T1].
- What the rules allow: SPL can go to 20% without an offer; above that it needs a full or partial takeover offer or Investore holders' approval (Takeovers Code). Buying the other 306.5m shares at NZ$1.10 to NZ$1.30 would cost NZ$337m to NZ$399m (python3 -I). Stride's market value is NZ$622m and SPL has NZ$166m of bank headroom [T2], so a bid would need new Stride shares at about two-thirds of Stride's NTA. It would also turn fee income, which is what Stride says it wants to grow, into property it owns: Stride's strategy is to be "New Zealand's leading listed property investor and fund manager", and it sold Silverdale into Investore rather than buy more [T4]. I give a Stride bid a low chance and don't use it in the middling case.
- The stake also stands in the way of anyone else. A third party would need SPL's acceptance to reach 90%, and would face a management agreement with limited termination grounds. Neither Investore nor Stride has announced an approach, a review or a sale process [S12, T5].

### Capital management
No buyback or capital return has been done or flagged; the only references are standard wording in the capital risk note [S1]. The dividend reinvestment plan has been suspended since the FY26 fourth-quarter dividend [S2, S7]. The board says it "will consider and evaluate portfolio recycling opportunities to optimise portfolio returns and strengthen the balance sheet" [S2]. With LVR at 38.1% (43.8% with the notes) and NZ$250m of bonds to replace, it has little room to copy Precinct's 30 October capital return and buyback [P4]. A new chair, Gráinne Troute, takes over on 31 December 2026 and Stephen Bull joins on 1 January 2027 [S8].

## Valuation
Shares 377.6m; NTA NZ$613.1m, NZ$1.62 a share [S1].

Method 1, NTA marked to sales evidence (what a buyer of the buildings would pay). Investore's own sales went 4.1% to 6.2% above book (Browns Bay, New Brighton, Greenlane) [S2]; its non-core properties lost about 3% in FY26 [S1 note 2.2]. Marking the Woolworths-anchored half up 3% (+4.4c), taking off 1.5% selling costs (-4.3c) and a management termination payment at the Vital multiple (-3c to -7c) gives about NZ$1.55 to NZ$1.60; about NZ$1.47 to NZ$1.51 if the notes convert at today's price. This is a value for selling the buildings or the company, which nothing in the filings suggests.

Method 2, AFFO at what the market pays peers. FY28 AFFO of about 6.1c (after the refinancing) at the peer median 6.48% is NZ$0.94; at Investore's own FY26 yield of 5.75% NZ$1.06; at Vital's 5.87% (the closest defensive peer) NZ$1.04. FY27's pre-refinancing 6.5c at 6.48% is NZ$1.00. Range NZ$0.92 to NZ$1.06; each 0.1c of AFFO moves it about 1.5c.

Method 3, multiples of NTA. Investore's own price to NTA at its last five balance dates was 0.74, 0.74, 0.66, 0.78 and 0.65 times, average 0.71 (data/prices/NZX/IPL.csv; NTA from S1, S4): NZ$1.15 on NZ$1.62. This overstates it against peers whose book earns more cash: Investore's AFFO is 3.6% of NTA in FY26 and about 4.0% in FY27, against 5.5% to 5.7% for Kiwi Property and Precinct (research/picks/NZ_ARG.md).

Range: as a listed company, NZ$0.92 to NZ$1.15, central about NZ$1.00 to NZ$1.05, depending mostly on FY28 AFFO (the refinancing rate and maintenance) and on the market keeping retail property at about a 6% to 6.5% cash yield. NZ$1.50 to NZ$1.60 only if the buildings or the company are sold.

## Why the market is wrong
On the buildings it probably is: NZ$1.01 is 0.62 times NTA, and supermarkets are selling above book. On cash, it isn't. Investore pays out more than its AFFO, has NZ$250m of cheap bonds to replace, and yields on AFFO what its peers yield. The discount to NTA is the sector's, with the same interest-rate reason the Argosy deep dive found, and closing it needs falling rates, a sale or capital management; none is on a calendar, and Stride's contract and stake make a sale less likely here than at an internally managed peer. No trade.

## What would make this wrong
"No case" is wrong if the price rises well above NZ$1.05. The two likeliest ways:
1. Rates fall and NZ property re-rates. Investore traded at NZ$1.24 (0.78 times NTA) at 30 September 2025 and NZ$1.275 a year ago (data/prices/NZX/IPL.csv). A return there is +23% to +26% without anything Investore-specific.
2. A cheap refinancing or capital recycling. If the bonds are replaced at 5.0% or less and maintenance falls back to FY25's level, FY28 AFFO is about 6.8c, worth about NZ$1.04 at the peer median yield and NZ$1.10 at 6.2%; a sale of non-core property at book with a buyback would add to that.

The case for anyone who did buy gets worse if the August 2027 bond has to be replaced near 6%, maintenance stays at FY26's level, or the 6.5c dividend is held while AFFO is about 6c, which uses the balance sheet.

Exit rule: no position. If one were taken, sell at the next open after FY28 dividend guidance below 6.5c, a refinancing above 6.0%, or NTA below NZ$1.50, and otherwise on 30 June 2028.

Reopen with a fresh deep dive if: the price falls to about NZ$0.84 or below while FY27 and FY28 dividend guidance stays at 6.5c and NTA at NZ$1.55 or more; both bonds are refinanced at 5.0% or less all-in; Investore announces a buyback, a capital return, or asset sales above book with money returned; the management agreement is changed, terminated or internalised; or SPL moves above 20%, sells its stake, or any other holder goes above 10% with a stated intention or an approach is announced.
