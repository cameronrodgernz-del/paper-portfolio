In short:
- The fact sheet's anchor doesn't survive the first test. The A$45m of ARR is a June 2027 figure, reported in August 2027, outside the six-month window. The base it grows from was restated, and 1.5 times is about what the market pays for contracted subscription software (Mach7 at 1.78 times), not for captioning billed largely by the minute in a business earning a 3% to 7% EBITDA margin. A buyer paying 1.5 times would be a bid, which is the good case, not the middle. Inside the window the realistic good case is about A$0.33.
- ARR. In August 2025 FY25 ARR was A$17.0m, shown as a slice of the year's recognised technology revenue and described as LEXI Text revenue. From February 2026 ARR became an "exit run-rate" of "contracted recurring" revenue, and June 2025 was restated to A$24.0m, which is exactly FY25's full-year SaaS revenue. The "+50%" therefore sets a June 2026 run-rate (A$36.0m) against a full-year revenue figure. No document explains the restatement. On one consistent basis (each half's SaaS revenue, doubled) recurring revenue grew 28% from June 2025 to June 2026 (A$27.6m to A$35.4m), and the latest half grew 8% on the half before (about 17% a year).
- Growing and shrinking. SaaS revenue rose every half (A$10.2m, A$13.8m, A$16.4m, A$17.7m). Hardware fell (A$8.7m, A$8.4m, A$4.7m, A$6.0m) waiting for the new encoders, and human services fell (A$12.9m, A$10.9m, A$8.6m, A$6.8m). No development cost is capitalised, so adjusted EBITDA already carries all A$7.7m of product spending. Cash earnings before working capital were about A$1.2m in FY26; second-half operating cash flow was negative (minus A$0.2m) as prepaid subscriptions unwound. At guidance FY27 cash earnings are about A$2.0m to A$2.5m.
- Cash. Of A$16.0m, about A$8.8m (4.2c a share) is free once tax owed, an allowance for the remaining run-down, a month's operating float and encoder funding are set aside. A full buyback at today's price would cost about A$4.1m.

## The company in plain words
AI-Media sells captioning, transcription and translation for live and recorded video, mostly to broadcasters, governments and companies [A1, A3]. The money comes from three lines:
- SaaS (A$34.1m in FY26): LEXI, its automatic captioning and translation software, and fees on the iCap network that carries captions from encoders to captioners. It is billed partly by subscription and partly by usage; FY26's auditor describes the A$34.1m as "subscription and usage-based revenue" [A1, auditor's report]. LEXI Text is "the vast majority" [A3, p. 5].
- Hardware (A$10.7m): caption encoders made by EEG, the New York business bought in May 2021. More than 8,000 are installed, 2,200 of them over five years old; new models (UHD592, AIX-1) were launched at NAB in April 2026 and began shipping in the first half of FY27, sold outright or rented as "hardware as a service" (HaaS) [A3, A4].
- Services (A$15.4m): human and hybrid captioning, being run down since 2021 and now called "Managed Services", which is transactional and outside ARR [A1, A4].

North America is A$41.4m of the A$60.2m (69%); "AMER Broadcast" alone is 55% of revenue, "driven by two products: LEXI Text and Encoders" [A1, note 4; A3, p. 22]. "The top 20 customers contribute 80% of revenue" (September 2025) and all top 20 were retained for a fifth year; no customer is over 10% [A13; A2; A1, note 4].

Who runs it: co-founder Tony Abrahams is CEO and holds 37,102,398 shares (17.65%) directly and through Pearlirose [D2]. John Martin chairs the board; Jason Singh is CFO [A4]. Who owns it: the top 20 hold 61.70%; substantial holders are Abrahams and Pearlirose, TIGA Trading (9.86%, notice of 29 November 2023), Salter Brothers Emerging Companies (9.15%, notice of 31 August 2026) and Jencay Capital (5.00%) [A1, shareholder information; S4; S5].

What changed. The August 2025 figure was a part of the year's recognised revenue (a full-year amount, limited to LEXI Text). From February 2026 ARR became a run-rate at the period end covering all recurring software revenue. The restated June 2025 figure, A$24.0m, is identical to FY25's full-year SaaS revenue (A$24.0m [A3, p. 16]), which is not a June run-rate: second-half FY25 SaaS revenue was A$13.8m, A$27.6m a year. So the FY26 "+50%" sets a June 2026 run-rate against a full-year FY25 revenue figure. Neither the February nor the August 2026 documents mention the change or reconcile A$17.0m to A$24.0m.

The rebuild on one basis. The half-year and full-year reports give SaaS revenue (subscription plus usage) for every half [A7, Appendix 4D commentary; A3, p. 16; A9, p. 21]. Doubling each half gives an annual rate that can be compared like for like (python3 -I, scratchpad calc/val.py):

| Half | SaaS revenue (A$m) | Annual rate (x2) | Company ARR at the half's end | Company ARR / annual rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1H25 (to Dec 2024) | 10.2 | 20.4 | 16.7 | 0.82 |
| 2H25 (to Jun 2025) | 24.0 - 10.2 = 13.8 | 27.6 | 24.0 (was 17.0) | 0.87 |
| 1H26 (to Dec 2025) | 16.4 | 32.8 | 30.0 | 0.92 |
| 2H26 (to Jun 2026) | 34.1 - 16.4 = 17.7 | 35.4 | 36.0 | 1.02 |

On this basis growth to June 2026 was 28% (35.4 / 27.6), full-year SaaS revenue grew 42% (34.1 / 24.0), and the latest half grew 8.1% on the one before (17.7 / 16.4), about 17% a year. The company's series doesn't hang together: a December 2024 run-rate of A$16.7m is below the A$20.4m rate that half's revenue actually ran at, while June 2026's A$36.0m is above its half. The measure has drifted up relative to revenue by about a quarter in two years. FY27's guided ARR (A$44m to A$46m, +22% to +28%) needs faster growth than the latest half's pace, or new recurring lines such as HaaS rentals, which the company says will lift "ARR per deployment" [A8].

How recurring is it? Less than "contracted" suggests:
- In FY25, A$15.20m of technology revenue was "consumption-based" (billed on hours used), from FY25's auditor, Deloitte, against about A$24m to A$26m of SaaS [A12, auditor's report]. Most SaaS revenue is usage.
- Prepaid subscriptions are shrinking: deferred revenue went A$4.3m (June 2024), A$10.5m (June 2025), A$10.4m (December 2025), A$8.1m (June 2026); billings into it fell from A$18.3m in FY25 to A$13.6m in FY26 [A1, note 17; A7, note 8]. ARR rose 50% while the contracted, paid-ahead part fell.
- A June "exit run-rate" that includes usage depends on the month's live events.
- New ARR-linked commissions: sales pay was moved to a "commission model directly tied to ARR growth" in FY26 [A13].

### 3. Earnings and cash earnings
- No development cost is capitalised: payments for intangibles were A$27k in FY26 and A$64k in FY25, and all A$7.7m of product and R&D plus A$2m of corporate IT was expensed [A1, cash flow statement; A3, p. 4]. So adjusted EBITDA is already after development spending, which is better than most small software companies.

### 7. Other things a quick reader would miss
- New auditor: Deloitte, auditor for nine years, was replaced by Grant Thornton after a tender, approved at the September 2025 AGM [A18]. Both opinions are unmodified [A12, A1].
- The half-year and full-year notes don't tie. The 1H26 contract-liability note shows billings of A$15.0m and A$15.3m moved to revenue in the half, against A$13.6m and A$15.8m for the full year; and A$16.2m of 1H26 revenue was "at a point in time" against A$11.5m for the whole year [A7, notes 5 and 8; A1, notes 5 and 17]. The second halves would be negative. The classifications changed between the reports without comment.

## Why the market is wrong
At A$0.195 the market value is A$41.0m and the enterprise value A$25.0m: 0.7 times recurring revenue (A$35.4m to A$36.0m), 0.41 times FY26 revenue, 6.7 times FY27 guided EBITDA after leases and 5.3 times the 2H26 run-rate (python3 -I). On FY26's actual adjusted EBITDA it is 13.4 times, and on FY27 cash earnings after tax, less only the free cash, about 14.6 times. So the price is low only if the guidance is real.

1. The market is pricing the past year (revenue down 7%, a statutory loss, targets withdrawn, ARR restated) and a poor record, as if FY27 guidance will be missed too. The guidance sits below the second-half run-rate and was set with a quarter in hand. If the 1H27 result shows adjusted EBITDA of about A$1.5m to A$2m in the weaker half and SaaS revenue of about A$19m (all figures the company reports in its Appendix 4D, not a redefinable ARR), the price should move toward 9 times guided EBITDA, about 23c.
2. Recurring revenue at 0.7 times against 1.8 times for Mach7, an ASX software company with less revenue, slower growth and a loss, is a bigger discount than the difference in quality (usage billing, concentration) explains, as long as growth holds near 15% to 20%.

Buyers are already moving: Salter Brothers at 21c to 24c, Jencay at about 27c, a director at 23.8c, and a company buyback of up to A$4m. The sellers who drove the fall, Regal and WAM, have gone.

Where the market may be right: much of the recurring revenue is billed by the minute, prepaid revenue is falling, second-half cash flow was negative, AI captioning keeps getting cheaper, and this management has missed most of what it promised. The case is marginal.
