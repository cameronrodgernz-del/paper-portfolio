## The company in plain words

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Size: A$118m market value; A$139m enterprise value including leases.
- Index: not in the S&P/ASX 300 since 22 September 2014 (Norgate, data/norgate/index_changes.csv). It was removed from the All Ordinaries on 23 March 2026 [A22, A23], so index funds tracking that index had to sell in March.
- Trading: A$81,203 a day median (20 sessions to 30 October; Norgate), with days as low as A$1,759. Too thin for most funds.
- Coverage: the company told ASX in March 2026 that three sell-side analysts cover it [A13]. FNArena (a secondary source) lists one current view: Shaw and Partners, Buy, target A$0.35 on 3 November 2026, with FY27 EPS of 2.2 cents and FY28 2.7 cents [S1]. Bell Potter initiated in January 2025 (its website; not read).
- Register: Thorney 21.55% and the top 20 53.7%.
- History: a restatement of FY24 (August 2025), four guidance cuts in two years, an ASX aware letter (February 2026), and a 71% fall from the July 2024 peak (80% to the October low).

So size, index, liquidity and a messy history all apply. But the cheapness has a plain reason the market can see: the guidance record.

### What the FY27 guidance needs
Guidance: "underlying EBIT from continuing operations, excluding FX movements" of A$17m to A$21m, given on 25 August and reconfirmed at the AGM on 30 October 2026 [A2, A6, A7]. Two points a quick reader would miss:
- The measure changed twice. FY26 started on "underlying EBIT" (A$40m to A$46m), switched on 26 February 2026 to "EBIT excluding FX", which the company told ASX was statutory EBIT excluding currency (A$14m to A$16m) [A13], and FY27 is back on "underlying". On the underlying basis FY26 was about A$13.9m (A$10.8m plus A$3.1m of non-currency material items) [A3, p. 8]. So guidance asks for 22% to 51% growth on a like basis, not 57% to 94%.
- The bridge from the clean underlying run-rate (A$10m to A$14m a year) to the A$19m midpoint is mostly Chile. If Chile's segment EBITDA goes from minus A$10.4m a year (the H2 rate) to about minus A$1m, that alone adds about A$9m. Asia Pacific (order book up 52%, Batam full) can add a little; North America (order book down 43% at 30 September, furloughs in early October, "softer first half") takes some away [A6, A7]. The guidance is achievable if Chile holds the improvement the AGM described, and not otherwise.

What the AGM said about FY27 so far [A6, A7]: order book A$139m at 30 September (A$118m a year earlier); October order intake of A$39.7m for the group to date, and A$35.3m of October orders to date in North America; Chile "positive unaudited year-to-date EBITDA contribution (before corporate overhead)" despite flooding in August; North America order book down 43%, "reflecting lower regional activity rather than any loss of market share". The first new order under the repriced Chile contract was only about A$6.7m [A14], against A$21.0m of revenue from the old contract in FY26 [A3, p. 10].

## Valuation
Worked from the filings and peers. All A$. Shares 623.1m. Net debt A$5.8m excluding leases, A$20.9m including them. Interest A$1.0m to A$1.3m a year. Tax 26%. Recurring costs that the company calls "material items" (inventory write-downs, restructuring, warranty top-ups) cost A$3.9m in FY25 and about A$3.5m in FY26 [A3, p. 8; A17]; I deduct A$2.0m a year from "underlying" EBIT to get to profit the market will see.

The earnings each outcome rests on (underlying EBIT, the blend of FY27 and FY28 the market would price at the HY27 result):
- Bad: A$13m. FY27 guidance cut again to about FY26's level (A$13.9m underlying).
- Middling: A$20m. FY27 near the midpoint (about A$19m) and FY28 a little higher (about A$21m).
- Good: A$24m. FY27 at the top or above (A$21m-plus) and FY28 on track for about A$26m, where Chile makes money and North America recovers.

### Method 2: a simple discounted cash flow
Cash flow a year = (underlying EBIT less A$2.0m) x 0.74, less net reinvestment of A$1.5m (capital spending A$7.0m plus lease principal A$4.2m, less depreciation A$9.7m, all FY26 levels [A1]), less A$1.0m of working capital for 2% growth (net working capital was 15% of revenue at June 2026 [A3, p. 16], on about A$340m of revenue). Discount rate 11% (a small, cyclical, mostly equity-funded industrial), growth 2%. Value of the business = cash flow / 9%; less A$5.8m of net debt (leases are already in the cash flow).

### Check: what the net tangible assets earn
Net tangible assets are A$108.9m (17.48 cents). On each case's profit the return on them is 6.6% (bad), 11.4% (middling) and 14.3% (good). Priced at that return over a 12% required return, they are worth 0.55, 0.95 and 1.19 times book: A$0.096, A$0.166 and A$0.208. This agrees with the other methods in the bad and middling cases and sits a little lower in the good case. The assets are working capital, plant and finance lease receivables; there is no property or surplus asset worth more than book, and no evidence a buyer would pay above book for a business earning under 12% on it.

### Range
Midpoint of methods 1 and 2: bad A$0.099, middling A$0.184, good A$0.244. Each depends on one thing: whether Chile's improvement holds (bad against middling), and whether North America's orders come back in the second half of FY27 and FY28 (middling against good). Shaw's A$0.35 target [S1] is 13 times its FY28 EPS of 2.7 cents; I can't see the basis for that multiple in this company's record.

### The fact sheet's anchors, tested
- "Our way" A$0.227: five-year average EBITDA (A$31.9m) less depreciation gives EBIT of A$22.2m, above the top of FY27 guidance; it deducts no recurring costs; and the average includes FY24 and FY25, when revenue was A$308m to A$377m at margins the business hasn't shown since. Would a buyer pay it? Only once earnings the market can see reach that level, which is the FY27 result in August 2027 at the earliest; there is no buyback running, no dividend to speak of and no bid. It is my good case. At A$0.19 it is 19.5% above the price, not 34%.
- "Against us" A$0.111 (FY26 repeated at 9.1 times) is close to my bad case (A$0.099).

## Why the market is wrong
At A$0.19 I can't say it is. The price sits about 3% above my middling value and 12% above the expected value from the outcomes below. The share was cheap at A$0.13 to A$0.15 in October, when the market priced the guidance as if it wouldn't be met; since the AGM reconfirmed it and Shaw published a Buy, the price has moved to roughly where the guidance midpoint, valued like Emeco, puts it. What would make the market wrong from here is evidence that the good case (FY28 at about A$26m of EBIT) is likely, and that evidence can only come from the HY27 result. Until then there is no gap to trade.
