## The company in plain words
Serko writes business travel booking software. It makes money three ways [S5, note 4 and "Revenue model"]:
- A share of the commission hotels pay on bookings made through Booking.com for Business, a booking site for small and mid-sized firms that Serko built and runs with Booking.com. Serko is paid only once the guest has stayed. Supplier commissions were NZ$73.4m in FY26 (year to 31 March 2026), 61% of revenue.
- Fees per booking from travel management companies and corporates using its Zeno platform in Australia and New Zealand, and GetThere in the US, bought from Sabre in January 2025 (travel platform booking revenue NZ$39.1m).
- Expense software (NZ$4.2m; the US InterplX business was sold in September 2025 [S11]), services (NZ$2.4m) and other (NZ$0.3m).

By where the customer is, FY26 revenue of NZ$119.4m was Europe and other NZ$75.1m, Australia NZ$25.6m, US NZ$16.0m and New Zealand NZ$2.6m [S5, note 4]. Two customers each gave more than 10% of revenue, together NZ$84.9m or 71% (FY25: NZ$65.4m) [S5, note 4]. One is Booking.com. The report doesn't name the other. Flight Centre Travel Group, which "accounts for a significant proportion of Serko's booking volume across Australia and New Zealand" and renewed in July 2025 for an initial term to June 2027 with a one-year automatic extension [S13], is the likely one (my inference).

Who runs it: co-founder Darrin Grafton is chief executive. In June 2026 the chief operating officer, Matthew Gerrie, who spent a decade at Booking Holdings and Booking.com, took over day-to-day running of every function, and Grafton turned to "where the industry is heading, where Serko needs to be positioned, and the external relationships that will get us there" [S17]. The chief financial officer, Shane Sampson, leaves in March 2027; a search is under way [S18]. Claudia Batten chairs the board.

Who owns it (31 March 2026, largest registered holders [S5, p. 81]): BNP Paribas Nominees 17.08% and Apex Custodian Nominees 12.11% (custodians), the Grafton Howe No. 2 trust (Darrin Grafton and Geoffrey Hosking) 8.24%, Robert Shaw and Michael Moore (co-founder Bob Shaw's interests) 7.33%, Custodial Services 6.24%, HSBC Nominees 4.94%, ACC 4.49%, Coronado Pte 4.33%, Citibank Nominees 4.11% and NZ Superannuation Fund Nominees 3.87%. Substantial holders then were FirstCape (Harbour) 16.43%, Grafton 9.57%, Fisher Funds 8.52%, Shaw 7.50% and ANZ New Zealand Investments 6.23% [S5]. FirstCape is now 15.36% [S15]. Booking Holdings took 4.3m shares at NZ$4.04 in October 2019 and holds rights, extended in the 2024 renewal [S8], under which Serko must tell it of any written proposal to buy 50% or more of the company or substantially all its assets, and won't issue shares to certain named parties [S9]. I couldn't confirm whether Booking Holdings still holds the shares, or who Coronado Pte is.

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
Mostly they aren't. Serko is an S&P/NZX 50 member (named as one on 5 February 2026 [S22]; the March, June and September 2026 reviews made no change to that index [S21]). It is dual-listed on the ASX but not in the S&P/ASX 300 (data/norgate/index_members.csv). Most New Zealand fund managers hold it: Harbour (FirstCape), Fisher, ANZ, ACC and the NZ Super Fund [S5]. Brokers cover it; a secondary site gave an average target of NZ$3.28 next to a NZ$1.68 price, so from about May or June 2026 [S25]. I don't rely on that figure.

What does apply is a sector out of favour and holders leaving. Software sold off on AI fears in February 2026: Good Returns had Serko "leading the benchmark lower" on 5 February after an AI start-up "rattled markets" [S22]. Listed travel names fell with it and with the Middle East conflict: from the end of January to the end of March 2026, Web Travel Group went from A$4.62 to A$2.61 and SiteMinder from A$5.08 to A$2.86 (data/norgate/universe/prices_2026.csv.gz). Those two have since recovered part of the fall (A$3.59 and A$3.40 on 30 October), while Serko's ASX line kept falling, from A$1.41 to A$0.98. Harbour has sold more than it bought since March [S15], and the chief executive sold 600,000 shares at NZ$2.90 in July 2025 [S16]. The market value has fallen from about NZ$480m at the end of March 2025 to NZ$153m, which puts Serko near the bottom of the NZX 50 and at some risk of removal at the December review (my inference; I haven't checked the cut-off).

## What the filings show

### The Booking.com agreement
From the renewal announced on 30 April 2024 [S8]:
- A five-year term from 1 April 2024 to March 2029.
- "A notice period of 12 months for mutual termination for convenience." March 2029 is the latest date the terms are fixed to, not the earliest date they could end.
- Non-exclusive: Serko is "currently the sole platform provider for Booking.com for Business", but the arrangement "allows for Booking.com to redirect traffic to other third-party technology platforms during the term of the Agreement, subject to agreed conditions".
- A revenue share with volume tiers: no meaningful change to the 50% share below 4.3m completed room nights a year; a blended share of about 40% at 6m and about 32% at 8.6m. The tiers are worked out monthly, on completed bookings [S4, slide 15].

Arithmetic (python3 -I): the share on room nights between 4.3m and 6.0m is (0.40 x 6.0 - 0.50 x 4.3) / 1.7 = 14.7%; between 6.0m and 8.6m it is (0.32 x 8.6 - 0.40 x 6.0) / 2.6 = 13.5%. At Q1 FY27's average commission of EUR19.39 a room night, a further million room nights above 6m adds 0.135 x 19.39 = EUR2.6m, about NZ$5.2m at the FY27 hedge rate of 1.9766 [S11, slide 16], against 0.50 x 19.39 x 1.9766 = NZ$19.2m below 4.3m.

It is already showing. In FY26 room nights rose 31% to 4.3m while Serko's revenue per room night fell 4% to EUR9.25 [S3]. In Q1 FY27 room nights rose 39% to 1.4m and revenue per room night fell 11% to EUR8.70; Serko's share of the commission fell from 49.5% (9.79 / 19.77) to 44.9% (8.70 / 19.39) [S6]. Serko's own slide shows gross revenue from the partnership rising from about NZ$78m at FY26 volumes to only about NZ$94m at 6.2m room nights and about NZ$105m at 8.5m [S4, slide 15]. Management calls tiering "a revenue headwind" and plans to grow outside it through defined US corporate customers, whose volume sits "outside the commission tiering model" [S10, pp. 38 and 45]. Two such organisations were signed in Q1 FY27 [S6], and the initiative is not expected to add much to FY27 income [S7].

The annual report's top risks include "over-reliance on a limited pool of customers, ie, Booking.com for Business", with "ongoing review of Booking.com's strategic direction" and "mapping out key steps between now and 2029's contract renewal" as mitigations, and "competitor products and substitute technologies disrupting the market for business travel" [S5, pp. 73 to 74].

## Valuation
Worked from the filings. I saw NZX's traded values and the recent price while measuring liquidity first, as the brief required; no input below comes from the price.

- Total income: FY27 NZ$133.5m (consistent with a modest rise in the NZ$128m to NZ$134m range), FY28 NZ$142.6m, FY29 NZ$151.1m.
- Total spend NZ$136m in FY27 (towards the middle of guidance [S7]), then +2% a year. It includes share pay, which I count as a cost because it is paid by issuing shares.
- Other cash items a year: equipment -1.5, lease payments -2.0, interest +1.4, tax -1.0.
- Owner earnings (income less spend and those items): FY27 -NZ$5.6m, FY28 +NZ$0.8m, FY29 +NZ$6.6m. Adding back about NZ$7.5m of share pay gives FY27 free cash flow of +NZ$1.9m on the company's measure, consistent with its guidance [S7].

### Method 1: the business as it is being run, with the Booking.com renewal
The owner earnings above to March 2029, discounted, plus the business's value at 31 March 2029 under three outcomes for the agreement:
- (a) renewed on similar terms, 55%: 1.15 times FY29 income, NZ$174m (about 8.4 times the core's earnings in Method 2);
- (b) renewed on worse terms, or traffic partly moved to another platform, 30%: NZ$50m;
- (c) ended, 15%: NZ$15m (Australasia and the US after a restructuring).

Expected 2029 value 0.55 x 174 + 0.30 x 50 + 0.15 x 15 = NZ$112.9m; discounted 2.39 years at 11% (factor 1.283), plus discounted owner earnings, plus cash: NZ$145.8m, NZ$1.16 a share.
Sensitivities: renewal chances 45/35/20 give NZ$1.07 and 65/25/10 give NZ$1.24; a renewal value of NZ$150m gives NZ$1.07, NZ$200m gives NZ$1.24 and NZ$226m (1.5 times FY29 income) NZ$1.33; a 9% discount rate gives NZ$1.19 and 13% NZ$1.13. Heavier risk (45/35/20) with the NZ$150m renewal value together give NZ$1.00. An extension now, taking the chances to 90/8/2, gives NZ$1.45 (NZ$1.60 at NZ$200m).
What it depends on: the 2029 outcome, and the value the market puts on a renewed business.

### Method 2: the core run for cash
What a buyer, or a board that stopped Serko.ai and the US corporate push, could take out. The growth spend removed (G) is my estimate of NZ$15m to NZ$25m a year, central NZ$20m: the platform investment line roughly doubled, by about NZ$18m a year, between Q1 FY26 and Q1 FY27, and external development rose NZ$3.8m [S4, S5]; not all of it is optional. The US corporate revenue goes with it.
Core owner earnings at G of NZ$20m: FY28 NZ$17.8m, FY29 NZ$20.6m (NZ$12.8m and NZ$15.6m at G of NZ$15m; NZ$22.8m and NZ$25.6m at NZ$25m). That fits the FY25 starting point of about NZ$2.7m after share pay [S12] plus about NZ$30m of extra Booking.com revenue in FY26 and FY27 at little extra cost. Tax would be small for years, given NZ$138.7m of New Zealand losses, if shareholder continuity holds [S5].
Value: core earnings to March 2029 discounted at 11%, plus at March 2029 the same three outcomes, with (a) at 6 to 8 times FY29 core earnings, (b) at 6 times 40% of them and (c) at NZ$10m, plus cash.
Results: G NZ$15m at 6 times, NZ$1.05; G NZ$20m at 7 times, NZ$1.33; at 8 times, NZ$1.40; G NZ$25m at 8 times, NZ$1.64.
What it depends on: how much of the spend is truly optional, and who would cut it. The board shows no sign of cutting: "The era of AI will reward the disruptors" [S17].

### The fact sheet's anchors, tested
- "If it goes our way", NZ$2.02 (1.5 times FY27 income at the top of the range, NZ$201m, plus cash): about 25 times FY27 EBITDAFI and above the top of Method 2. The market can't see it in earnings inside any window, because the board is spending the core's earnings; no capital return or buyback is in prospect. Only a bid gets there, and the natural bidder is Booking Holdings, which already has a right to hear of any proposal [S9]. It is a good-case value at most, not a middling one.
- "If it goes against us", NZ$0.60: close to my floor of NZ$0.52. It is the tail if Booking.com leaves, not the bad case for a six-week pick.

## Why the market is wrong
I can't say it is. At NZ$1.21 the market values the business outside cash at NZ$98.5m (152.6 - 54.1), about what Method 1 gives for the business run as the board is running it (NZ$1.16 central). The gap to Method 2 (NZ$1.33 to NZ$1.40) is real, but it belongs to whoever stops the investment, and nothing in the filings says the board will or that a buyer is circling. The market's reasons are in the filings: the tiers, the 12-month exit clause, the board's own concern about substitute technologies, dilution of about 5% a year, and an FY27 that still loses money once share pay is counted.
