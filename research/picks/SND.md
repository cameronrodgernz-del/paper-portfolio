## What the price assumes
The business is valued at about A$95m: about A$108m of shares once the earn-out shares are issued (about 149m shares in 2028), less about A$13.4m of net cash after the earn-out [S3, S15].

FY26 [S1, S2, S3]:
- revenue A$305.6m, up 42.5% with the Aqua Metro purchase;
- adjusted EBITDA A$9.8m (3.2% of revenue);
- profit after tax excluding amortisation and acquisition costs (NPATA) A$3.9m; a statutory loss of A$0.9m;
- the halves were very different: the first half made adjusted EBITDA of A$0.4m and an NPATA loss of about A$1.6m; the second half adjusted EBITDA of about A$9.4m and NPATA of about A$5.4m on A$162m of revenue, a 3.4% margin [S9, S3]. Corrected in the debate: the first half's EBITDA included a A$2.848m property gain, and first-half amortisation was A$1.723m, so second-half NPATA was about A$5.0m, a 3.08% margin.

The company has said twice, in August and at the 27 October AGM, that FY27 revenue will grow and the second-half NPATA margin will be held through FY27 [S1, S4]. On revenue of about A$330m that is NPATA of about A$11.1m (about A$10.2m at the corrected 3.08% margin).

So the business is valued at:
- 26 times FY26 NPATA;
- 8.5 times the company's FY27 outlook, net of cash (9.8 times including it);
- before FY25, Saunders earned A$9.5m and A$9.4m (4.7% and 4.3% of revenue) in FY23 and FY24 and traded at about A$1.00 to A$1.20 in 2022 and 2023, about 12 times [S14, S15].

The price assumes the outlook won't be met. That is a fair reading of the record: FY25 guidance was cut and the managing director resigned in June 2025 [S18]; a business update in October 2025 warned the first half would be worse [S18]; FY26 guidance was cut in May 2026 [S7]. One analyst covers it, with a A$1.10 target [S19].

## The one thing the market is missing
A trade buyer with a strategy that fits Saunders has spent about A$19m building 16.2%, mostly at prices above today's, and the price carries no allowance for a bid.

At A$0.725 that isn't enough to pass, because the business case alone is only fair value at today's multiple. At about A$0.70 the two together pass.

## The test outside the filings
Done on 9 November 2026, under docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md step 2. The questions the filings can't answer:
1. Is the second-half margin the business, or a good half?
2. What is Ahrens doing, and could it bid?
3. Who else might sell, and is there an overhang?
4. Does the federal fuel package change the demand for tanks, and when?

What the evidence showed:

1. The second-half margin is close to Saunders' own record, and the work is there, but the company has missed three times in 18 months.
   - FY23 and FY24 net margins were 4.7% and 4.3% [S15]. The second half of FY26 was 3.4% (NPATA). FY25 and the first half of FY26 were the exceptions, and the company named their causes: project delays, weather, a heritage find on the Melbourne sewer job and Defence base access in the Northern Territory [S7, S9].
   - Work in hand was a record A$571.9m at June (against Saunders' own A$169m a year earlier plus Aqua Metro's A$360m, mostly long programmes), 42% of FY26 revenue came from panels and programmes rather than one-off projects, and four new water panels in October give access to about A$2bn of programmes over five years, with no guaranteed volume [S1, S6].
   - The register: the federal contract register lists one contract to Saunders itself, CN4271325, published 24 August 2026, A$26.1m including GST, a limited tender for fuel installation upgrades to 30 September 2027 [S13]. That matches the A$23m Butterworth (Malaysia) head contract announced on 16 July [S8]. Saunders' other Defence work comes through head contractors (for example A$20m at Learmonth through CPB [S9]) and doesn't appear under its name.
   - What this changes: it supports the middling case. It doesn't remove the risk that a thin margin (3.2% EBITDA) is wiped out by one bad half, which the record shows happens.
2. Ahrens has been buying steadily for two years and fits the profile of a bidder, but hasn't moved past 20%.
   - Its notices [S11]:

     | Date of change | Shares | Holding | Close that day |
     | --- | --- | --- | --- |
     | 2 Dec 2024 | 6.33m (A$4.78m, A$0.754 each) | 5.3% | A$0.77 |
     | 2 Mar 2025 | 8.83m | 7.3% | A$0.91 |
     | 17 Jul 2025 | 17.58m | 13.4% | A$0.84 |
     | about 13 Nov 2025 | 17.27m (diluted by the Aqua Metro shares) | 12.8% | A$0.78 |
     | 14 Jan 2026 | 18.22m | 13.1% | A$0.88 |
     | 30 Mar 2026 | 22.62m | 16.2% | A$0.875 |

     On 30 March 4.0m shares traded, nearly 300 times a normal day [S14].
   - Ahrens bought Taylors Engineering (2023), Farrelly Construction Services (March 2025), ABFI Steel (2025), Vaughan Constructions (October 2025, after which it said sales passed A$1bn) and SBS Tanks (August 2026) [S16]. It is a serial buyer of private businesses. I found no record of it bidding for a listed company.
   - Ahrens has no board seat, and the AGM on 27 October passed every resolution with 85% to 100% support [S5].
   - Reading: Ahrens has paid about A$19m (my estimate: the first block's stated price, then each later block at that day's close) for a stake it can't easily sell in a stock that trades A$11,000 a day. It either wants control, a blocking position against another buyer, or a long-term strategic holding. I put a bid or other control deal before August 2028 at 20%. Ahrens can buy to 19.99% at any time; above that it needs a bid, a scheme with the board, or 3% every six months once it has held 19% for six months.
3. The other holders [S3, S12]:
   - Desmond Bryant, a long-time holder, 18.4% (with his company Debry); he moved 2m shares to Debry at A$1.00 in February 2025 and hasn't sold.
   - NAOS 11.0%: sold from 15.6% to 10.0% between March 2025 and June 2026, then bought back to 11.0% in August (1.5m shares on 28 August), so it isn't simply a seller.
   - M Wade Holdings (the Aqua Metro vendor) 6.7%, out of escrow since 30 June 2026. The "substantial holder left" notice of 2 July was Saunders' own escrow interest ending, not a sale [S12].
   - Anacacia 6.6%, down from 10.6% (sold in June 2026).
   - Timothy Burnett, a former chair, 5.5%, a seller since 2024.
   - About 63% is held by these six. That is why it trades so little and why a buyer of size has to go through the big holders, most likely Bryant.
4. The fuel package is real but slow.
   - The 2026-27 Budget committed A$3.2bn to a government-owned reserve of 1bn litres of diesel and jet fuel, a higher minimum stockholding for importers with co-investment in storage, and a A$7.5bn facility for fuel and fertiliser supply and storage [S17]. The government is still consulting on how the reserve will work, and new storage on that scale takes years to procure and build [S17].
   - Saunders' shares rose from A$0.85 in mid-May, when the package was announced alongside its own guidance cut, to a high of A$1.11 on 22 June [S14]. They have given all of it back.
   - What this changes: it supports the good case after 2028 more than the middling case before it.

Summary: the evidence finds a credible bidder the price ignores, and a business that is fairly priced on its own outlook if the outlook is met. Neither is enough alone at A$0.725.

## The company in plain words
Saunders builds and maintains infrastructure for liquids: fuel tanks and terminals, water and sewer works, mine plant, and Defence fuel facilities. It listed in 2007 [S15]. Mark Benson ran it from 2015 to 2025; Angelo De Angelis, chief operating officer since 2021 and before that a senior executive at Transfield Services and Ventia, has run it since October 2025 [S18]. In July 2025 it agreed to buy Aqua Metro, a Victorian water contractor, completing in October for A$13.4m in cash and A$7.0m in shares, plus an earn-out of up to A$11.85m (A$6.85m cash and A$5.0m in shares), fully provided for and due in the first half of FY27 [S3].


The board: Nick Yates (chair, former chair of BSA), Angelo De Angelis, Greg Fletcher and Andrew Bellamy (a former Austal chief executive), who bought 20,000 shares on market at A$0.77 on 30 October 2026 [S10]. The managing director holds 95,658 shares and 1.16m performance rights [S10].

## The players and what the rules allow
- Ahrens Group, 16.2% [S11]. Private, family owned, sales above A$1bn [S16]. Its average cost is about A$0.84 (my estimate from its notices and the day's closes). It can buy to 19.99% on market at any time. Beyond 20% it needs a takeover bid, a scheme agreed with the board, or the 3% creep allowance every six months after holding 19% for six months. A bid at A$1.08 would cost it about A$136m for the rest of the shares; that is about 13 times the FY27 outlook, net of cash, against the 15 to 30 times listed contractors trade at.
- Desmond Bryant, 18.4% [S3]. With Ahrens, about 35%. If Ahrens bid, his acceptance would most likely decide it.
- NAOS, 11.0% [S12]. Sold for a year, then bought in August. A block to Ahrens would take it to 19.99% at most without a bid.
- The board: no major holder on it. A scheme would need its support.
- If nothing happens: the bad, middling and worse cases below. The bad outcome for a special situation is that value. Here it is A$0.52 on the card.

The dividend was cut to 1.0 cent for FY26 (2.25 cents in FY25), with no interim [S1].

## Why the market is wrong
In part. On the business alone, the market is roughly right: it pays 8.5 times an outlook it doesn't trust, from a company that has disappointed three times in 18 months. Where it is probably wrong is in giving no weight to Ahrens. A strategic buyer with A$1bn of sales, a tank strategy and about A$19m already in, under water, is the kind of holder that eventually either bids or sells to someone who will. My 20% chance of a bid by August 2028 may be low.

For the card, the first two combine into one bad case: 35% at A$0.52 (the chance-weighted average of A$0.36 and A$0.59). The card would read 35% at A$0.52, 45% at A$0.94 and 20% at A$1.12.

Expected value: 0.35 × 0.521 + 0.45 × 0.938 + 0.20 × 1.123 = A$0.83, or +14.4% before costs. After about 4.8% of costs it's about +9.5%. The costs are A$15 brokerage each way on about A$2,250 (1.3%), about 3% of spread across both trades in a stock this thin, and the 0.5% currency fee. The minimum over 661 days is 12.9%, so that test fails at A$0.725.

The value test (an ordinary pick):
- The bad case of A$0.52 is 28.1% below A$0.725, inside the 30% limit.
- Against that fall the middling case needs +42.1%, and it's +29.4%. It fails.
- It qualifies at about A$0.69 (the middling test); the expected-gain test allows up to A$0.70.

As a special situation (the Ahrens build-up), the value test doesn't apply. The expected-gain test passes at about A$0.70; at A$0.725 it passes only if the bid chance is about 30% (with the middling case at 35%).
