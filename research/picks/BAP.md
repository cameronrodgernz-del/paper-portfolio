# Bapcor Limited (ASX:BAP): deep dive, the case for buying

Written 2026-11-09 by Claude for the one-company-at-a-time process (rulebook section 5a, docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md). This is the case for buying. A second model writes the case against without seeing this file, then the two are argued out in research/debate/BAP.md, which ends with Cam's decision.
Price at writing: A$0.855 (close of 6 November 2026, ASX key statistics). Market value: A$575.2m (672,753,078 shares on issue, ASX company data). Net bank debt A$116m at 30 September 2026 [S3], so the business is valued at about A$691m before leases.
Status: finished. Not a value pick at A$0.855 (see "Where it lands"). This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Order of work: the company was chosen partly on its price history (the sweep tags falls from the high), so the price was in view from the start. The valuation below works from the filings, and the scenario values come from earnings and multiples, not from steps off today's price.

## Where it lands
Wait. The turnaround is real and the market is paying for only part of it, but at A$0.855 the case fails the rulebook's value test: the bad case is about 35% below the price (the limit is 30%), and the middling case is about +23% (it would need about +52% against a 35% fall). The expected gain after costs, about +18% to +19% to 31 August 2028, does clear the 12.9% minimum. Almost all the upside sits in the good case, which is the shape the value test is there to keep out. On these numbers it qualifies at about A$0.75 or below, or after a first-half FY27 result that shows the margin coming back (the reopen conditions are at the end).

## What the price assumes
The business is valued at about A$691m: A$575.2m of shares plus A$116m of net bank debt. Leases are left out of that figure, so it is compared with earnings after rent, which is how the company's own banking covenants measure it [S2].

On that basis Bapcor earned A$66.8m in FY26 (underlying EBITDA of A$152.5m less A$85.7m of rent) [S2]. The price is therefore 10.3 times last year's earnings after rent, or 8.8 times the A$78.5m the banks count once one-off IT spending and share-based pay are added back [S2].

Super Retail Group, the closest listed peer (Supercheap Auto, rebel, BCF, Macpac), is valued at about 6.9 times its FY26 earnings after rent: A$3,094m of shares plus A$14m of net debt [S20, S21], against about A$453m of earnings after rent (segment EBITDA of A$774m less A$252.5m of lease principal and A$68.1m of lease interest) [S20, S22]. Bapcor itself was valued at about 11.3 times when the business was healthy, at the FY23 result (A$6.77 a share on about 339m shares plus A$251.7m of net debt, against A$225.5m) [S14, S25]. Bain Capital's A$5.40 proposal of June 2024, which the board rejected as too low, was worth about 11.6 times FY24's A$187.6m [S13, S16].

Run backwards, at 7 to 7.5 times (Super Retail's level) the price needs about A$92m to A$99m of earnings after rent each year. On about A$1.95bn of sales, that's a margin of 4.7% to 5.1%. The history is below, on the same basis:

| Year | Sales (A$m) | Earnings after rent (A$m) | Margin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FY23 | 2,021 | 225.5 | 11.2% |
| FY24 | 2,037 | 187.6 | 9.2% |
| FY25 (restated) | 1,959 | 155.9 | 8.0% |
| FY26 | 1,924 | 66.8 | 3.5% |
| What today's price needs | about 1,950 | 92 to 99 | 4.7% to 5.1% |

Sources: FY23 [S14], FY24 [S15], FY25 and FY26 [S1, S2]. FY25 is the restated figure (A$233.1m less A$77.2m of rent). FY23 and FY24 are the company's "pro forma" figures from the time, so they aren't perfectly comparable.

So the market is paying for the margin to recover about a third of the way from FY26 back to FY25, and to stay there. It isn't paying for a return to FY23 or FY24.

## The one thing the market is missing
Stated as the rulebook asks: the market is valuing Bapcor as if most of FY26's margin loss is permanent, when much of it came from operating failures that were fixed between March and August 2026 (half-empty shelves, store-level discounting of 16%, staff turnover above 40%, emergency freight, and about A$11m of one-off IT spending), on the same network that earned an 8% margin a year earlier.

The evidence:
- In-stock levels in August 2026 were 94% at Burson, 92% at JAS and 96% at Autobarn, up from 84%, 53% and 74% in March 2026 [S2].
- Burson's store-level discounting fell from 16% of sales at December 2025 to 8% at June 2026 [S2].
- Team turnover fell from 41.8% to 35.5%, and Burson sales representatives rose from 56 to 64 [S2].
- Emergency deliveries from distribution centres fell from 15.4% of lines ordered in the first half to 12.2% in the second [S2].
- Trade parts sales grew 2.5% in the first quarter of FY27 (1.6% like for like), with a higher market share. Networks sales grew 4.1% [S3].
- Net debt fell from A$387.3m at December 2025 to A$116m at September 2026, helped by the A$200m raising and A$68.5m of working capital released in the second half [S1, S3, S6].

How big that gap is matters, and it isn't large. My middling case for FY28 is about A$106m of earnings after rent, against the A$92m to A$99m the price already needs. Where the price could be most wrong is the good case, a return close to FY25's A$156m. That makes this a turnaround with a long right tail, not a clearly cheap business.

## The company in plain words
Bapcor sells car and truck parts, accessories and workshop equipment across Australia and New Zealand, from about 900 locations [S2]. In FY26 it had four segments [S2]:
- Trade (A$776.9m of sales; EBITDA before rent of A$105.2m, from A$151.7m): Burson Auto Parts, which supplies mechanics from 247 branches, plus trade equipment.
- Networks, formerly Specialist Wholesale (A$620.9m; A$67.7m, from A$63.1m): the truck parts business (CVG), JAS auto electrical and wholesale distribution, from 111 branches.
- Retail (A$393.8m; A$38.7m, from A$42.8m): Autobarn and the other retail brands, with 332 stores of which 213 are franchised.
- New Zealand (A$158.1m; A$19.4m, from A$27.9m): 72 company stores and 120 licensee stores.

Group and head-office costs rose from A$52.4m to A$78.5m [S2].

The last two years were turbulent:
- June 2024: Bain Capital proposed A$5.40 a share, which the board rejected [S16].
- July 2025: a balance sheet review found supplier disputes and receivables that couldn't be collected [S17]. FY25 was later restated, cutting opening retained earnings by A$18.2m [S1].
- October and December 2025: guidance was cut twice. Trade cut prices to win back market share, which hurt margins [S18, S19].
- November 2025: Lachlan Edwards, a restructuring adviser, became Chair [S24].
- January 2026: Chris Wilesmith started as CEO. He was Managing Director of Supercheap Auto for seven years, part of Super Retail Group's leadership for 13, and later CEO of Mitre 10 New Zealand and of Jaycar [S23].
- February 2026: Bapcor raised A$200m at A$0.60 a share, 65% below the last close of A$1.715 [S6]. That roughly doubled the number of shares.

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
Big investors aren't overlooking Bapcor. They've been selling it, often for reasons that have nothing to do with what it's worth today:
- Index funds: Bapcor left the S&P/ASX 200 on 22 December 2025 and the S&P/ASX 300 on 21 September 2026 (Norgate index changes; S&P's September rebalance notice [S30]), so funds tracking those indexes had to sell.
- Investors who joined in the raising are selling at a profit. Tanarra Capital sold about 44m shares between 22 October and 2 November, at about A$0.81 to A$0.86, taking its stake from 16.9% to 10.3% [S26, S27]. Hostplus sold about 23m shares between 22 and 30 October, going from 11.4% to 8.0% [S28, S29]. Together that's about 10% of the company in under a fortnight. Australian Retirement Trust ceased to be a substantial holder on 28 October [S31]. In the 2026 announcements list there are more than a hundred substantial holder notices.
- Screens read it as a loss-maker: the FY26 statutory loss was A$431.6m, after A$442.4m of post-tax significant items, 99% of them non-cash write-downs of goodwill and trademarks [S1, S2].

Against that, five directors bought shares on market with their own money in the two weeks after the AGM trading update [S3, S32 to S36]:

## What the filings show
FY26, against restated FY25 [S1, S2]:
- Underlying revenue fell 1.8% to A$1,924.1m.
- Gross margin fell from 46.1% to 45.3%.
- Costs of doing business rose from 34.2% to 37.4% of sales.
- Underlying EBITDA fell 34.6% to A$152.5m, and underlying net profit fell 85.0% to A$10.8m.
- The halves were flat on the measure before rent: A$76.9m in the first half and A$75.6m in the second [S5, S2]. The second half absorbed the fuel, freight and interest rate effects of the Middle East conflict that began in late March, which cut the May guidance [S4].

Where the A$89m fall in earnings after rent came from: Trade lost A$46.5m, group costs rose A$26.1m, New Zealand lost A$8.5m, Retail lost A$4.1m, Networks gained A$4.6m, and rent rose A$8.5m [S2]. Most of the fall is costs and Trade's price cuts, not lost sales: revenue fell only A$35m. About A$11m of IT project spending sits inside underlying costs, which the banks treat as one-off [S2].

Cash and balance sheet:
- Operating cash flow was A$166.9m, and cash conversion rose to 109.4% from 86.5% [S2].
- Capital spending was cut to A$29.2m from A$61.6m, and free cash flow was A$55.2m [S2].
- At 30 June 2026 [S2]:
  - net debt was A$135.0m, with A$322m of undrawn committed facilities and an average of about three years to maturity (the first is A$180m in July 2028, of which A$35m was drawn);
  - lease liabilities were A$229.0m;
  - book equity was A$565.2m (A$0.84 a share), including A$221.2m of intangibles, so tangible assets are about A$0.51 a share.
- Net debt was A$116m at 30 September 2026 [S3].

Covenants [S2, S7]:
- Net debt is limited to 3.5 times adjusted earnings after rent at 31 December 2026, then 3.0 times from June 2027. The figure was 1.72 times at June 2026, so this isn't the pressure point.
- The pressure point is the fixed charge cover. It measures earnings before rent against interest plus rent, was 1.62 times at June 2026, and can fall to 1.30 at December 2026 and June 2027. But it returns to 1.75 times at 31 December 2027. With rent near A$86m and interest near A$10m, 1.75 times needs adjusted earnings after rent of about A$82m, against A$78.5m in FY26. A turnaround that stalls would meet that test in December 2027.

Outlook [S1, S3]:
- FY27 sales are expected to grow modestly.
- Underlying net profit is expected to be "materially weighted" to the second half, and the benefits of the turnaround will be "largely reinvested in technology and people", so FY27 is unlikely to show the full recovery.
- A portfolio review is looking at selling smaller non-core businesses.
- The first-quarter update reaffirmed this. Retail was soft, and group like-for-like sales grew 0.5% in constant currency.

The impairment test [S1]:
- The write-downs used pre-tax discount rates of 14.8% for Australia and 16.1% for New Zealand, up from 12.6% and 13.2%.
- The company says the cash flow forecasts "have been adjusted down to reflect recent performance levels", with a "more conservative assessment of execution risk".
- Goodwill and trademarks in Networks and Retail were written off entirely, and A$176.2m of goodwill remains in Trade.
- Book equity of A$565.2m is therefore close to what the business is worth if the turnaround delivers little, and the market value of A$575.2m is almost exactly that.

## Valuation
Both methods are worked at August 2028, after the FY28 result, about 22 months out.

The assumptions behind both:
- Sales grow 1.5% in FY27 (guidance says "modest") and 3% in FY28, to about A$2,012m.
- Depreciation and amortisation other than leases stay at about A$35m (FY26: A$101.1m in total, less A$65.9m on leased assets) [S1].
- Bank interest is A$6m to A$12m a year depending on the case, and tax is 30%.

Method 1, value of the business against earnings after rent (EV/EBITDA before leases). The multiple is 7 to 8.5 times: Super Retail is at about 6.9, Bapcor's own healthy level was about 11, and Bain's proposal was about 11.6.

| Case | FY28 margin | Earnings after rent | Multiple | Net debt | A$ a share, with dividends |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Worse | 3.0% | A$60m | 6.5x | A$130m | 0.39 |
| Bad | 3.65% | A$73m | 7.0x | A$100m | 0.62 |
| Middling | 5.25% | A$106m | 7.25x | A$60m | 1.07 (incl. 2c) |
| Good | 7.75% | A$156m | 8.5x | A$40m | 1.95 (incl. 4c) |

Arithmetic for the middling case: A$106m times 7.25 is A$768m, less A$60m of net debt, gives A$708m. Divided by 672.75m shares that's A$1.05, plus 2c of dividends.

Method 2, earnings multiple (P/E of 13.5 times, close to Super Retail's 13.4 times FY26 normalised earnings per share of 100.0c at A$13.41 [S20]):

| Case | Profit after tax | Earnings a share | A$ a share, with dividends |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Worse | A$9m | 1.4c | 0.19 |
| Bad | A$19m | 2.9c | 0.40 |
| Middling | A$44m | 6.5c | 0.90 (incl. 2c) |
| Good | A$80m | 11.9c | 1.65 (incl. 4c) |

Arithmetic for the middling case: A$106m, less A$35m of depreciation, less A$8m of interest, is A$63m before tax and A$44m after it. That's 6.5c a share, times 13.5 is A$0.88, plus 2c.

The two methods differ most in the poor cases, because debt, depreciation and rent take most of the profit when the margin is low. In a poor case the market is likely to value the business on its assets and its network rather than on a few cents of earnings: tangible assets are about A$0.51 a share, and the raising price was A$0.60. In August 2028 the market will also be pricing FY29, which in a recovery would lift the middling value above the trailing figure.

Ranges used: worse A$0.30 to A$0.45 (taken as A$0.40), bad A$0.50 to A$0.65 (A$0.62), middling A$0.90 to A$1.20 (A$1.05), good A$1.65 to A$1.95 (A$1.85). The main thing each depends on is the FY28 margin. Each point of margin is worth about A$0.21 to A$0.25 a share at these multiples.

## Why the market is wrong
1. It treats a bad year of operating failures as the new normal. Shelves were half empty, discounting was uncontrolled, staff churned, and head office grew. The fixes started in February 2026 and the measures had improved sharply by August [S2]. FY26's margin was 3.5%, on the same network that earned 8.0% in FY25 and 11.2% in FY23.
2. The register is still digesting the raising. Holders who paid A$0.60 in March are selling, and index funds have had to leave. That supply weighs on the price whatever the business is worth, and it should ease as those holders finish.

Neither point makes Bapcor clearly cheap today. The price already pays for a recovery to about a 5% margin.

## Outcomes and chances
Resolve date: 31 August 2028, after the FY28 result (661 days away). Prices include dividends that go ex before then: none in the bad cases, about 2c in the middling case and about 4c in the good case.

| Outcome | What happens | Chance | Price by resolve date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Worse | The turnaround fails: margin near 3%, the December 2027 fixed charge test is missed, and another raising or covenant reset follows | 10% | A$0.40 |
| Bad | The turnaround stalls: margin about 3.6%, held back by fuel, wages and price cuts; valued at 7 times | 25% | A$0.62 |
| Middling | Partial recovery: margin about 5.25% in FY28 (A$106m after rent) and rising; dividends restart | 45% | A$1.05 |
| Good | Strong recovery or a bid: margin back near FY25's 7.75% (A$156m), valued at 8.5 times, or a takeover proposal | 20% | A$1.85 |

For the card, which takes three outcomes, the first two combine into one bad case: 35% at A$0.56 (the chance-weighted average of A$0.40 and A$0.62). The card then reads 35% at A$0.56, 45% at A$1.05 and 20% at A$1.85.

Expected price: 0.35 × 0.56 + 0.45 × 1.05 + 0.20 × 1.85 = A$1.037, or +21.3% before costs. After an estimated 2% to 3% of costs it's about +18% to +19%. That covers A$15 brokerage each way on an order of about A$2,250, half the spread each way, and the 0.5% currency fee. The rulebook minimum over 661 days is 12.9% (the 2.13% cash rate plus 5% a year), so that test passes.

The value test (rulebook section 5a):
- The bad case of A$0.56 is 34.6% below A$0.855, over the 30% limit.
- Against that fall the middling case would need to be +51.9%, and it's +22.8%.
- So the test fails, on both counts.

The price at which it would pass is about A$0.75, solved for both conditions. At A$0.75 the bad case is 25.3% below, and the middling case is +40%, against the +38% it would need.

## What would make this wrong
- The margin doesn't come back because it was never mostly about fixable failures: Trade's price cuts stick, competitors such as Repco match them, wages and rent keep rising faster than sales, and the "reinvested in technology and people" spending becomes permanent.
- A softer economy and higher interest rates hit workshop and retail demand for longer, with Retail and New Zealand already weak [S3].
- More accounting surprises. FY25 was restated, and stocktake losses were found at Precision [S1, S2].
- The fixed charge test at December 2027 forces another raising.

Exit rule, if it's ever bought: sell if the FY27 result shows earnings after rent below A$80m, or the company signals it will miss the December 2027 fixed charge test, or another equity raising is announced. A close 25% below the buy price means a review, not a sale (rulebook section 5a).

## Questions Cam is better placed to judge
- How quickly does a trade parts network win back mechanics after a period of poor stock availability? Is a recovery from 53% to 92% in-stock at JAS enough, or do lost accounts take years to return?
- Is halving store-level discounting (16% to 8%) while cutting list prices on 10,000 Burson products a sound way to rebuild margin, or does it just move the discount?
- What does a 35% staff turnover rate mean for a branch network like this, and what should a well-run one achieve?
- What earnings margin should Autobarn reach under a CEO who ran Supercheap Auto? Supercheap Auto made A$203.3m of segment profit before tax on A$1.6bn of sales in FY26 [S20]. Bapcor Retail made A$38.7m before rent on A$393.8m.

## Reopen conditions
- At A$0.75 or below with the FY27 outlook unchanged: send to the case against as a value pick.
- At the 1H FY27 result, re-run if:
  - underlying earnings after rent for the half are A$40m or more (about A$34m a half in FY26 on the covenant measure, derived from [S2]);
  - Trade like-for-like sales are still growing;
  - net debt is A$110m or less;
  - the company says it's on track for the December 2027 fixed charge test.

  That would lift the middling case and shrink the bad one.
