# Trajan Group Holdings (ASX:TRJ): deep dive (not a value pick)

Verdict: wait for a named event, the half-year trading update (about 5 February 2027) and the first-half result (about 26 February 2027). No card. Not a value pick (rulebook section 5a): the bad case (A$0.14) is 45% below the A$0.255 price, far outside the 10% the rule allows. As an ordinary pick the expected gain after costs is about +3.6%, short of the desk's guide bar of about 9% for a six-month hold.

> The desk's view in one line: the fact sheet's A$0.88 "our way" value doesn't survive. It needs about 12.5 times after-lease EBITDA, a multiple paid for bigger, steadier lab-tools businesses, and Trajan has missed its earnings guidance three years running (FY24, FY25 and FY26, not two). Worked from cash earnings and from what the market pays comparable small, indebted peers, the business is worth about A$0.31 a share if FY27 runs at the bottom half of guidance, A$0.48 if it runs near the top and the market starts to believe it, and A$0.14 if the first half disappoints again. The FY27 guidance is mostly cost cuts already made, which is the good news, but the first-half result can't settle the question on its own, because each of the last three years went wrong in the second half or after a "guidance maintained" first half. On 35/45/20 chances the expected price is A$0.2845, +11.6% before costs and about +3.6% after an 8% round trip in a share this thin. The founder's 50.4% adds little inside six months: he hasn't bought since November 2025 and has said nothing about control. Reopen at A$0.22 or below with guidance unchanged, or after a trading update that shows first-half nEBITDA of A$8.0m or more and net debt of A$32m or less.

## The company in plain words
Trajan makes the small, precise parts that laboratories use to analyse samples: syringes, chromatography columns and inlet parts, emitter tips for mass spectrometers (the Components and Consumables segment, A$102.6m of FY26 revenue); automated sample-handling systems sold mostly through Axel Semrau in Germany (Capital Equipment, A$52.9m); and blood microsampling devices plus a small portable instrument still being commercialised (Disruptive Technologies, A$5.9m) [S1]. About 85% of revenue is in US dollars and euros, from seven factories in Australia, Malaysia, the US and Germany, with under 600 staff after cuts from about 645 in September 2025 [S3, S5]. The money is made in consumables: segment EBITDA of A$32.1m in FY26 against A$8.6m from Capital Equipment and a loss of A$0.6m in Disruptive, less corporate costs of about A$27m [S1, S3].

Founder Stephen Tomisich is chief executive and managing director. His family company (Tomisich Family Pty Ltd) owned all of Trajan before the June 2021 listing at A$1.70, sold 23.5m shares into the float (about A$40m) and kept 76,470,588 [S22]. It now holds 76,978,851 shares, 50.36% [S1 remuneration report, S25]. Other substantial holders: MA Financial Group 16,051,646 (10.53%, notice of 2 September 2025, not the 9.53% the annual report lists) and AustralianSuper 9,958,728 (6.55%, May 2023 notice) [S26]. Investors Mutual sold out in June and July 2026 (fact sheet, scanned notices). The top 20 hold 127.9m shares (83.6%); there are 1,781 holders, 877 with less than a marketable parcel [S1].

Five years: revenue A$107.6m (FY22), A$162.2m (FY23, after four acquisitions), A$155.0m (FY24), A$166.5m (FY25), A$161.4m (FY26) [S21, S16, S11, S1]. Group nEBITDA A$19.5m in FY23 (restated), then A$12.3m, A$15.5m and A$13.2m [S16, S11, S1]. Statutory results: profit A$1.9m in FY23, then losses of A$25.3m (FY24, after a A$26.7m impairment, mostly the Neoteryx microsampling business), A$4.5m and A$0.6m [S29, S17]. The shares listed at A$1.70, a 2022 placement was at A$2.00, and the price is now A$0.255, down 85% [S22, S23].

Against the first guidance each year (arithmetic in python3 -I, scratch script trj_val.py, not committed):
- FY24: revenue 8.8% under the bottom; Core nEBITDA 33% under the bottom. Causes given: customer destocking in H1, then slow conversion of orders at higher cost (overtime and contractors) and weak pharmaceutical capital equipment [S18, S16].
- FY25: revenue above the range; nEBITDA 8.8% under the bottom. Causes: a A$0.8m currency revaluation, about A$1m of microsampling orders not shipped by 30 June, lower margins on capital equipment sold through distributors in price-sensitive Asian markets, and tariff disruption [S12].
- FY26: revenue 5.1% under, nEBITDA 17.5% under the bottom, and 5.7% under the A$14m implied in May. The pattern matters for the dated events: guidance was "maintained" at the half in all three years, and in FY25 the first half itself looked on track (A$7.9m) before the year missed. A good first half has not been a reliable sign of the full year.

The revenue guidance for FY27 has already been trimmed once: "mid-single digit organic revenue growth" on 27 August became A$162m to A$166m, about 2% at the midpoint, on 28 October, both at current exchange rates [S2, S5]. The Australian dollar averaged about US$0.70 in both August and October (data/fx/fx_daily.csv), so this was not a currency change.

### What "normalised" leaves out
nEBITDA is statutory EBITDA before restructuring costs, acquisition costs and the revaluation of forward currency contracts [S1]. The items added back were A$1.6m in FY25 (redundancies A$0.6m, consultants A$0.5m, refinancing legal fees A$0.5m) and A$1.2m in FY26 (redundancies A$0.3m, ERP rollout A$0.6m, refinancing legal fees A$0.3m); in H1 FY24 restructuring was A$1.2m [S1, S11, S19]. Management says "one-off restructuring costs are part of that progression" in FY27 [S3]. These recur, so I deduct about A$1.0m to A$1.2m a year. nEBITDA does include the balance-sheet currency revaluation (a A$1.3m cost in H1 FY26) and share-based pay [S6]. Below EBITDA sit bank charges (A$0.43m) and "early settlement charges" (A$0.25m) in finance costs [S1]. No development spending is capitalised: R&D of A$6.6m was expensed, and capital spending (A$3.4m) was about equal to depreciation of plant (A$3.5m) [S1].

The forward currency contracts run to June 2027 and their gains are excluded from nEBITDA, but at 30 June 2026 they were a A$0.2m liability, so they don't hide a material cash gain [S1].

### How much of the FY27 bridge is already done
FY27's midpoint (A$16.75m) is A$3.55m above FY26's A$13.2m. From the filings:
- Costs in H1 FY26 not expected to recur: currency revaluation A$1.3m, tariff timing A$0.4m, in-region set-up and freight A$0.4m; about A$2.1m [S6].
- Cost cuts made in FY26 and only half counted in it: Project Neptune headcount and facilities A$1.2m, corporate headcount A$1.2m and supply chain A$0.9m, all per half in H2 [S3]; FTEs fell by 45 to under 600 [S3]. Their extra effect in H1 FY27 is up to about A$3.3m.
- Price rises from 1 January 2026, targeted at A$1.3m a half [S7].
- Against: the Australian dollar averaged about US$0.655 in H1 FY26 and about US$0.70 since; the same move cost A$2.4m in H2 FY26 [S3; data/fx].
Net, the self-help already in place covers more than the A$3.55m bridge. Put another way, H2 FY26 ran at A$8.1m a half at today's exchange rates, A$16.2m a year, so the guidance asks only that the business hold its H2 FY26 rate and add about A$0.5m from 2% sales growth. On that reading about 85% to 100% of the bridge is cost cuts already made and at most about A$0.5m depends on growth still to come.

The caution is Q1. At A$3.5m it is below the H2 FY26 average of A$4.05m a quarter; the rest of FY27 needs A$4.17m to A$4.67m a quarter [S4, S5]. Q1 gross margin was above 42% and employee costs down 8% on a year earlier, but Capital Equipment revenue fell 1.2% and the June 2026 order book (A$8.7m) was below December's (A$10.8m) [S5, S3, S6].

### Debt, facilities and headroom
- Lender: National Australia Bank, which took over the HSBC loan late in FY25 [S11 note 18]. Facilities at 30 June 2026: revolving cash advance A$40.0m, term cash advance A$5.5m, overdraft A$5.0m, credit card A$0.2m, all at the bank bill rate plus a margin; plus Axel Semrau's unsecured EUR1.5m (A$2.5m) overdraft with HypoVereinsbank, EUR1.3m drawn [S1 note 18].
- Drawn: A$47.9m of A$53.2m, A$5.3m unused (A$7.8m in December 2025, A$11.3m in June 2025). With A$12.6m of cash, about A$17.9m of liquidity at 30 June 2026 [S1, S6].
- Maturity: the loans sit in "between 2 and 5 years" in the liquidity table; the date isn't disclosed. Average rate 6.8%; about A$236,000 a month of interest [S1 note 24].
- Covenants: "standard covenant requirements", tested on 31 December, 31 March, 30 June and 30 September, with the loan "repayable on demand if the covenants are not met" [S6 note 13]. The levels and definitions are not disclosed. Trajan's own target was net debt below 2.0 times nEBITDA [S12].
- Ratios: net debt A$34.6m was 2.6 times FY26 nEBITDA (3.45 times with A$10.9m of leases); on the rolling A$16.2m and September net debt of A$33.0m it is 2.0 times (2.7 with leases) [S1, S4].
- History: the A$26.7m impairment in FY24 broke a minimum equity covenant [S17]; the whole A$43.1m HSBC loan was shown as current at 30 June 2024 [S11 note 18 comparatives], and the December 2024 half-year had a going-concern note with net current liabilities of A$4.1m and a review of the borrowing arrangements [S14]. Refinancing legal fees of A$0.5m in FY25 and A$0.3m in FY26 [S1] suggest the facility was worked on again in FY26, the year it moved to principal-and-interest; the filings don't say why.
- Goodwill risk: the Chromatography Research Supplies goodwill was folded into the wider consumables cash-generating unit in FY26, and that unit's headroom is thin: revenue growth 1.0 points lower or margins 1.4 points lower would trigger an impairment [S1 note 11]. Another impairment would cut equity, which has tripped a covenant before.

## Valuation
Done from the filings and peers' prices. The measure the market uses for small lab-tools makers is enterprise value over EBITDA. Peers reporting under US rules carry lease costs inside EBITDA, so the fair comparison is Trajan's enterprise value without leases over EBITDA after lease payments. Trajan's after-lease EBITDA, net of the recurring "one-offs", was A$8.7m in FY26, A$11.75m on the rolling 12 months to September, and A$11.75m, A$12.5m and A$13.25m at the bottom, middle and top of FY27 guidance.

### Testing the fact sheet's 10.7 times
The fact sheet's "our way" value of A$0.88 applies the average of Trajan's three year-end multiples (16.4, 9.8 and 5.8 times pre-lease nEBITDA with leases in the enterprise value) to the FY27 midpoint. Two problems:
1. The 16.4 times at June 2024 was paid while the market still expected Core nEBITDA of A$15.7m to A$17.1m and a return to growth; group nEBITDA came in at A$12.3m [S18, S16]. Each later year-end multiple fell after a miss. The average describes a company the market no longer believes in.
2. 10.7 times A$16.75m is an enterprise value of A$179m, or about 12.5 times after-lease EBITDA (13.7 times after the recurring one-offs). That is where Mesa Labs trades, not where a micro-cap with three misses and 2.6 times debt to earnings trades.

### Method B: cash flow to the whole business, capitalised
Free cash flow to the business is after-lease EBITDA less capital spending (A$3.5m) less tax, grown at 2% to 3% and discounted at 10.5% to 12% (a small, indebted, founder-controlled company):
- Bad: A$9.0m less A$3.5m less A$0.8m = A$4.7m; at 12% and 2%, A$47.9m; less A$34.0m: A$0.091.
- Middling: A$12.0m less A$3.5m less A$1.4m = A$7.1m; at 11.5% and 2%, A$76.2m; less A$32.5m: A$0.286.
- Good: A$13.25m less A$3.5m less A$1.7m = A$8.05m; at 10.5% and 3%, A$110.6m; less A$31.5m: A$0.517.
The input that moves this most is the discount rate: a point either way moves the middling value by about 8c.

### Method C: the market's own multiple on rolling pre-lease nEBITDA
In September 2026 the market paid about 5.2 times FY26 nEBITDA (A$0.155, before the Q1 update); today it pays 5.1 times the rolling figure. At 4.3 times on A$14.5m (bad), 5.75 times on A$15.9m (middling) and 6.75 times on A$16.6m (good), less net debt and A$10.5m of leases: A$0.117, A$0.317 and A$0.458.

### Range
Bad A$0.09 to A$0.15 (I use A$0.14); middling A$0.29 to A$0.33 (A$0.31); good A$0.46 to A$0.52 (A$0.48). The middling value depends on the business holding its H2 FY26 rate through FY27; the good value depends on the market paying a Harvard Bioscience-type multiple before the full year is in; the bad value depends on net debt staying above A$34m. A trade buyer paying 9.5 times after-lease EBITDA for the top of guidance (A$126m less A$31.5m) gives about A$0.62, still well short of A$0.88, and it would need the founder to sell.

What the rules let Tomisich do (Corporations Act chapter 6 as summarised in the Allens 2026 handbook [L1, L2]):
- Creep: above 19%, a holder may buy up to 3% every six months without a bid. He can do this at any time outside the company's closed periods; he last did so in November 2025, and only 0.07%.
- Takeover bid: as a holder of 30% or more, his bid would need an independent expert's report in the target's statement. Compulsory acquisition needs 90% and 75% of the shares he doesn't already own; MA alone (10.53%) caps him at 89.5% if it refuses.
- Scheme: 75% of votes cast and a majority by number of holders voting, with the bidder usually voting in a separate class; an expert's report is near-certain. MA and AustralianSuper together hold 34% of the shares he doesn't own, enough to block on a typical turnout.
- Approval by ordinary resolution (item 7) can't be used to buy 100% without ASIC relief [L1].
- No dividends, no buy-back, and the bank's consent would likely be needed for any debt-funded buyout.

What he could afford: buying the 75.9m shares he doesn't own costs A$22.8m at A$0.30, A$27.3m at A$0.36 and A$30.4m at A$0.40, more than his own stake is worth at today's price (A$19.6m). The family realised about A$40m in 2021 and a family entity sold the Ringwood property Trajan used to lease in May 2024 [S22, S1], but what is left of that, and whether the shares are pledged, isn't disclosed. A buyout would most likely need a financial partner.

What minority holders might be offered: in the repo's deal database, nine buyouts by majority holders (all NZX, 2015 to 2026) paid 21% to 83% over the undisturbed price, most between 24% and 73%; the two with a controller near Tomisich's level were Pulse Energy (55.9% holder, 83%) and Marsden Maritime (53.6%, 73%, by scheme) [L4]. Applied to A$0.255: A$0.31 at 21%, A$0.36 at 40%, A$0.47 at 83%. An expert valuing control on the methods above would put fair value around A$0.31 to A$0.50, so an offer much under A$0.35 would struggle to be called fair, and MA, which paid about A$0.80, would be the swing voter.

Statement of intent: none. The 27 March 2026 letter says the valuation is "dramatically out of line with our industry peers" and that "over time, the true value of our business will be recognized and realized" [S8]. The AGM chair said the board's response to the share price is "to stay focused on the fundamentals" [S5]. Nobody on the board bought below A$0.40.

The "nothing happens" value is the operating case: A$0.31 if the business holds its run rate, A$0.14 if it doesn't. A founder proposal inside six months is possible but unsupported by any filing; I put it at about 3% and fold it into the good outcome, since a typical offer (A$0.36 to A$0.47) is no better than the good operating case.

## Why the market is wrong
It is only partly wrong. At A$0.255 the market pays about 6 times after-lease EBITDA on the rolling 12 months, below the closest small, indebted peer (Harvard Bioscience, about 8 to 9 times) and below what Trajan itself paid for its bolt-ons, for a business whose FY27 guidance rests mostly on cost cuts already made. If the first half shows the H2 FY26 rate holding (about A$8m) and net debt falling under the new repayment schedule, the shares are worth about A$0.31.

Why that may not change by March 2027: the market has seen three years of "guidance maintained" at the half followed by misses, a CFO leave mid-turnaround, and a bank start amortising the loans. A good first half is necessary but not sufficient; full belief probably waits for the FY27 result in August 2027, outside the six-month window. And at our size the round trip costs about 8%.

## What would make this wrong
1. Passing is wrong if the first half comes in well above A$8m and net debt falls fast: the market could pay 8 to 9 times, about A$0.50, and the cost of waiting is that move. The reopen rule below catches the case where the update is good and the price has not yet run.
2. Buying would be wrong if the second half of FY26's gains were partly one-off: Q1 FY27 at A$3.5m is already below the H2 FY26 quarterly average, Capital Equipment is still falling, and a CFO is leaving. A miss with debt near 2.5 times could bring the bank to the table and a share issue at a large discount, below the A$0.14 bad case.
3. A founder or trade proposal would change the case entirely; at 50.4% nothing happens without Tomisich.

Exit rule: no position. Reopen with a fresh look (and the red team) on any of:
- the price at A$0.22 or below before the trading update, with FY27 guidance unchanged (expected gain after costs about +21% on these chances);
- a trading update or result showing H1 nEBITDA of A$8.0m or more, net debt of A$32m or less and guidance reaffirmed, with the price at A$0.30 or below;
- any Tomisich purchase of 1% or more, a stated intention, or a proposal (then a special situation write-up);
- a substantial holder notice from MA Financial or AustralianSuper, or a new holder above 5%.
It becomes no case on a guidance cut, a covenant waiver or a capital raising.
