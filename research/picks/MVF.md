## The company in plain words
Monash IVF runs fertility clinics. In Australia it has 24 IVF clinics and 4 service centres, with a presence in every mainland capital city, 4 day hospitals, 18 ultrasound clinics (it describes itself as a provider of "specialist women's imaging"), a genetics laboratory in Adelaide and endocrine and andrology laboratories; outside Australia it has 4 clinics in Kuala Lumpur, Johor Bahru, Singapore and Bali [M3]. It is the second largest IVF provider in Australia, with 19.5% of Australian stimulated cycles over the twelve months to June 2026 (20.2% in the second half) [M3]. Virtus Health, private since 2022, is the largest.

Money comes from patient fees for IVF treatment (a stimulated cycle, where eggs are collected, and frozen embryo transfers), part of which Medicare pays back to the patient, plus ultrasound scans, embryo genetic testing (PGT), day surgery and storage fees. FY26 revenue was A$269.5m: A$248.2m in Australia (IVF and ultrasound) and A$21.2m international [M1]. The cost base is mostly clinical staff, scientists, rent and laboratories, which the company calls "predominantly fixed" [M2], so a small fall in cycles takes a large share of profit.

Management and board: Dr Victoria Atkinson, CEO and Managing Director since 5 January 2026 (formerly Chief Medical Officer of Healthscope) [M4, M5, M18]. Her predecessor Michael Knaap resigned on 12 June 2025, two days after the second embryo incident was announced; CFO Malik Jainudeen acted as CEO until she started [M6]. Finance has had three interim or departing chiefs since: Andrew MacLachlan as interim CFO from July 2025, Jainudeen's resignation as CFO announced in January 2026, and Birol Akdogan as interim CFO from March 2026; Matt Harrington starts by 4 January 2027 [M7, M31]. ## What the filings show

### The incidents and what they cost
- Brisbane: the embryo of one patient was transferred to another, and a child was born. The company learned of it in February 2025 and confirmed it after a newspaper report on 10 April 2025 [M15]. The shares fell from A$1.055 on 9 April to A$0.69 on 11 April 2025 [D1].
- Clayton (Melbourne): on 5 June 2025 a patient's own embryo was transferred instead of her partner's, as the treatment plan required [M6]. The independent review by Fiona McLeod AO SC found human error in Brisbane and, at Clayton, human error at several stages plus IT system limits [M16].
- The Victorian Department of Health put conditions on the Victorian clinic registrations from 28 November 2025 (patient matching, the patient system, witnessing, training, audits and reporting). The company said they had already been put in place and were not expected to be material [M17].
- Costs: A$4.5m before tax in FY26 for "professional service costs and additional measures arising from clinic-specific incidents" (A$1.3m in FY25), excluded from underlying profit; up to A$2.8m is expected back from insurers in FY27 and isn't booked [M1, M3]. The company says claims "have been lodged and settled" [M1]. A donor couple sued in the Supreme Court of Victoria in May 2026 [X3, a secondary source]; the amount isn't public.
- What the incidents did to the business: by the end of October 2025 domestic stimulated cycles were down 12% on a year earlier, market share was down 2.4 points and new patient registrations were down 12%, "with weakness largely in Victoria and NSW" [M18]. For 1H26 the domestic cycle count fell 11.7%. Price rises were held back on the east coast [M3, M20].

Details a quick reader would miss, all in Genesis's notice [S2]:
- The swap (Annexure C): physically or cash settled at Genesis's choice, cash by default; its unwind was scheduled to start nine months after its 24 November 2025 start, and Genesis could extend that by notice to no later than twelve months, about 24 November 2026. Physical settlement is conditional on not needing a foreign investment approval. Until settled, Jarden votes any hedge shares at its own discretion.
- A collar (Annexure D): on 17.4m shares Genesis holds cash-settled put options at A$0.80 and has sold call options at A$0.96. Its losses below A$0.80 are covered on those shares and its gains above A$0.96 are given up. The expiry isn't disclosed.
- The co-operation agreement (Annexure B): it binds both sides to vote against and not accept any competing proposal and not to sell, but only for the "Exclusivity Period", four months from 21 November 2025 "or such other later or earlier date as the parties may agree", and the agreement "terminates at the end of the Exclusivity Period" (clauses 3, 4 and 7.2).

Where they stand now: no substantial holder notice has been filed by either since 24 November 2025 [D4]. The April 2026 proposal came after the four months had run, so the agreement was presumably extended; whether it still binds them isn't public. If it has ended, each holds under 10% and should have filed a notice; none has been. The register at 30 September 2026 still shows Magenta with 23.4m shares [M10], so the swap hadn't been physically settled by then. Heavy trading from 1 to 16 September 2026 (16.9m shares, A$13.4m, at rising prices) and a 9.8m-share trade on 21 October 2026 (A$7.0m) [D1] are the size of the swap, but no notice links them to it, and Brunneis Investments' 19.0m holding is on the 30 September 2026 register but wasn't among the top 20 (smallest 1.3m) a year earlier [M10, M32]. Who Brunneis is, I couldn't find.

What they've said: nothing public since the April proposal. They didn't nominate a director for the AGM (nominations closed 8 October 2026 [M28]; the notice has only board nominees [M29]).

What they can afford: buying the 80.4% they don't own at A$0.90 costs about A$282m, plus refinancing A$110m of bank debt. Soul Patts is a large listed investment house and can fund that alone; the board cited "uncertainty in respect of the financing arrangements" in November 2025 [M25]. Genesis's fund size isn't public.

What the rules let them do:
- Creep (item 9 of section 611): having held over 19% since 21 November 2025, they could add 3 points in any six months from about 21 May 2026, to 22.6%. They haven't.
- A takeover bid at any time, at any price, without the board's support. The minimum price rule doesn't bind (their last purchase, at A$0.80, was more than four months ago). A private equity buyer rarely bids without due diligence; Soul Patts could.
- A scheme needs the board to propose it, then 75% of votes cast and a majority by number of the other holders, and the court.
- A block: at the 2025 AGM about 134.5m votes were cast on the remuneration report [M30]. A rival scheme with, say, 60% turnout would see the consortium's 61.2m registered shares cast about a quarter of the votes, enough to block it; a rival takeover bid can't reach 90% compulsory acquisition without them. Any other buyer has to deal with the consortium first, as CapVest had to deal with BGH at Virtus [X5].
- At the AGM: a "first strike" needs 25% of the votes cast on the remuneration report against it. Last year 134.5m votes were cast and 8.3% were against [M30]; the consortium's 61.2m registered shares voting against would, at that turnout, be well over a third of all votes on their own. A strike in 2026 and another in 2027 would put a board spill to the 2027 AGM.

### Comparable cases
- Monash IVF itself: the consortium withdrew A$0.80 after a month and returned 4.5 months later 12.5% higher, calling that its highest.
- Virtus Health: the bidder that already held about 20% won control six months after its first approach was rejected, at 15% above its first proposal [X5].
- The repo's history test of ASX deals that failed after becoming binding: 2 of 22 drew another bid within the holding period [D5]. This case never reached a binding agreement, so it's only a rough guide.

## Why the market is wrong
I can't say that it is. At A$0.74 the market pays 19 times FY26 attributable underlying earnings and 10.5 times EBITDA after rent, above the median of three steadier listed peers (15.6 and 9.1 times). That price is consistent with either a recovery to about A$58m of FY27 EBITDA with no bid, or the business at FY26 earnings (A$0.59) plus about a 50% chance of the consortium's A$0.90. Both look generous: management has missed four times in fifteen months, and the consortium called A$0.90 its highest before the business fell further. The triage saw the shares 22% below the A$0.90; the filings show that the A$0.90 was a better price than the business has since justified, and the "nothing happens" value is lower than the April undisturbed price because earnings have fallen since.

## What would make this wrong
The two things most likely to make "wait" the wrong call:
1. The consortium comes back soon. It holds 19.6% at a loss, its 19.6% blocks everyone else, the board now has a new chair and has seen a fourth miss, and Genesis's swap forces a decision by about late November. An agreed scheme at A$0.90 would be +22%; even at A$0.85 it would be +15%.
2. The second half was the bottom. If restored pricing, the 13 new specialists, the Brisbane clinic and the cost programme show up in the AGM update, FY27 EBITDA of A$60m or more would be worth A$0.76 to A$0.86 on its own, and the market would likely add something for the consortium.

And the two things that would hurt anyone holding: the AGM update shows 1H27 still below 1H26, putting the value near A$0.50 to A$0.59; or Genesis cash-settles its swap and Jarden's sale of up to 15.2m shares lands on a market that trades about 385,000 shares a day.

## Corrections to the triage
1. "Nothing happens" is about A$0.61 including the interim dividend, not A$0.66. The April 2026 undisturbed price came before the June profit warning and the A$5.7m second half; at FY26 earnings the business is worth about A$0.59 at peer multiples.
