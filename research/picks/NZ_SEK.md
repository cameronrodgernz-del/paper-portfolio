# Seeka Limited (NZX:SEK): deep dive

Written 2026-11-01 by the research desk. Price at writing: NZ$5.04 (close on 30 October 2026, FinFeedAPI, data/prices/NZX/SEK.csv; the NZX quote page shows the same last price, with a bid of NZ$4.99 and an offer of NZ$5.04 [S1]). Market value: NZ$226.6m (shares on issue 44,969,317, NZX [S1]).
Status: finished. This is a simulation and not financial advice.

> The desk's view in one line: Seeka was the best of this week's NZX quick looks, and it's cheap on this year's numbers, but on the chances below nearly all of the expected gain to 30 April 2027 is the final dividend, which the price already allows for. The desk sees no edge over the New Zealand market within the window and recommends watching, not a card (see "Outcomes and chances").

## The company in plain words
Seeka packs, cool-stores and ships kiwifruit for New Zealand growers (its post-harvest business), grows kiwifruit and avocados on orchards it owns, leases or manages, sells second-grade and imported fruit in New Zealand (SeekaFresh), and runs orchards and a packhouse in Victoria, Australia. Post-harvest is the business: in FY25 it earned NZ$105.0m of the NZ$123.2m of segment EBITDA, or 85% [S9].

The money comes from fees per tray for packing and storing growers' fruit. Those fees are paid out of the Zespri receipts that a related company, Seeka Growers Limited, collects on the growers' behalf. NZ$272.7m of cash came in that way in FY25, 62% of revenue of NZ$439.6m [S8, note 26]. Zespri is the only exporter of New Zealand kiwifruit to markets other than Australia, under statutory regulations [S8, financial risk note]. So the company's income rests on one fruit, one exporter and the size of each year's crop.

Seeka packed 45.4m trays of first-grade (class 1) kiwifruit in 2026, against 47.1m in 2025 [S4]. The whole New Zealand crop was a record of about 225m trays in 2026, after 216m in 2025 [S21]. Seeka's share therefore fell from about 21.8% to about 20.2%.

Michael Franks has been chief executive since December 2006 and Nicola Neilson chief financial officer since May 2022 [S26]. Hayley Gourley became chair on 20 August 2026; the previous chair, Mark Dewdney, stayed on as a director [S17].

The register is spread across growers, iwi entities and small investors. Largest holders at 16 January 2026 [S8, p. 92]: Tomlinson Group Investments 7.34%, Masfen Securities 4.86%, Seeka Share Trustee 4.04% (shares held for the grower and staff share schemes), Te Awanui Huka Pak 3.89%, Sumifru Singapore 3.62%, Custodial Services 3.44%, Omega Kiwifruit 3.17%, New Zealand Depository Nominee 2.76%, Accident Compensation Corporation 2.22% and Eastern Bay Orchards 2.00%. The substantial holder notices on file at 31 December 2025 were Tomlinson (December 2020), Masfen (December 2022) and Sumifru (September 2015, now below 5% on the register) [S8, p. 90]. ACC is the only fund manager named in the top 25.

The business is seasonal: almost all the year's profit is made in the first half, during harvest, and the second half makes a loss. FY23 shows the downside: a warm, wet winter, cyclones and hail cut Seeka's trays to 29.8m, it lost NZ$21.0m before tax, suspended dividends and relied on covenant waivers from its banks [S20].

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Size: NZ$227m market value.
- Index: outside the NZX 20 (config/nzx_top20.csv) and not in the S&P/NZX 50, whose smallest member at 30 October 2026 was Kathmandu at NZ$135m [S22]. Index funds tracking the NZX 50 don't hold it.
- Register: grower and iwi holders, with ACC (2.22%) the only fund manager among the top 25 [S8, p. 92].
- Trading: about NZ$120,000 to NZ$140,000 a day [S1, S23]. A fund wanting NZ$10m would need months.
- Coverage: the company publishes "analyst briefing packs" with each result [S4, S9], so some analysts follow it. I found no broker research cited in its filings and did not check broker databases, so "little coverage" is likely, not proven.
- History: the FY22 and FY23 years, and the covenant waivers, are recent. The auditor listed as a key audit matter that Seeka's market value was below its net assets at 31 December 2025 (NZ$205m at the NZ$4.66 close against NZ$298.1m), which required an impairment test [S8, auditor's report and note 11].

## What the filings show
Guidance, counted from the announcements themselves
- FY24: first guidance on 19 June 2024 of NZ$15m to NZ$19m profit before tax; raised on 22 August to NZ$17m to NZ$21m; raised on 16 October to NZ$21m to NZ$25m ("the second guidance upgrade"); raised on 13 December to NZ$23m to NZ$27m ("the third positive guidance revision"); raised again on 21 January 2025 to NZ$27.5m to NZ$31.5m. Four upgrades. Result NZ$29.7m [S11].
- FY25: first guidance on 16 June 2025 of NZ$33m to NZ$37m; raised on 20 August to NZ$35m to NZ$39m; raised on 17 September to NZ$39m to NZ$43m; restated unchanged at the 16 October stakeholder update [S12]; raised on 15 December to NZ$44m to NZ$48m. Three upgrades. Result NZ$47.5m [S10, S7].
- FY26: first guidance on 17 June 2026 of NZ$38m to NZ$42m [S5]; raised on 20 August to NZ$39m to NZ$43m [S2]; raised on 16 September to NZ$41m to NZ$45m [S6]. Two upgrades so far. I found no further change up to 30 October 2026.
- In both completed years the result landed inside the last range given, and the last range was well above the first.

What the current range implies for the second half
- First-half profit before tax was NZ$62.6m [S2]. A full year of NZ$41m to NZ$45m implies a second-half loss of NZ$17.6m to NZ$21.6m.
- The last two second halves lost NZ$15.3m (FY24: NZ$29.7m less NZ$45.0m [S11]) and NZ$11.9m (FY25: NZ$47.5m less NZ$59.4m), or NZ$12.6m in FY25 leaving out other income [S3, S8].
- Reasons the second half of FY26 could be weaker: fewer trays to store and ship, Hayward yields on Seeka's own orchards down 18%, Australian volumes down 16% after a hot, dry summer, and more depreciation on the new packlines [S4].
- Reasons it could be better: interest cost is falling (NZ$3.6m in the first half against NZ$5.0m) [S3], and the September upgrade already cited Zespri returns, citrus and insurance savings [S6].
- If the second half of FY26 matched FY25's second half without other income, the year would come to about NZ$50.0m. My central estimate is NZ$45m to NZ$47m. This is my inference, not the company's figure.

One-offs, checked
- FY25's NPBT of NZ$47.5m included NZ$3.8m of "other income", of which NZ$3.1m came in the first half; the cash flow shows NZ$2.5m of insurance proceeds [S3, S8]. The first half of FY26 had only NZ$0.1m. On a like-for-like basis FY25 was about NZ$43.7m, so FY26 guidance is about level with FY25's underlying profit, not a fall.
- First-half profit before tax rose 5% as reported, and about 11% without other income (NZ$62.5m against NZ$56.3m).
- The 20% rise in first-half profit after tax is mostly a lower tax charge (27.4% against 36.4%), not trading [S3].
- An invoicing fraud found in late 2025 cost under NZ$350,000, with a NZ$200,000 effect on FY25 earnings [S16]. Small, and genuinely one-off.

Volumes and share, the thing the headline numbers hide
- The industry crop rose 4% to about 225m trays in 2026 (gold about 162m, green about 58m, red about 5m), with green yields more than 14% lower [S21].
- Seeka's class 1 trays fell 4% to 45.4m: SunGold 29.9m to 30.4m (up 2%), Hayward and other varieties 17.2m to 15.0m (down 13%) [S4]. Seeka handled about 18.8% of the 2026 gold crop.
- So in a record year for the industry, Seeka handled less fruit. With green yields down, the industry's growth must have come in gold and red, yet Seeka's gold volumes rose only 2%; the extra gold fruit went to other packhouses. Seeka says it can handle more than 50m trays [S11, 22 August 2024] and has the capacity "to handle an increase in New Zealand kiwifruit volumes" [S3].

Debt and cash
- Net bank debt was NZ$100.3m at 31 December 2025, down from NZ$172.4m two years earlier [S7, S9], and NZ$119.8m at 30 June 2026, the usual seasonal level; NZ$55.0m was collected in July [S2].
- The banks renewed the syndicate in 2026 and Seeka cut the facility by NZ$20m to NZ$184.0m. Term loans now fall due on 28 February 2028 (NZ$26m) and 28 February 2029 (A$17m and NZ$50m) [S3, note 12]. At 31 December 2025, NZ$50m had been due on 26 February 2027 [S8, note 17]; that is now refinanced, so no debt falls due inside the window. The long-term covenants are 3.25 times for net debt to earnings and 2.0 times for interest cover [S8, note 17].
- My own debt ratio, net bank debt over EBITDA after rent (EBITDA less lease principal and lease interest paid), was 100.3 / 77.2 = 1.30 times at December 2025, which matches the banks' figure of 1.30 times [S9]. On the same basis it was about 1.50 times at June 2026 on trailing earnings; the company quotes 1.22 times [S2], which I could not reconcile. Counting leases of NZ$81.7m, debt was 1.9 times EBITDA at December 2025.
- Lease liabilities were NZ$83.4m at June 2026 [S4].
- Land and buildings are carried at independent fair value, NZ$292.45m at December 2025, valued mainly on market rents (CBRE in New Zealand, a third of the portfolio each year) [S8, notes 10 and 28; S3]. FY25 added a NZ$9.6m revaluation gain after tax [S8].
- Net tangible assets were NZ$278m, NZ$6.31 a share, at December 2025 [S9]. Equity less intangibles on the balance sheet gives NZ$273.1m (NZ$298.1m less NZ$25.0m) [S8]; the company's figure is used here. The NZ$7.12 a share at June 2026 [S4] is seasonal: it includes the first-half profit before the second-half loss.
- FY25 cash flow: operating cash NZ$79.0m, capital spending NZ$21.1m, new orchard plantings NZ$4.6m, lease principal NZ$12.2m [S8]. Tax paid was only NZ$4.7m against a NZ$15.5m charge, and NZ$2.5m was insurance money. The tax liability at June 2026 was NZ$26.3m against NZ$16.6m a year earlier [S4], so FY26 will carry much larger tax payments.
- First-half capital spending was NZ$15.0m against NZ$9.6m, with NZ$6.4m more committed [S3, notes 17].

Dividends
- Policy: 50% to 75% of underlying profit after tax, normally paid in October and April [S3, note 13].
- Paid: 10 cents in January 2025, 5 cents in April 2025, 15 cents in October 2025, 10 cents in January 2026, 25 cents in April 2026 and 20 cents on 15 October 2026 [S1, S3]. The dividends declared from FY25's profit came to 50 cents (15, 10 and 25), a 9.9% yield at NZ$5.04, but that included the extra January payment.
- On 19 August 2026 the board said it will go back to two dividends a year, considered in August and February and paid in October and April [S13]. So there is no January 2027 dividend. The next is the FY26 final, to be considered in February 2027. In 2025 and 2026 the final went ex on 19 March and was paid on 15 April [S1].
- Under the policy, FY26 profit after tax of about NZ$30.5m (at NZ$43m before tax) supports 34 to 51 cents for the year, so a final of 14 to 31 cents after the 20-cent interim.
- The dividend reinvestment plan applies at a 2% discount; 362,075 shares were issued at NZ$5.1122 on 15 October 2026 [S14].

Shares and dilution
- No placements in 2025 or 2026. Shares rose from 44,029,543 at December 2025 [S8, p. 90] to 44,607,242 before the October issue [S15] and 44,969,317 after it [S1, S14].
- On 15 October 2026 shareholders approved a new grower loyalty scheme: up to 1.5m new shares (3.3% of the company), paid for with loans from Seeka of up to NZ$9m. 16.3% of the votes cast were against, on a turnout of 32% [S15]. I could not read the explanatory notes, so the issue price and loan terms are not confirmed here. The 2024 scheme issued shares at NZ$2.5444, held by a trustee [S8, p. 88].

Directors' dealings
- Small and routine. Ms Gourley bought 4,000 shares at NZ$5.20 on 21 August 2026, her first [S18]. Mr Dewdney bought 5,000 at NZ$5.08 on 24 August 2026 [S18], as he did a year earlier (5,000 at NZ$4.20 on 21 August 2025) [S8, p. 88]. Other directors took shares through the dividend plan [S18, S8]. None of this is a signal.
- Director Peter Cross is associated with 2,300,040 shares through Te Awanui Huka Pak and the Ngāi Tukairangi No. 2 Trust [S8, p. 88].

Related parties, audit and other points
- An NZ RegCo waiver (October 2024) lets Seeka sign its standard post-harvest contracts with growers linked to directors without a shareholder vote; those growers paid NZ$12.3m in FY25 [S8, p. 90].
- Grant Thornton gave a clean audit opinion on FY25; the key audit matters were the goodwill impairment test (post-tax discount rate 9.1%) and the land and building valuations [S8].
- The fuel price rise this year is passed back to growers through a weekly fuel adjustment paid to transport operators [S19]. I found no figure for Zespri's sales to the Middle East in the documents read.
- Regional exposure: the orchards and packhouses are in Northland, Coromandel, Bay of Plenty and Gisborne, plus three sites in Victoria [S8]. Australia holds 12.7% of group assets (NZ$86.7m at June 2026) but earned only NZ$1.4m of EBIT in FY25 on NZ$78.0m of assets [S4, S9].

## Valuation
Shares: 44,969,317 [S1]. All per-share figures use all shares on issue, including the 1.73m held by the share trustee, which the company leaves out of its earnings per share [S4]. Tax is taken at 29%, between the first half of FY26 (27.4%) and FY25 (32.7%) [S3, S8]. The arithmetic was run in Python.

Method 1: price to earnings
- FY26 at the NZ$43m midpoint: NZ$43m x (1 - 0.29) = NZ$30.5m after tax, 67.9 cents a share. The price is 7.4 times that. At NZ$45m it is 7.1 times (71.0 cents); at NZ$47m, 6.8 times (74.2 cents).
- On FY25's reported 76 cents [S9] the price is 6.6 times. On 27 February 2026, the day after the FY25 result, Seeka closed at NZ$5.21, 6.9 times (data/prices/NZX/SEK.csv).
- Listed food and horticulture companies on the NZX, on trailing earnings: Scales 11.5 times (EPS 57.0 cents at NZ$6.55) and Sanford 8.9 times (77.1 cents at NZ$6.85) [S25]; the desk's earlier quick looks put Delegat at about 10.7 times and T&G Global at about 17 times (research/picks/INDEX.md). None is a close match: Scales and T&G sell apples, and Seeka is mainly a service business paid per tray.
- Over the cycle the picture is different. Average profit after tax for FY21 to FY25, using FY24 before its one-off tax charge, was (14.9 + 6.5 - 14.5 + 21.2 + 32.0) / 5 = NZ$12.0m, so the price is 18.9 times the five-year average. On FY24 to FY26 only (21.2, 32.0 and 30.5), the average is NZ$27.9m and the price is 8.1 times.

On my FY26 estimate (EBITDA about NZ$90.6m, NZ$69.9m after rent) the figures are 4.5 and 4.7 times.

Method 2: net tangible assets and what they earn
- NTA at December 2025: NZ$6.31 a share [S9]. My estimate for December 2026: NZ$278m + NZ$30.5m profit - NZ$20.1m of dividends declared in 2026 (NZ$11.2m in April [S3] and about NZ$8.9m in October) + NZ$4.6m of shares issued under the dividend plan (362,075 shares at NZ$5.1122 in October, plus the 577,699 shares issued between December and October, taken as the April plan at NZ$4.8061) = NZ$293.0m, NZ$6.51 a share. That leaves out any revaluation. The price is 0.80 times the December 2025 figure and 0.77 times my December 2026 estimate.
- A fair multiple of NTA is (return on NTA - growth) / (required return - growth). With a required return of 10% and growth of 1%:
  - On the FY21 to FY26 average return of about 5.9% (profit after tax as above, over average NTA of about NZ$255m): 0.55 times, NZ$3.56 a share.
  - On the FY24 to FY26 average of about 10.3% (NZ$27.9m over about NZ$270m): 1.04 times, NZ$6.76 a share.
- The property backs the debt with room to spare (NZ$292m of land and buildings against NZ$100m to NZ$120m of bank debt), but it is mostly specialised packhouses and coolstores. The realistic buyers are another post-harvest operator or a sale-and-leaseback investor, and a sale and leaseback would add rent.

Method 3: cash to owners
- Owner cash = operating cash less capital spending, orchard plantings and lease principal. FY25: 79.0 - 21.1 - 4.6 - 12.2 = NZ$41.1m; putting tax at the year's charge rather than the low amount paid (less NZ$10.8m) and taking out the insurance money (less NZ$2.5m) gives NZ$27.8m. FY24 on the same basis: NZ$28.0m [S8].
- Allowing for a bad year every five or six years, I use NZ$22m to NZ$26m a year:
  - NZ$26m, 11% required return, 1.5% growth: NZ$26m x 1.015 / 0.095 = NZ$277.8m, NZ$6.18 a share.
  - NZ$22m, 11%, 1.5%: NZ$235.1m, NZ$5.23 a share.
  - NZ$22m, 12%, 1%: NZ$202.0m, NZ$4.49 a share.

Range: NZ$4.50 to NZ$6.80 a share, central about NZ$5.50, roughly 9% above the price. The single thing that moves it most is whether FY24 to FY26 (about NZ$28m after tax a year on 43m to 47m trays) is the new normal, or whether years like FY22 and FY23 come back. One year like FY23 would cost a full year's profit and more.

## Why the market is wrong
1. Guidance is set low and raised through the year. There were four upgrades in FY24, three in FY25 and two so far in FY26, and the current range implies the weakest second half in three years. If the second half is like the last two, FY26 profit before tax would be NZ$46m to NZ$50m against the NZ$43m midpoint. The price has moved only 2% to 3% on each recent upgrade (NZ$4.50 to NZ$4.65 on 15 December 2025; NZ$5.28 to NZ$5.38 on 16 September 2026, data/prices/NZX/SEK.csv), so the market doesn't appear to price the pattern in advance.
2. At about 7 times this year's earnings and 0.8 times tangible assets, the price still carries the bad years of FY22 and FY23, although bank debt has fallen from NZ$172.4m to NZ$100.3m since December 2023 [S7] and the packhouses are now more automated [S4].

Why this may not change within the window: FY27 profit depends on the 2027 crop, which won't be clear until the harvest from March to June 2027, and Seeka lost share in 2026. On the chances below, the price after the final dividend is expected to end the window about where it is now, and the expected gain is the dividend itself. That is not an edge.

## What would make this wrong
1. Weather or pests: hail, frost, a cyclone (January to March) or a biosecurity incursion. FY23 is the reminder: 29.8m trays and a NZ$21.0m loss [S20].
2. Further loss of share: Seeka's trays fell 4% in 2026 while the industry's rose 4% [S4, S21]. If gold growth keeps going to other packhouses, the automation pays back more slowly.
3. Zespri's returns and the wider market: lower grower returns hurt Seeka's own orchards and its growers' ability to pay; the war in the Middle East raises fuel costs and could hit the NZ market as a whole, as in March 2026, when Seeka fell from NZ$5.51 on 18 March to NZ$4.48 on 31 March, partly the 25-cent dividend (data/prices/NZX/SEK.csv).
4. Use of capital: the company is "considering future capacity and growth opportunities" [S3], which could mean a debt-funded purchase, and the grower scheme adds up to 3.3% more shares [S15].
5. Tax cash: FY26 will pay much more tax than FY25 [S4, S8], and the board could set the final dividend below 25 cents to protect cash.

Exit rule, if bought: sell the whole position at the next open after any of: FY26 guidance cut, or FY26 profit before tax below NZ$41m; Seeka's or NZKGI's 2027 crop forecast more than 10% below the 2026 crop; a share issue other than the dividend plan or the approved grower scheme; a debt-funded purchase over NZ$30m. Otherwise sell on or after 30 April 2027, after the final dividend is paid and the April tray estimate is out, unless a fresh deep dive and card renew it. A takeover offer is a fresh decision.
