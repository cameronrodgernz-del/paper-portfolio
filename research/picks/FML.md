# Focus Minerals (ASX:FML): deep dive (not a value pick)

Verdict: wait for a named event, the 31 December 2026 Ore Reserve and Mineral Resource statement in the 2026 annual report (about 30 March 2027), read with the December quarterly report (about 30 January 2027). No card. Not a value pick (rulebook section 5a): the bad case (A$1.30) is 27% below the A$1.79 price, far outside the 10% the rule allows, and the middling case (A$1.85) is only 3% above it. As an ordinary pick the expected price on 30 April 2027 is A$1.835, 2.5% above the price before costs and about 2% below it after them, against a minimum of 3.4%.

Written 2026-11-08 by the research desk. Price at writing: A$1.79 (last trade on 6 November 2026, ASX company data fetched 7 November 2026 [D2]; Norgate's last close in the repo is A$1.82 on 30 October 2026 [D1]). Market value: A$512.9m (286,558,645 shares on issue, half-year report [F2]).
Status: finished. This is a simulation and not financial advice.
Fact sheet: research/facts/FML.md, grade A (only just). The question it set: how long does Coolgardie last at today's gold price, and will the board pay the cash out?

Order of work. The brief and the fact sheet both state the price (about A$1.81), so it was known before the work began. The cost, mine life, cash and tax work below takes nothing from Focus's share price: it comes from the quarterly cash flow reports, the reserve and resource statements, the accounts and the Perth Mint price. The peer method uses the peers' own prices. Focus's price is used only in "Why the market is wrong", "Outcomes and chances" and the liquidity check.

In short: the fact sheet's A$2.59 "if it goes our way" is the good case, not the middling one, and nothing inside six months forces it. It takes the September quarter's cost (the best quarter Focus has had, A$3,106 an ounce) and applies it to every reserve ounce, counts the undeveloped Brilliant pit as certain, counts Bonnie Vale's remaining ounces on the most generous basis, and assumes all the cash is paid out with no tax claim. Rebuilt from the filings at the Perth Mint's A$5,891 bid, the business is worth about A$1.40 a share on its reserves alone and about A$2.02 with a 50% chance of Brilliant and a small value for the resources outside reserves; at A$5,000 gold those become A$1.12 and A$1.54. At A$1.79 the market already pays for the producing reserves, the cash less the tax owed, and a half share of Brilliant. What it doesn't pay for is a longer life at today's gold price, and the filings don't show that yet: the open pit reserves were last set in October 2022 at A$2,200 an ounce and Bonnie Vale's in November 2023 at A$2,500, and the company says new reserves will come "following feasibility works on updated Mineral Resources" [F9]. That statement is the event to wait for.

Four things the filings show that the fact sheet doesn't:
1. Bonnie Vale, which makes most of the money, is mining below its reserve grade. Its 2026 ore averaged 4.61 g/t against a 5.93 g/t reserve; stope ore averaged 4.87 g/t against the reserve plan's 6.46 g/t [F3][F4][F5][F18]. Counting by tonnes rather than ounces, the reserve has 84,000 to 109,000 ounces left, not 121,000.
2. Cost per ounce swings with Bonnie Vale's grade. Built from the cash flow reports, the all-in site cost (production, staff, administration, development, plant and exploration) was A$3,106 an ounce in the September quarter but A$3,927 over the last four quarters, A$4,540 in the March quarter [F3][F4][F5][F6].
3. The dividend is paced by tax paid. The 8c fully franked dividend (A$22.9m, needing A$9.8m of franking credits) was declared on 10 September 2026, just after Focus paid A$12.7m of income tax in the September quarter, the first tax payment in any quarterly report since the restart [F3][F5][F6][F7][F8]. Most of the tax on 2026's profits (about A$64m by my estimate) won't be paid until mid-2027, so a large franked payout is unlikely before the September 2027 half-year, which is when last year's dividend came.
4. Shandong built its majority cheaply and has bought nothing since 2021. It paid 5c a share (A$2.50 after the 2015 1-for-50 consolidation) for 51% in 2012, was diluted to 49.53% in 2013, and went to 63.18% by taking up its full entitlement in a 1-for-1 non-renounceable issue at A$0.25 in December 2021, when the other holders took up only about 13.3m of their 92.2m entitlement [S1][S2][S3][S4][S5][S7]. It rejected Theta Gold's 2021 to 2022 bid [S9].

## The company in plain words
Focus mines gold at Coolgardie, in Western Australia's Goldfields, and processes it in its own Three Mile Hill mill (nameplate 1.2Mtpa; 1.5Mt went through it in 2025, including about 509,000t of other companies' ore) [F1]. Ore comes from the Bonnie Vale underground mine (stoping since October 2025) and the CNX, Dreadnought and (until September 2026) Alicia open pits [F1][F3]. All gold is sold unhedged to the Perth Mint [F2]. Focus sold 49,154oz in 2025 at A$5,556/oz and 61,008oz in the nine months to September 2026 [F1][F3][F4][F5].

It restarted Coolgardie in mid-2023 after years on care and maintenance, funded by loans from Shandong Gold entities at 6.5% (RMB loans) and SOFR or LIBOR plus 3% (US dollar loans), A$155.1m drawn at the end of 2024 [F14 note 14]. In June 2025 it sold its other project, Laverton (3.94Moz of resources, 546,000oz of reserves), to Genesis Minerals for A$250m cash and repaid Shandong A$174.8m of loans and interest [F1][F14][F15].

Who runs it: executive chairman Wanghong Yang (a former Shandong Gold International executive), executive director Lingquan Kong, non-executive Wenli Cui (head of the finance department of a Shandong Gold Group subsidiary, appointed 30 March 2026) and two independents [F1][F13]. Nobody on the board holds shares except Mr Fahey (25,640) [F1].

Who owns it: Shandong Gold International Mining Corporation 63.18%, ultimately owned by the Shandong provincial state assets commission [S3][S7]. Next are nominee accounts: BNP Paribas (Interactive Brokers retail clients) 6.19%, BNP Paribas (Clearstream) 3.05%, Citicorp 2.05%, then Mr Nansen Naidoo 1.58%. The top 20 hold 84.30%; 3,673 holders in all [F1]. At the May 2026 AGM about 194.5m votes were cast, of which Shandong's were 181m, so holders of the other 105.5m shares cast only about 13.5m [F11].

Five years: no production and losses until 2023; profit of A$3.0m in 2024; A$272.4m in 2025 (A$68.9m from Coolgardie, A$203.5m from the Laverton gain); A$85.8m in the half to June 2026 [F1][F2]. The first dividend in the company's history, 8c, was paid on 1 October 2026 [F8].

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Free float: the market value is about A$513m, but only 105.5m shares (36.8%, about A$189m) are outside Shandong's hands, which probably keeps it out of the S&P/ASX 300 (S&P weights by free float); it entered the All Ordinaries in March 2026 and wasn't in the September 2026 ASX 300 changes [F21].
- Little to analyse: Focus gives no production, cost or earnings guidance, publishes no cost per ounce, and its only presentation is the AGM's, which says its contents are "not intended to be forecasts" [F9]. The directors' report withholds likely developments to avoid "unreasonable prejudice" [F1]. I found no broker research.
- Register: a state-owned majority holder, the rest mostly retail nominees, few minority votes at meetings [F1][F11].
- A messy record: the 2024 annual report was late and the shares were suspended for a day in April 2025; the 2024 half-year accounts were restated by A$23.1m; in March 2026 the company had to deny a German website's report that Coolgardie had stopped [F20].
- Volume has faded: the shares averaged about A$2.3m traded a day in January 2026 and about A$0.37m in October [D1].

### Reserves, resources and what is economic now
Ore Reserves at 31 December 2025: 5.66Mt at 2.10 g/t for 381,500oz: Bonnie Vale underground 0.87Mt at 5.93 g/t for 165,700oz; Brilliant open pit 3.46Mt at 1.48 g/t for 164,000oz (undeveloped, "undergoing approval process"); CNX open pit 1.18Mt at 1.19 g/t for 45,200oz; stockpiles 6,600oz. The 2025 statement only took out what was mined; the reserve inputs date from 2022 (A$2,200/oz) and 2023 (Bonnie Vale, A$2,500/oz) [F1][F9][F16][F18]. Mineral Resources: 42.6Mt at 1.89 g/t for 2.59Moz (Measured and Indicated 1.58Moz), with many deposits still on 2004-code estimates [F1].

What is left at 30 September 2026, after the nine months' mining [F3][F4][F5]:
- Bonnie Vale: 84,400oz to 121,200oz depending on how depletion is counted (correction 3 above). I use 105,000oz as the middle.
- CNX: 45,200 less 8,543oz mined = 36,700oz.
- Brilliant: 164,000oz contained, 151,000oz recoverable at the reserve's 92%.

What is economic, at the Perth Mint's A$5,891 bid (7 November 2026 [G1]) and at A$5,000:
- The whole operation. At A$234 a tonne all-in, the break-even head grade is A$234 / (A$189.7 a gram x 95.5%) = 1.29 g/t at A$5,900, and A$234 / (A$160.8 x 95.5%) = 1.52 g/t at A$5,000. Head grades in 2026 were 1.80 to 2.22 g/t, so the mill as now fed makes money at both prices, but at A$5,000 the margin is thin in a weak Bonnie Vale quarter (the March quarter's A$265 a tonne needs 1.73 g/t at A$5,000).
- Bonnie Vale. The 2023 reserve's fully costed stope cut-off was 3.05 g/t at A$2,500 with mining at A$133 a tonne of rock and processing at A$31 [F18]. With costs 40% higher (my assumption), the same cut-off is about 3.05 x 1.4 x 2,500 / 5,900 = 1.8 g/t at today's price and 2.1 g/t at A$5,000. Everything in the reserve pays, and the Inferred 325,500t at 2.58 g/t (27,000oz) and lower-grade stopes left out in 2023 would pay at today's price if drilled into reserve [F1].
- The low-grade open pits (0.73 to 0.85 g/t). With about nine tonnes moved per tonne of ore at roughly A$5.90 a tonne (the 2022 rates of A$8.35 a cubic metre plus A$3.36 of mining overhead [F16], at about 2.6 tonnes a cubic metre, inflated 30%), about A$40 a tonne to process (the half-year's processing line was A$31.7 a tonne before staff [F2]) and A$3 of grade control, the ore costs about A$97 a tonne. After 92% recovery and royalties of about 2.9% of revenue (A$7.5m on A$256.4m in the half [F2]), break-even is about 0.57 g/t at A$5,900 and 0.68 g/t at A$5,000. Economic at today's price, marginal at A$5,000. These pits fill the mill around Bonnie Vale; on their own they wouldn't cover the site's overheads.
- Brilliant (1.48 g/t, A$2,200 reserve with a 0.56 g/t cut-off [F16]): comfortably economic at both prices. Open-pit-only running costs of A$128 to A$150 a tonne (about A$45m a quarter, my estimate) against about A$258 a tonne of gold at A$5,900 and A$219 at A$5,000. I use A$3,000/oz all-in plus A$30m to start.
- Outside reserves: at A$5,900, much of the 1.58Moz Measured and Indicated near the mill would pass a cut-off: Dreadnought (160,000oz, mined now without a reserve), CNX (152,000oz resource against 45,200oz of reserve, with a Stage 2 design), Green Light (45,600oz, "advancing open pit mining feasibility") and Patricia Jean (27,000oz) [F1][F19]. Until the company runs feasibility work and states reserves, none of it can be counted as mine life.

So, on the filings: about six to seven quarters of Bonnie Vale and CNX at the 2026 rate (to about the March quarter of 2028), then Brilliant for about two and a half years if it is approved, then whatever the resources turn into. The fact sheet's "about two years plus Brilliant" is right in outline.

### Cash, tax and the ATO
Cash at 30 September 2026: A$184.6m in the bank plus a A$95.1m term deposit, A$279.7m. No borrowings; bank guarantees of A$5.6m secured on deposits [F3].

What is owed against it:
- Tax on 2026 profits. Income tax payable at 30 June 2026 was A$40.8m: A$14.75m for 2025, less a A$4.7m prior-period credit, plus A$30.7m of current tax for the half [F2 note 12] (14.75 - 4.72 + 30.75 = 40.78). Focus paid A$12.7m in the September quarter, the first income tax shown in its quarterly reports [F3][F4][F5][F6][F7]. I estimate a further A$18.3m on the September quarter (30% of about A$60.9m: 23,434 newly produced ounces at A$5,989 = A$140.3m, less A$66.4m of production, staff and administration payments and about A$14.4m of depreciation, plus A$1.1m interest). About A$46.4m is owed. Most of it should fall due around mid-2027, after the 2026 return, with quarterly instalments before that; that timing is my reading of how a company with a December year-end pays, not something Focus states.
- The ATO. Focus used about A$253.5m of past losses against the Laverton gain under the continuity of ownership and business continuity tests. The ATO opened a pre-lodgement review; Focus made a submission; at 30 March 2026 it "remains under review". If the losses are refused, Focus estimates about A$88m more tax "before any interest and penalties and subject to confirmation and acceptance of capital loss balances". It judges the treatment probable to be accepted and has made no provision; the auditor made it a key audit matter; the accounts show no contingent liability [F1 notes 4, 19 and 21]. The half-year report says nothing about it [F2]. The tests are genuinely open to argument: Shandong held only 49.53% from May 2013 to December 2021 [S7], so the ownership test may be hard to meet for losses in that period, and the business went from producer (2012) to care and maintenance to producer again. I give the ATO a 35% chance of winning a material part, and A$101m (A$88m plus 15% for interest) if it does: an expected cost of about A$35m.
- Rehabilitation: A$24.4m of non-current provisions at 30 June 2026 [F2], about A$17m after tax, spent at closure.
- Capital commitments of A$8.1m within a year at 31 December 2025 [F1 note 19].

How much is free? Cash A$279.7m, less A$46.4m of tax owed, less A$17m of rehabilitation: A$216m. Less the expected ATO cost, A$181m (63c a share); less the full ATO claim instead, A$115m (40c). The board also says it wants "an appropriate liquidity buffer" and capacity to "evaluate future growth and acquisition opportunities" [F8]; a quarter's spending is about A$80m. Holding that back leaves about A$101m with the expected ATO cost and A$136m ignoring it (35c to 47c) free today. The fact sheet counted A$233.3m.

The cash will keep growing while Bonnie Vale runs: the nine months to September made A$142.1m after all capital spending and A$12.7m of tax (A$166.8m operating cash flow less A$21.0m of plant, A$3.7m of exploration) [F3].

What the rules let Shandong do:
- Creep: it may buy up to 3 percentage points every six months without a bid, having held at least 19% for six months [R2]. Each purchase needs FIRB approval [R1]. From 63% it would take about four and a half years to reach 90%. It hasn't crept since 2021, even when the shares closed 2022, 2023 and 2024 at 25.5c, 18.5c and 17c [F1, remuneration report].
- Bid: compulsory acquisition follows if it reaches 90% and has acquired at least 75% of the shares it bid for [R3]. With 105.5m shares outstanding it would need about 79.1m of them, taking it to 90.8%. A scheme needs 75% of votes cast and a majority by number, with Shandong usually voting as a separate class [R4]. A selective capital reduction that cancels the minority's shares would also need a 75% vote of those holders, voting on their own [R4]. A proportional bid needs holder approval under the constitution adopted in May 2026 [F10].
- Approvals for a buyout: FIRB [R1]. On the Chinese side, under the NDRC's 2018 rules a local state-owned company files (rather than seeks approval) for a non-sensitive investment, with the local NDRC below US$300m; approval is only for sensitive countries or industries, which gold mining in Australia isn't; and a non-sensitive deal under US$300m made through a controlled offshore company, such as Shandong's Hong Kong holder, needs no NDRC filing at all [R5]. Commerce and foreign-exchange registrations may also apply; I haven't checked them. The 2012 placement needed Chinese approvals and got them in about three months [S1][S2]. None of this blocks a buyout; it adds weeks or months.
- A payout needs no foreign investment or Chinese approval. A buy-back or capital reduction that raises Shandong's percentage isn't notifiable because it already holds a direct interest, though it may be a "significant action" [R1]. What shapes the payout is tax: Australia has no tax treaty with Hong Kong [R7], so unfranked dividends to Shandong lose 30% to withholding, and fully franked ones carry none [R6]. A controller in Shandong's position would want franked dividends, and franking credits come only as tax is paid: about A$149m of fully franked dividends (52c a share) once 2026's estimated A$64m of tax is paid, mostly around mid-2027.

Comparable cases:
- Zijin and Norton Gold Fields (2015): Zijin, holding 82.43% of a Kalgoorlie gold miner, proposed a scheme at A$0.20 (42.9% over the undisturbed A$0.14), conditional on FIRB and Chinese approvals [C1]; it was raised to A$0.25 and approved by the court in June 2015, about five months after the first approach [C2, secondary].
- Theta Gold for Focus (2021 to 2022): a minority bid with no chance once Shandong said no; 1.1% acceptances [S10].

The value if nothing happens: Focus keeps mining, pays franked dividends as tax is paid, and keeps a buffer: Method 1's reserves-only A$1.40 at A$5,900, A$1.12 at A$5,000. A Shandong proposal within six months looks unlikely (I'd put it near 3%): nothing in four years of filings points to one, and the AGM notice in April 2026 said no director was "aware of any proposal by any person to acquire, or to increase the extent of, a substantial interest in the Company" [F10].

Take off the free cash after tax owed, rehabilitation and the expected ATO cost (A$180.8m) and the market pays A$332m for the mines: almost exactly Method 1's A$219m for the producing reserves plus half of Brilliant's A$227m. The EV of A$233m is about 1.2 times the last four quarters' all-in margin (1.5 times at A$5,900), and A$92 per resource ounce against a peer median of A$151, but with a reserve life of under two years outside Brilliant, that is fair, not cheap. The market gives nothing for a life beyond Brilliant at today's gold price and discounts the cash for being held by a controller who pays out slowly. The one thing that would show the market is too cautious is a reserve statement at a current gold price that adds years of Bonnie Vale, CNX Stage 2, Green Light or Brilliant.
