# Duratec Limited (ASX:DUR): deep dive, the case for buying

Written 2026-11-09 by Claude for the one-company-at-a-time process (rulebook section 5a, docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md). This is the case for buying.
Price at writing: A$1.925 (last sale, ASX company data, read 9 November 2026) [S19]. Shares on issue 260,997,492 [S15]. Market value about A$502m. Net cash about A$30m at 30 June 2026 after bank debt and earn-outs owed (cash A$78.8m, less A$39.0m of borrowings and A$10.0m of earn-outs) [S2], so the business is valued at about A$473m.
Status: finished. Wait: it qualifies at about A$1.65, 14% under the price (see "Where it lands"). No case-against prompt is filed, because the case is not close at today's price; write it if the price reaches the watch level. This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Order of work: chosen by Cam on 9 November 2026 from three non-software options on the re-ranked weekly sweep. The sweep tagged it only for net cash and a small escrow release, so it came without a forced-selling story. The bad case was roughed out from the filings first at about 25% to 33% below the price. That is on the 30% limit rather than clearly past it, and the order book could lift it, so the work went on to the evidence outside the filings.

## Where it lands
A wait, and not a close one. On my numbers:
- the bad case is 32.6% below the price, just outside the 30% limit;
- the middling case is +12.9%, against the +48.8% the value test then asks for;
- the expected gain after costs is about +6%, against the 12.9% minimum.

All three tests fail at A$1.925. It qualifies at about A$1.65, or A$1.59 to A$1.71 if the middling multiple moves a turn or the middling profit moves A$3m. The low of the past year was A$1.695 on 19 November 2025 [S18].

The evidence outside the filings mostly agrees with the market:
- Defence work is stepping up. Federal contract notices to Duratec in July to October 2026, leaving out the big wharf contract, are about A$137m on contracts that run past December, against A$28m to A$50m in the same months of the past two years [S17]. That supports the strong FY27 the analysts already forecast.
- The bigger of the two HMAS Stirling planning contracts, for the nuclear-regulated Controlled Industrial Facility, was cut by A$2.68m on 31 July and ran out on 30 September 2026 with no building contract added [S17]. The company had pointed to "an initial opportunity of $600m+ for the JV" there in March [S7], and its August results don't mention it [S1, S2]. That takes away the main upside the April run to A$2.96 was pricing.
- The market already pays only about 14 times the analysts' FY27 profit, because Duratec has twice talked of growth and delivered flat revenue (FY25 guidance cut in May 2025, FY26 revenue down 0.5%) [S13, S1]. I don't have evidence that the discount is wrong.

So there is no edge at today's price. The middling case would need FY29 profit of about A$52m (19.5 cents a share, 23% a year growth) to pass at A$1.925, which is above the board's own stretch target for the chief executive's incentive (15% a year) [S4].

## What the price assumes
The business is valued at about A$473m: A$502m of shares less about A$30m of net cash [S2, S19].

FY26 [S1, S2, S3]:
- revenue A$570.3m, down 0.5%;
- normalised EBITDA A$58.5m (10.3% of revenue, from 9.2%), after A$7.4m of adjustments; reported EBITDA A$51.2m;
- profit after tax A$23.8m, up 4.1%, on an effective tax rate of 18%;
- underlying earnings per share 10.46 cents, the board's own measure, which adds back acquired-intangible amortisation and acquisition costs [S4]; statutory 9.25 cents;
- operating cash flow A$26.4m, less A$12.1m of plant, A$3.0m of leases, plus A$4.6m of asset sales and joint venture dividends: about A$16m of free cash flow.

There's no FY27 guidance; the company says FY27 revenue is "expected to grow strongly" [S2]. Six analysts forecast FY27 revenue of about A$705m (+24%) and earnings per share of about 13 cents (12 to 15) [S20].

So the business is valued at:
- 18.4 times FY26 underlying earnings, or 20.8 times statutory;
- 13.9 times the analysts' FY27 profit, net of cash (14.8 times including it);
- a free cash flow yield of about 3.4%, low because working capital grew with the new projects.

The price assumes Duratec delivers most of the forecast FY27 growth but isn't paid for growth after that. At the April 2024 low (A$0.96) it traded at about 11 to 12 times trailing profit; at the April 2026 high (A$2.96) about 30 times [S18, S3]. Listed contractors now trade at 16 to 32 times trailing profit: Civmec 15.9, Lycopodium 19.4, NRW 18.5, Ventia 18.0, Monadelphous 22.6, Service Stream 23.9, SRG Global 30.1, GenusPlus 31.8 [S19]. Duratec sits in the middle.

## The one thing the market is missing
At today's price I can't write this sentence, which is why the verdict is wait and not buy. The evidence confirms what the price assumes: near-term growth that is mostly in hand, and no reason yet to pay for more.

At about A$1.65 it would read: the market would be pricing Duratec's FY27 as if the order book were in doubt, when federal contract notices show the Defence work arriving faster than in any recent year. At that price the bad case is inside the limit and the middling case pays enough.

## The test outside the filings
Done on 9 November 2026, under docs/RESEARCH_PROCESS.md step 2. The questions the filings can't answer:
1. Is the FY27 growth already arriving, or does it again depend on awards that keep slipping?
2. What happened to the second HMAS Stirling contract, the one the company valued at "$600m+" for its joint venture?
3. Why are the shares down 35% from April with no bad news: the company, or contractors in general?
4. Is the market's multiple low against peers and history?

What the evidence showed:

1. The Defence work is arriving.
   - AusTender, the federal contract register, in the repo's full file [S17]. Notices to Duratec's ABN (including its JV with Ertech), leaving out the wharf contract:

     | Months published | New contracts and changes, A$m | On contracts ending after December |
     | --- | --- | --- |
     | Jul to Oct 2024 | 50.4 | 50.1 |
     | Jul to Oct 2025 | 32.5 | 27.7 |
     | Jul to Oct 2026 | 165.2 | 137.1 |

   - The 2026 figure includes the A$27.0m Yongah Hill immigration detention centre redevelopment for Home Affairs (25 September, to August 2028), which Duratec did not announce separately, and scope added to about a dozen live Defence estate contracts. About A$28m is on contracts that end within months, which is likely final accounts on work already done.
   - Sense check: the register's full-year totals for Duratec (A$122m in FY25, A$133m in FY26, including GST and other federal agencies) are the same order as its reported Defence revenue (A$181.4m and A$157.8m) [S1]. They can't match exactly: the register counts contract value when it is lodged, and revenue counts work done. The jump this year is too large to be a reporting quirk, though agencies do lodge changes in batches.
   - The wharf contract itself: CN4104389, which began as the A$1.9m planning contract in October 2024, was raised by A$302.3m including GST on 31 July 2026, and runs to 30 June 2029 [S17]. That is consistent with the "just under $300m" announced in April [S8], and the end date is a year later than the "approximately 24 months" the company gave.
   - What this changes: it supports the middling case's FY27 and lowers the chance that growth slips a third year. It is already in the analysts' numbers.
2. The second Stirling contract has gone quiet.
   - In October 2024 the JV won two planning contracts at HMAS Stirling: A$1.9m for the maritime works and A$8.1m for the "nuclear regulatory compliant" Controlled Industrial Facility and supporting works, each to be followed by a delivery phase "anticipated to commence in 2025" [S10].
   - In January 2026 the second award was "expected in Q4 FY26" [S9]. In February landside work was due to start in "late Q3 or early Q4" [S6]. In March the company called it "an initial opportunity of $600m+ for the JV" [S7].
   - The register shows the facility's planning contract (CN4103138, A$8.91m including GST, published 22 October 2024) was cut by A$2.68m on 31 July 2026, with its end date left at 30 September 2026 and no delivery phase added [S17]. The maritime contract was extended to 2029 and grew by A$302m on the same day.
   - The August results and the September conference deck don't mention the facility [S1, S2, S2b]. The ASX announcements list shows no award since.
   - Reading: the delivery phase has not gone to the JV, at least not yet and not through this contract. It may come later under another procurement, or another contractor may build it. This is a reading of the register, not something Duratec has said.
   - What this changes: it lowers the good case's chance. A Stirling award of that size would be worth about A$300m of revenue to Duratec over three years.
3. Part of the fall is contractors, part is Duratec giving back its April jump.
   - Total returns since 30 June (Norgate) [S18]: Duratec -21.8%, GenusPlus -25.8%, Southern Cross Electrical -17.4%, Service Stream -14.6%, NRW -13.8%, SRG Global -12.9%, Civmec -11.0%. Contractors as a group fell.
   - Since 17 April Duratec is down 31% while most peers are up. Its price is back where it was at the end of 2025 (A$1.925 on 31 December), before the run-up to the wharf award [S18].
   - There's no seller to explain it: no substantial holder notice since September 2025, short positions under 0.01% of the shares lately and 0.12% at their July peak (ASIC) [S21], and not in the S&P/ASX 300, so no index selling [S18]. Director notices in October were dividend reinvestment, not trades [S16].
4. Cheap against its own history, not against peers.
   - 18.4 times underlying trailing earnings, against about 11 to 12 at the 2024 low and about 30 at the 2026 high [S18, S3].
   - In the middle of listed contractors on trailing profit [S19].
   - Analysts' price targets average about A$2.91 (A$2.70 to A$3.10), set on 27 August [S20]. They haven't moved with the price, which tells us little.

Summary: the evidence confirms the market rather than contradicting it. The growth that is visible is forecast already; the larger upside the company pointed to has gone quiet.

## The company in plain words
Duratec repairs, protects and refurbishes ageing structures: concrete wharves, steel tanks, building facades, mine plant and Defence facilities. It started in Perth in 2010 and listed in November 2020 [S3]. It has about 1,580 staff and works in every state [S2].

FY26 revenue by sector [S1]:

| Sector | Revenue A$m | Change | Gross margin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Defence | 157.8 | -13% | 17.4% |
| Building and facade | 138.8 | +24% | 19.9% |
| Mining and industrial | 114.3 | -16% | 23.0% |
| Energy | 91.6 | +11% | 25.3% |
| Marine, transport and water | 67.8 | +12% | 18.2% |

About A$185m (32.5%) came from maintenance agreements and repeat work, which sit outside the order book [S2]. No customer was more than 10% of revenue [S3].

It has bought a small business most years: EIG (fuel and electrical) in August 2025, RGK Resources (inspection) in January 2026, and Pacific Welding Australia (Newcastle fabrication) in June 2026, for up to A$12m with half on an FY27 to FY28 earn-out [S3, S2, pwa]. It owns 49% of DDR Australia, a majority Aboriginal-owned contractor (FY26 revenue A$108.7m), and 70% of DXP Energy Solutions; both are equity accounted [S1].

Who owns it (31 July 2026) [S3]:
- Ertech Holdings (the Giumelli family) 18.3%. Ertech is also Duratec's 50:50 partner in the Stirling JV.
- Chris Oates, managing director and co-founder, 9.2%.
- Kent Colony Ventures (the Diprose Richards family, a co-founder) 9.2%.
- Phil Harcourt, director and co-founder, 7.8%, after selling 3.8m shares at A$1.50 in August 2025 [S3, S23].
- So founders and Ertech hold about 45%.

Its order book is a record A$650.8m (up 67%), with tenders of A$1.3bn and a "pipeline" of A$4.8bn [S1]. The order book includes Duratec's shares of DDR and DXP, which aren't in revenue.

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Not in the S&P/ASX 300 despite a A$502m value, because about 45% is held by founders and Ertech [S18, S3].
- About A$0.5m a day traded [S18].
- Six to nine analysts cover it, so it is not undiscovered [S20].

None of this explains a mispricing. Analysts and the funds that read them have the order book.

Two things I'd want explained, neither a warning by itself:
- The effective tax rate was 18% (A$5.2m on A$29.0m) against 22.5% in FY25. The tax note puts it down to franking credits on joint venture dividends (A$1.35m), A$1.65m of "other non-allowable items" and share-based payments [S3]. At 30% FY26 profit would have been about A$3.5m lower, so part of the profit growth was tax.
- The normalisation of EBITDA (A$7.4m) is 13% of the total. It is mostly the tax inside joint venture profits and acquisition costs [S1]. The board's underlying EPS is the cleaner measure, so I use that.

Shares: 8.66m performance rights are outstanding (3.3% of the shares) [S15]; 2.43m vested to staff on 10 September 2026. With the dividend reinvestment plan, the share count grows by about 1% a year.

The board's targets: the managing director's new rights vest on underlying EPS growth from 10.46 cents over FY27 to FY29 of 7.5% a year (half), 12.5% (all) and 15% (stretch), and the same thresholds for shareholder return from an August 2026 average price of A$2.15 [S4]. The board doesn't expect 20%-plus growth even at its stretch level.

Governance [S3, S14]: chair Martin Brydon (former Adelaide Brighton chief executive) is independent. Gavin Miller, audit committee chair since before the listing, leaves after the AGM on 26 November. ## Valuation
Worked at 31 August 2028, after the FY28 result, about 22 months out. By then the market prices FY29 (the year to June 2029).

The assumptions:
- Shares about 266m: 261.0m today, plus rights vesting and dividend reinvestment.
- Dividends going ex before the resolve date: the FY27 interim (March 2027), the FY27 final (September 2027) and the FY28 interim (March 2028). The payout policy is about 46% [S2].
- The middling case uses today's multiple, 13.9 times the coming year's profit net of cash, so it assumes no re-rating.

Arithmetic for the middling case:
- Profit: 14.5 cents in FY29 is 11.5% a year from 10.46 cents, inside the board's 7.5% to 12.5% range.
- Value: 13.9 times A$38.5m is A$535m. Plus A$25m of net cash is A$560m, or A$2.11 a share on 266m shares. Plus 6.75 cents of dividends: A$2.17.
- Net cash grows only slowly: free cash flow of about A$20m to A$25m a year goes on dividends (about A$12m), small acquisitions and earn-outs (about A$15m a year at the FY26 pace) and working capital for the bigger projects.

What the value depends on most: the multiple and FY29 profit. Each turn of multiple is worth about A$0.15 a share in the middling case; each A$3m of profit about A$0.16.

## Why the market is wrong
I don't think it is at A$1.925.

The market has the order book and the analysts' FY27 forecast. It pays 13.9 times that forecast, not 18 to 20, because Duratec has said "growth" for two years and delivered flat revenue, and because Defence awards keep arriving later than flagged. The register shows FY27's growth arriving, which supports the forecast but doesn't beat it. It also shows the larger Stirling prize has gone quiet, which the market may not have noticed but which would only lower my good case.

## Outcomes and chances
Resolve date: 31 August 2028, after the FY28 result (661 days away). Dividends as in the valuation table.

| Outcome | What happens | Chance | Value by resolve date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Worse | A project loss and slower awards; profit falls to about A$22m, valued at 10 times | 10% | A$0.87 |
| Bad | Follow-on awards slip again; profit about A$30m, valued at 12 times | 25% | A$1.47 |
| Middling | FY27 about as forecast, modest growth after, today's multiple | 45% | A$2.17 |
| Good | AUKUS and fuel work step up, re-rated toward peers | 20% | A$3.29 |

These chances were set after the evidence. The register's federal work is why the middling case is the likeliest. The quiet Stirling facility contract keeps the good case at 20%, not higher. Duratec's two years of slipping awards and its largest-ever fixed-price job keep the worse and bad cases at 35% together.

For the card, the first two combine into one bad case: 35% at A$1.30 (the chance-weighted average of A$0.87 and A$1.47). The card would read 35% at A$1.30, 45% at A$2.17 and 20% at A$3.29.

Expected value: 0.35 × 1.298 + 0.45 × 2.173 + 0.20 × 3.293 = A$2.09, or +8.6% before costs. After about 2.3% of costs it's about +6.3%. The costs are A$15 brokerage each way on about A$2,250, about 0.5% of spread across both trades, and the 0.5% currency fee. The rulebook minimum over 661 days is 12.9%, so that test fails.

The value test (rulebook section 5a):
- The bad case of A$1.30 is 32.6% below A$1.925, outside the 30% limit.
- Against that fall the middling case would need +48.8%, and it's +12.9%.
- So the test fails.

The qualifying price is set by the middling test: about A$1.65, where the bad case is 21% below and the middling case 32% above. The bad-case limit alone would allow up to A$1.86, and the expected-gain test up to A$1.82.
