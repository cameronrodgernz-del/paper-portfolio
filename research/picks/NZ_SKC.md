## The company in plain words
SkyCity runs casino and entertainment precincts in Auckland (casino, Sky Tower, three hotels with 938 rooms until the Grand is sold, 15 food and beverage outlets and the New Zealand International Convention Centre, opened 11 February 2026), Hamilton, Queenstown and Adelaide, and a small online casino run from Malta by a third party (Gaming Innovation Group, under a Malta licence held by Silvereye Entertainment) [S1][S2]. FY26 underlying revenue was NZ$822.7m, of which gaming NZ$550.2m and non-gaming NZ$272.4m [S2].

Licences: Auckland's casino premises licence runs to 30 June 2048 (extended under the convention centre agreement); Hamilton's 25-year term from 19 September 2002 ends in September 2027 and a 15-year renewal has been applied for; Queenstown's was renewed by the Gambling Commission on 10 November 2025 for 15 years from 7 December 2025; Adelaide's approved licensing agreement runs to 30 June 2085, with casino exclusivity in South Australia to 30 June 2035 [S1][S2].

It is a New Zealand company with a foreign exempt listing on the ASX, so the NZX rules and the NZ Takeovers Code govern a takeover [S1]. The chair is Julian Cook and the chief executive Jason Walbridge [S5]. On 5 November 2026 the board said it intends to appoint Lauren Quaintance as a director, subject to approval by the gaming regulators in each jurisdiction, which "normally takes some months" [S20].

Holders. Substantial holders at 30 June 2026 were Allan Gray 12.55%, the Accident Compensation Corporation (ACC) 11.84% and AustralianSuper 8.32% [S1]. Since then AustralianSuper ceased at 4.85% (53,535,789 shares, 17 July 2026) and Allan Gray fell to 11.463% (126,447,628 shares, 28 October 2026, trades on market) [S11][S12]. The register at 1 August 2026 shows ACC with 127,834,019 shares (11.59%) and the next holders as custodians; the 20 largest registered holders hold 76.27% and the 393 holders of more than 100,000 shares hold 89.69% [S1]. The fact sheet's list of NZ Super Fund, Spheria and Lazard notices (each now below 5%) checks out against the NZX list [S21].

## Valuation
Two methods on cash earnings, then the company's own asset values as a check on what a buyer might argue for.

**1. EBITDA after rent, against listed casino operators.** The measure buyers and the independent expert in the 2025 Reef Casino takeover used is enterprise value (excluding lease liabilities) over EBITDA before the lease accounting, that is after rent. Listed multiples at 25 September 2025 (Lonergan Edwards, independent expert's report in Reef Casino Trust's target's statement, ASX) [S23]:

| Company | FY26 EV/EBITDA |
| --- | --- |
| SkyCity | 7.1 (on broker forecasts that turned out too high) |
| Full House Resorts (US regional) | 8.2 |
| Century Casinos (US and Canada regional) | 7.1 |
| Gamehost (Canada) | 8.1 |
| Reef Casino Trust, before Iris's approach | 8 to 9 (trailing) |
| The Star | not meaningful (normalised FY26 EBITDA a loss of A$16.1m, research/facts/SGR.md) |

SkyCity merits a discount to these: capital spending has run at 50% or more of EBITDA, Adelaide uses cash, earnings have missed guidance repeatedly, and the regulators can and do close floors. I use 5.5 to 7.0 times on after-rent EBITDA of NZ$148.5m (FY27 NZ$168m less NZ$16m of rent less the Grand's part year of about NZ$3.5m), less end-FY27 net debt excluding leases of NZ$181m:

```
5.5x: 817 - 181 = 636 -> NZ$0.58
6.0x: 891 - 181 = 710 -> NZ$0.64
6.5x: 965 - 181 = 784 -> NZ$0.71
7.0x: 1,040 - 181 = 858 -> NZ$0.78
(per share on 1,103.1m shares)
```

**2. Cash earnings in a normal year (FY28), capitalised.** FY28 EBITDA of NZ$175m to NZ$195m (FY27 plus the further NZ$40m of targeted savings, less cost inflation, less the full-year loss of the Grand, online about break-even); less rent NZ$16m, sustaining capital spending NZ$65m (FY26 business-as-usual spending was NZ$62.5m [S2]), interest about NZ$12m on about NZ$180m of net borrowings, and tax NZ$18m to NZ$24m. Cash earnings NZ$64m to NZ$78m, or 5.8c to 7.1c a share. At an 8% to 10% cash yield, which I'd want for a casino with this record and no dividend until FY28:

```
175: 175 - 16 - 65 - 12 - 18 = 64 -> at 10%: NZ$0.58; at 8%: NZ$0.73
185: 185 - 16 - 65 - 12 - 21 = 71 -> at 10%: NZ$0.64; at 9%: NZ$0.72; at 8%: NZ$0.80
195: 195 - 16 - 65 - 12 - 24 = 78 -> at 10%: NZ$0.71; at 8%: NZ$0.88
```
The single biggest input is capital spending: if it stays at the FY27 level of NZ$80m to NZ$100m rather than NZ$65m, cash earnings fall by NZ$15m to NZ$35m and the value by NZ$0.14 to NZ$0.32 a share at 10%.

**3. The company's own asset values (what the board can point to).** At 30 June 2026 the Auckland precinct (one cash-generating unit including casino, Sky Tower, hotels and convention centre) was valued at NZ$1,598m to NZ$1,856m on a 9.2% post-tax discount rate and 7.7% a year EBITDA growth for five years; Hamilton has "sufficient headroom"; Adelaide was valued by Deloitte at A$119.0m to A$172.6m [S1]. Net tangible assets are NZ$0.86 a share on NZX's measure [S22]. A sum of the parts (Auckland less the Grand's NZ$221m, Hamilton and Queenstown at 8 times NZ$31.4m, Adelaide at Deloitte's range in NZ$, online at nil to cost, corporate costs of NZ$40m at 7 times, less net debt and leases) gives NZ$1.05 to NZ$1.37 a share. That rests on 7.7% a year growth from a company that has cut guidance five times since June 2024, and no bidder has come near it. It explains the board's stance; it is not a price anyone has offered.

**What a buyer would pay.** Control transactions in Australian and NZ casinos (from the same expert's table [S23]): Crown Resorts to Blackstone, 11.5 times FY19 EBITDA (enterprise value A$9,257m, announced November 2021); SkyCity Darwin to Delaware North, 7.5 times (2018); Casino Canberra to Iris, 7.6 times (2022); Lasseters to Iris, 9.9 times on pre-COVID EBITDA (2021); Jupiters Townsville, 10.0 times (2014). The expert found The Star's 2025 recapitalisation gave no meaningful multiple [S23]. Most of these were single, owned, high-margin casinos bought in better years; the typical control premium is 30% to 35% on equity, or 20% to 25% at the enterprise level [S23]. The rejected bids in today's terms (after the asset sales, on the FY27 run-rate): NZ$0.70 is 6.4 times and NZ$0.75 is 6.8 times; on May 2026 numbers, treating the sold property at its sale prices, they were 5.9 and 6.3 times. A bid at 7.0 to 8.0 times after-rent EBITDA would be NZ$0.78 to NZ$0.91 a share.

**Range.** Standalone (nothing happens, process still live): NZ$0.58 to NZ$0.79, centred about NZ$0.70; it depends most on FY28 EBITDA reaching NZ$185m and capital spending falling to about NZ$65m. Undisturbed market value: NZ$0.594 (below). A recommendable bid: about NZ$0.85 (NZ$0.80 to NZ$0.90); it depends on a buyer accepting 7.5 times depressed earnings plus the regulatory approvals.

## The players and what the rules allow
- **The board** (seven directors once the new appointment is approved) has rejected NZ$0.70 and NZ$0.75 unanimously and runs the process through a sub-committee with UBS and Chapman Tripp [S3][S4][S5]. Its stated floor is "greater value for shareholders than [the] strategy" [S5]. In my view a recommendation needs about NZ$0.85: near net tangible assets (NZ$0.86), a 40% premium to the undisturbed NZ$0.605, about 7.5 times after-rent EBITDA, and without the conditions it called problematic. No director has bought on market in the last year (the NZX list shows only share right grants and a former manager's notice in 2026 [S21]).
- **Oaktree** (US special situations fund): offered NZ$0.70 in May, has not improved, but SkyCity says it has "continued to engage with both parties" [S4]. Its May terms (exclusivity, stop asset sales, keep the debt) suggest a buyer that wanted the property too.
- **The second party** (implied NZ$0.75, about NZ$827m in total): unnamed by SkyCity; Iris Capital per trade press only [W1]. If it is Iris, it now owns Christchurch Casino, which raises a Commerce Commission question for New Zealand casinos and online that I have not tested.
- **Allan Gray** (11.46%, 126,447,628 shares) has been trimming on market (from 12.555%) [S11]. **ACC** (about 11.6% on the register, last notice 11.84%) [S1]. AustralianSuper, NZ Super Fund, Spheria and Lazard have all gone below 5% in the last year (fact sheet and [S12]). The sellers are motivated; the two largest left are value-minded long holders.
- **The rules.**
  - Scheme of arrangement (the usual route for a recommended deal): approval by 75% of the votes cast in each interest class and by more than 50% of all the company's voting rights, two High Court hearings, and a Takeovers Panel no-objection statement [S24]. At the 2026 meeting 766.6m shares voted (69.5% of the shares) [S6]; at that turnout 25% of votes cast is 191.7m shares, 17.4% of the company. Allan Gray and ACC together hold about 23.1%, enough to block; neither can alone.
  - Full takeover offer under the Code: acceptances above 50% (the bidder can set up to 90%), and compulsory acquisition only at 90% [S24]. Allan Gray or ACC alone (each over 10%) can stop a bidder reaching 90%, which matters to a debt-funded private equity buyer that needs 100%.
  - Partial offer: possible, scaled back if over-accepted [S24], but it leaves the existing debt and listed minority in place.
  - Overseas Investment Office: an overseas buyer of SkyCity's business (assets far above NZ$100m; NZ$656m in 2026 for some Australian investors; NZ$200m for some treaty partners) needs consent under the investor test and may face the national interest assessment [S25]. The Grand alone needed consent [S7]. Whether any SkyCity land is also "sensitive" land I have not checked.
  - NZ casino licence: a person controlling 20% or more of the voting rights of the licence holder has "significant influence" (Gambling Act 2003, section 7(1)(c)), and the Act tests the suitability of such persons under section 124 (the Commission can't renew a licence unless they are suitable, section 137) [S26]. I couldn't read the change-of-control procedure itself. SkyCity says regulator approval of a new director "normally takes some months" [S20]; Iris's Christchurch Casino purchase took about ten months from conditional agreement (December 2025) to settlement (8 October 2026) [W2].
  - South Australia: a transaction giving anyone "control or significant influence over the licensee" must be notified within 14 days and, if not approved by the Commissioner, can be undone by order (Casino Act 1997, section 14); selling the Adelaide business needs the Commissioner's approval (section 13) [S27]. The June heads of agreement adds direction powers over SkyCity itself [S9].
- **Comparable cases.** Crown went to Blackstone at 11.5 times pre-COVID EBITDA [S23]. Reef Casino Trust holders were offered A$3.87 a unit by Iris in 2025 (Cairns press); the Queensland casino approvals took until July 2026 (trade press), so that deal ran for more than a year [S23][W4]. SkyCity's own holders were offered nothing binding at NZ$0.70 to NZ$0.75 and then saw the price fall to NZ$0.465 in June 2026 before the asset sales and bid news [S22].

The "nothing happens" price. The undisturbed price is NZ$0.605, the close on 21 August 2026 (after the FY26 result and the Grand heads of agreement; the price jumped to NZ$0.665 on 24 August, ahead of SkyCity's 25 August response to media reports of talks with Oaktree) [S22]. ENZL in NZ dollars moved from 81.58 (21 August) to 80.07 (5 November), down 1.85% (data/prices/US/ENZL.csv, data/fx/fx_daily.csv), giving NZ$0.594. That is 5.7 times after-rent EBITDA, inside my standalone range at its low end. I use NZ$0.60 as the bad case. It could go lower: a process that ends without a deal reads as a failed sale, and the H1 result could show the fourth-quarter slump continuing.

## Why the market is wrong
I can't say that it is. At NZ$0.69 the market prices the standalone business at about 6.3 times after-rent EBITDA, the middle of my standalone range, and so already includes some chance of a bid. The board's view that the business is worth more rests on its impairment-test values and 7.7% a year growth, which its record doesn't support, and no bidder has offered more than NZ$0.75 in a year in which the price has been as low as NZ$0.465. The asset sales at full prices (the Grand at NZ$221m) have helped, but the market already moved from about NZ$0.51 (14 July) to NZ$0.66 (22 July) on them and settled at NZ$0.605 by 21 August, before the bid news [S22]. Without a proposal on the table, there is no gap to buy.

Why 20%: in favour, a formal process with two advisers and a sub-committee, two parties who have stayed in contact, "continued interest", a cleaner balance sheet now that the asset sales are done (removing one of the conditions the board objected to), and motivated sellers on the register. Against: no new or improved proposal by 21 October; the chair's own "will take some time"; the bidders wanted at least eight weeks of due diligence; at least three regulators' approvals follow any agreement; the unpriced online class action; the Adelaide sale running in parallel; and a board that rejected NZ$0.75 facing bidders who would not pay it.

Gain after costs: -2.6% to -4.6%

By outcome: bad -13.0%, middling +1.4%, good +17.4% (before costs). No case at NZ$0.69.

What it would take: at the middle of the costs (3.2%), an entry price of 0.687 / (1 + 0.0348 + 0.032) = NZ$0.644 or less; at the high end, NZ$0.638. With the same prices, the good case would need a 64% chance at NZ$0.69 (0.35 x 0.60 + (0.65 - g) x 0.70 + g x 0.81 = 0.736). Sensitivities: an offer of NZ$0.90 (good case NZ$0.855) gives an expected NZ$0.696 (+0.9% before costs); a 30% chance of a NZ$0.85 deal with the bad case at 30% gives NZ$0.703 (+1.9%). ## What would make this wrong
1. A bidder returns quickly with NZ$0.85 or more. The "wait" then misses about 17%. The chance is real (two parties still talking, the property sold, the board keen to show a result), but I can't see it in anything filed since 21 October.

Exit rule if a position is ever taken: sell if the board ends the process without a proposal, or at the resolve date if no binding deal; a binding deal moves to the deal sleeve.
