# Beacon Minerals (ASX:BCN): deep dive. No case at A$2.73; not a value pick (bad case A$1.05 is 62% below the price, middling A$2.00 is 27% below)

Verdict: no case. The price already pays for most of the study's grade: at A$2.73 the cash flow value implies Iguana recovers about 0.99 g/t, 94% of the study's 1.057 g/t, while the September quarter recovered 0.737 g/t and the company's own December quarter guidance implies 0.57 to 0.76 g/t. If the study holds, Beacon is worth about A$3.10 to A$3.24 a share at today's gold price, 14% to 19% above the price; if September's grade persists, about A$0.96 to A$1.05. Expected price by 30 April 2027 A$1.64, 40% below the price before costs and about 45% below after them, against a minimum of +3.4%. Watch triggers: A$1.50 on today's odds; about A$2.24 if the December quarter (about 15 January 2027) shows the study's grade. No card.

Order of work. The brief and the fact sheet both state the A$2.73 price, so it was known before the work began. No input to either valuation comes from Beacon's share price: tonnes, grades, costs and the balance sheet come from the filings, the gold price from the Perth Mint, and the peer multiples from the Kaiser Reef deep dive's table of other producers' prices and filings. The price was set against the values only in "Why the market is wrong" and the outcomes.

In short: the fact sheet's A$3.45 "our way" figure is the study's own value, not a price the market can be expected to pay from A$2.73 with much room to spare. Rebuilt with sustaining capital the operation actually spends (the study allows A$2.5m in total for nine years of an owner-run fleet and mill), an allowance for tailings, the 5% management fee the insiders' companies pay Beacon, Beacon's own stockpiles and spot gold of A$5,920, the study case is worth A$3.24 a share. The "against us" case is about A$0.96 to A$1.05. The only evidence since the reserve was published points to the low end: September's Iguana ore recovered 70% of the study's grade at unit costs per tonne that are in line with the study, so the whole gap in cost per ounce is grade, and the company guided the December quarter, the first with the bigger mill, to 5,500 to 6,500oz, which at 265,000 to 300,000 tonnes is no better a grade than September. The first test from the brief kills the case: even the good outcome is worth only 14% to 19% more than today's price.

## The company in plain words
Beacon mines gold near Coolgardie in Western Australia. It owns the Jaurdi carbon-in-pulp mill (about 1.05 Mtpa, lifted to about 1.2 Mtpa by a A$11.5m tank farm expansion whose wet commissioning began on 13 October 2026) and an owner-operated mining fleet of three 120-tonne excavators and nine 100-tonne trucks [B1, B2]. Since MacPhersons Reward finished in February 2026 its ore comes from the Iguana open pit on the Lady Ida project, trucked 43km to Jaurdi [B2, B4]. Beacon does not own Lady Ida outright: it owns 25% of the tenements and 50% of a joint venture with Lamerton Pty Ltd and Geoda Pty Ltd ("GL"), companies controlled by the executive chair and managing director Graham McGarry and the director Geoffrey Greenhill. It takes 100% only after 72,500oz have been produced, when GL receives a 4% net smelter royalty for the life of the project [B1, B7]. Revenue is gold sold to the Perth Mint at spot, unhedged [B2].

Board: McGarry (executive chair and managing director), Greenhill (non-executive), Sarah Shipway (non-executive and company secretary) and Brett Hodgins (non-executive, appointed 3 September 2024 as the fourth director the Lady Ida deal required, and also Technical Director of Forrestania Resources) [B1, B12, B7].

Owners: Colin Petroulas held 28,000,000 shares on the register at 29 September 2026 (27,233,014 plus 766,986), about 24.06% of today's shares; his last notice (3 August 2026) says 27,060,000, 23.26% [B1, B14]. McGarry's interests hold 18,022,474 (15.49%) after an on-market buy of 36,035 shares at A$3.05 on 5 October 2026 [B15]. Greenhill's super fund holds 7,448,917 (6.40%) [B1]. These three hold about 46%; the top 20 hold 60.80% and there are 2,991 holders [B1].

Five years: Beacon bought Jaurdi in 2017 for A$100,000 and a royalty and has milled a run of small deposits there since (Jaurdi, MacPhersons, Geko stockpiles) [B7]. Production was 25,639oz in FY25 and 24,854oz in FY26 [B5]. FY25 was a A$14.0m loss as MacPhersons was written down; FY26 a A$45.5m profit, of which A$19.7m was the gain on selling MacPhersons to Forrestania [B1].

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Size and trading: market value about A$318m; median value traded A$86,803 a day over 2 to 30 October 2026 [D1]. Too small and thin for most funds.
- Index: added to the All Ordinaries from 23 March 2026 [B20]; not in the S&P/ASX 300 (not among the March or September 2026 additions; the June review was not checked).
- Register: three holders own about 46%, two of them directors [B1, B15].
- Coverage: a web search on 8 November 2026 found no broker research.
- A messy structure: the main mine is a joint venture with the chair's and a director's companies, which take half the output until 72,500oz and a royalty after.

These explain a thin following. They don't make it cheap: the price has already priced in a lot of the study, as the valuation shows.

## Why the market is wrong
I can't say it is too low. At A$2.73 the EV is about A$284m (A$317.7m less A$27.69m of cash and A$14.0m of Beacon's bullion, plus A$7.89m of finance): 13 times the A$21.7m a year of cash margin Beacon made at the September quarter's rate, against 2.2x to 4.5x for its peers, and 2.9 times what it would earn at the study's grade once it owns 100%. On the cash flow method the price implies a recovered grade of about 0.99 g/t (a head grade near 1.12 g/t), 94% of the study's. So the market has already given Beacon most of the study.

The filings point the other way: Lady Ida ore recovered 0.737 g/t in September, the ore stockpiled at the mine sits at 0.73 g/t, a further 511,000t was set aside at 0.40 g/t, and Beacon's own guidance for the first quarter on the bigger mill implies no better grade than September.
