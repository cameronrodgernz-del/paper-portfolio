# Maritana Minerals (ASX:MRT): deep dive (not a value pick)

Not a value pick (rulebook section 5a): the bad case (about A$0.25) is 52% below the A$0.52 price and the cash-less-obligations floor (about A$0.32) is 38% below it, far outside the 10% the rule allows, though the middling case (A$0.79) does clear the 25% test.

Order of work. The brief and the fact sheet both quoted the A$0.52 close, so the price was in view from the start. The filings section and the three valuation methods below take no input from Maritana's own share price (the peer methods use the peers' prices). Maritana's price is used only in "Why the market is wrong", "Outcomes and chances" and the liquidity check.

> The desk's view in one line: if Black Swan is built close to plan, the shares are worth well over A$1, and the market prices them as if the build is more likely than not to go badly. But nothing in the filings shows the market is wrong yet: the revised build cost has been "coming" since 8 June, the main construction contract isn't signed, no loan is in place, and a third of the plan's ore isn't yet in reserves. On my chances the expected price on 31 October 2028 is about A$0.70, about 31% above today after costs, with a 40% chance of losing about half. That downside rules out a value pick. Wait for the revised build cost, mine plan and funding package.

## The company in plain words
Maritana Minerals is the former Horizon Minerals. It took its new name and the code MRT in April 2026 to avoid confusion with similarly named companies [M5], and consolidated its shares 15 to 1 in December 2025 [M1]. It is a gold company in the Kalgoorlie and Coolgardie districts of Western Australia with a 1,335 km2 tenement package [M2].

Where the money came from: in FY26 (to 30 June 2026) revenue was A$80.6m, almost all gold: Boorara ore sold or milled under an ore purchase agreement (10,826oz invoiced at an average A$5,838/oz, A$63.2m) and its share of the Phillips Find joint venture's toll-treated ore [M1][M5]. Boorara mining finished in December 2025 and the ore purchase agreement was ended; Phillips Find finished its last campaign. Both are in care and maintenance and there were no gold sales in the September 2026 quarter [M2][M3].

What it is doing now: converting the old Black Swan nickel concentrator, which came with the 2025 takeover of Poseidon Nickel [M1 note 31], into a 2.5 million tonne a year gold plant about 50km northeast of Kalgoorlie. The plan is a "hub and spoke": about ten small open pits and underground mines, between 42km and 110km away, trucking ore to one plant [M13]. First gold is targeted for the second half of calendar 2027 [M2]. It also holds the Nimbus silver-zinc deposit (20.2Moz silver) and a 3% royalty on the Windarra tailings project, where Encore decided to build in September 2026 and paid A$400,000 [M2].

Who runs it: Grant Haywood (Managing Director), Ashok Parekh (Chair), Elizabeth Jones (Chief Operating Officer from 26 August 2026), Doug Flanagan (Project Director), Brendan Shalders (CFO) [M2][M13]. GR Engineering Services runs the plant engineering and early works; Zeal Engineering is the owner's engineer; MineBuild Global prepares the mines [M9][M10].

Who owns it (9 September 2026 [M1]): 10,474 holders; the top 20 hold 67.4%. Citicorp Nominees 22.6% and J P Morgan Nominees 11.3% (custodians, largely for the institutions in the 2026 placement), Add New Energy (Hong Kong Gold Industry Group) 9.8% in its own name, BNP Paribas retail 5.4%, HSBC 3.3%, Golden Crane Holdings 2.1%, the Matterson family 1.9%. Substantial holders: Add New Energy 19.97% counting a conditional purchase, and Franklin Resources 7.47% (from 14 April 2026, the day the second placement tranche was issued) [M1][M25]. Directors and executives hold about 2.45m shares (0.7%) [M1].

Five years: a small explorer and miner with losses of A$1.0m (FY23), A$3.5m (FY24) and A$23.8m (FY25, after a A$7.0m impairment), then a A$62.9m profit in FY26 that came almost entirely from one asset sale [M1][S1].

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Size and index: A$194m market value. It joined the All Ordinaries in March 2026 [M5]; it isn't in the S&P/ASX 300.
- Liquidity: median value traded A$307,461 a day over the 20 sessions to 30 October 2026 (Norgate); too small for most funds to build a position quickly.
- A messy history: a name change, a 15-to-1 consolidation, the Poseidon scheme, the Lake Johnston sale and a switch from selling ore to building a plant. The headline earnings multiple is meaningless (see below).
- A crowded and uncertain register: two custodians hold 34%; a Hong Kong-listed company holds 9.8% and has agreed to buy another 10.1%, subject to FIRB; and 166.3m shares (45% of the register) were bought at A$1.08 in February to April 2026 [M5][M1].
- Out of favour: gold developers have been sold down as the Australian dollar gold price fell about 19% from February (PMGOLD, Norgate).
- Analyst coverage: the IMARC deck has a "Research Coverage" panel but its text doesn't name brokers [M13]. I couldn't verify coverage.

## Valuation
Per share figures use 377.4m shares: 373.5m on issue plus 3.2m performance rights and the 0.7m proposed for the Managing Director. The arithmetic was run in python3 -I; the inputs are all above.

### Method 1: cash less obligations (the floor)
Cash A$214.5m, plus listed shares A$2.3m and security deposits A$3.8m, less early works still to pay about A$26.5m, two years of head office A$27.0m, two years of minimum tenement spending about A$5.8m, two years of care and maintenance at an assumed A$2m a year (not disclosed) A$4.0m, and rehabilitation A$53.0m: A$104.3m. Add two years of interest at 5.25% on about A$150m, A$15.8m: A$120.1m, or **A$0.32 a share**.

That gives nothing for the 2.15Moz or the plant. If the project were paused, a buyer would pay something for them, but there's no market test here: at A$10/oz and nothing for the plant, A$0.38; at A$20/oz and A$30m for the plant, A$0.51; at A$30/oz and A$60m, A$0.65. And a pause is unlikely to come before more of the cash is spent: the early works run to October 2027.

### Method 2: the project's value under stress (NPV)
A yearly model of the study: the production profile above; all-in cost (excluding royalties) of A$3,097/oz, which reproduces the study's A$959m of free cash flow at A$5,500; royalties at 3.79% of revenue; the A$76.4m of later development over the first four years; first gold on 1 January 2028 unless stated; head office of A$13.5m a year before first gold and A$6m a year on top of the study's corporate line afterwards; A$10m of conversion drilling; tax at 30% after the A$190.8m of losses and A$9.8m of raising costs, capital written off over the mine life; 8% discount, valued at 1 October 2026; plus cash and listed shares, less rehabilitation. Check: the model moves A$441m for a 20% move in gold, against the study's A$421m rolled forward; its level sits about 6% above the study's NPV rolled forward, so I deduct A$40m.

### The range
- What the business is worth on its own figures: A$0.32 a share at the strict floor, up to about A$1.75 if the plan works at today's gold price with capital 30% over, valued at 8% after tax. It depends first on the revised capital and how it's paid for, then on how much of the inferred ore becomes mineable, then on gold.
- What the market would likely pay by October 2028: about A$0.25 if the build goes badly (Method 2's bad case on Rox's multiple), about A$0.79 in the middle, about A$1.44 if it goes well (Medallion's multiple). ## Why the market is wrong
At A$0.52 the market value is A$194.2m, about A$22.6m less than cash and listed shares: minus A$11 for each resource ounce, against A$63 to A$96 for unfunded peers. On study terms, A$194.2m is 0.23 of a pre-tax NPV of A$664m (A$5,800 gold, capital +30%) plus cash and listed shares less rehabilitation: between the unfunded peers (0.10 to 0.17) and the funded builders (0.42 to 0.56).

Holding the middling and good prices below, today's price implies about a 64% chance of the bad outcome (53% if the shares also have to earn 8% a year). I put it at 40%. Two plain reasons the price may be too low:
1. Forced and nervous sellers, not news. 166.3m shares (45% of the register) were bought at A$1.08 between February and April and are 52% under water; the controlling family of the largest named holder sold 13.6m shares on one day in March; and 37.7m shares (10%) sit in a purchase agreement waiting on FIRB. Maritana fell 58% from 16 February to 30 October, against a 19% fall in the Australian dollar gold price and a median fall of about 17% for the six peers (Saturn -28%, Astral -45%, Horizon Gold -3%, Barton -28%, Medallion -6%, Rox +7%) (Norgate).
2. The uncertainty has a date coming, if not a fixed one. Since the 8 June capex warning the shares fell 22% while gold fell 9% and Northern Star, Vault and Ramelius rose 12% to 30% (Norgate). The revised capital, mine plan and funding will narrow the outcomes either way.

The market may well be right, though. The study is scoping level, only 41% of the plan is in reserves, the timetable has slipped, a 17-year-idle mill is being refurbished, there's no construction contract or loan, the directors say more money is needed, and small WA developers often overrun. I can't show from the filings that the market has missed anything; the edge rests on my chances, which is not enough for a value pick.

## What would make this wrong
