> The desk's view in one line: the low EBITDA multiple is mostly an accounting effect. Atturra's "underlying EBITDA" of A$30.1m turns into far less cash: over five years (FY22 to FY26) A$123.2m of underlying EBITDA produced A$30.2m of free cash after leases and capital spending, while A$155.5m went on at least 17 acquisitions and their earn-outs, paid for with A$163.5m of new equity raised at A$0.50 to A$1.05 a share. FY26 revenue fell about 10% organically once acquisitions are stripped out. On normal cash earnings of about A$9m to A$12m the business is worth A$0.28 to A$0.42 a share as a listed company (central about A$0.35), which puts A$0.32 near the middle of its range: between DXC (shrinking, about 4.6 times free cash flow) and Cognizant (about 12 times). A bid is the route to the triage's A$0.45, but the nearest precedent (Pact Group, 2023 to 2026) shows a controller opening at the market price, settling near a 24% premium and delisting the holdouts. The fall in the week to 6 November was measured from a 30 October close set on 273 shares; the real move came around the 22 October AGM on modest volume, and there was no hidden news. Wait for the half-year.

## The company in plain words
Atturra is an Australian IT services firm: consulting, putting in and running business software (SAP, Microsoft Dynamics, Infor, Boomi and others), data and integration work, cloud, and managed IT services, mostly for government (local, state and federal), defence, education, utilities and mid-sized companies, with more than 1,300 staff [A1, A6]. In FY26, 51% of revenue was recurring and 78% "predictable" (recurring plus long-term clients) [A2]. Gross margin is about 33% [A2]. It also builds its own products, chiefly Scholarion (a school management system on Microsoft Dynamics, 6 contracted schools) [A2].

It listed in December 2021 at A$0.50 a share (A$100.3m) [A19] and has grown by buying: at least 17 businesses since listing, including two ASX-listed ones (MOQ in 2022 and Cirrus Networks in 2023) [A1, A13, A16, A17, A18, A26, A27]. It raised equity at A$0.85 (November 2022), A$0.80 (December 2023) and A$1.05 (November and December 2024) [A21, A22, A20].

Management: Stephen Kowal, chief executive and a director; Kunal Shah, finance chief since 6 July 2026 (Herb To retired) [A1]. The board has four members: Shan Kanji (non-executive chair and controlling holder), Kowal, Nicole Bowman (deputy chair and lead independent director) and Jonathan Rubinsztein (independent) [A6].

Owners (register at 26 August 2026, before the chair's September purchase) [A1]: Kanji's Driftwood IT 115,724,809 (31.44%) and 263 Finance (95,470,647 plus 16,000,000 in two lines); JP Morgan Nominees 50,693,496 (13.77%, which includes Richmond Hill Capital's 27.8m [H6]); HSBC Custody 11.4m; UBS 9.1m; BNP 5.1m; Kowal's family company 5.1m; the employee share trust 4.7m; Rubinsztein's fund 3.5m; Microequities 2.3m. ## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- They aren't so much overlooking it as leaving. It left the All Ordinaries on 21 September 2026 [A28], and the month-end price fell from A$0.695 in January to A$0.365 in October, mostly between announcements rather than on them: the last nine results and updates moved the price between -11.5% and +6.9% on the day [M2].
- Size and float: A$118m in all, but the free float after the chair, directors and the employee trust is about 120m shares, about A$38m at A$0.32.
- Liquidity: a median of A$18,642 a day over the 20 sessions to 30 October, with three sessions of no trades [M2]; the quoted spread at the 6 November close was A$0.30 to A$0.365 [M1].
- Coverage exists: Ord Minnett, Shaw and Partners and Morgans covered it in 2025, with Moelis outside the daily set (FNArena, a secondary source [N3]). I didn't find current broker targets in primary sources.
- Register: one holder at 63.87% who can't be outbid, and a second at about 7.3%.
- Sector: IT consulting to Canberra has been cut back (the FY26 impairment was for government and advisory businesses there [A3]), and listed IT services firms are priced for AI eating into consulting hours (DXC at about 4.6 times free cash flow; see "Valuation").
- History: guidance missed in FY25 (revenue) and FY26 (both revenue and EBITDA, twice), a contract dispute, a A$22.8m impairment, a finance chief change, and heavy acquisition spending funded by equity now worth a third of its issue price.

### The disputed contract
On the evening of 16 December 2025 a subsidiary received notice of immediate termination of a fixed-term contract with "an Australian public sector body"; the contract ran to November 2026 and had been signed in May 2025. Atturra called it a purported wrongful termination and said it would dispute it. It cut FY26 guidance from A$384m revenue and A$40.3m underlying EBITDA to A$364m to A$374m and A$30m to A$31m [A4, after a trading halt, A5]. It did not name the customer, saying under ASX Compliance Update 02/25 that the identity was not material [A4]. In the half year it wrote off A$5.1m of unbilled work [A6, note 4] and capped work in progress at A$2m without board approval [A7]. In February the chief executive said "we can't even say the client or what it's about" (CRN, secondary [N1]) and "we had a heap of work in progress predominantly sitting in that contract" (techpartner.news, secondary [N2]). The annual report says the parties "have agreed to participate in mediation prior to 31 December 2026" and that the outcome "remains uncertain" [A1, note 43]. No amount claimed has been disclosed by either side, and no counterclaim either.

What is at stake, on the filings: at least the A$5.1m written off, plus margin on the eleven months the contract had left. The guidance cut was about A$10m of EBITDA, a fair upper guide to what a full recovery could be worth: about 2.7c a share before legal costs and tax. I searched the repo's AusTender file (Commonwealth contract notices, data/austender) for every Atturra group company: no contract signed in May 2025 to run to November 2026 has a cancellation amendment, so the customer may be a state, territory or local body, which AusTender doesn't cover [M5]. I couldn't identify it.

### The FY27 outlook
"Record revenue and underlying EBITDA", weighted to the second half (AGM, 22 October 2026 [A8]). The July update set out the first-half drag: A$3m more on AI capability (about A$2m of cost in the first half), more than A$1.5m on SAP sales staff, and more than A$4m on Scholarion, which is expected to lose about A$2.4m in FY27, mostly in the first half, and break even in FY28 [A3]. ### Creep: he buys exactly to the ceiling
Item 9 of section 611 lets a holder of 19% or more for six months add up to 3 percentage points in any six months; the test is voting power after the purchase against voting power six months earlier plus 3. The triage cites ASIC's Regulatory Guide 6 (RG 6.41 to 6.46), which says ASIC won't let a holder leave out shares gained under other exceptions; buy-back increases therefore use up headroom [reports/special_situations_triage_nzx.md; I didn't reopen RG 6]. Rebuilding his voting power from the cancellation dates in note 22 [A1] (creep.py):

| Purchase | Voting power after | Voting power six months earlier | Ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 23 Jan 2026 | 60.88% | 58.01% | 61.01% |
| 31 Mar 2026 (last of the March buys) | 61.73% | 58.80% | 61.80% |
| 2 Sep 2026 | 63.87% | 60.89% | 63.89% |

Each purchase landed within 0.02 to 0.13 points of the ceiling. Room now: about 3.4m shares (to a ceiling of about 64.8%), rising to about 4.1m from late November, then about 11.0m (a ceiling of 66.87%) from about 3 March 2027, about A$3.5m at A$0.32. Creep can't take him to 90% (96.2m more shares) in any useful time.

### The other routes, and who can stop them
- A takeover bid. Anyone may bid; nobody else can win against 63.87%. Compulsory acquisition needs 90% of the shares and 75% of those bid for, so about 36.8m shares not accepting would block it. Richmond Hill Capital (27.8m at its last notice) plus about 9m others (the CEO's 5.7m, Rubinsztein's 3.5m, the employee trust) would be enough [H6, A1].
- A scheme. Kanji's shares would not count in the vote of the other holders, who would need 75% of votes cast and a majority by number. On the AGM's turnout (61.8m minority votes on the remuneration report [A8]) Richmond Hill alone would be about 45% of the vote and could block it.
- Delisting. ASX Guidance Note 33 usually requires a special resolution (75% of votes cast) and at least a month to sell afterwards [R1, section 2.7]. A controller can normally vote, but ASX may exclude a holder with a "material informational advantage", which "will generally arise in the context of a controlling security holder or directors" (footnote 38), and its stated concern is a removal used "to coerce them into accepting a current or planned offer from a controlling security holder to buy their securities at an undervalue" (section 2.1). Within 12 months of a bid, the bidder's votes are excluded unless the section 2.10 conditions are met, which need fewer than 150 holders with A$500 parcels besides the bidder; Atturra has 1,368 holders [R1; A1].
- More buy-backs. A new on-market buy-back (another 10% in 12 months without a vote) lifts his percentage without his buying, and also uses up creep headroom.

### Comparable controller buy-outs, and what holders received
- Pact Group (ASX, 2023 to 2026): Kin Group (Raphael Geminder, over 50%) made an unconditional cash takeover bid at A$0.68, 0.7% above the undisturbed A$0.675, citing Pact's removal from the S&P/ASX 300 and "low institutional investor support" [C1]. Kroll found it neither fair nor reasonable, valuing Pact at A$1.06 to A$1.51 on a control basis and A$0.83 to A$1.24 on a minority basis [C2]. The bid was raised to A$0.84 (a 24% premium), the independent directors then recommended it [C3], and Kin reached about 88%, short of 90% (MarketScreener, secondary [C5]; Pact's own February 2026 notice says 88.04% [C4]). Pact was delisted on 16 July 2025 (last close A$0.91); in February 2026 Kin offered the remaining holders A$1.10 through a sale facility, ahead of compulsory acquisition [C4]. Holders who accepted the bid got A$0.84 in 2024; those who held out got A$1.10 about two and a half years after the first offer, with no market in between.
- Boral (ASX, 2024): Seven Group, with 71.6%, offered scrip and cash worth A$6.05 to A$6.25, declared best and final, against a pre-offer price Seven Group put at about A$5.80 (a 4% to 8% premium) [C6]. Boral's committee recommended it only after the cash part was raised to the maximum and a franked dividend added [C6].
- New Zealand majority buy-outs in the deal database: final prices 6% to 83% above the undisturbed price, median about 50% (eight cases with prices) [C7]. New Zealand's Takeovers Code is different, so this is a loose check.
- Arm's-length bids for small ASX IT services companies (Atturra's own): 29% to 54% for Cirrus, 20% more than the first offer for MOQ [A26, A27].

What minorities would likely be offered: a controller in Kanji's position, with a blocking holder in the register, would most likely bid (not propose a scheme), open near the market as Kin and Seven Group did, and end near minority value plus a modest premium: about A$0.38 to A$0.45, against my control value of A$0.46 to A$0.56, which an independent expert would probably call not fair but reasonable. A bid at A$0.45 is the good case, not the middling one.

### The board and the company
The two independent directors would have to form a committee and commission an expert, as Pact did. Kowal holds 5.7m shares, 2.3m rights and 7.48m options vesting in September 2029 [A12, A10], so management's own interest is in the listed price over three years.

### The "nothing happens" value
No bid, Kanji creeping about 3 points every six months, the business earning what it has: the minority value at the low-to-central end of the range, A$0.26 to A$0.32. If the half-year result disappoints, the low end (A$0.26) is the price: 8 to 9.5 times A$9m of free cash flow, or about 3.5 times EBITDA after leases. Creep buying (A$1m to A$3.5m a window) is too small to hold the price above that while other holders leave.

## Why the market is wrong
I can't make a strong case that it is. On cash the market prices Atturra at about 9.7 times normal free cash flow (A$0.32), the middle of my listed range, between a shrinking DXC and a steady Cognizant. The low EBITDA multiple that made it look cheap ignores the earn-outs, the leases, the costs left out of "underlying" and a business that shrank organically in FY26 under a management that has missed its forecasts two years running.

Two things could make the market wrong, and neither can be shown before the half-year result: (1) a second half of FY27 that proves the "record" year and turns more of it into cash, which would justify A$0.37 to A$0.42; (2) a Kanji bid, which would pay A$0.38 to A$0.45. The first is the company's forecast and its record is poor; the second has no sign beyond creep buying, which a controller uses precisely because it costs no premium.

## What would make this wrong
For waiting:
1. Kanji bids before the half-year. The price would likely go to A$0.38 to A$0.45 and we would miss it. I put the chance before March 2027 at about 7%: he has bought at the creep ceiling in every window, which is what a holder does when he wants control cheaply rather than all of it now. Pact shows the other path is real, and Atturra has just had the same trigger (index removal).
2. The second half of FY26 (A$22.8m of underlying EBITDA and A$22.5m of operating cash flow [A8, A2]) was the true run rate, not a seasonal high. If the half-year shows that, the price could re-rate before we act.

For anyone buying now:
1. The cash doesn't come. Five years of 37% conversion before M&A costs, a business that shrank about 10% organically in FY26, and a forecast record built on the second half.
2. A low-ball controller bid with a delisting threat behind it. Guidance Note 33 limits this, but Pact's holdouts waited two and a half years with no market.
3. The customer counterclaims, or the mediation fails and the dispute goes to court.

Exit rule, if it is ever bought: sell after a half-year or full-year result that withdraws or cuts the year's earnings outlook, or shows a second straight half of operating cash outflow; on a Kanji bid, hold to the independent expert's report and accept only within or above its minority range; otherwise sell on the resolve date.
