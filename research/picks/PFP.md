## The company in plain words
Propel owns funeral homes, crematoria and cemeteries in Australia and New Zealand. It says it is the second largest private provider of death care in the two countries, with 213 locations including 42 cremation facilities and 9 cemeteries [P1]. It grows by buying family funeral businesses and keeping their local names. Money comes from each funeral: about 22,850 funerals in FY26 at an average of A$6,673 each [P1].

It is run by co-CEOs Fraser Henderson and Lilli Rayner, both up for performance-rights grants at the 2026 AGM [P4]; the former managing director, Albin Kurti, held 10,166,373 shares at 31 August 2025 and Mr Henderson 7,264,582 (5.3%) [P2]. Board changes were announced on 25 June 2026 and Sonia Petering AM joined on 25 August 2026 [P5, P4]. I could not read the top 20 and substantial holder pages of the annual report (the copy I could open ends before them). The ASX list shows a "Notice of Initial Substantial Holding from SOL" (Washington H. Soul Pattinson) on 18 May 2026, and a run of becoming and ceasing notices from June to September 2026, which is typical of index and trading funds crossing 5% [P5]. I could not open those notices, so the size of any holding is not confirmed here.

Five years of the share price (calendar-year closes, Norgate): 2021 A$4.43, 2022 A$4.33, 2023 A$5.16, 2024 A$5.87, 2025 A$4.92, and A$2.66 now. Results for the last two years [P1, P2]:

## Why big investors may be overlooking it
- Index: Propel left the S&P/ASX 300 at the September 2026 rebalance, effective 21 September (Norgate, data/norgate/index_changes.csv; S&P's announcement is on Propel's ASX list for 4 September [P5]). On 18 September, the last session before, 7,636,330 shares traded, 5.5% of the company in a day, against a median of about 156,000 shares a day over the 60 sessions before it (Norgate). That was index funds selling because they had to, not because of news.
- Size and trading: A$367m market value, about A$461,000 traded on a median day over 60 sessions (Norgate).
- Mood: the shares have fallen 46% this year, from A$4.92 to A$2.66, while operating profit fell 4% (Norgate; [P1]). Funeral companies are meant to be dull and defensive, and holders who bought them for that have been selling.
- Coverage: I didn't check broker databases; this is a known name among small-cap funds, so "overlooked" here means "sold by index funds", not "unknown".

## What the filings show
- FY26 came in inside guidance: revenue A$226.6m against A$225m to A$230m, operating EBITDA A$55.3m against A$54.5m to A$56.5m, given on 4 June 2026 [P1, P3]. Operating EBITDA margin 24.4%, down 0.5 points [P1].
- Volumes: total funerals up 1.1% to about 22,850, but comparable volumes (businesses owned in both years) fell about 2% [P1]. Comparable revenue per funeral rose about 2% [P1].
- Currency: a more than 10% rise in the Australian dollar against the New Zealand dollar cost about A$3m of revenue and A$1m of EBITDA [P1].
- Cash flow: operating cash flow A$38.0m (FY25 A$40.0m), cash conversion 100.7% [P2, P1]. Capital spending A$19.0m: A$10.4m maintenance (4.6% of revenue) and A$8.6m growth [P2].
- Debt: net debt A$151.2m (borrowings A$162.8m less cash A$12.5m), net leverage about 2.2 times against a 5.0 times covenant, gearing about 31% [P2]. The A$275m senior facility was extended from October 2027 to October 2029 and a A$50m accordion added [P2]. No debt falls due before the dated events.
- Property: A$252m of freehold property at cost less depreciation, about 61% of operating properties owned [P2]. That is 69% of the market value at A$2.66. No independent valuation is published.
- Leases: lease liabilities A$23.5m (A$4.1m current) [P2], small next to the debt.
- Net assets A$337.5m, A$2.45 a share; the price is 1.09 times book [P2].
- Dividends: 14.4 cents fully franked for FY26, the same as FY25; the final 6.9 cents was paid on 1 October 2026 [P1, P2]. Dividends paid in FY26 were A$20.0m [P2], about the same as statutory profit, so the payout is close to 100% of earnings.
- Acquisitions: about A$12m on five New Zealand businesses during and since FY26, plus A$7.2m of other property [P1]; the three bought in June cost up to A$9.1m for about A$4.0m of revenue, with no multiple disclosed [P3]. About A$314m has gone into acquisitions since the 2017 listing [P1].
- Trading since June: July 2026 revenue was about A$21.5m, including A$0.6m of currency drag; comparable revenue per funeral grew more than 3% in July; but the company reported "a material contraction in industry death volumes" in Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria and Tasmania in July, after the lowest flu season in five years, which it expects to be temporary [P1, P2]. A FY27 trading update is promised for the AGM [P1].
- Capital raisings: none in 2026 [P5].

Deaths, the thing that drives volumes
- The ABS counted 73,997 deaths in January to May 2026, 1.0% more than in 2025 and 1.3% fewer than in 2024; age-specific death rates were lower than in both years [P7]. Deaths are rising slowly because the population is ageing, but the rate of death at each age is falling, and the flu season was mild. The next ABS release is on 27 November 2026 [P7].

## Valuation
Shares: 137,973,594 [P6]. Market value at A$2.66: A$367.0m. Enterprise value (market value plus net debt of A$151.2m): A$518.2m, or A$541.7m including leases.

Method 1: enterprise value to operating EBITDA, against a takeover and Propel's own history
- Now: A$518.2m / A$55.3m = 9.4 times (9.8 times with leases). On operating earnings per share, 17.7 times.
- InvoCare, the largest listed funeral company, was taken private by TPG in 2023 at A$12.70 a share, an enterprise value of A$2.2bn, about 18 times FY22 EBITDA (before lease accounting) and about 37 times earnings [P8]. FY22 was a weak year for InvoCare's earnings, which flatters that multiple, and InvoCare was much larger, so I don't use it directly.
- Propel's own history, on FY25 EBITDA of A$56.2m and FY25 net debt of A$132.0m: at the 2024 close of A$5.87 it was valued at about 16.8 times; at the 2025 close of A$4.92, about 14.4 times (Norgate; [P1]).
- I use 10 to 12 times, well below both, because volumes are flat and debt is rising:
  - 10 times: A$553m less A$151.2m net debt = A$401.8m, A$2.91 a share.
  - 11 times: A$608.3m less A$151.2m = A$457.1m, A$3.31.
  - 12 times: A$663.6m less A$151.2m = A$512.4m, A$3.71.

Method 2: cash flow to owners, valued as a growing stream
- Owner cash = operating cash flow A$38.0m (already after interest and tax) less maintenance spending A$10.4m less lease repayments of about A$4.1m (the current lease liability) [P2] = about A$23.5m, or 17 cents a share. Growth spending and acquisitions are left out on both sides.
- Value = A$23.5m x (1 + growth) / (discount rate - growth):
  - 9% discount, 3% growth (deaths up 1% to 2% a year plus 2% price growth, less some share loss): A$23.5m x 1.03 / 0.06 = A$403m, A$2.92 a share.
  - 10% discount, 2.5% growth: A$321m, A$2.33.
  - 8.5% discount, 3% growth: A$440m, A$3.19.

Cross-check: assets
- Freehold property at cost of A$252m covers the A$151.2m of net debt with about A$101m (73 cents a share) to spare [P2]. Funeral sites are specialised, so "at cost" is not a sale price, but it does mean the debt is well secured.

Range: A$2.33 to A$3.71, central about A$3.00, about 13% above the price. The single input that moves it most is the long-run growth in funerals: each half-point of growth is worth about 23 to 28 cents a share in method 2 (at a 9% discount rate).

## Why the market is wrong
1. Index selling, not news, pushed the last leg down: Propel left the ASX 300 in September and 5.5% of the company changed hands on the rebalance day (Norgate). That kind of selling doesn't repeat.
2. The price assumes a short dip in deaths lasts. Comparable volumes fell 2% in FY26 and deaths were weak in July after a mild flu season [P1], but the ABS count for January to May was still 1.0% above 2025 [P7], and pricing per funeral is rising faster than its long-run rate (more than 3% in July) [P1]. At 9.4 times EBITDA the market values a steady, cash-generating business at a little over half its own price of a year ago.

Why this may not change soon: the next two dated events, the AGM update on 9 November and the first-half result in February, cover July to December 2026, a half that started with weak industry deaths. They are as likely to confirm the market's worry as to dispel it. The case is better over two years than over four months.

## What would make this wrong
1. Deaths stay low for longer: falling death rates at each age can offset the ageing population for several years, and the flu season matters a lot to one half [P7, P1].
2. Debt-funded buying: net debt rose A$19m in FY26 [P2] while the dividend took about all of statutory profit. If acquisitions continue at a falling share price, the equity carries more risk.
3. Price regulation or competition: funeral pricing is politically sensitive, and price growth above 3% is what is holding revenue up [P1].
4. A management change under way (co-CEOs, board changes in June and August 2026 [P4, P5]) could bring a reset of expectations.

Exit rule, if bought: sell the whole position at the next open after FY27 operating EBITDA guidance or the H1 run rate points below A$53m, or net leverage above 3.0 times; otherwise sell on or after 1 March 2027 unless a fresh card renews it.
