# Research picks: the write-up

Written 8 November 2026 by the research desk, and kept up to date as the research changes. The hub's Research view (Portfolio tab) shows it with every company's line from research/picks/INDEX.md, the watch list and the dated events, which refresh each night. A simulation, not financial advice.

- Since 1 November the desk has screened both exchanges, ranked what was left, and written fact sheets, deep dives and red team reviews from the filings. The counts at the top of this page come from the index and update each night.

The evening desk checks both every weeknight and tells Cam only when a share is reopened.

None passed: Garda and EML came back "wait", GDI and IVE "don't buy" at today's price.

Earlier deep dives had read it both ways; Helloworld and Argosy were worked on price alone, so their next look will count dividends.

## How a company gets bought

Every research pick has to clear each of these steps, in order. Nothing is bought on a hunch.

1. **Screen.** Every ASX and NZX company, less funds, shells, delistings and anything trading under A$10,000 a day (NZ$12,500 on the NZX), so a position of about A$2,000 (NZ$2,500) stays within a tenth of two days' trading.
2. **Ranking.** Points for cheap earnings, cheap assets, net cash, yield, steady profits, a big fall or an index exit. It only decides where research time goes; the points have no tested edge.
3. **Fact sheet.** The numbers in a standard form from the filings, the one question the price seems to get wrong, and what the business is worth if the answer goes our way or against us. Grade A (a gap of 40% or more and an answer inside six months) goes to a deep dive; B waits for its event; C stops.
4. **Deep dive.** Values the business two ways before looking at the price, then sets three outcomes with chances. An ordinary pick needs a dated event inside six months that shows the answer. A value pick may run up to two years with no event, but its bad case must be no more than 10% below the price and its middling case at least 15% above it.
5. **The minimum gain.** Every pick needs an expected gain after costs of at least the cash rate plus 5% a year: about 3.6% for six months and about 14% for two years at today's 2.13%. The engine refuses a card below it.
6. **Red team.** A separate reviewer that sees only the deep dive re-checks the facts and tries to break the case.
7. **Card.** Only then is a decision card written, and the engine checks it against every rule before an order is placed, at most 2.5% of the portfolio in one company and five companies in all.

## Why nothing has passed

The same few things came up again and again.

- **The fact sheets' best values were what a buyer of the whole company might pay, not where the shares would trade.** Cedar Woods' A$9.15 was a takeover-level value, in line with what an independent expert put on a bid for its peer Peet; Integrated Research's A$0.58 assumed revenue held when it was still falling 7% to 11% a year; Focus Minerals' A$2.59 used its best quarter's cost for every ounce. Valued the way the market actually prices them, most came close to today's price.
- **Listed property is priced on cash earnings, not on its book value.** Argosy, Investore, Stride and Dexus Convenience Retail all looked 30% to 40% cheap against their net tangible assets, but on cash earnings after maintenance, leasing incentives and fees each was priced like its peers.
- **Cash on the balance sheet was often already spoken for.** Grange's cash is committed to its underground mine; Maritana's goes on its build; Webjet's, Helloworld's and Redbubble owner Articore's is mostly customers', artists' and suppliers' money; Step One's sits behind a founder with 58%, and the company has never bought back shares; Garda's sale cash goes mostly into new loans.
- **"One-off" costs that come back every year.** EML Payments left out A$11m to A$34m of costs in each of the last four years, a new kind each year, and four years turned A$193m of its "underlying" profit into about minus A$5m of free cash. IVE Group's left-out costs recur too, though it guides them well.
- **Contracts and approvals that end.** Fleetwood's Karratha village approval runs only to January 2029, and Jumbo's four lottery contracts end in August 2030, so neither is worth a multiple that assumes they run for ever.
- **Profit flattered cash.** Australian Agricultural's profit was 93% herd revaluation; Scott Technology's cash went into working capital on its projects; Trajan was left with A$0.7m of cash in FY26 after leases, capital spending, interest and tax; Bhagwan Marine's leases and the upkeep of its fleet take about three quarters of its EBITDA.
- **The bad case was too far below the price.** Some looked cheap but could lose 40% to 50% if the question went against us: EDU Holdings under the new student visa rules, Trajan on its guidance record, Kaiser Reef on its grade, Maritana on its build.
- **Rising rates weigh on the borrowers.** The Reserve Bank's rise to 4.85% on 4 November adds about A$18m a year to Autosports' interest bill, which leaves FY27 profit near FY26's even if delayed car deliveries come through, and in the last rate-rise cycle Australian Finance Group's lending margin fell from 163 to 113 basis points and its profit from A$51m to A$29m. G8 Education's shares sit on A$613m of leases and A$128m of bank debt.
- **The red team cut the best cases further.** All nine deep dives it has reviewed came back "wait" or "don't buy". In the latest round it also caught two errors that flattered the case: IVE's lowest multiple was measured on the wrong year's earnings, and GDI's interest rate protection was read the wrong way round.

The broad finding: smaller companies on both exchanges look about fairly priced on the cash they actually earn. Where there is an edge, it is in the price. Several would qualify 5% to 15% lower, which is what the watch list waits for.

## Rules changed along the way

- **1 Nov:** research picks added to the rules, up to 10% of the portfolio in at most five companies, each after a deep dive and a red team. The cap per company went from 2% to 2.5% the same day, since the smallest NZX order is NZ$2,500.
- **2 Nov:** the funnel rebuilt. A ranking on the exchanges' own data replaced quick looks, standard fact sheets replaced judgement calls, and deep dives value the business before looking at the price. Pre-deal and holder situations (special situations) may be research picks.
- **8 Nov:** the trading floor dropped from A$50,000 to A$10,000 a day, to reach the smaller companies where a small portfolio has an edge.
- **8 Nov:** value picks added: up to two years, no event needed, bad case no more than 10% below the price, middling at least 25% above it, lowered to 15% the same day (Cam's call).
- **8 Nov:** the minimum gain for every pick, the cash rate plus 5% a year (Cam's call), enforced by the engine.
- **8 Nov:** fact sheets value listed property on cash earnings at its peers' yields, and count cash a controlling holder keeps out of reach only through a buyback, capital return or bid.
- **8 Nov:** Cam confirmed nothing is excluded for now (SkyCity and Betr included), so gaming, wagering and racing companies are open to the research.
- **8 Nov:** dividends count in every research pick's outcomes, including both value pick legs, at what each outcome's own earnings and cash would pay; a dividend funded by borrowing counts only if that outcome's price carries the extra debt (Cam's call).
- **8 Nov:** the watch list goes nightly: each trigger is checked against the latest price, and the evening desk reopens a share whose trigger is hit and whose other conditions still hold.

## Other places we looked

- **Discounted listed funds.** 70 listed investment companies and trusts on the screen, 39 at a discount of 10% or more. Only Barramundi had a firm date tied to a way of closing the gap (a decision on its manager by 31 March 2027). About one or two tradable cases a year, so no rule change (reports/lic_discount_screen.md).
- **Special situations.** Takeover approaches, strategic reviews and controlling holders on both exchanges were triaged (reports/special_situations_triage_asx.md and reports/special_situations_triage_nzx.md), and eleven were deep-dived as special situations, among them Sanford, KMD Brands, SkyCity, Humm, EROAD and Betr. None passes: in each the price already allows for the likely outcome, or the "nothing happens" value is too far below it.
- **Trading rules tested on history.** Fourteen rule-based ideas were tested on past prices before any money was used: a rules-only value screen, US director buying, US and ASX cash deal spreads, failed deals, a takeover target model, the June sell-off, placements, listed options, short covering, franked specials, ACCC reviews, government contracts and index additions. All fourteen failed after costs, so none is traded, which is why the research picks lean on reading the filings rather than on a rule. (ASX scrip deals and NZX deals still trade in the deal sleeve, under its own rules.)
