# Helloworld Travel (ASX:HLO): red team review. Wait for the H1 FY27 result; on a like-for-like market multiple the expected gain after costs is about +0.4%, below the 2.3% minimum

Written 2026-11-08 by the red team, against docs/RED_TEAM.md (research pick checklist) and RULEBOOK.md sections 3, 4 and 5a as they stand today. This is a simulation and not financial advice.

What I reviewed: research/picks/HLO.md (the deep dive, written 8 November 2026, verdict "wait for the H1 FY27 result"). No card has been drafted. Price: A$1.355 (last trade on 6 November 2026, data/research_data/asx_company.json; the session before also closed at A$1.355; bid A$1.345, ask A$1.355). 52-week range A$1.31 to A$2.10.

- Every research pick: expected gain after costs of at least the cash rate (2.13%) plus 5% a year over the days to the resolve date. To 5 March 2027 that is 117 days and 2.29%; to 16 March 2027, 128 days and 2.50%.

Order of work: I read the deep dive, the checklist and the rulebook, then downloaded every filing below myself from the ASX. In short:
1. The facts I re-checked held, with one correction that matters: Helloworld's H1 FY26 underlying EBITDA of A$30.5m included a A$1.4m loss on the Webjet shares, which the FY26 accounts have since taken out of "underlying". On today's basis H1 FY26 was about A$31.9m, so the deep dive's bad-case trigger and its third reopen condition use the wrong comparison.
2. The deep dive's middling price (A$1.50) is the market's trailing multiple (5.5 times EBITDA after leases) laid on forward earnings. Like for like, the market pays about 5.1 times the deep dive's own middling FY27 figure today, and paid about 4.6 times FY26 guidance in January. If that holds through the result, which is what "middling" means, the price is about A$1.35 to A$1.40 after the interim dividend, not A$1.50.
3. The H1 result is a poor event for this stock: the last six results moved the price +4.8%, -9.6%, -19.0%, -2.5%, -3.6% and +0.3% on the day (about -5% on average), no result since February 2024 has lifted it, and the A$78m to A$84m FY27 guidance in October lifted it 2% for one day.
4. The deep dive counts a 4.7c interim dividend inside a 5 March resolve date. The ex-date was 2 March in 2026 but 11 March in 2025, so it may fall after 5 March.
5. On my numbers (bad 30% A$1.05, middling 55% A$1.40, good 15% A$1.75, plus the interim) the expected gain is +2.9% before costs and about +0.4% after them, against a 2.3% to 2.5% minimum. It clears only if the middling price is A$1.45 or more, or if the price falls to about A$1.33 (A$1.30 to leave room for error) with nothing else changed.

## 2. Is it really overlooked, or is it cheap for a reason?

Who is selling: FIL, the only large outside institution, sold 933,342 shares in nine sessions into the Crown completion and still holds 14.1m (about 100 days of median volume). A substantial holder need not file again until its holding moves by another 1%, so I can't tell whether it is still selling; the price drifted from A$1.37 to A$1.32 while it sold. The board itself sold 12.5% of the enlarged company to Crown's vendors at A$1.44 a share [R4]. A board that thought the shares were worth A$1.80 would be slow to issue them at A$1.44. There has been no insider buying at A$1.31 to A$1.40: the only director's interest notice in Helloworld's 2026 announcement list is Martin Pakula's 2,500 shares in June, and the Burnes group, which could creep 3 points every six months, last bought at A$1.72 in March 2025 (per the deep dive's notice [H24], not re-read).

Industry trouble: Helloworld is down 22% in 2026 (Norgate total return to 30 October), Flight Centre 21%, Web Travel 25%, so this year's fall is the sector's, not a company-specific discount opening up. Since the June guidance cut, though, Helloworld is flat while Flight Centre is up 6.5% and Web Travel 47%: the market has not paid for Helloworld's recovery guidance or for Crown. The Middle East conflict that cut FY26 is still named in the FY27 guidance [R5].

Debt falling due: the vendor loan is said to be "repayable over three years" (secondary source only [S1]), about A$11m a year on top of a dividend that now costs A$18.7m a year. Citibank runs to October 2028. The NAB facility's term is not disclosed. Nothing falls due before the H1 result as far as the filings show; the first covenant test with Crown in the numbers is 31 December 2026. On the bank's measure (borrowings less reported cash) the covenants look comfortable.

Guidance record: FY25 cut in May 2025, FY26 cut in June 2026 after being reaffirmed at the H1 result on 25 February 2026 [R16]. Both formal cuts came months after the half-year result, but the February 2025 result day still took 19% off the price, and the February 2026 reaffirmation was followed by a 17% fall over the next month (section 4). A reaffirmation at H1 has not been worth much to holders.

There is also a point on how "underlying" is drawn. In FY25 the A$5.0m Webjet gain was inside underlying EBITDA and lifted the July 2025 guidance raise; in FY26, the year the stake lost A$34.3m, Webjet revaluations were taken out and FY25 restated. The new definition is the better one, but it changed in the year it helped.

So it is overlooked (outside the ASX 300, thin, little coverage), but it is also cheap for reasons that won't go away by March: a controlled register, a borrowed acquisition done without a vote, a falling dividend (14c to 10c), a chief executive with options open on Webjet, and a large holder selling.

## 3. The valuation

### Own cash and net debt
The deep dive's own-cash measure is sound and I reproduce it (minus A$4.3m at 30 June 2026). Net debt of A$140.6m (A$35m Citibank, A$4.3m of negative own cash, A$101.25m borrowed for Crown) is right in total: A$135m less A$33.75m of vendor shares leaves A$101.25m from NAB and the vendor, whatever the split. Two cautions:
- It leaves out the A$8.2m final dividend paid in September, Crown's deal costs and Crown's banknote float. By 31 December six months of trading should bring net debt down to about A$130m to A$135m on my rough cash flow, so A$140.6m is a fair round figure for the H1 accounts.
- Crown's stock of foreign banknotes will show as cash at 31 December 2026. The deep dive's fourth reopen test (own cash no worse than minus A$10m) could be passed by Crown's float rather than by Helloworld's earnings. The test should leave out cash held by Crown.

### Crown's store rent
Undisclosed, as the deep dive says. One piece of evidence the deep dive didn't use: the A$14m for eight months in guidance is close to eight twelfths of A$22m (A$14.7m). If Crown's A$22m were struck after rent, Helloworld's own lease accounting would lift it by the rent (A$4m to A$10m a year) and the eight-month figure would be nearer A$17m to A$21m. So the guidance supports the deep dive's reading that the A$22m is before rent, unless Crown earns much less from November to June than from July to October, which I can't check (my inference). The stores I found listed are in major shopping centres (Westfield Sydney, Tuggerah and Pacific Fair among them) [S2], which fits a rent bill of several million dollars. The deep dive's central A$7m is reasonable and its range (A$4m to A$10m) is honest.

What the H1 accounts will show: a half-year report must disclose the provisional assets and liabilities of a business bought in the half, including right of use assets and lease liabilities. That will give a first measure of Crown's lease commitments even if rent isn't stated. It is a better reopen test than waiting for Crown's EBITDA after rent to be "disclosed", which it may never be.

### The multiple the market pays, and whether it holds through the result
This is where I disagree with the deep dive, and it decides the verdict.

| Same date, on what FY26 actually delivered | A$261m | A$48.5m | 5.4 times |

The "5 to 5.6 times this year" is a multiple of earnings already reported. On the earnings the market was looking forward to at the time, it paid about 4.6 times in January (and was right to be wary: FY26 guidance was cut) and about 5.1 times today. The deep dive's middling A$1.50 puts 5.5 times on forward FY27 earnings; that is a re-rating of about 9% above anything the market has paid this year on a forward basis.

If the multiple holds like for like through an in-line result, the arithmetic is: 5.1 times A$69.2m, net debt about A$133m at December, associates and Webjet as now: about A$1.39 a share before the interim dividend and about A$1.35 after it. Giving some credit for Crown's numbers taking away uncertainty (5.2 times) gives about A$1.40 after the dividend. That is my middling price. It amounts to the price holding and the holder collecting the dividend, which is what "guidance reaffirmed, Crown as expected" should mean for a stock the market has already priced on that guidance.

### The bad and good prices
- Bad: the deep dive's A$1.10 is the average of its DCF (A$1.10 to A$1.14) and 5 times earnings after leases (A$1.03 to A$1.06). The market prices this stock on multiples, not on Helloworld's own 11.5% discount rate, so the multiple figure is the one to use: 5 times A$59.2m, net debt about A$138m, Webjet at A$0.26 gives about A$1.06 before the dividend, A$1.02 after. At 4.5 times, a fair multiple for a company that has just cut guidance a third time with leverage at 2 times, it is about A$0.86. A$1.10 is not the floor. I use A$1.05.
- Good: the deep dive's A$1.80 needs 6 to 6.5 times within four months. On A$72m of earnings after leases at 6 times I get about A$1.79 after the dividend. I keep about A$1.75 but cut the chance (section 5). A premium bid for Webjet by someone else adds only about 4c a Helloworld share for each 10c on Webjet's price, so it can't carry the good case on its own.

### The "underlying" items that recur
Confirmed: cash costs left out of underlying were A$9.7m in FY26 and A$4.2m in FY25, and new business start-up costs have been added back inside the segments two years running [R7]. H1 FY26 alone carried A$4.9m of acquisition costs [R11]; H1 FY27 will carry Crown's. The deep dive's A$6m a year is fair and it already deducts it in the DCF; the market, judging by the multiple, deducts it too.

### The Webjet stake and a debt-funded bid
Webjet is about 9% of Helloworld's market value at A$0.305 (78.25m shares, A$23.9m). The creep window opens about 18 to 19 November and the AGM says options are being assessed [R6]. A bid for the 79.5% Helloworld doesn't own at A$0.35 to A$0.40 would cost A$106m to A$122m and take net debt on the deep dive's measure to about A$247m to A$262m: 3.6 to 3.8 times Helloworld's own earnings after leases, or roughly 2.5 to 3 times once Webjet's earnings and spare cash come in on the deep dive's Webjet figures (not re-checked). When Helloworld proposed A$0.90 in November 2025 its own price rose 1.7% on the day, but it then had about A$35m of net debt, not A$140m. I give a Helloworld bid before March about a 10% chance and count it in the bad case. The bank covenant on its own measure wouldn't stop it; NAB's terms are unknown.

### Peers and the DCF
Flight Centre and Web Travel at 8.7 to 9.2 times are fair comparisons for size and business, but not for register or record; Corporate Travel and Webjet are not clean comparisons, as the deep dive says. I did not re-check the peer figures. The single input that moves the DCF most is the discount rate and growth pair; the single input that moves the market price is the multiple, and that is the one the market has already set.

## 6. Special situations only

Not a special situation pick, and the deep dive doesn't claim to be one. The one player question that bears on it: can Helloworld do something with Webjet that moves its own price? It can creep about 2.5 points from mid-November for about A$3m (harmless), or bid with borrowed money (harmful to Helloworld's holders in the short run, as above). Someone else's premium bid for Webjet would help Helloworld modestly. None of these is likely enough or large enough to be the dated event.

## Value pick test (the new rules)

It fails, as the deep dive says. The bad case is 19% below the price on the deep dive's figures and 22% to 25% below on mine, against a 10% limit. The middling business value (A$1.80, +33%) passes the 15% leg, but the price the market would actually pay by a 24-month resolve date is the harder question. At the market's multiple of about 5 to 5.5 times on FY29 earnings after leases (about A$72m to A$75m) and net debt paid down to about A$100m, I get about A$1.60 to A$1.90, so the middling leg could pass on deleveraging alone. The bad-case leg can't pass until Crown's costs and the debt terms are known, which is the deep dive's reopen route, and I agree with it.

## Three points most likely to make the pick wrong (if it were bought now)

1. The multiple doesn't move. The market has paid 4.6 to 5.1 times forward earnings after leases all year and didn't re-rate on the A$78m to A$84m guidance. An in-line H1 then leaves the price near A$1.35 to A$1.40, and the pick earns little more than the dividend, less costs.
2. A harder H1 comparison than the deep dive assumed. On the restated basis the business without Crown has to beat about A$31.9m, having lost about A$1.1m of Webjet dividend, after a first quarter that was only "line ball" even with MTA fully owned. A headline that includes two months of Crown may look fine while the core looks flat or down.
3. Capital allocation. A debt-funded Webjet bid, with the AGM saying options are being assessed, would take net debt to about A$250m on the deep dive's measure. Add FIL's 14.1m shares still to sell, and the left tail is fatter than the right.

## Verdict

Wait for a named event: Helloworld's H1 FY27 result (about 25 February 2027).

Reasons:
1. On a like-for-like market multiple the expected gain after costs is about +0.4%, below the 2.29% minimum to 5 March (2.50% to 16 March). The deep dive's +7% rests on a middling price of A$1.50 that needs the market to pay 5.5 times forward earnings after leases, when it has paid 4.6 to 5.1 times all year and didn't re-rate on the FY27 guidance.
2. The event is poor for this stock: no result since February 2024 has lifted the price (one H1 result took 19% off it in a day), the FY27 guidance lifted it for one day, and the interim dividend the deep dive counts may go ex after its resolve date.
3. The tail is on the downside: a harder H1 comparison than the deep dive used (about A$31.9m, not A$30.5m), a possible debt-funded Webjet bid, and FIL's remaining 14.1m shares.

What would reopen it, as a 24-month value pick after the H1 accounts (the deep dive's route, with these changes):
1. Crown's leases in the half-year acquisition note imply annual store rent of A$8m or less (or Crown's EBITDA after rent is stated at A$15m a year or more).
2. Total borrowings at 31 December 2026 of A$140m or less, the NAB and vendor terms disclosed at an average cost of 7% or less, and covenants met.
3. H1 FY27 underlying EBITDA without Crown of at least A$32m (the restated H1 FY26 of about A$31.9m, not A$30.5m), and A$78m to A$84m reaffirmed.
4. Own cash on the deep dive's measure, leaving out cash held by Crown, no worse than minus A$10m at 31 December 2026.
5. No Helloworld bid for Webjet and no further debt-funded purchase.
The middling price for that value pick should be the market's multiple (5 to 5.5 times) on FY29 earnings with the debt paid down, not 6.5 times.

Or reopen as an ordinary research pick only if the price falls to about A$1.30 or lower with no company news and the travel peers (Flight Centre, Web Travel) not down more than 5% since 6 November. If it is ever carded, resolve by about 16 March 2027 so the interim dividend's ex-date is inside the window, put the dividend into the outcome prices (bad 30% A$1.09, middling 55% A$1.45, good 15% A$1.80 on my numbers), and exit at the next open after a cut to FY27 guidance, a Helloworld bid for Webjet funded by debt, net debt on the deep dive's measure above A$160m, or Crown's EBITDA after rent below A$12m a year.

Don't reopen if Helloworld cuts FY27 guidance or bids for Webjet with borrowed money.
