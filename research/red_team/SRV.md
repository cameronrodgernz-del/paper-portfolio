# Servcorp Limited (ASX:SRV): red team

Written 2026-11-01 by the red team, against the deep dive research/picks/SRV.md (price at writing A$5.19). I read only the deep dive and the research-pick checklist in docs/RED_TEAM.md, then went back to the sources. This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Sources marked [S1] to [S15] are the deep dive's own, re-opened. Sources marked [R1] onwards are new and listed at the end. I did not open the repository's Norgate price files, so the claims that rest only on them (index exit in 2019, 60-session median value, the September volume figures) are checked only as far as the public price table [R7] allows.

## 1. Are the facts right?

### What checks out
I re-opened S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6, S7, S8, S9 and S10 to S14. Most of the numbers the case rests on are right.

- Shares on issue 101,855,088, last close A$5.20 on 29 October, 52-week range A$5.13 to A$8.16 [S9]. Market value at A$5.19 is A$528.6m.
- Revenue A$363.7m and net profit A$65.6m for FY26, and the FY23 to FY26 table, match the ASX data [S9].
- Underlying NPBIT A$87.0m, statutory profit before tax A$80.9m, tax A$15.3m, basic EPS 66.0 cents, diluted 64.3 cents [S2].
- Cash and cash equivalents A$137.8m (A$137,795k) and cash and investments A$153.1m (A$153,083k) at 30 June 2026 [S2]. "The Company has no external debt" [S1, Chairman's message].
- Lease liabilities A$89.9m current and A$247.5m non-current, A$337.4m in all, against A$363.2m a year earlier [S2]. Bank guarantees A$30.3m at 31 December 2025, and no contingent liabilities at that date [S4].
- Net assets A$255.0m [S2]; net tangible assets A$2.32 a share at 31 December 2025 [S3, S4].
- Dividends of 16 cents and 16 cents, each 10% franked [S2]. FY27: dividends "not expected to be below 34.0 cps", underlying NPBIT A$85.0m to A$90.0m "subject to no worsening near-term economic conditions globally", underlying free cash of more than A$105.0m, all "subject to currencies remaining constant" [S1, S2].
- Mr Moufarrige's holding after the 13 October purchases: 547,436 direct and 53,720,065 indirect, 54,267,501 in all, 53.3% [S7]. Each of the notices S10 to S14 matches the deep dive to the share and the dollar. The A$1.62m total is right.
- The AGM is on Thursday 12 November 2026 at 4:00pm AEDT, with only the remuneration report, Tony McGrath's re-election and John Wigglesworth's election on the agenda [S6].
- FY26 guidance was raised on 21 January 2026 from A$72m to A$76m to A$80m to A$84m, and free cash guidance from A$90m to A$100m [S5].
- No capital raising in 2026: the only new shares came from option exercises [S8, R6].
- The valuation arithmetic in both methods and the expected-price sum are correct as written.

### What is wrong or overstated
1. "A management team that raised its FY26 guidance twice and then beat it" (Why the market is wrong, point 1). It was raised once, on 21 January 2026 [S5], and only reaffirmed with the half-year result [S3]. The deep dive's own earlier section says once.
2. "Non-cash impairments of floors (about A$6.1m in FY26: A$87.0m less A$80.9m)". The company's reconciliation shows something different: A$5.0m of operating losses from new floors before they mature and from closed floors after closure, and only A$1.1m of impairment, closure and other non-recurring costs [S2]. The A$5.0m is trading losses, mostly cash, and it recurs every year Servcorp opens floors. FY27 has at least six new floors, so it is likely to be larger, not smaller.
3. "About A$20m more went somewhere ... the most likely use is 333 Sussex Street". The arithmetic doesn't produce A$20m. Net operating cash flow A$176.6m, less cash rent A$91.8m, less capital spending A$44.8m, less dividends A$29.9m, plus option money A$4.1m gives A$14.2m, against a rise of A$8.7m: a gap of about A$5.5m, and the 4E notes A$8.6m of rent paid in FY26 that relates to future years [S2]. Land and building additions in the first half were only A$5 thousand [S4], and second-half capital spending was about A$33.6m (A$44.8m less A$11.2m in the first half [S4]), so the property most likely sits inside the A$44.8m. A three-storey building at 333-337 Sussex Street sold for A$12.25m in April 2026 to a buyer the report doesn't name [R10]; if that is Servcorp's purchase, it fits "at least $20 million in real estate assets" [S1] together with the older A$3.1m of freehold [S4] and renovation. This correction helps the pick a little (some of FY26's capital spending was one-off), but the deep dive's story of hidden spending isn't supported.
4. The list of "director dealings since the FY26 result" starts on 2 September and leaves out 24 August 2026, when Mr Moufarrige bought 50,000 shares on market for A$300,000 (A$6.00) [R1] and the chairman, Mark Vaile, bought 1,000 [R2]. More important, it presents the buying as new. It isn't: Mr Moufarrige bought 100,000 shares for A$656,946 (about A$6.57) on 24 April [R3] and 49,969 for A$307,309 (about A$6.15) on 13 May [R4]. Since April he has bought about A$2.9m of shares, not counting options, at A$5.60 to A$6.57, and the price has fallen from about A$6.57 to A$5.19 through all of it.
5. Smaller points. FY25 underlying NPBIT is A$70.0m in the 4E summary but A$69.1m in the directors' report [S2], so FY26 growth is 24% or 26% depending on the page; this is the company's own inconsistency. The trailing yield on 32 cents is 6.2% at A$5.19, not 6.5%; the 6.5% and 6.6% figures are both the forward yield on 34 cents.

### What the deep dive left out
- The Middle East war. This is the biggest omission. Europe and the Middle East is Servcorp's largest profit region (A$50.0m of mature segment profit [S2]), and most of it is not Europe: of its 57 floors, 40 are in Saudi Arabia (22), the UAE (7), Qatar (4), Bahrain (3), Lebanon (3) and Kuwait (1), with 2 more in Turkey, against 15 in France, Belgium and the UK [S1, geographic spread pages]. The US and Israel struck Iran on 28 February 2026; Iran answered with missiles and drones on US-allied Gulf states and closed the Strait of Hormuz, and on 8 April alone Kuwait, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Bahrain all reported attacks or interceptions [R8]. A ceasefire collapsed on 8 July [R8]. Qatar's non-energy activity was still contracting in May and UAE hiring intentions weakened into the September quarter [R9]. I could not establish where the conflict stood at the end of October. Servcorp's own 4E says it is "expecting a short-term negative impact across the Middle East due to the current geopolitical situation" [S2]. The deep dive doesn't mention any of this.
- Where the second-half slowdown came from. Europe and Middle East mature segment profit was A$27.6m in the first half [S4] and A$50.0m for the year [S2], so about A$22.4m in the second half, down 19%, on mature revenue down about 5% (A$83.4m to about A$79.6m). Australia, New Zealand and South East Asia (A$7.6m then A$7.6m) and North Asia (A$7.8m then A$7.9m) were flat. So most of the A$7.0m drop from A$47.0m to A$40.0m that the deep dive flags came from the region at war. (The set of "mature" floors can shift slightly between the half and the full year, so treat the half-year splits as close, not exact.) For balance, the second half was still up on the year before: about A$22.4m against A$21.2m for the region, and A$40.0m against about A$35.0m for the group. Growth stalled; it didn't reverse.
- The FY27 growth plan leans into the same region: "We have already committed to growth in the 2027 financial year, particularly in Saudi Arabia", with three new Saudi locations in the pipeline [S1].
- The chairman is leaving. Mark Vaile, director since 2011 and chairman since 2019, won't stand at the 12 November AGM; Tony McGrath becomes chairman and John Wigglesworth is expected to chair the audit committee [R5]. The founder and CEO is about 85 [R11], and nothing on CEO succession is public [S6].
- The quality of the free cash rise. Free cash rose from A$86.7m to A$99.8m, but that came from cash rent paid falling from A$115.3m to A$91.8m while floor numbers stayed at 137; net operating cash flow before tax actually fell, from about A$197.1m to A$191.9m [S2]. Nothing I read explains the fall in rent paid. If it reflects rent holidays or lease timing that reverses, the A$105m free cash guide is harder than it looks.
- What "underlying" includes. Underlying NPBIT contains A$3.6m of interest and franchise income and A$1.4m of currency gains; underlying operating profit was A$80.9m [S2]. That matters for the valuation (section 3).
- No lawsuits, regulator action or auditor qualification turned up (KPMG is the auditor [S2]). I could not read the related-party note (note 29), because the copies of S1 and S2 I could reach end before it. No substantial holder notices appear in the 2026 announcement lists [S8, R6].

## 2. Is it really overlooked, or is it cheap for a reason?

Partly overlooked, mostly cheap for a reason that is visible.

The deep dive's structural points are fair: out of the index, a 47% free float, about A$428,000 traded a day (the last 25 sessions in [R7], 37,796 to 280,034 shares a day, are consistent with that), and 10% franking. None of that is new, though, and none of it explains a 36% fall since January.

What does explain it:
- The timing fits the war. The A$8.16 high was in January 2026 [S9], before the first strikes on 28 February [R8]. I couldn't reach a day-by-day price series for February and March to prove the link, but the price fell through a year in which the company was reporting records, and the region that carried the record is the one at war.
- Growth has stopped. FY27 guidance is flat at A$85m to A$90m on A$87.0m [S2], after two years of 20% to 40% profit growth. Management says the payoff from its current spending comes in FY28, where it aims to "move beyond $95 million" [S1, R15]. That is after the resolve date.
- Who is selling. The deep dive is right that someone sold heavily around the 16 September ex-dividend date, and volume stayed high into October (280,034 shares on 16 October [R7]). The only steady buyer is the founder, and his buying has not stopped the slide in six months. As a 53% holder he can add up to 3% every six months without a bid, so his buying may be about control over the long run rather than a view on the next half.
- The industry is not the problem. IWG reported first-half 2026 revenue per room up 11% and held its guidance [R14]. That makes Servcorp's weakness more particular to Servcorp: the Gulf, the yen (North Asia revenue fell on a "decade-low" yen [S2]) and a founder-run business at a point of board change.
- No debt falls due. The fixed commitment is rent: A$89.9m of lease liabilities are due within a year [S2].
- Management's record against guidance is good, and this is the strongest point for the pick. FY25 guidance at the November 2024 AGM was A$61m to A$65m [R12] and the result was about A$69m to A$70m; FY26 started at A$72m to A$76m and came in at A$87.0m [S5, S2]. But the record has a gap: in February 2026 management said first-half momentum "is expected to continue into the second half" [S3], and the second half came in 15% below the first.

## 3. The valuation

Peers. I agree there is no clean listed peer. But Servcorp's own 12.4 times high is a pre-war multiple on a year of 26% growth, so it is a ceiling, not a normal level.

The earnings base is too generous in three ways. Underlying NPBIT leaves out the losses on new floors (A$5.0m in FY26, likely more in FY27), includes interest earned on the same cash the deep dive then adds back separately, and includes a currency gain [S2]. On a cleaner base for FY27: A$87.5m, less about A$6.0m of new-floor losses, A$1.0m of closure costs, A$3.6m of interest and franchise income and A$1.4m of currency gains, gives about A$75.5m before tax and A$61.2m after tax at 18.9%, or 60.1 cents a share. Using the deep dive's own method:

| Multiple of core after-tax earnings, plus A$100m cash | Deep dive | Restated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 times | A$5.86 | A$5.19 |
| 8.5 times | A$6.91 | A$6.09 |
| 10 times | A$7.95 | A$6.99 |

On a clean base the price today is exactly 7 times core earnings plus A$100m of cash. That is cheap-ish for a business with no debt, but not the 6 times the deep dive quotes.

Operating leverage. Rent is fixed at about A$92m a year against A$364m of revenue [S2]. The second half of FY26 showed what that means: in Europe and the Middle East, revenue fell about 5% half on half and profit fell about 19%. A small slip in occupancy in the Gulf goes almost straight to profit.

Net assets and cash. Book value says little here, and I agree. The question is the cash. The A$153.1m includes listed shares and bank hybrids (A$14.9m and A$4.8m at December [S4]), and sits alongside A$33.2m of other financial liabilities, mostly client deposits [S2]. The company says its cash "positions Servcorp to capitalise on our growth strategy" and has started buying property [S1]; it bought back no shares in FY26. The controlling holder decides what happens to the cash, so A$100m of "surplus" for a minority holder is generous. A$50m to A$75m is more realistic, and every A$25m is about 25 cents a share.

The cash-flow method. The input that moves the answer most is owner cash, meaning free cash less tax less capital spending. FY27 has six or more new floors plus the 333 Sussex Street renovation. FY26's A$44.8m probably included the property, so fit-out spending was perhaps A$30m to A$32m with five openings. A$35m to A$45m for FY27 is a fair range; A$35m is the low end.

| Free cash | Capital spending | Owner cash | 11% and 2% | 12% and 1% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A$105m | A$35m (deep dive) | A$53.5m | A$6.93 | A$5.80 |
| A$105m | A$40m | A$48.5m | A$6.38 | A$5.35 |
| A$105m | A$44.8m | A$43.7m | A$5.84 | A$4.92 |
| A$97m (Middle East takes A$8m) | A$40m | A$40.5m | A$5.49 | A$4.63 |

Each includes A$100m of cash. The deep dive's sensitivity (about A$1.00 a share per A$10.5m of free cash) is about right. An 11% discount rate is reasonable for a controlled, thinly traded small company with a war-zone exposure and fixed rents; 12% is just as defensible.

My range is A$5.20 to A$7.00, with a central value of about A$6.00 to A$6.10, against the deep dive's A$5.80 to A$8.70 and A$6.90. There is value, but most of the gap to A$6.00 depends on FY28 profit growth that won't be visible by 1 March 2027.

## 4. The dated events and the exit

The AGM on 12 November is not a test. In 2024 the chairman's address restated guidance and said the first quarter had "performed well", with no figures [R12]; the 2025 AGM release I found carried only poll results [R13]. The likeliest outcome is a restatement already in the price. The AGM can hurt (a Middle East or currency warning) more than it can help. It is also Mr Vaile's last meeting as chairman.

The half-year result in late February 2027 is the real test, but it is tilted against the pick.
- The comparison is H1 FY26's A$47.0m, a record half earned entirely before the war began. If FY27 lands on the A$87.5m midpoint and splits evenly, H1 is about A$43.75m, down 7% on the year before. The headline will say profit fell, even on guidance.
- The deep dive's bad case and exit trigger is H1 below A$41m. The latest half was A$40.0m. If the next half just repeats the last one, the exit fires. The good case needs a rebound back above A$45m within one half, which in practice means the Gulf recovering and new floors filling quickly.
- The resolve date of 1 March 2027 leaves about a week after the result. The interim dividend goes ex in early March (last year's record date was 5 March [S3]), so it isn't collected, but the price on the resolve date will still carry it.

Liquidity is not a problem at our size. A$1,600 is under 0.5% of a median day's trading, so the position can be sold in any week. The risk is a gap on result day, which no exit rule can avoid.

The exit rule is sound. I would add one trigger: sell if the AGM address qualifies FY27 guidance for the Middle East. The "Mr Moufarrige selling" trigger is very unlikely to fire.

## 5. The stated chances

| Outcome | Deep dive chance | Deep dive price | My chance | My price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bad: guidance cut, or H1 FY27 below A$41m | 30% | A$4.40 | 35% | A$4.40 |
| Middling: guidance held, H1 A$42m to A$45m, 17 cent interim | 45% | A$5.40 | 45% | A$5.25 |
| Good: H1 at or above A$45m, or a capital return | 25% | A$6.80 | 20% | A$6.50 |
| Expected price | | A$5.45 (+5.0%) | | A$5.20 (+0.2%) |

Where I differ, and why:
- Bad, up from 30% to 35%. The trigger sits A$1m above the most recent half. The Middle East is unresolved, the company has already warned of a short-term hit there, and the second half showed how hard a small revenue slip hits profit. Against that, management has beaten guidance two years running and set FY27 guidance with the war already six months old, which is why I don't go higher.
- Middling price, down from A$5.40 to A$5.25. A half that is 5% to 10% below the year before is unlikely to lift the price 4%. The stock has drifted down for six months on similar news, and A$5.25 already includes the 17 cent dividend still attached on 1 March.
- Good, down from 25% to 20%, and the price from A$6.80 to A$6.50. It needs a Gulf rebound and fast fill of new floors within a single half. A buy-back or special dividend is unlikely when the company is talking about its growth strategy, has just bought its first building and bought back nothing in FY26. A$6.50 is about the top of my restated range at 8.5 to 10 times core earnings.
- I agree with the bad-case price of A$4.40.

On my numbers the expected gain is about 0.2% before round-trip costs of 1% to 2%, so negative after costs. Even on the deep dive's own numbers the margin is thin: 5.0% against 1% to 2% of costs.

## The three points most likely to make the pick wrong
1. The Middle East. About 40 of Servcorp's 137 floors are in the Gulf and Lebanon, inside the region that earns the most. The region has been at war on and off since 28 February 2026, the company has warned of a short-term hit, and that region's profit fell about 19% from the first half to the second. The deep dive doesn't mention it.
2. The February test is tilted. H1 FY27 will be compared with a pre-war record of A$47.0m. The exit trigger of A$41m is only A$1m above the latest half, and even a half on guidance will show a fall.
3. The earnings and the cash are overstated. Underlying profit leaves out recurring new-floor losses and includes interest and currency gains. The "surplus" cash belongs to a company whose controlling holder is spending it on growth and property. On a clean base the price is already 7 times core earnings plus A$100m. The central value of about A$6.10 depends mostly on FY28, after the resolve date, and six months of founder buying at higher prices has not held the price up.

## Verdict
Don't buy.

On a clean earnings base, and once the Middle East exposure the deep dive left out is allowed for, the expected price by 1 March 2027 is about where the stock trades now, which is a loss after costs. The one event inside the window, the February half-year result, is measured against a pre-war record half, with an exit trigger set just above the latest half. A good outcome needs a Gulf recovery that nobody can date. The stock may well be worth more on a two-year view, but this card's four-month window doesn't give the market a fair chance to show it is wrong.
