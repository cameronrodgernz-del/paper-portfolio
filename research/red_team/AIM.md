# AI-Media Technologies (ASX:AIM): red team review of the research pick

Written 8 November 2026 by the red team, from the deep dive at research/picks/AIM.md (8 November 2026), the research pick checklist in docs/RED_TEAM.md and rulebook sections 3, 4 and 5a. I was not given a draft card. Price at review: A$0.195 (last trade on Friday 6 November 2026, bid A$0.195, ask A$0.21) [M2]. This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Verdict: don't buy. The reasons are in the last section.

In short:
- The facts mostly hold. I re-opened 29 filings and the ASX announcement lists, and checked 19 of the deep dive's figures against them and the repo's market data; every figure held. Two things are wrong or go beyond the filings: Salter Brothers did not sell out in 2025 and did not build its 9.15% at 21c to 24c, and the FY27 guidance did not come "with the first quarter's trading" in any form a reader can see, because the AGM pack gives no first-quarter figures or comment.
- The case is weaker on prices than on chances. The deep dive's middling price (A$0.23, +18%) carries nearly half its expected value, and its good price (A$0.33, +69%) is a move the stock has never made in a result week. In twelve past results, the price moved less than 9% over the following week eight times; the best weeks were +22% to +30% and the worst was -50%. Moving only those two prices to what that record supports takes the expected return after costs from +9.3% to about nil.
- December ARR, the company's headline measure, is likely to look soft. The June 2026 figure (A$36.0m) was the highest ever relative to the revenue it sits on, June 2026 included three weeks of the FIFA World Cup in North America, and every December figure has printed lower relative to revenue than the June before it. The deep dive's outcome table doesn't count a weak ARR as bad.
- The market already had the good news and sold it. The FY26 result rise (+27%) has fully reversed, the guidance and buy-back day opened 12% up and closed 2% down, the company bought no shares in the first two weeks of its buy-back, and the only director purchase near today's price was A$4,755.
- My figures: bad 40% at A$0.145, middling 45% at A$0.215, good 15% at A$0.27. Expected price A$0.195, about nil before costs and about -4% to -5% after.

### Where the deep dive is wrong or goes beyond the filings
1. Salter Brothers. The deep dive says Salter "sold out at about 57c" in September 2025 "then came back", and that it "built 9.15% at 21c to 24c (March to August 2026)". Neither is right. Salter held 18.2m shares (8.72%) in July 2024 [R21], cut to 12.08m by September 2024 [R22, R23], and its September 2025 notice is a ceasing notice for falling below 5% after selling 2,505,000 more [R20], which leaves about 9.6m shares (4.6%), not nil. Its March 2026 notice lists purchases from 30 September 2025 to 18 March 2026 [R17, R18], when the price ran from about 89c down to about 30c. So only about 7.1m of its 19.2m shares were bought at 21c to 24c (April to August 2026); the rest is older and was mostly bought well above today's price. This matters for the story: Salter is a fund that has sold this stock into strength before, not a new holder that has only ever bought at these levels.
2. "Set with the first quarter in hand." The guidance was issued on 30 September, the last day of the first quarter, but the AGM pack contains no first-quarter revenue, ARR or trading comment at all [R3]. The deep dive treats the timing as support for the guidance; the filing gives no evidence either way.
3. "Every on-market director trade in two years has been a buy." True, but at today's prices the buying is token: one purchase of A$4,755 since December 2025 (above).

### Left out of the deep dive
- The market's response to the guidance and buy-back. On 30 September 2026 the shares opened at A$0.28, 12% above the prior close, and closed at A$0.245; two sessions later they were A$0.215. They recovered to A$0.26 on 7 October and slid to A$0.195 by 6 November [M1, M2]. The FY26 result lifted the price 27% (A$0.205 to A$0.26 on 27 August) and that rise has fully reversed: the price is now below where it was before FY26 was reported, with the second-half turnaround, the guidance and the buy-back all known.
- EEG Video Holdings LLC, the vendor of the EEG encoder business, sold its last 5,312,500 shares on market on 16 December 2024 for A$4,145,100, about 78c [R26]. Old, but it means the people who built the hardware business have left the register.
- About a quarter of the share-unit plan left in its first year: 2,360,000 of the 9,900,000 units granted were forfeited in FY26 "due to the termination of the service agreement" [R5 note 33].
- Alison Loat left the board in September 2025 (retirement announced 26 August 2025) [R30]. Minor.
- The ACMA found a captioning-quality breach on a legacy service for Seven Adelaide in December 2024 [R27]. The company said it wasn't material and I agree.
- S&P reclassified the stock to Application Software from 1 December 2025 [R28]. Neutral, but it now sits in software screens, where its growth and margins look weak.
- Heavy selling at the low. On 6 November 451,853 shares traded between A$0.19 and A$0.21, about 4.5 times the 20-session median of 99,248 shares [M2, M1]. No notice names a seller yet. Jencay holds exactly 5.00% [R24], so any sale by it needs a ceasing notice within two business days (by 10 November for a 6 November trade). That is worth checking before any order.

## 2. Is it really overlooked, or is it cheap for a reason?
Who sold. In about 18 months Regal (out on 26 February 2026), Wilson Asset Management (6 March 2026), Deanne Weir (September 2025) and the EEG vendor (December 2024) left, and Salter halved its stake before rebuilding it. The register shows the scale: the Citicorp Nominees line fell from 21.8m shares to 8.1m and UBS Nominees from 19.6m to 11.7m between June 2025 and June 2026 [R11, R5]. Regal, WAM and Salter are among the best-known small-company investors in Australia. A stock they owned and sold in the past year is not overlooked in the sense rule 5a means; it is cheap for reasons that informed holders acted on, and the question is whether the selling overshot.

Who replaced them. Salter (see above) and Jencay, which ceased to be substantial in March 2024 [R30] and came back at 5.00% in June 2026. Both have traded this stock in and out before, so both are plausible sellers into any rally toward 25c to 30c, which caps the good case.

TIGA (Thorney). Its 2023 notice shows its shares registered with UBS Nominees [R25]; that line fell 7.9m in FY26 while Thorney International Pty Ltd appeared in the top 20 with 7.72m [R5]. Most likely a move inside the Thorney group, which needs no notice, but its current holding isn't known.

Insiders and the company at today's price. The CEO, with 17.65%, last bought on market at 52.5c in June 2025 and has not bought since the price fell below 30c. The other directors' last purchases were in December 2025 or earlier, at 55c to 86c, apart from Cubis's A$4,755. The company announced its buy-back with the shares at A$0.245 and bought none in the first two weeks, while they traded at A$0.195 to A$0.225 [R30, M1]. Board discretion is stated plainly in the announcement [R2], but a buy-back that isn't used at prices below its announcement price says little about the board's view of value.

Industry. I agree with the deep dive: AI captioning keeps getting cheaper, and the deep dive's own evidence is that most SaaS revenue is billed by the minute [R11], which is exactly the revenue cheaper AI pressures. Listed neighbours trade low (ZOO Digital 0.45 times sales, Dubber 0.17 times ARR).

Debt. None, so nothing falls due before the event.

Management's record. The deep dive sets it out fairly. I'd weight it more than the deep dive does: the last first-half result (26 February 2026) withdrew targets reaffirmed five months earlier and the price halved within a week. The new guidance is the company's first EBITDA guidance, and its first test is a seasonally weak half.

## 3. The valuation
Peers. The three ASX subscription peers (Mach7, ReadyTech, Nuix) sell contracted software; AI-Media's "ARR" is mostly usage. The deep dive allows for that with a 40% discount to their median in Method 2 (1.1 times). Dubber, at 0.17 times ARR, is the closer match on AI risk. At 0.8 times, halfway between, Method 2's middle is about 21.7c, not 26.6c.

Normal or peak earnings. Method 1's middle applies 9 times to the FY27 guidance midpoint (A$4.0m), which is nearly twice FY26's actual A$2.1m but in line with FY24 (A$4.1m) and FY25 (A$4.6m). So the guidance is not a peak; it is a return to the old level. The issue is credibility, not peak earnings: a middle that is "guidance delivered and believed" is the good case for a company with this record. Weighting the guidance 60% and FY26's actual 40% (A$3.2m) gives about 19.4c at 9 times.

Share-based pay. I tested whether Method 1 flatters AI-Media by using adjusted EBITDA before the A$2.3m share-based payment charge while peers' EBITDA is after it. It mostly doesn't: the charge is booked at the 68c grant value [R5 note 33], and at today's price the 5.9m remaining units are worth about A$1.2m, which the deep dive's diluted share count already takes off. The risk is a new grant if the price stays low, which is not in any figure.

Net assets. The cash is real; the A$43m of North American goodwill rests on 16% yearly revenue growth [R5 note 15] that the region didn't achieve in FY26, and no buyer pays for goodwill. Net cash after commitments (A$15.3m, about 7c a share) is a floor well below every outcome price.

The DCF. The deep dive is right that the gap between SaaS growth and cost growth decides it, and three-quarters of the value is after FY31. One input can be checked against the trend: SaaS revenue growth half on half, adjusted for the exchange rate (taking about 76% of technology revenue as North American [R5 note 4]), was about 31%, 22% and 14% in the last three halves [M3]. That is a point in the deep dive's favour on the latest half (the reported 8% understates it), but the rate is falling by about a third each half. If it keeps falling at that pace, FY27 SaaS growth is about 15% and FY28 single digits, which sits between the DCF's slow path (12.5c) and sceptical path (24.8c), about 18c to 19c. The guidance path (45.7c) needs growth to hold at 15% or more for two more years.

My read: the middle is about 19c to 23c, close to today's price, with a range of about 13c to 31c. The gap the case needs only opens if the guidance is delivered and the market believes it.

ARR at December. ARR is the company's chosen headline measure and the base of its guidance, and the result will report it at 31 December. The deep dive's own table shows company ARR against each half's SaaS revenue doubled: 0.82 (December 2024), 0.87 (June 2025), 0.92 (December 2025), 1.02 (June 2026). June 2026 is the highest ever, and June 2026 included 11 to 30 June of the FIFA World Cup, held across the United States, Canada and Mexico from 11 June to 19 July [W1], in the region that gives AI-Media 69% of its revenue and where live television must be captioned. Whether that lifted June depends on how ARR is calculated and whether the World Cup broadcasters used LEXI or iCap, neither of which I could verify. But on 1H27 SaaS of A$19.0m (the deep dive's middle), December ARR at the December 2025 ratio would be about A$35m and at the June 2026 ratio about A$39m. The deep dive's middling band (A$39m to A$41m) needs the measure at or above its highest level relative to revenue. A December ARR of A$36m to A$38m, flat on June, would very likely be read as bad, whatever SaaS revenue and EBITDA show, yet it falls in none of the deep dive's three buckets. For scoring and for the exit rule, add "ARR at 31 December below A$38m" to the bad outcome.

The buy-back before the event. The window runs from 26 October to 31 December [R1, R14], and it reopens the business day after the result, inside the resolve week. Nothing bought in the first two weeks. I'd give it no weight until a buy-back notice appears.

The exit rule. It fires at the first session after the result, which is after the gap. On the one weak first half in the record the price fell 38% on the day. The rule limits holding on after bad news; it can't limit the loss from the news itself. I'd add the ARR trigger above and a check of the ASX list for a Jencay ceasing notice before any order.

Liquidity. At A$2,016 the position is inside the rulebook limit (two sessions at 10% of the A$23,100 median is A$4,620). On a thin day (A$2,045 traded on 16 October) the 10% fill cap would spread a sale over several sessions; after a bad result volume is high (6.5m shares on 26 February 2026), so the position can be sold, but at the gapped price.

Costs. The deep dive allows 1.5% in all for the spread. At A$0.195 one price step is A$0.005, 2.6% of the price, so the spread can't be less than one step: at least 2.6% for a round trip, and the 6 November quote was three steps wide. The simulation's own spread estimate (rulebook section 7) may charge less, but the true cost is about 5.5% (2.6% spread, 1.7% size, up to 1.5% brokerage and 1.0% currency) rather than 4.5%. It doesn't change the verdict.

## 6. Special situations only
Doesn't apply: this is an ordinary pick. The "or an approach" in the good case is the only special-situation element, and I've given it about 3%. The CEO's 17.65% means a bid without his support is unlikely to succeed.

## The three points most likely to make the pick wrong
1. The middling and good prices are above anything the stock's results have delivered. Eight of its twelve result weeks moved less than 9%, and the best moved +30%. The case's expected gain sits mostly in a +18% re-rating on an in-line half and a +69% good case.
2. December ARR is likely to look flat or soft against a June 2026 figure that was the highest ever relative to revenue and may have been lifted by the World Cup. That would be read against a record of a redefined measure and withdrawn targets, and the deep dive's outcome table doesn't count it as bad.
3. Nobody with inside knowledge is buying at this price. The company hasn't used its buy-back, the CEO hasn't bought since 52.5c, and the only director purchase is A$4,755, while the funds that replaced Regal and WAM have sold this stock into rallies before and the market faded both the FY26 result and the guidance.

## What I couldn't verify
- How ARR is calculated (last month times twelve or otherwise), whether usage is in it at the month's actual level, and whether the World Cup broadcasters used LEXI or iCap. No document says.
- What Salter paid for the shares in its March 2026 notice: the consideration it states (A$185,727.83 for 12.1m shares) can't be right, as the deep dive also found.
- Thorney's current holding: the nominee line moved, no notice has been lodged.
- Who sold the 451,853 shares on 6 November. I didn't check ASIC short position data or broker coverage.
- Trading and cash since June 2026: no quarterly reports are published, and the AGM gave no first-quarter figures.
- I didn't re-open the international peer pages (stockanalysis.com) or the peers' ASX filings; I relied on the deep dive for peer multiples.
- I re-opened the Salter, Jencay, TIGA, EEG vendor, Cubis and July 2026 Abrahams notices but not the Regal, WAM, Weir notices or the other director notices; for those I checked only the announcement titles and dates on the ASX lists.
