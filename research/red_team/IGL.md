### Are the bad case's dividends real?

The deep dive's bad case: guidance missed, catalogue decline speeding up, FY28 EBITDA after rent about A$106m, rent fixed to 2041, net debt about A$180m, and about 25c of dividends over the three payments.

What the board has said and done:
- Payout policy. At the 2025 AGM the chairman said the 18.0c for FY25 was a 53.4% payout, "below the Group's historical 65%-75% dividend payout range, with the incremental funds retained used to fund the on-market share buyback and pay down senior debt", and that the board "intends returning to a dividend payout ratio based on 55%-65% of underlying earnings" [S13]. The FY26 release confirms 55% to 65% of underlying pre-AASB 16 earnings "for the 2027 financial year" [S2]. The presentation calls 55.3% "rebased from >70% in FY22 and prior to retain capital for growth" [S3, page 14]. There is no stated policy for FY28, which is where the third dividend (the FY28 interim) falls.
- Record under stress. In March 2020 IVE cancelled an interim dividend it had already declared (8.6c) and suspended the FY20 final, "to continue strengthening the balance sheet" [S15]. Dividends came back at 14c in FY21 [S16].
- The habit in normal years has been to hold the dividend flat (18.0c in FY23, FY24 and FY25) and let the payout ratio fall as profit rose [S17, S13].

What the bad case does to each dividend (underlying pre-AASB 16 profit, about 152.5m shares):

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bad case, 55% payout (bottom of the FY27 range) | A$27.0m: 9.7c | A$21.0m: 7.6c | A$25.0m: 9.0c | 26.3c |
| Bad case, board pays 50% | 8.9c | 6.9c | 8.2c | 23.9c |
| Deeper miss, 50% | A$25.5m: 8.4c | A$19.5m: 6.4c | A$23.5m: 7.7c | 22.5c |

The bad-case profits are my reading of the deep dive's bad case: FY27 about A$48m against the guided A$52.5m, FY28 lower again. The 1H26 interim of 9.5c was 51% of that half's profit; the FY26 final of 9.0c was about 58% of 2H26 [S6, S2].

The pressures in the bad case, one by one:
- Bank covenants. The facility has "leverage and fixed charges ratios", tested next at 31 December 2026 and 30 June 2027 [S1, note 18]. The levels aren't disclosed. At about 1.7 times net debt to EBITDA before AASB 16 in the bad case, a typical leverage covenant (2.5 to 3.0 times) wouldn't bind, and fixed-charge cover (EBITDA plus rent over interest plus rent) would be roughly 2.7 times. I don't expect the covenants to force a cut, but I can't confirm it.
- IVE's own gearing benchmark. IVE calls 1.5 times its "agreed internal benchmark" [S1, page 19] and guided below 1.5 times at June 2027 "subject to any strategic initiatives" [S2]. Motio takes the pro forma figure to 1.67 times [S7]. In the bad case it is about 1.7 times at June 2027 and June 2028. A board that cut its payout range "to retain capital for growth" and is above its own benchmark is more likely to sit at 55% than at 65%, and could go below 55%.
- Refinancing. The A$330m facility matures on 14 May 2029 [S1]. Unless it is extended before 30 June 2028, it shows as a current liability in the FY28 accounts, released about the resolve date. In the bad case the banks set new terms on a shrinking printer, inside the window.
- Motio. About A$20.9m in cash at most, from the facility [S7]; already in the A$180m to A$192m net debt. Daily Press adds up to A$10m of earn-outs over calendar 2026 and 2027 [S3, page 21].
- The buy-back. It ends on 10 March 2027 [S10]. It would be dropped before the dividend, so it protects the dividend a little.

My view: the deep dive's 25c is not inflated against the policy; at the 55% floor the bad case gives about 26c. But the floor is only promised for FY27, and in the bad case every pressure points the board to the bottom of the range or below it. I use 24c (range 19c to 26c). Reworked with 24c, the deep dive's own bad leg is A$1.87 + 24c = A$2.11, -7.9%, still a pass; it would need 4.33 times on A$106m to stay inside 10%. So the dividend alone doesn't flip the verdict. The multiple does (section 3).

### How I think the desk should read the rule

My view: count dividends, because the ledger credits them to the holder (rulebook section 7), src/decide.py scores a card on its outcome values without adding dividends separately, and the success tests measure returns with dividends; leaving them out would make the test disagree with how the pick is scored and bias the sleeve against high-yield companies. But count only dividends that go ex before the resolve date, at the amount each outcome's own earnings and balance sheet would support (in the bad case, the bottom of the payout range or lower where gearing or refinancing points that way), and apply the same reading to every value pick, including the earlier ones. That's Cam's call, and the rulebook should say which reading applies so the desk stops deciding it case by case.

## 2. Is it really overlooked, or is it cheap for a reason?

Both, and more of the second than the deep dive allows.

- Overlooked: a A$351m company, outside the S&P/ASX 300 (data/norgate/index_members.csv shows it not a member at 30 October 2026), with 28.6% held by one family and thin coverage. That part holds.
- Who is selling: the price is down 25% since the FY25 result and 14% since the FY26 result (A$2.67 to A$2.29) with no company news except the Motio deal. The 15 October block is the clearest sign of an informed seller. The managing director holds 250,934 shares (about A$575,000) after selling in 2025 [S18]; insiders aren't buying.
- Industry: Quad fell about 18% the day after its third-quarter result (the deep dive's secondary source; I didn't check the price). Quad's sales are falling only 1% to 3% a year [P1]; IVE's fell 5.2% without acquisitions in FY26 and 7.2% in 1H26 [S2, S6]. In IVE's favour, the 2H26 decline was only about 3% (2H26 underlying revenue A$460.9m less about A$27.3m from acquisitions, against A$447.0m in 2H25; my sums from S3 and S6).
- Margins at a record: material margin 51.4% against 49.3% in FY25 and 48.5% in 1H25; EBITDA margin before AASB 16 12.0% against 11.5% [S3, S6]. Profit has held because margins rose while revenue fell. The multiples are being applied to peak-margin earnings.
- Debt: nothing falls due before the resolve date, but the facility (14 May 2029) has to be extended or refinanced in FY28, inside the window.
- Guidance record: FY24 met, FY25 beaten, FY26 cut to "the bottom end" at the 2025 AGM and then met [S14, S2]. Fair, not strong. The 25 November AGM is the first test of FY27.

## 3. The valuation

### The floor multiple is a trailing figure

The deep dive measures IVE's multiple on the last reported EBITDA. From FY22 to FY26 that EBITDA rose from A$75.1m to A$112.6m, about 10% a year, mostly through acquisitions. When earnings are rising, a multiple on last year's earnings is higher than the multiple the market is paying on the year it is pricing. Today FY27 is guided flat, so the two are the same, and comparing today's 4.7 times with a trailing history overstates how cheap IVE is.

The same picture: IVE's ordinary multiple on the year ahead is about 4.5 to 4.7 times, the FY26 result day was its high, and today's 4.7 times is at its median.

What this does to the outcomes:
- Middling (guidance holds, FY28 EBITDA about A$114m, a flat year ahead): the deep dive's 5.0 times needs a re-rating to above IVE's forward median, for a business whose core still shrinks. I use 4.7 times (today's multiple, held). With net debt of A$176m (some acquisitions to hold EBITDA flat: the deep dive's middling says acquisitions offset the decline but carries only A$172m) and 29c of dividends: A$2.35, +2.5% on price; A$2.64, +15.2% with dividends. At 4.6 times it is +11.9% with dividends and fails.
- Bad (guidance missed, decline speeding up): by August 2028 the market is pricing FY29, likely lower again (about A$102m). A bad case deserves a below-median multiple: between IVE's forward 10th and 25th percentiles, and level with print peers. I use 4.3 times forward on A$102m (about 4.2 times trailing on A$105m), net debt A$182m, 24c of dividends: A$1.67, -26.9% on price; A$1.91, -16.4% with dividends.

### Peers

Quad (4.2 times) and Transcontinental (about 4.0 times) are fair comparisons for the print core. Quad's sales are falling more slowly than IVE's. The premium IVE gets over them rests on 3PL, packaging and creative growing while catalogues shrink. In the bad case (catalogue decline speeding up) that premium is the first thing to go. oOh!media is an outdoor advertiser and its pre-bid multiple tells us about Motio's price, not IVE's.

### The new leases

The two new sites moved lease liabilities from A$112.5m to A$283.5m and fixed them to 2041 [S1, S3]. The pre-AASB 16 measure deducts rent, so it shows the higher rent, but not that a shrinking business has given up the option to leave its sites for 15 years. On enterprise value including leases over post-AASB 16 EBITDA, IVE was at 4.8 times on the FY25 result day and is at 5.6 times today. Investors who screen on that basis don't see a cheap stock.

### Cash

The deep dive's cash method gives A$2.50 on FY27 guidance, A$2.77 with normal capital spending and A$2.18 in a bad year, at a 12% free cash yield. IVE traded at yields of 11.7% and 15.6% on its last two clean result days; at 13.5%, the same three cases give A$2.22, A$2.46 and A$1.93. And the deep dive's own bear reading (about A$15m a year of acquisitions just to stand still) gives A$1.70. On cash, A$2.29 looks like a fair price, with the dividend as the return.

### The input that moves the answer most

The exit multiple. Each 0.1 times on A$106m to A$114m of EBITDA is about A$11m of enterprise value, 7c a share, 3 points of return. EBITDA comes second: each A$3m is about 4 points at 4.4 times.

## 4. The dated events and the exit

- AGM, 25 November 2026: today's price already assumes FY27 is delivered (4.7 times guided EBITDA). A clean reaffirmation is unlikely to lift it much; the last four events fell on the day. A cut would hurt. The event is lopsided against a buyer before it.
- 1H27 result, about 25 February 2027: the real test, and the deep dive's conditions (a) to (g) are the right ones. If they're all met, I'd raise the bad case to about A$2.06 with dividends (FY28 about A$108m, FY29 about A$105m, 4.35 times forward, net debt A$178m) and lower its chance to 25%, and the case would qualify, with dividends counted, at about A$2.28 or below (the bad leg binds; the middling needs A$2.29).
- Liquidity: a position of about A$2,016 is 0.6% of a median day (A$359,022) and 1.7% of the thinnest day in the last 20 (A$120,124). It can be sold in one session in a bad week.
- Exit rule: the deep dive's rule is sound. I'd add: sell if the bank facility hasn't been extended or refinanced by the 1H28 result (about February 2028); sell on any equity raise; and cut the net debt trigger from 2.0 times to 1.8 times, since IVE's own benchmark is 1.5 times and 2.0 times is well past the point where the dividend is at risk.

## The three points most likely to make the pick wrong

1. The multiple. Today's 4.7 times is IVE's ordinary multiple on the year ahead, not its floor. The deep dive's bad case needs at least 4.33 times on A$106m, which on a lower FY29 is close to IVE's forward median, in a scenario where guidance has been missed; its middling needs a re-rating to 5.0 times for a business whose core still shrinks.
2. A shrinking core at record margins with rent fixed to 2041. Revenue without acquisitions fell 7.2% in 1H26 and 5.2% in FY26. Profit held on margin gains that can't repeat forever, and the new leases (A$384.6m of payments to 2041) take away the option to shrink the footprint with the volumes.
3. Where the cash goes. The 2030 target (A$1.2bn to A$1.3bn of revenue) and the bigger facility point to more debt-funded acquisitions, with gearing already above IVE's 1.5 times benchmark after Motio and the facility to refinance before May 2029. The dividend carry, which is the case's engine, depends on a board that has cut its payout range once and cancelled a declared dividend before.

## Verdict

Prices only: don't buy. It fails both legs at A$2.29 on the deep dive's own numbers and on mine; on mine it would need about A$1.86, 19% lower. A fall that large would probably come with news that changes the numbers, so it isn't a useful watch price.

Dividends counted: don't buy at A$2.29; wait for a named event. The deep dive's pass rests on a trailing multiple that overstates how cheap IVE is. On a like-for-like basis the bad case is about 16% below the price, the middling sits on the 15% line, and the expected gain after costs (+10.8%) is below the 12.9% minimum. Two things would change that:
- the price falling to about A$2.12 or below with FY27 guidance intact after the 25 November AGM (a watch trigger, then a fresh test); or
- the 1H27 result, about 25 February 2027, meeting all of the deep dive's conditions (a) to (g), after which it would qualify at about A$2.28 or below (re-test at that day's price, and before the interim dividend goes ex).

If either happens and a re-test passes, the card I'd want: resolve by 31 August 2028; outcomes bad 30% at A$1.91, middling 50% at A$2.64, good 20% at A$3.48 (with dividends, or 25%/55%/20% with the bad case at about A$2.06 after a clean 1H27); the deep dive's exit rule plus the three additions in section 4; and the 2.5% cap, which binds.

Which reading of the dividends question applies is Cam's call. Under the deep dive's numbers the answer turns on it; under mine it doesn't, because the case fails both ways at today's price.
