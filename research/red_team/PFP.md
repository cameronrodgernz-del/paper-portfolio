# Propel Funeral Partners Limited (ASX:PFP): red team

Written 2026-11-01 by the red team, working only from the deep dive (research/picks/PFP.md), the red team checklist, the primary sources and the raw Norgate price file. Price: A$2.66 (close on 30 October 2026). This is a simulation and not financial advice.

In short: most of the numbers in the deep dive are right, but three things it leans on are weaker than it says. The second half of FY26 was clearly worse than the first and points to Propel losing share while deaths rose. The valuation multiple mixes two lease bases and so looks cheaper than it is. And the AGM update compares against a record quarter that July trading has already fallen short of.

What is wrong or overstated

1. The EBITDA multiple mixes two bases. Propel's operating EBITDA of A$55.3m already excludes rent: the presentation says it "includes $5.8m relating to AASB 16" [R1], and the company's own debt ratio uses operating EBITDA "calculated on a pre AASB16 basis" [R1]. So enterprise value without leases (A$518.2m) should be set against EBITDA after rent (about A$49.5m), which gives 10.5 times, not 9.4. With leases in the enterprise value it is 9.8 times. The InvoCare 18 times the deep dive cites was also measured before lease accounting [P8], so 10.5 is the figure to compare with it. This feeds into Method 1 (see section 3).
2. Debt is higher against earnings than "about 2.2 times" suggests. The company's 2.2 times leaves out its A$40m working capital facility and adds a full year of earnings from businesses bought during the year [R1, footnote 5]. Counting all A$151.2m of net debt against EBITDA after rent of about A$49.5m gives about 3.1 times. That matters for the exit rule, which uses "net leverage above 3.0 times" without saying which measure: on the plain measure it is already at about 3 times.
3. "No debt falls due before the dated events" isn't what the balance sheet shows. A$39.7m of borrowings were current at 30 June 2026, up from A$24.7m a year earlier [P2]. It looks like the working capital line that gets rolled over each year, and with about A$169m of undrawn capacity [P2] the risk is low. But it is debt due within 12 months, not none.
4. Small arithmetic point: "borrowings A$162.8m less cash A$12.5m" comes to A$150.3m, not A$151.2m. Net debt is drawn senior debt of A$163.7m less cash [R1]; A$162.8m is the balance sheet figure after capitalised borrowing costs [P2].
5. "Index selling, not news, pushed the last leg down" is only partly right. Index selling explains the drop from A$3.19 on 4 September to A$2.87 on 18 September. But the price bounced to A$3.02 by 22 September and then fell to A$2.55 on 15 October (684,357 shares, with no announcement), and at A$2.66 it now sits below the rebalance-day close. Most of the year's 46% fall came on company news: down 9.6% on the 1H FY26 result (24 February), and from A$3.54 to A$3.00 in the three sessions after the 4 June guidance (Norgate).
6. "A management change under way": the co-CEOs and the new CFO started on 1 September 2025 [P2]. That is a year ago. The 2026 board changes are routine renewal [P5, P4].
7. Book value is mostly goodwill. Of the A$337.5m of equity, A$199.8m is goodwill [P2], so tangible net assets are about A$1.00 a share. The price is about 2.7 times tangible book, not 1.09 times "book". Equity also fell A$17.6m in FY26, almost all of it a A$17.4m currency translation loss on the New Zealand business [P2].
8. Substantial holders, which the deep dive couldn't open: Soul Pattinson (SOL) became a substantial holder on 14 May 2026 with 6,944,482 shares (5.03%). It bought 1,567,124 of them between 10 March and 14 May at A$3.50 to A$4.31 [R6], and has filed no change since [P5], so it hasn't bought or sold another 1%. Norges Bank went above and below 5% more than once between August and September, mostly through securities-lending collateral [R7, R8]. Citigroup was over 5% only on 24 to 25 September, through stock lending [R9]. Eleven other 2026 notices I couldn't open, so I can't confirm whether the founder, Albin Kurti, still holds the 7.4% he had at 31 August 2025.

Anything left out

- The second half of FY26 was worse than the first, and worse than deaths. 1H FY26 comparable volumes were flat and EBITDA rose 1.3% to A$30.3m [R2]. The full year had comparable volumes down about 2% [P1], which puts the second half at roughly minus 4%. Second-half EBITDA was about A$25.0m against A$26.3m a year earlier (down about 5%; derived from [R2], [P1] and the 1H FY25 figure of A$29.9m). Over the same months the ABS counted Australian deaths up 1.0% (January to May) [P7], and New Zealand registered 1.9% more deaths in the year to June 2026 than a year earlier (Stats NZ, [R10]). A gap of three to five points between Propel's comparable funerals and the death count suggests Propel is losing share, or that its regional catchments are doing worse than the country. The deep dive treats weak volumes as an industry dip. The numbers point at something specific to Propel as well.
- Comparable volumes have now fallen in two of the last three halves and were flat in the other. H2 FY25 fell about 3% [R2, R3] and FY25 as a whole fell 1% [R4]. In February 2025 management said organic volumes had "largely rebased" after the pandemic (FNArena write-up of the 1H FY25 call, [R11]).
- July 2026 revenue was flat in Australian dollars. July 2025 revenue "exceeded $21.5 million" [R4], and July 2026 was "about $21.5 million" [P1], even though comparable price per funeral rose more than 3% and five New Zealand businesses had been added. Even putting back the A$0.6m currency drag, that leaves little room for volume growth.
- Recent acquisitions "generate below network Average Revenue Per Funeral" [R2], so buying more small New Zealand businesses lowers the average.
- The dividend is being held up, not grown. FY24, FY25 and FY26 all paid 14.4c. The final was trimmed from 7.0c to 6.9c, and the payout rose from 93% to 97% of distributable earnings [R4, R1]. Owner cash of about A$23.5m covered the A$20.0m dividend, but growth capex (A$8.6m), businesses bought (A$7.6m) and property bought were paid for with debt, which rose A$19m [P2].
- No dividend falls inside the holding window. The interim has gone ex on 4 March 2024, 3 March 2025 and 3 March 2026 (InvestSMART dividend history, [R12]), which is after the 1 March 2027 resolve date. The 5.4% yield doesn't help this card.
- The same weakness shows up at the big US peer. Service Corporation International, the largest US operator, cut its 2026 outlook in October because of "lower funeral volumes than we originally anticipated"; its comparable funerals were flat in the September quarter [R13]. Soft deaths after the pandemic are not just an Australian winter story.
- Nothing found on a capital raising, a lawsuit, a regulator action or related-party dealings. The escrow releases in July and August are small (for example 57,654 shares on 10 August [R14]). Short interest is about 1% [R15]. I couldn't read the audit report itself (the auditor is Nexia Sydney [P2]), so I can't rule out an emphasis of matter.

## 2. Is it really overlooked, or is it cheap for a reason?

Mostly cheap for a reason, with a short technical sell-off on top that has now passed.

- Who's selling: the heavy days came on results and guidance (February and June), well before the index exit. 22 to 24 June saw 4.1 million shares trade, away from the index rebalance date and with no announcement, which looks like a large holder leaving (Norgate). Since the index exit, the median day is about 148,000 shares, much the same as before, and the price has still drifted down. Ordinary holders who know the company are still selling. Soul Pattinson, the one patient buyer on record, paid A$3.50 to A$4.31 and hasn't added below A$3.
- Industry trouble: falling death rates at each age [P7], the mildest flu season in five years [P1], and the biggest US operator lowering its volume outlook [R13].
- Debt: there's plenty of headroom against the covenant, but on the plain measure debt is about three times earnings after rent, and it is rising because the dividend takes most of the free cash.
- Management's record: it hits guidance it gives in June with two months of the year left [P3], but its broader story has kept slipping. The 2025 AGM called Q1 FY26 "a record quarter" and "a positive start to FY26" [R3], and the year still ended with EBITDA down 1.6%. Brokers in early 2025 expected FY26 EPS growth of around 15% (FNArena, [R11]); it fell 4%.

## 3. The valuation

Method 1, enterprise value to EBITDA, on a consistent basis (EBITDA after rent about A$49.5m, net debt A$151.2m):

| Multiple | Enterprise value | Equity | Per share | Deep dive's figure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 times | A$495.0m | A$343.8m | A$2.49 | A$2.91 |
| 11 times | A$544.5m | A$393.3m | A$2.85 | A$3.31 |
| 12 times | A$594.0m | A$442.8m | A$3.21 | A$3.71 |

The price is 10.5 times now. Propel's own history at 14 to 17 times was set when brokers expected growth, and InvoCare's 18 times was a takeover price for a business four times the size. Neither is a fair guide for a company with two years of falling comparable volumes.

Method 2, cash to owners: the arithmetic is right (A$23.5m of owner cash). The input that moves it most is long-run growth, as the deep dive says, and 3% is generous. Organic revenue growth was about nil in FY26 (comparable volumes down about 2%, price up about 2% [P1]). At 9% and 2% growth the value is A$342m, or A$2.48 a share; at 9% and 1.5% it is A$318m, or A$2.31. 3% only holds if comparable volumes return to growth.

Net assets: property at A$252m is book cost for specialised buildings. The realistic buyer is a sale-and-leaseback investor, and every dollar raised that way adds rent and lowers EBITDA. So the property backs the debt, but it isn't extra value on top of the earnings value. Tangible net assets are about A$1.00 a share.

My range is A$2.30 to A$3.20, central about A$2.75, roughly 3% above the price. That is inside the error of the inputs and below the cost of a round trip plus the risk of a bad AGM.

## 4. The dated events and the exit

- The AGM on 9 November will compare Q1 FY27 (July to September) with Q1 FY26, which was revenue of A$63.5m, EBITDA of A$17.0m and "a record quarter" [R3]. July 2026 is already flat in Australian dollars, industry deaths contracted in July [P1], and the currency drag goes on. A down quarter is more likely than not. Last year's AGM update moved the price 1.3% on the day (Norgate), so a "steady" update may not move it much either.
- Can it show the market is wrong? Only partly. A flat Q1 with flat comparable volumes would ease the fear, but one quarter can't settle whether Propel is losing share. The ABS release on 27 November (deaths to August) is the first check of Q1 volumes against the industry, and the H1 result in late February is the real test.
- The exit rule needs tightening before any card is written. "The H1 run rate points below A$53m" doesn't work for a seasonal business: H1 FY26 was A$30.3m and H2 A$25.0m, so doubling H1 overstates the year by about A$5m. Use company guidance, or H1 FY27 EBITDA against H1 FY26's A$30.3m. If Q1 FY27 comes in at about A$16.3m and is scaled by Q1's 31% share of FY26, it already points to A$53m, so the rule could fire straight after the AGM. The 3.0 times debt test should name the company's covenant measure, because the plain measure is already at about 3 times.
- Liquidity is not a problem at this size: about A$380,000 trades on a median day and the card is about A$1,600.

## 5. The stated chances

| Outcome | Deep dive | Red team | Price (deep dive's) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bad | 35% | 50% | A$2.25 |
| Middling | 45% | 38% | A$2.80 |
| Good | 20% | 12% | A$3.35 |
| Expected price | A$2.72 (+2.2%) | A$2.59 (minus 2.6%) | |

Where I differ and why:
- Bad is more likely, at 50%. The bad case as written ("FY27 EBITDA tracking below A$55.3m, comparable volumes down again") is what the evidence already points to. Q1 is up against a record quarter, July revenue was flat, comparable volumes have fallen in two of the last four halves with the latest about minus 4%, the currency drag continues and staff costs are a third of revenue. Even if the bad price is softened to A$2.35 because a lot is already in the price, the expected return is about minus 0.7%, still below costs.
- Good is less likely, at 12%. EBITDA tracking above A$58m means 5% growth within one half after a 5% fall in the last one. That would need a sharp turn in deaths through spring or a large acquisition. A takeover bid is a real outside chance (a private buyer paid 18 times for InvoCare, and Soul Pattinson holds 5%), but there is no sign of one, and it is part of this 12%.
- Middling at 38% covers a flat-to-slightly-down Q1 that the market has already priced.

## Three points most likely to make the pick wrong

1. Propel looks to be losing share, not just riding out a quiet winter. Comparable funerals fell about 4% in the second half of FY26 while deaths in Australia and New Zealand rose 1% to 2%. Two months won't fix that, and the deep dive's recovery case depends on it being temporary.
2. The AGM update is up against a record Q1 (A$17.0m EBITDA), and July revenue was already flat. The first dated event is more likely to confirm the bad case than to show the market is wrong, and the exit rule as written could fire straight after it.
3. It's less cheap than shown. On a consistent lease basis it trades at 10.5 times EBITDA, not 9.4. Debt is about three times earnings after rent, not 2.2. Tangible assets are about A$1.00 a share. And the dividend, the main support for holders, falls outside the window and is now about 97% of earnings.

## Verdict

Wait for a named event: the AGM trading update on 9 November 2026. On my chances the expected return before costs is slightly negative, and the AGM is eight days away and speaks directly to the bad case, so there is no reason to buy ahead of it. Reopen the pick only if Q1 FY27 operating EBITDA is at or above A$17.0m and comparable volumes are no worse than flat. If Q1 EBITDA comes in below about A$16.5m, or comparable volumes fall again, treat it as don't buy.
