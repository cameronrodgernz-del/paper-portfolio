# Trajan Group Holdings (ASX:TRJ): red team review

Reviewed 2026-11-08 by the red team, a separate agent that read the deep dive (research/picks/TRJ.md), docs/RED_TEAM.md and RULEBOOK.md sections 3, 4 and 5a, and no other research file, before writing this. Price: A$0.255, the last trade on 6 November 2026 (bid A$0.255, ask A$0.26, previous close A$0.245; data/research_data/asx_company.json). This is a simulation and not financial advice.

Why this review: the deep dive said "wait", partly against an informal bar of about 9% for a six-month hold that the rulebook never adopted. The rule adopted on 8 November 2026 asks instead for an expected gain after costs of at least the cash rate (2.13%) plus 5% a year over the days to the resolve date: 7.13% x 142 / 365 = 2.77% from 9 November 2026 (the first session after a decision tonight) to 31 March 2027, or 2.79% counted from today. The deep dive's own numbers give +3.6%, which clears that by 0.9 points, so the case is tested here as an ordinary research pick with two dated events: the half-year trading update (about 5 February 2027) and the first-half result (about 26 February 2027).

## In short
- Value pick: no. The bad case has to be no more than 10% below the price (A$0.2295 or higher). The deep dive's A$0.14 is 45% below and mine (A$0.16) is 37% below. My middling price (+12%) also misses the 15% the rule asks for.
- The minimum: the deep dive's +3.6% clears 2.77% by 0.9 points. Any one of these changes erases that margin: the middling price half a cent lower (A$0.305), the bad price 0.6c lower, the good price 1.1c lower, or 1.3 points of chance moved from good to middling or from middling to bad.
- My numbers: bad 35% at A$0.16, middling 50% at A$0.285, good 15% at A$0.40. Expected price A$0.2585, +1.4% before costs and about -6.6% after a 7.9% round trip. That fails the minimum by about 9 points.
- I differ more on the prices than on the chances. The middling A$0.31 needs the market to re-rate the shares by March on a half-year that only confirms the bottom of guidance. That hasn't happened after recent confirmations here, and the deep dive itself says belief probably waits for the August 2027 result. The deep dive's own cash-flow method puts the middling case at A$0.286.
- New facts against the case: Investors Mutual didn't sell out (it fell below 5% with about 7.4m shares left); Trajan's one customer over 10% of revenue bought 35% less in H2 FY26 than a year earlier; and trailing nEBITDA at today's exchange rates is about A$14.9m, below the A$16.0m bottom of guidance. One point goes the other way: on the arithmetic, the October revenue range doesn't show the revenue guidance being trimmed.
- Verdict: wait for the first-half result (about 26 February 2027). Don't buy at A$0.255.

The revenue "trim". The deep dive reads the change from "mid-single digit organic" growth (27 August) to about 2% (28 October) as a cut, and rules out currency because the Australian dollar was about US$0.70 in both months. That misses the base. "Organic" growth at today's rates is normally measured against FY26 translated at today's rates, whereas October's 2% is measured against FY26 as reported, and half of FY26 was earned at about US$0.655. On the company's own figure (the stronger dollar took A$5.0m off H2 revenue of A$82.3m at H1 rates), H1 FY26's A$84.1m would be about A$79.0m at today's rates, so FY26 restated is about A$156.3m. Against that base, A$162m to A$166m is growth of 3.7% to 6.2% (4.9% at the midpoint), which is mid-single digit. The AGM's own explanation names both the exchange rate and Capital Equipment. The two statements can be read either way; I don't count it as a trim. Meanwhile the nEBITDA guidance firmed, from "double-digit growth" (A$14.5m or more) to A$16.0m to A$17.5m.

What the deep dive left out:
- The register overhang. Three institutions, MA Financial (16.05m), AustralianSuper (9.96m, no notice since May 2023) and Investors Mutual (about 7.44m when last disclosed), hold about 33.4m shares. That's 21.9% of the company and 44% of the shares the founder doesn't own: more than 500 days of median trading. MA bought at A$0.70 to A$0.95, and IML sold about 3.8m shares between its notice before June's and 22 July. Any of them selling into a good result would cap the rise. The 2.16m-share session on 11 September 2026, at A$0.145 to A$0.16, came after IML stopped having to report; who sold isn't known.
- The largest customer. One "analytical instrumentation manufacturing company" bought A$34.2m in FY25 and A$28.2m in FY26 (FY26 annual report, note on major customers), about 17% of revenue. It bought A$16.4m in H1 FY26 (H1 FY25: A$16.0m; interim report note 2), so H2 FY26 was A$11.8m against A$18.2m a year earlier: down 35%, of which currency explains perhaps 6 points. The filings I read neither name the customer nor explain the fall. Capital Equipment fell from A$28.7m in H2 FY25 to A$23.9m in H2 FY26, which fits a customer buying systems.
- "Guidance set with Q1 in hand" was also true last year. At the 28 October 2025 AGM, the A$16m to A$19m guidance was "maintained" when Q1 FY26 nEBITDA had been about A$0.5m. That figure came out only in the 5 February 2026 trading update (03057100).
- Nothing else turned up: no capital raising since 2022, no lawsuit or regulator action in the 2026 list, and no director selling. Directors' 2026 dealings were option exercises (Lewin, Lyon), plus Lewin's 17,721 shares bought at A$0.40 (Appendix 3Y, 2 Apr 2026, 03079131: held). A Tomisich family company leased Trajan the Ringwood site until it was sold to a third party in May 2024; FY26 related-party costs were A$152,000, the wages of a director's family member.

## 2. Is it really overlooked, or is it cheap for a reason?
Mostly cheap for reasons. The neglect is real (no broker research, out of the All Ordinaries, a free float under A$15m), but it sits on top of these:
- Record. Three misses in a row. FY24's guidance was cut at the half (27 Feb 2024) and again in May. In FY25 and FY26 it was "maintained" at or after the half: on 27 Feb 2025 after a good half, and on 28 Oct 2025, 5 Feb 2026, 26 Feb 2026 and 27 Mar 2026 ("we remain committed to our projections") after a weak one. Both years still missed, so a "maintained" at the half has told holders little.
- The latest quarter is below the rate guidance needs. Q1 FY27 had every FY26 cost cut in it (employee costs down 8%, gross margin above 42%) and still made about A$3.5m, which is A$14m a year. To reach A$16.0m to A$17.5m, the next three quarters must average A$4.17m to A$4.67m, 19% to 33% above Q1. Q2 FY26 made A$4.5m, but at an Australian dollar about 7% weaker. On the company's own H2 FY26 figure (the stronger dollar cost A$2.4m of nEBITDA a half, about A$1.2m a quarter), Q2 FY26 would be about A$3.3m at today's rates. So trailing nEBITDA at today's rates is about A$14.9m (A$3.3m + A$8.1m + A$3.5m), not A$16.2m. The deep dive's point that "about 85% to 100% of the bridge is cost cuts already made" is true as a sum, but those cuts were already in the quarter that ran below the guided rate. What could still close the gap is seasonality: Q2 FY26 was a record revenue quarter (A$45.4m against A$38.7m in Q1), though Q1 FY26 was unusually weak, and I couldn't find quarterly figures for earlier years.
- Revenue against harder comparisons. Q1 FY27's 3.8% growth at fixed exchange rates came against a soft quarter. Trajan didn't give Q1 revenue in dollars; at today's rates it was probably about A$38m (A$38.7m a year earlier, plus 3.8%, less about 6% for the stronger dollar). If so, guidance needs Q2 to Q4 revenue of A$41.4m to A$42.7m a quarter, about 3% to 7% above FY26's Q2 to Q4 at today's rates (about A$40.0m a quarter), and against a record Q2.
- Cash and debt. FY26 operating cash flow was A$0.24m and debt rose A$5.8m. The bank moved the loans to principal-and-interest at 30 June 2026, after refinancing legal fees in FY25 and FY26. Net debt of A$33.0m is about 2.2 times trailing nEBITDA at today's rates, above the company's own target of "less than 2.0X" (market update, 5 Aug 2025). No loan falls due before the dated events: the term loans sit in the two-to-five-year band, with A$3.0m of principal due within a year.
- Impairment at the half. A market value of A$39m against net assets of A$98.5m is one of the signs the accounting standard lists for testing assets for impairment at a half-year, and the consumables unit has 1.0 point of revenue-growth headroom. An impairment cut equity and broke a covenant in FY24. The 31 December accounts will be the first signed without the departing CFO.
- The sector: small, indebted lab-tools makers trade on low multiples. The nearest peer, Harvard Bioscience, recently refinanced and took a goodwill impairment of its own (per the deep dive; not re-checked).

## 3. The valuation
- Today's multiple. Enterprise value without leases is A$72.0m (A$39.0m of shares plus A$33.0m of net bank debt). That's 6.1 times after-lease EBITDA at the guidance bottom (A$11.75m), but 6.9 times the trailing figure at today's rates (A$14.9m less A$3.25m of lease payments and A$1.2m of recurring "one-offs" = A$10.45m). Against the deep dive's Harvard Bioscience range of 8.2 to 8.9 times, the discount on actual earnings is smaller than it looks. I didn't re-check the peer figures.
- The middling case needs two things by March: earnings at the guidance bottom (not yet shown by any quarter at today's rates) and a re-rating from about 6 to 7 times on those earnings before the second half is known. On trailing earnings at today's rates, the deep dive's Method A at 7 times gives A$0.266.
- The deep dive's three methods give A$0.325 (multiple), A$0.286 (cash flow) and A$0.317 (the market's own multiple) for the middling case, and it uses A$0.31. The cash-flow method, the only one that doesn't lean on a market multiple, gives A$0.286.
- The input that moves the cash-flow method most is the discount rate. The deep dive says a point moves the middling value by about 8c; on its own inputs (A$7.1m of free cash flow growing at 2%, less A$32.5m of net debt) it's about 5c to 6c: A$0.345 at 10.5%, A$0.286 at 11.5% and A$0.239 at 12.5%. Its 11.5% looks reasonable for a small, indebted, founder-run company with three misses behind it, and the answer stays under A$0.31 unless the rate is below about 11%.
- Book value is no floor: A$80.9m of A$98.5m equity is goodwill and acquired intangibles, and tangible assets are about 11.5c a share.

## 4. The dated events and the exit
- Will the events show the market is wrong? Probably not on their own. The deep dive's own judgement is that "full belief probably waits for the FY27 result in August 2027", which is outside the window. The price's behaviour supports that. The AGM's numeric guidance moved it one tick (A$0.25 to A$0.26, back to A$0.25 two days later). The Q1 update came after a run from A$0.235 to A$0.29 and faded to A$0.24 within four sessions. The closest match to the middling case, the H1 FY25 result (27 Feb 2025: A$7.9m, guidance maintained), lifted the price from A$0.83 to A$0.96 in two days and A$0.92 a week later, about +11%, and that year still ended in a miss. The February trading update isn't a fixed habit (none in 2024 or 2025), so the H1 result may be the only event, leaving five weeks to the resolve date.
- Selling in a bad week. Bad news has brought volume here (1.09m shares on 27 Aug 2026, 2.16m on 11 Sep), so a A$2,016 position could be sold, but at the gapped price, which the bad-case price already includes. In a quiet week it can't: five of the last 20 sessions traded under A$2,000, and with fills capped at 10% of a session's volume, a buy or a sale takes two sessions or more on a median day.
- The real round trip. On the rulebook's formula the deep dive's 7.9% is right: brokerage A$15 each way (0.74% each way), half of a one-tick spread each way (0.97%), the size cost capped at 2% each way, and 0.5% to convert currency. The repo's Corwin-Schultz estimate gives a 1.5% spread (7.5% in total), and a buy inside the plan's monthly cover saves 0.74 points. Against that, a tick is 3.1% of A$0.16 and 3.5% of A$0.14, so a bad-case exit pays about 0.8 points more in spread. Weighted by the outcomes, that roughly cancels against the cheaper exits at the higher prices. I use 7.9%. The bigger effect of the thin trading isn't the cost; it's the overhang in section 1.

## 6. The founder (a holder situation, so section 6 of the checklist applies)
- Is the "nothing happens" value right? It's the operating case, as the deep dive says. Without a founder move, the bad and middling prices above are the "nothing happens" range.
- Can he act? He holds 50.36% and already controls the company, so he has no need to creep to protect control. Buying the other 75.9m shares costs about A$22.8m at A$0.30 and A$30.4m at A$0.40, more than his own stake is worth today, with NAB's consent likely needed and his outside wealth undisclosed. A scheme needs 75% of the votes cast, and the founder would vote in a separate class or not at all. At the 2026 AGM, about 35.6m votes outside the founder and executives were cast on the remuneration report. If MA's 16.05m were among them, MA alone would be 45% of that turnout and could block; with AustralianSuper the two would be 73%. MA paid A$0.70 to A$0.95.
- Is he likely to act by 31 March 2027? No. He bought A$65,053 at about A$0.65 in November 2025 and nothing at A$0.145 to A$0.29 since, including the ten weeks after the August result and the days after the AGM. The trading policy also bars him from dealing in blackout periods (its dates are on the company website, which I didn't open), which will cover the run-up to the February result. His only public statement is the March 2026 letter: the value "will be recognized and realized" "over time". I agree with the deep dive's 2% to 3%, inside the good case.
- Comparable cases: the deep dive's NZX majority buyouts (premiums of 21% to 83%) are reasonable as an offer range, but none of them is evidence that this founder will move.
- It isn't a binding deal, so it doesn't belong in the deal sleeve.

## The rules as they stand from 8 November 2026
- Value pick: fails on the bad case. It must be A$0.2295 or higher; the deep dive's is A$0.14 and mine A$0.16. My middling case (+11.8%) would also miss the 15% the rule asks for.
- The minimum gain: 2.77% to 31 March 2027. The deep dive's +3.6% passes on paper, by a margin that a half-cent change in its middling price removes. Mine fails at -6.6%.
- Liquidity limit (section 3): two sessions at 10% of the 20-session median is A$3,227, above the A$2,016 cap position, so the limit is met. If the median falls below about A$10,100 it stops being met; five of the last 20 sessions were under A$2,000.

## Three points most likely to make the pick wrong
1. The middling price. Half the weight of the case sits on a 22% re-rating that only needs the company to confirm the bottom of its guidance by March. This market hasn't paid for confirmations from this management (one tick at the AGM, a fade after the Q1 update, an 11% rise in February 2025 that ended in a miss), and the deep dive itself places belief at the August 2027 result. Half a cent off A$0.31 takes the expected gain below the minimum.
2. The run rate. Q1 FY27, with every cost cut in it, ran at A$14m a year, and trailing nEBITDA at today's exchange rates is about A$14.9m against a guidance bottom of A$16.0m. The largest customer cut its H2 FY26 buying by 35% for reasons the filings don't give. A fourth miss, or a weak half with an impairment test that the 1.0-point headroom can't absorb, takes the price back to the September range or below.
3. Supply on any rise. About 22% of the company sits with three institutions that are deep in loss, one of them a proven seller whose remaining 4.9% no longer has to be disclosed, in a share that trades about A$16,000 a day. A good half could be sold into rather than re-rated, which caps the good and middling prices and makes a sale in a bad week slower.
