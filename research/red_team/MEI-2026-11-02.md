# Red team: MEI deal card of 2 Nov 2026 (Lynas scheme, 0.0207 LYC per MEI)

Simulation only, not financial advice. Reviewed against research/cards/2026-11-02-MEI.json, research/deals/MEI.md, the MEI row of reports/deal_monitor.csv, reports/deal_odds.md and the price files data/prices/ASX/MEI.csv and LYC.csv. Prices are closes to 2 Nov 2026 (LYC A$12.12, MEI A$0.22).

## 1. Is the price really mispriced?

Applies, and this is the weakest part of the card.

**The spread is mostly a Lynas price move, not a widening doubt about the deal.** The daily gap between MEI and 0.0207 x LYC since announcement:

| 2 Nov | 12.12 | 0.2509 | 0.220 | 14.0% | 62% |

For most of October the market implied 75% to 85%, close to the card's own 78%. The jump to 14% came in three sessions (27 to 29 Oct) when LYC rose 16% (A$11.27 to A$13.03) and MEI did not follow: MEI closed at A$0.22 on 27 Oct with LYC at A$11.66 and again at A$0.22 on 2 Nov with LYC at A$12.12. Either MEI is lagging a short Lynas bounce, or the 29 Oct releases (Caldeira licence delayed to early 2027; implementation "by March 2027" now stated as conditional on the Brazilian approval; Lynas calling it "any approval that may become required") added doubt. In both readings, the card's "62% implied" is a three-day reading. If LYC gives back its late-October bounce, the gap closes by the offer value falling, not by MEI rising, and the trade earns nothing.

**No hedge is possible.** With no shorting, holding MEI is holding about 0.78 to 1.0 x LYC. LYC's daily moves over the last 120 sessions give about 45% a year, about 26% (one standard deviation) over four months and more over eight. That is nearly twice the 14% gross spread. LYC is down 42% in six months on cost pressure, a revenue miss and the Meteoric dilution (fool.com.au, 23 Oct 2026), and fell 8.6% on announcement day. The card's "completion price assumes Lynas flat" is not a spread, it is a directional bet on a share in a downtrend. A 15% LYC fall at completion turns A$0.2509 into A$0.213, a loss.

**The market is not obviously wrong.** Professional arbitrageurs can short LYC; a hedged 14% for four to eight months is a large return, which suggests they see real timing and approval risk (a brand new Brazilian council). MEI volume on 2 Nov (6.5m shares) was the lowest since announcement against a 17.7m median since 5 Oct, so the 0.22 close is a thin print. No sign of a large holder exiting, though Kumova (6.7%) has reserved the right to sell.

**Break price.** The card uses the undisturbed A$0.1701 moved by VAS (-0.6%) to A$0.1691. The rulebook says "or lower where the evidence says so". The evidence does: the nearest listed rare earths peer, LYC, is down 12.4% since 30 Sep (A$13.83 to A$12.12); MEI itself had fallen from A$0.195 to A$0.17 in the week before announcement; a CIMCE refusal would mark Caldeira as hard to sell to any foreign buyer; and Meteoric would owe Lynas on the A$35m loan with a large capex bill and no funder, so a raise would follow. The A$8.8m reverse fee is worth only about A$0.003 a share. My break price: about A$0.15.

Chance of failure or loss on this ground: not a completion risk, but I put a 40% chance that the position loses money even with the deal completing on time, from LYC alone.

## 2. Approvals

Applies. Brazil CIMCE is the deciding condition. No ACCC, FIRB, OIO or CADE condition (CADE threshold not met per Meteoric's warranty).

- **Status.** Law 15,506 and Decree 13,118 (16 Sep 2026) create a change of control homologation power with no deadline and no criteria. A decree of 16 Oct (published 19 Oct) created three sub-secretariats under the industry ministry (MDIC) to staff the council, one of them to review requests; the internal rules are still to be proposed by the Executive Committee, with no date (Brasil 61, 27 Oct 2026). The council must be installed by about 15 Dec 2026. The Executive Committee meets monthly and needs a Ministry of Mines and Energy opinion. No Lynas filing has been announced.
- **Refusal.** Less likely than the card implies. Mines and Energy Minister Alexandre Silveira publicly praised the deal as a "grande marco" (eixos.com.br, 1 Oct 2026), and Lula's re-election on 25 Oct (Canadian Mining Journal, 26 Oct) keeps the same ministers in charge. But Lula's agenda is "domestic industrialisation and capturing more value" (S&P Global, 27 Oct). The likelier outcome is approval with conditions on processing in Brazil, and Lynas need not accept a "materially burdensome condition". A A$8.8m reverse fee on a deal of roughly A$730m at today's price makes that a cheap option for Lynas to walk if its own position worsens.
- **Timing.** This is the real risk. Council installed by mid-December, rules after that, a filing, a monthly committee and a ministry opinion: a decision before February 2027 (needed for early-March implementation) looks unlikely. The End Date of 1 Apr 2027 extends by about three months only "by agreement", so either side can decline; Lynas tying a further A$75m of funding to the extension suggests it intends to agree.
- **At the End Date:** if the approval is refused, unobtainable or not obtained, either side can terminate and Lynas pays A$8.8m.

My chances: refusal or a burdensome condition Lynas walks from: 8%. Not obtained by the extended End Date (about 1 Jul 2027) and not extended again: 5%. Chance the approval pushes payment past early March: 75% (card's deal file: 50%).

## 3. The vote (schemes)

Applies. 75% of votes cast and a majority by headcount; retail-heavy register; only 9.3% with stated intentions, and Kumova can sell. The fixed ratio has cut the value from A$0.286 at announcement to A$0.251, but that is still 48% above the A$0.17 undisturbed price, which most holders will take. No expert yet. A further LYC fall before a January (or later) meeting is the main threat. Chance the vote fails: 4%. Chance the expert says not in best interests: 3%.

## 4. The bidder

Applies in part. No financing risk: the consideration is new Lynas shares and an issue under a scheme needs no Lynas holder vote. Lynas is already funding Meteoric (A$35m drawn 6 Oct). The concern is Lynas's own position: its share price is in a six-month fall of about 42%, costs are rising, and its holders sold the deal on announcement. A Lynas MAE is unlikely, but a weaker Lynas makes the burdensome-condition exit more attractive. Chance this ground breaks the deal: 2%, included in the Brazil figure above.

## 5. The target

Applies. The Caldeira installation licence has slipped from late 2026 to early 2027 after FEAM information requests. Meteoric says this does not affect the timetable, but a further slip or a refusal could be argued as a material adverse effect. No dividend, so nothing is lost in the price there. Chance of failure on this ground: 2%.

## 6. Timing

Applies strongly. No date in the deal file is confirmed. The base rate says schemes needing overseas approvals pay in a median 147 days, a quarter taking 163 days or more (to about 13 Mar 2027 here). Those approvals had established procedures; CIMCE has none. No ticking fee. With the 90-day bank bill at 4.80%, cash earns about 1.6% to early March and about 3.3% to late June. At my numbers (78% at A$0.2509, 22% at A$0.15) the expected value is A$0.229, a gross 4.0%; after brokerage, half the spread and the hedge's cost (about 1.5%) that is about 2.5%, below cash if payment slips to mid-2027. Even at the card's break price, the return to a June payment is about 4.4% net, barely above cash, for a position that carries 26%-plus Lynas volatility.

The card's resolve date of 30 Apr 2027 is not realistic. It sits after the original End Date but before the extended one, so the likeliest state on 30 Apr is "still waiting on Brazil". My chance of payment by 30 Apr 2027: about 40%.

## 7. A better bid

Applies weakly. Caldeira is a rare asset, but any rival faces the same CIMCE review and Lynas has matching rights. USA Rare Earth (with a US government stake) has just bought Serra Verde, so Western interest exists, but a US-linked bidder would face a harder Brazilian review under Lula. Chance of a rival: 3%. A rival would also lengthen the timetable.

## 8. The card itself

- **Chances and prices.** 0.78 + 0.22 = 1. Break price A$0.1691 matches the deal monitor. Arithmetic of the adjustments is right: 95.6 - 10 - 3 - 2 - 2 = 78.6, rounded to 78%.
- **Is 78% justified by the adjustments?** The final number is about right; the route is not. The +5 points for "this deal's features" comes from a model in which needing an overseas approval scores +0.30 (it reflects routine foreign clearances with set procedures). That credits the deal for the very Brazilian approval the -10 then takes away. Net of that, Brazil carries about -7 to -8 points, which is fair for refusal but leaves no allowance for timing, which the card pushes into the resolve date instead. The vote adjustment (-3) partly double counts Lynas price risk, which already sits in the completion value.
- **The 62% vs 78% claim.** The 62% uses the card's break price and ignores time value. With a break of A$0.15 the market implies 69%; allowing for six to eight months of carry and the Lynas risk an arbitrageur would demand pay for, the market is close to 75%. The edge the card claims is mostly the three-day LYC bounce described in section 1.
- **Loss on break within 1%?** At the card's break price: 0.22 to 0.1691 is a 23.1% fall (the card's notes say 22%), and 4% x 23.1% = 0.92% of the portfolio. It fits, with 0.08 points of room. At my break price of A$0.15 (a 31.8% fall), 4% x 31.8% = 1.27%, over the limit. The largest stake that keeps a A$0.15 break within 1% is 3.1%. And because there is no hedge, a completion with LYC down 25% costs 4% x 15% = 0.6% on top of nothing gained, so the downside is not limited to the break case.
- **Labels.** Industry "materials" and group "Brazil CIMCE" are right. The ledger shows only core orders (VT, VAS, ENZL) waiting, so 4% sits well inside the 15% industry and 10% group limits. Liquidity is no constraint: about 16,000 shares against a 17.7m median daily volume.

## Three points most likely to make the card wrong

1. **The 14% spread is a Lynas price bounce, not a mispriced deal.** For most of October the market implied 75% to 85%, the same as the card. The gap opened only when LYC rose 16% in three sessions and MEI did not follow. With no shorting, the position is a long Lynas bet (about 45% a year volatility, down 42% in six months) whose one-sigma four-month move (26%) is nearly double the spread.
2. **Brazil timing will push payment well past the card's resolve date.** CIMCE has staff (decree of 16 Oct) but no rules, no installed council until about mid-December, monthly meetings, a required ministry opinion and no deadline. Early-March payment needs a ruling by February. I put only about 40% on payment by 30 Apr 2027; the likely resolve is near the extended End Date of about 1 Jul 2027, at which point the return is at or below the 4.8% bank bill rate after costs.
3. **The break price is too high and the 1% limit is only met on paper.** LYC, the closest peer, is down 12% since the undisturbed date, and a Brazilian refusal would impair Caldeira for any foreign buyer while leaving Meteoric owing Lynas A$35m with no funder. A break near A$0.15 puts a 4% stake's loss at 1.27% of the portfolio, over the limit. (The refusal risk itself is, if anything, lower than the card says: the Mines Minister praised the deal and Lula's win keeps him in place.)

## Verdict

**Wait for a named event:** Lynas or Meteoric announcing the CIMCE filing with an expected decision date, or a CIMCE ruling that the regime does not apply ("não incidência"), whichever comes first. This should come around the council's installation deadline (about 15 Dec 2026) and the scheme booklet (December). At that point re-run the card with a resolve date tied to the stated CIMCE timetable and a break price of no more than A$0.15, and size at no more than 3% so a break stays within 1%. If the event arrives with MEI still 10% or more below the offer value, the timing risk will have been removed for the same price and the case is much better than today.

My completion chance: 78% eventually (about 40% paid by 30 Apr 2027). My resolve date: 30 Jun 2027.
