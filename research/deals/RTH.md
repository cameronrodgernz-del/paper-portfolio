## In short
UK-based The Press Association (part of PA Media Group, owner of the Podium racing data business) signed a binding Scheme Implementation Deed on 18 October 2026, announced 19 October, to buy all of RAS Technology Holdings for A$1.04 cash a share. That is an equity value of about A$49.8 million on 47,885,899 shares, so it is under the A$100m screen but was asked for specifically. The board recommends it unanimously and the two co-founders, who hold 34.0%, intend to vote for it, so the vote is very likely to pass if the expert says the price is in shareholders' best interests. There is no FIRB, ACCC or financing condition, so timing is mainly the court and booklet process, with payment indicated for 10 February 2027. The last price was A$1.015 (bid 1.01, ask 1.015 on the ASX quote page), a gross spread of about 2.5% to A$1.04 over roughly three and a half months.

## Risks to completion
- Independent expert says the scheme is not in shareholders' best interests: low, about 5%. The price is a 93% premium to the last close (A$0.54) and 80% to the 90-day VWAP (A$0.576), and the board picked it. Would rise only if the expert's value range came in above A$1.04.
- Vote fails: low, about 3%. The co-founders' 34.0% intend to vote for it and Harvest Lane (12.60%) bought in after the announcement. A blocking stake of 25% of votes cast would need a large holder against, and none has appeared. The headcount test is a minor risk with a small register.
- Key employee condition: low to moderate, about 5% chance of a delay or renegotiation. It's an unusual condition: if a named key person leaves before the second court date, The PA can walk away. It can be waived by The PA.
- Material adverse change: low, about 3 to 5%. The A$800,000 recurring EBITDA threshold is small against a business with FY26 revenue of A$28.9m and a net loss, so a weak trading update or loss of a big data contract could test it. Watch the AGM trading comments on 20 November and any customer contract news.
- Regulatory: very low. No FIRB or ACCC condition. A UK buyer of an Australian racing data business could in theory draw interest, but no approval is a condition.
- Timing slip: moderate chance (about 20 to 25%) that the booklet or meeting moves a few weeks, for example if the expert's report takes longer over the summer holidays; this delays payment rather than breaking the deal.
- Competing bid (helps a holder): low, about 5 to 10%. Racing data has a few logical buyers, but the deal has no-shop, no-talk and matching rights, the founders' support is "in the absence of a superior proposal", and the break fee is only about A$0.5m. The share price trading below A$1.04 suggests the market isn't pricing a contest.
