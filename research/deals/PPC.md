## In short
Ingenia agreed on 26 August to buy Peet for A$0.68 cash plus 0.3367 Ingenia securities per share, but Ingenia itself has since become a takeover target. Warburg Pincus (now at A$5.45 plus up to 5 cents of distribution, A$5.50 total) and AVID Property Group (A$5.35 plus 5 cents) are competing for Ingenia, and both proposals are conditional on Ingenia terminating the Peet deal. The Peet deed lets Ingenia walk away at any time up to 8am on the second court date if its board decides a proposal for Ingenia is a Bidder Superior Proposal, and its only cost is a A$10 million reverse break fee (about 2.1 cents a Peet share); Ingenia says it has not made that decision yet. The Peet timetable is moving: the Federal Court ordered the meeting on 3 November, Kroll finds the scheme fair and reasonable, the booklet went out on 5 November, holders vote on 7 December and implementation is set for 4 January 2027. On 6 November Warburg Pincus lifted its Ingenia proposal to A$5.55 plus about 5 cents and Peet signed the Flagstone JV deed. On 10 November AVID matched at A$5.55 plus up to 5 cents and its exclusivity was extended to 14 December, after the Peet vote (7 Dec) but before the second court date (15 Dec), so the Ingenia contest may still be open when Ingenia must decide whether to walk. Peet closed at A$1.42 on 30 October against a paper value of about A$2.47, which says the market thinks the deal is unlikely to complete.

## Risks to completion
- Ingenia terminates for a Bidder Superior Proposal: about 65%. Every live proposal for Ingenia (Warburg Pincus and AVID) requires the Peet deed to be terminated. A$5.50 cash is about 28% above Ingenia's A$4.28 ten-day average used to price the Peet deal, and the Ingenia board has moved from rejecting Warburg Pincus to running a matching contest between two bidders. Ingenia keeps this option until 8am on the Peet second court date, and the most Peet can claim is A$10 million. What would lower the chance: AVID and Warburg Pincus failing to firm up (Warburg Pincus still needs 6 to 8 weeks of due diligence, debt and investment committee approval), or the Ingenia board deciding the Peet deal is worth more. What would raise it: either bidder signing a binding scheme deed with Ingenia.
- Circular value problem (not a break risk but it decides the payoff): the scrip is worth about A$1.79 at Ingenia's A$5.31 close because of the bids for Ingenia. If the Peet deal goes ahead, those bids fall away and Ingenia would likely drop back towards its pre-bid level, so the realistic "deal goes ahead" value is nearer A$2.12 (A$0.68 plus 0.3367 x A$4.28) than A$2.47. If the deal ends, Peet holders keep a standalone Peet plus about 2.1 cents a share from the reverse fee.
- Timetable stall: about 70% that the scheme is not implemented by late December 2026 even if it survives. The first court hearing has not been announced, Peet has not said why, and the 30 Oct update does not mention the scheme.
- Flagstone JV not completed: about 10% on its own. The Brown-Neaves partner still had confirmatory due diligence to do at signing.
- ACCC: about 5%. Phase 1 to 20 Nov; land development overlap only.
- Vote: about 5% if it gets to a vote. The Lennon family (16.23%, buying more on market between A$1.40 and A$1.65 from September to 28 Oct) is bound by a voting deed in favour, subject to the usual qualifications.
- Overall chance the scheme completes on current terms: roughly 15 to 25%. The A$1.42 price implies the market puts it lower than that.
- Competing bid for Peet itself (good for holders): about 5%. Peet ran a strategic review (outlined at its 27 Nov 2025 AGM) before choosing Ingenia, and no other bidder has appeared since; the Lennon family's buying is a sign of support for Peet's value but not a bid.
