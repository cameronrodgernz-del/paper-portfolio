## In short
Amari Metals Australia, related to Metro Glass's 51% holder Metro Glass Investments, gave notice on 16 November 2026 that it will make a full takeover offer at NZ$1.65 cash a share, about NZ$40.6m for the company. That is 64.2% above the NZ$1.01 close on 13 November, the last close before the notice. The 51% holder will accept within one working day of the offer going out, and that meets the only condition reported (more than 50%), so the offer should go unconditional soon after dispatch. The questions are whether the offer actually goes out on these terms (the Code requires it within 30 days of the notice) and what the independent committee and its adviser say; minority holders can't block it, but if Amari falls short of 90% the rest stay holders in a company that won't pay dividends. MPG traded about NZ$1.52 on 16 November, an 8.6% gap to the offer.

## Conditions
- Offer sent within 14 to 30 days of the 16 Nov 2026 notice: open (Takeovers Code; secondary coverage of the notice).
- Acceptances above 50%: expected to be met on dispatch by Metro Glass Investments' lock-up (51%) (TipRanks and The Bottom Line summaries of the notice, 16 Nov 2026).
- No OIO or other regulatory condition reported; to confirm from the offer document.

## Key dates
- 13 Nov 2026: last close before the notice, NZ$1.01 (b2bnews, secondary).
- 16 Nov 2026: takeover notice announced; trading halt pre-open, resumed the same morning; MPG up about 51% to NZ$1.52 (The Bottom Line, secondary).
- 30 Nov to 16 Dec 2026: window for the offer to be sent (Takeovers Code; the desk's reading, not a date from the notice).
- Target company statement with the independent adviser's report: due within the Code's period after the offer is sent. Not dated.
- 15 Jan 2027: the desk's estimate of payment; indicative only.

## Risks to completion
- Offer not sent, or sent on different terms: about 3%. A rule 41 notice binds the bidder under the Code to send an offer on terms not materially less favourable within 30 days.
- Other conditions in the offer document (financing, no material adverse change, an OIO consent if any) that could let it lapse: about 5% until the document is read.
- Rival bid: low, because the 51% holder is locked up to the Amari offer.
- Timing: payment could slip into February 2027 if the offer goes out late and the lock-up acceptance is slow to be processed.
