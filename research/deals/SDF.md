## In short
A consortium of Dragoneer and KKR (through Starboard BidCo) is buying Steadfast for A$6.00 cash less permitted dividends, with Amwins taking the underwriting agencies business straight after. The 12.75 cent FY26 dividend has already been paid, so anyone buying now gets A$5.8725 in cash in total, possibly split as A$5.80 from the scheme plus a 7.25 cent fully franked special dividend. The booklet is out, the expert (Kroll) says fair and reasonable, the vote is on 27 November and payment is scheduled for 22 December 2026. What decides the date is the stack of regulatory approvals still open (FIRB, ACCC, UK FCA, Singapore MAS and Texas), above all the ACCC, which at 16 October had only had a draft notification and was still asking questions; the NZ OIO consent is already in.

## Risks to completion
- Regulatory timing slip: about 35% chance the 1 Dec second court date moves. Five separate approvals were still open at 16 Oct. If the ACCC formal notification went in around 16 to 20 Oct, a full 30 business day Phase 1 would run to about the end of November, which is right on top of the hearing date. A slip of a few weeks would push payment into January 2027 but would not by itself break the deal; the End Date can run to about May 2027.
- Regulatory block or unacceptable conditions: about 3%. Private equity buyers with a carve-out to a US wholesale broker; the OIO has already consented and the FCA and MAS have no open questions.
- Vote fails: about 3%. Premium of 51.9% to the undisturbed price, expert says fair, and the stock trades at A$5.80 (30 Oct), only 7.25 cents under the A$5.8725 total, which suggests the market sees little vote risk. AustralianSuper at 9.65% is the holder to watch; I found no public statement from it.
- Financing: about 2%. Equity commitments from Dragoneer and KKR funds, binding Amwins commitments and third-party debt; no financing condition; reverse break fee A$66.7m.
- Material adverse change: about 2%.
- Overall chance the scheme does not complete: roughly 5 to 7%.
- Competing bid (good for holders): about 3%. The process has run since June, the board says no superior proposal has emerged, and there is a matching right and a A$66.7m break fee.
