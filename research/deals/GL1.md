## In short
Titan, part of a private UAE lithium refining group, signed a binding Scheme Implementation Deed on 22 September 2026 to buy all of Global Lithium for A$1.15 cash a share, about A$333 million fully diluted. The board recommends it and directors holding 12.5% will vote for it. Titan is also lending up to A$120 million to keep the Manna project moving during the deal. FIRB, ACCC and court approvals are still needed, with the vote expected on or about 22 December 2026 and payment in mid-January 2027. The shares last traded at A$0.925 (bid 0.89, ask 0.925), a gross spread of about 24% to A$1.15. That is wide for a recommended, unconditional-on-financing cash deal, and suggests the market doubts the bidder's ability to close, the approvals, or both.

## Risks to completion
- Bidder's ability to pay: the main risk, rough chance 15 to 20% of failure. Titan is private and its cornerstone UAE refinery is not yet producing (first production targeted for 2027). The equity commitment letter and the bridging loan help, but the market price suggests doubt. What would lower this risk: evidence of funds, such as a confirmation in the booklet that funding is in place, or Titan meeting its bridging loan drawdowns on time.
- FIRB: moderate, about 10%. A UAE buyer of an Australian lithium project with an offtake history to Chinese groups will get close attention; conditions are likely, refusal less so.
- ACCC: low, about 3%. Titan doesn't own Australian lithium assets, so a waiver or quick determination is likely. I couldn't confirm the filing on the ACCC register.
- Vote: low to moderate, about 5 to 10%. The premium was 73% to the last close of A$0.665, but large holders such as Mineral Resources (about 10% by their old notice) and Canmax haven't stated their intentions.
- Material adverse change: low, about 3%; Manna construction problems could test it.
- Timing: moderate chance (about 30%) the vote or implementation slips into February 2027, which the bridging loan terms anticipate.
- Competing bid (helps a holder): low, about 5%. Lithium developers are out of favour and the deed has no-shop, no-talk and matching rights.
