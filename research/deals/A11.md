## In short
Zhejiang Huayou Cobalt signed a binding Scheme Implementation Deed on 7 May 2026 to buy all of Atlantic Lithium for US$0.25486 cash a share, about US$210 million (about A$292 million) fully diluted. FIRB cleared it on 17 September and the first court hearing is set for 5 November 2026, so the booklet and the vote should follow in November or December. The deal still needs Chinese outbound approvals, ECOWAS competition clearance and several Ghana items (securities regulator, tax ruling, the Barari subsidiary transaction), which is where delay or failure would come from. The price is fixed in US dollars, so the A$ amount depends on the exchange rate: at the RBA rate of 0.7040 on 30 October 2026 it is about A$0.362, against a last trade of A$0.32 (bid 0.32, ask 0.325), a gross spread of about 13%. A spread that wide says the market is pricing real risk on timing or the foreign approvals.

## Risks to completion
- Chinese approvals (MOFCOM, NDRC, SAFE): moderate, about 10% chance of failure and 30% chance of delay past December. These outbound filings for a Ghana lithium mine are usually granted for a large listed buyer like Huayou, but timing is out of the holders' control. Watch for an announcement that PRC approvals are received.
- Ghana items (SEC exemption, tax ruling, Barari transaction): moderate, about 10% chance of failure or renegotiation, higher chance of delay. Ghana's government holds rights over the mining lease and has its own interest in who owns it. The tax ruling has to be "acceptable to the Bidder", which gives Huayou some room.
- ECOWAS clearance: low, about 3%, mainly a timing item.
- Independent expert and vote: low, about 5%. The premium was only 26.6% to the last close (A$0.280), and lithium prices move a lot, so if the expert's value range rises above the price the deal could stall. Assore's 26.4% support helps with the vote.
- End date: the end date of 7 March 2027 leaves room for a slip of about three months from the December target before either side can walk.
- Currency: the price is in US dollars. A rise in the Australian dollar lowers the A$ amount received; this is a hedging matter for the portfolio, not a break risk.
- Overall, the 13% spread looks wider than these risks alone would justify. My reading is that the market is pricing the foreign approvals plus timing.
- Competing bid (helps a holder): low, about 5%. Huayou now holds the joint venture partner's rights in Ewoyaa, which makes a rival bid hard to put together.
