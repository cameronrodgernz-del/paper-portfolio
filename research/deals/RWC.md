## In short
Brookfield Capital Partners is buying Reliance Worldwide for US$3.38 cash a share, paid in Australian dollars unless a holder elects US dollars. The process has moved much faster than the "Q1 2027" first flagged: the ACCC, US and German approvals are already in, the booklet was registered on 30 October, the meeting is on 4 December and payment is scheduled for 18 December 2026, so the ticking fee from 1 April 2027 is now unlikely to matter. The go-shop ran to 16 October and produced no rival bid. The thing that decides this deal is the vote: AustralianSuper has built an 18.07% stake, buying at about A$4.33 as recently as 22 October, well below the roughly A$4.81 on offer, and it was reported in August to oppose the deal. The share price of A$4.39 on 30 October, about 9% under the offer value, shows the market takes that risk seriously.

## Risks to completion
- Vote fails because of AustralianSuper: about 30%. AustralianSuper holds 18.07% and has kept buying, including about 14.2 million shares at about A$4.33 on 21 to 22 Oct, which is not what a holder planning to accept would usually do at a 10% discount. A Mergermarket report of 28 Aug (secondary) cited a source saying the fund was "clearly opposed" to the proposal. At the 23 Oct AGM, 109,025,146 votes were cast against the Chair, very close to the 109,812,203 shares AustralianSuper held before 20 Oct; that fits AustralianSuper voting against him, but it is my inference, not disclosed. On AGM turnout (about 597 million votes cast) AustralianSuper's current stake would be roughly 21 to 23% of votes cast, so it needs only a few per cent more against to pass the 25% blocking line. What would lower the chance: AustralianSuper saying it will vote for, Brookfield raising the price, or a heavy turnout of other holders. What would raise it: another large holder (for example Allan Gray, which has been buying at about A$4.34) siding with AustralianSuper.
- Brookfield raises the price to win the vote (good for holders): about 25%. Brookfield has already raised three times (A$4.15, then A$4.25, A$4.50 and A$4.75, the last converted to US$3.38). The independent expert's top value is US$3.45, only 7 US cents above the offer.
- Remaining regulators (FIRB, Ukraine): about 3% chance of blocking; about 15% chance of pushing the meeting or the second hearing back by a few weeks.
- Financing: about 2%. US$1.5 billion equity commitment from Brookfield Capital Partners VII and US$1.65 billion of committed debt; equity is limited to the reverse break fee if the debt does not fund.
- Material adverse change: about 3%. FY27 guidance was below market expectations in August but was reconfirmed on 23 Oct.
- Overall chance the scheme does not complete on current terms: roughly 30 to 35%. If it fails, the expert and the board both point to a lower standalone price (undisturbed close was A$3.61 on 17 Aug).
- Rival bid: about 3%. The go-shop tested strategic and financial buyers and produced nothing.
- Currency (a payoff point, not a break risk): the AUD amount depends on the AUD/USD rate locked in by Reliance's forward contract before implementation. At 0.7031 (26 Oct) US$3.38 is about A$4.81; at 0.6947 (16 Oct) it was A$4.87.
