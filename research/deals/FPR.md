## In short
SG Fleet, controlled by Pacific Equity Partners funds, has agreed to buy FleetPartners by scheme for A$5.30 cash per share, less any FY26 final dividend (up to 14 cents) and special dividend (up to 12 cents) paid before completion. The price implies an equity value of about A$1.131 billion and came out of a contested auction against ORIX, a Sumitomo consortium and Element. The deal is at the start: no booklet, expert or regulator filings have been announced. The main question is competition review, because SG Fleet and FleetPartners are direct rivals in fleet management, leasing and salary packaging in both Australia and New Zealand; the ACCC and NZ Commerce Commission reviews will decide both whether and when holders are paid. There is no reverse break fee, and the last price was A$5.11, about 3.6% below A$5.30.

## Conditions
- FIRB (clause 3.1(a)(1), cannot be waived): no objection notice, or the Treasurer is time-barred. Status: not announced. FIRB does not publish pending applications.
- NZ OIO (clause 3.1(a)(2), cannot be waived): all consents under the Overseas Investment Act 2005 and Regulations, on terms acceptable to SG Fleet. Status: no decision on the LINZ decisions list as at 1 Nov 2026. Under the reforms in force since 6 Mar 2026, the statutory timeframe on the new consent pathway (which covers significant business assets) is 15 working days, and LINZ aims to assess most applications within 5 working days.
- NZCC (clause 3.1(a)(3), SG Fleet can waive): either a standard-form letter that the NZCC does not intend to look further after a confidential notification, or clearance or authorisation under s66 or s67 of the Commerce Act 1986. Status: no case register entry found; a confidential notification would not appear publicly.
- ACCC (clause 3.1(a)(4), cannot be waived): waiver determination, a determination that the acquisition may go ahead (including deemed), a public benefit determination, or a Tribunal or Federal Court outcome. Status: not announced. Under the ACCC's process guide, Phase 1 is up to 30 Business Days and Phase 2 up to 90 Business Days, each subject to extension.
- Shareholder approval (clause 3.1(b), cannot be waived): meeting expected February 2027.
- Independent expert (clause 3.1(c), FleetPartners' benefit): report must conclude best interests before ASIC registers the booklet. Status: not released.
- Court approval (clause 3.1(d), cannot be waived): pending.
- Restraints (clause 3.1(e)): no order, investigation or application restraining the deal or requiring divestment. Note this is broader than usual: an investigation or application to a Government Agency that could restrain the deal is enough to trip it.
- No FleetPartners Prescribed Occurrence, Regulated Event or Material Adverse Change (clauses 3.1(f) to (h), SG Fleet can waive): no issue announced. Regulated Events include buy-backs (FleetPartners had a daily buy-back running until early August 2026), asset deals over A$5m, capex over A$6m, legal settlements over A$2m, and funding warehouse stop or amortisation events.
- Relevant consents (clause 3.1(i), SG Fleet can waive): change of control consents from counterparties to listed contracts, including certain warehouse funding contracts. Status: not announced.
- No financing or due diligence condition. Funding is from existing cash, debt and PEP fund equity.

## Risks to completion
- ACCC competition review (main risk): SG Fleet and FleetPartners are two of the larger listed-company fleet management and novated leasing providers in Australia, so this is a horizontal deal that could go to Phase 2. If notified in November 2026, Phase 1 (up to 30 Business Days) would end around late December 2026 or January 2027; a Phase 2 (up to 90 more Business Days) would push a decision to around mid-2027, close to the 30 Jun 2027 End Date and well past the March 2027 target. Rough chance of Phase 2: 40%; rough chance the ACCC blocks it or the parties abandon it: 15%. What moves it: the ACCC register entry, any market inquiry letter, and whether SG Fleet offers remedies.
- NZ Commerce Commission: both groups operate in New Zealand fleet leasing. The condition can be waived by SG Fleet, but the NZCC could still act. Rough chance of material delay: 10%.
- FIRB and OIO: OIO has a 15 working day statutory timeframe on the new pathway; SG Fleet obtained OIO consent for its LeasePlan NZ purchase in 2021. Rough chance either delays implementation past March 2027: 10%; chance either stops the deal: 2%.
- No reverse break fee: if regulators block the deal, holders get nothing from SG Fleet, and the undisturbed price was A$2.83 (31 Jul 2026), so the downside is large compared with the 3.6% spread.
- Funding warehouse and change of control consents, and the wide Regulated Event list: rough chance one of these causes a problem: 5%.
- Shareholder vote and expert: 87.3% premium to the undisturbed price after an auction. Rough chance of failure: 3%.
- Competing bid (helps holders): ORIX and the Sumitomo consortium both reached A$4.65 and have done due diligence; either could return, especially if SG Fleet runs into competition trouble, since they may face fewer overlap issues. SG Fleet has matching rights and the reimbursement fee is only A$11.2m. Rough chance of a rival proposal: 10%.
