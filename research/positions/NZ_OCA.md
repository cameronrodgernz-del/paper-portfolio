## The values

- Cash value, the model's own calls: NZ$0.854 (Executive Summary F8), on a 9.18% discount rate. Lower bond yields added 11 cents to the 28 October figure and the repairs took 10 off.
- Cash value, the reviewer's case: NZ$0.774, with five inputs set to the reviewer's own calls (savings that last, care uplift, unit turnover, bonds at fair value, an ongoing Holidays Act cost).
- With no building after FY31: NZ$0.09 on the model's calls. About four-fifths of either cash value is terminal value and building after FY31.
- What the sector trades at (Ryman 0.46 and Summerset 0.43 times asset value): NZ$0.72. Asset value (NTA): NZ$1.62 a share, so the price is 0.41 times.
- Expected price: about NZ$0.68 at 30 June 2027 and NZ$0.74 at 30 June 2028 (NZ$0.70 without a takeover), about 7% a year against a required return of 10.8%. The price that pays the required return is about NZ$0.63, which is why this is a hold until the two November events and not a long-term hold at NZ$0.66.

## Dates to watch

| Date | Event | Good looks like | Bad looks like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 19 Nov 2026 | Half-year result | Cash from operations near break-even before collections; unsold new stock at or under 200 units; asset value at or above NZ$1.60 | Cash still well negative; receivables not collected; property values or the care book marked down |
| Late Nov 2026 | Ryman half-year | Resales above departures; valuer inputs unchanged | Valuer cuts its price growth assumptions |
| 27 Nov 2026 | Final election count, then coalition talks | Current government returns; nine-month repayment rule for new contracts only (about 1 cent) | A three-month rule that covers existing contracts (6 to 8 cents) |
| May 2027 | Full-year result | First dividend, however small | Dividend pushed out again |
| 19 Oct 2027 | NZ$125m bond at 2.30% matures | Refinanced near 5.6%, about NZ$4m more interest a year | Needs an asset sale or new shares to repay |

## What would change the view

Better: cash from operations positive without collections, a first dividend, the nine-month repayment rule confirmed, or a bid. Worse: stock building up, property values marked down on 19 November, or a three-month repayment rule on existing contracts.
